**Related Works**

The literature on probability calibration can be broadly categorized into parametric and non-parametric post-hoc methods, as well as joint optimization frameworks that integrate calibration into the training process. Parametric approaches typically employ fixed functional forms to map raw scores to probabilities. Early methods include Platt scaling [2][3] and Beta calibration [5], which use logistic or beta distributions to adjust classifier outputs. More recent work has explored distribution-specific parametric calibrators, such as Gaussian and Gamma calibration for personalized ranking models [6]. While these methods are computationally efficient, they often suffer from limited expressiveness, as their fixed forms may not capture complex, non-linear relationships between raw scores and true probabilities in diverse data distributions [4].

Non-parametric and flexible functional approaches aim to overcome the rigidity of parametric models by learning arbitrary mapping functions. Binning methods, such as Isotonic Regression and its variants, offer distribution-free guarantees but often sacrifice individual sample resolution by mapping multiple inputs to the same output value [1]. To address this, tree-based methods like Multiple Boosting Calibration Trees (MBCT) incorporate feature awareness to improve binning schemes [1]. In the context of online advertising, field-aware calibration methods have emerged to handle data sparsity and distribution shifts. Neural Calibration leverages field-aware information to learn post-hoc adjustments [9], while AdaCalib employs a doubly-adaptive approach using isotonic function families guided by posterior statistics [10]. Further advancements include Confidence-Aware Multi-Field Calibration, which adjusts calibration intensity based on sample confidence to mitigate bias amplification [7], and Deep Ensemble Shape Calibration (DESC), which decomposes multi-field calibration to enhance data utilization in e-commerce scenarios [12]. Despite their flexibility, these post-hoc methods often operate independently of the ranking objective, potentially leading to sub-optimal trade-offs between ranking accuracy and calibration quality [11].

A third line of research focuses on joint optimization, where calibration is integrated directly into the ranking model’s training via multi-objective loss functions. Scale Calibration of Deep Ranking Models introduces techniques to ensure that ranking scores are also scale-calibrated [13]. Subsequent works have proposed various strategies to balance pointwise regression losses (for calibration) and pairwise or listwise ranking losses. For instance, JRC jointly optimizes ranking and calibration by constraining predicted probabilities to be functions of logit subtractions, preserving the interpretability of logits [14]. Similarly, Regression Compatible Ranking (RCR) ensures that ranking and regression components are mutually aligned to improve the Pareto frontier of performance [15]. Other approaches, such as the Self-boosted Framework for Calibrated Ranking, address the conflict between conflicting loss functions and the inefficiency of aggregating full candidate lists in mini-batches [11]. Additionally, Calibration-compatible Listwise Distillation extends privileged features distillation to include listwise objectives that respect relative item order [16], and Bayesian approaches have been explored for joint optimization beyond binary preferences [17]. However, many of these joint optimization methods still rely on predefined transformation structures or suffer from the inherent tension between optimizing for relative order and absolute accuracy, limiting their ability to learn highly flexible, unconstrained monotonic mappings without distorting the original predictions [11][14][15].
