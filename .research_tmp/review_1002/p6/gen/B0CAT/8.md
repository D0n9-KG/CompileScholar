**Related Works**

**Fairness Evaluation in Large Language Models**
Research on fairness in Large Language Models (LLMs) encompasses holistic evaluation frameworks, taxonomic surveys, and specialized bias benchmarks. Holistic frameworks like HELM [1] and BIG-bench [13] evaluate multiple metrics including fairness, while surveys categorize fairness research into intrinsic and extrinsic biases for medium and large-sized models [3][6]. Specific benchmarks target social biases in question answering [16], open-ended generation [14], and compositional tasks [7]. Gender bias in coreference resolution has been extensively studied using datasets like WinoBias [11][12], Winogender schemas [12], and large-scale real-world corpora [17]. Recent work confirms that LLMs amplify gender stereotypes in occupation predictions [15] and that intrinsic biases persist through prompt adaptations [4]. Despite these advances, conventional metrics often focus on discrete accuracy, failing to capture the nuanced relationship between model confidence and group-specific performance [3][6].

**Uncertainty Quantification in Language Models**
Uncertainty quantification (UQ) in LLMs involves exploring sources of uncertainty, developing estimation methods, and benchmarking these techniques. Surveys categorize uncertainty sources into input, system, and output types [19] and distinguish between aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty [18][22]. Estimation methods include semantic entropy [26], attention-based heuristics [27], and self-evaluation via probability calibration [28]. Benchmarks such as LM-polygraph [21][30] and exploratory studies [20] assess the reliability of these methods. Recent work highlights that UQ evaluation can be distorted by spurious interactions with response length [31]. However, most existing UQ research focuses on general reliability or hallucination detection rather than integrating uncertainty specifically into fairness metrics to reveal hidden biases [8][20].

**Intersection of Fairness and Uncertainty**
The intersection of fairness and uncertainty is an emerging area that leverages uncertainty signals to improve or evaluate fair decision-making. Some approaches use aleatoric uncertainty to balance fairness and utility in classification tasks [35], while others propose uncertainty-aware predictive modeling for fair data-driven decisions [34]. Recent studies investigate whether uncertainty can serve as a direct fairness measure [33] and examine the impact of debiasing on model reliability [32]. Additionally, fairness dynamics during training have been monitored using metrics like Average Rank and Jensen-Shannon Divergence to detect sudden bias emergence [5]. Despite these contributions, there is a lack of fine-grained, uncertainty-aware fairness metrics specifically designed for modern LLMs that can detect confidence disparities between groups even when accuracy is similar [33][34].

**Limitations of Current Datasets**
Current datasets for fairness evaluation suffer from issues related to size, diversity, and clarity. While large-scale datasets like BUG [17] and BOLD [14] offer diversity, many standard benchmarks like WinoBias [11] are small and synthetic, potentially leading to overfitting or lack of generalizability [17]. Surveys of algorithmic fairness datasets highlight a "documentation debt" and sparsity of information [9]. Furthermore, existing bias evaluation efforts often employ inconsistent metrics, making cross-dataset comparison difficult [7]. This gap motivates the need for new, diverse datasets tailored for modern LLMs, such as the gender-occupation co-reference resolution dataset proposed in this work, to ensure robust and comparable fairness evaluations [7][9].
