**Related Works**

Classical iterative optimization methods for inverse problems, including gradient projection [1], iterative hard thresholding [2], fast iterative shrinkage-thresholding [3], and alternating direction methods [4], established the foundational algorithms for sparse reconstruction and compressed sensing. These approaches rely on explicit regularization terms, such as $\ell_1$-norms or nonlocal low-rank constraints [5], to guide the recovery of structured signals. While theoretically grounded, these methods often require careful tuning of parameters and suffer from slow convergence rates, particularly when dealing with complex, non-convex regularization objectives.

Plug-and-Play (PnP) approaches leverage off-the-shelf denoisers to implicitly perform regularization within iterative schemes, extending frameworks like Approximate Message Passing (D-AMP) [6] and Regularization by Denoising (RED) [28]. Recent advancements have sought to ensure convergence by modeling the denoiser as a gradient step [7] or a proximal operator [8], while others utilize stochastic restoration priors to handle structured artifacts [10]. Despite their flexibility in handling varying compression ratios, PnP methods are generally limited in reconstruction accuracy and computational speed compared to learned end-to-end models, and their convergence guarantees often depend on restrictive assumptions about the denoiser’s properties.

Deep unrolling networks transform truncated iterative optimization processes into trainable neural networks, offering improved accuracy and inference speed. Representative architectures include non-iterative CNNs like ReconNet [11], scalable CNNs [12], and deep residual networks [13]. Optimization-inspired unrolling frameworks have been developed for specific algorithms, such as Learned D-AMP [14], ISTA-Net [15], and ADMM-CSNet [16]. More recent works incorporate advanced components to address information loss and global context, including memory-augmented modules [18], controllable arbitrary-sampling networks [19], generalized unfolding with gradient estimation [20], hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures [21], cross-attention transformers [22], spatial-attention unfolding [23], fixed-point continuous networks [24], and multi-scale feature fusion [25]. In the specific domain of single-pixel imaging, dual-scale transformer-based unrolling networks have been proposed to handle large-scale reconstructions [26]. However, a significant limitation of these CR-specific unrolling networks is their lack of generalizability; they typically require fine-tuning or complete retraining when the compression ratio changes, limiting their practical deployment in dynamic imaging scenarios.

Learned proximal networks aim to bridge the gap between data-driven models and optimization theory by ensuring that the learned restoration operator approximates the proximal operator of an explicit regularizer. This approach provides a framework where the network’s output is guaranteed to correspond to a valid proximal mapping, facilitating convergence analysis and interpretability [9]. By training the network to match the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer, these methods offer a principled alternative to black-box denoisers. Nevertheless, the challenge remains to integrate these proximal guarantees into efficient, high-performance unrolling architectures that can flexibly handle varying compression ratios without sacrificing the speed and accuracy advantages of deep unrolling.
