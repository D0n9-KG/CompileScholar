**Related Works**

Sequential recommendation has evolved significantly from early recurrent neural network (RNN) approaches to modern Transformer-based architectures. Early foundational work applied RNNs to model session-based user behaviors, introducing ranking loss functions to improve viability over matrix factorization [11]. Subsequent studies enhanced these models by personalizing RNNs with cross-session information transfer via hierarchical structures [6] and by employing self-attention mechanisms to balance long-term semantics with sparse prediction capabilities, as seen in SASRec [8]. The field further advanced with bidirectional encoders like BERT4Rec, which utilized Cloze tasks to overcome the limitations of unidirectional left-to-right processing [9]. More recent Transformer variants have addressed specific structural challenges, including sparse attention for efficiency [4], personalized transformers [3], and multi-behavior modeling [5]. Additionally, frequency-domain enhancements have been introduced to capture periodic characteristics obscured in the time domain [1]. Despite these advancements, standard attention mechanisms suffer from quadratic computational complexity, which becomes prohibitive for long-term user sequences, and often fail to effectively distinguish high-frequency information or inherent periodicities in user behavior [1][10].

To address the computational inefficiency of attention, recent research has explored linear attention mechanisms and state-space models (SSMs). Linear attention approaches, such as LinRec, theoretically improve efficiency by reducing complexity to linear while preserving learning capabilities [10]. Concurrently, the Mamba architecture has emerged as a powerful alternative, utilizing selective state spaces to perform content-based reasoning with near-linear scalability [12]. Theoretical frameworks have further demonstrated the close relationship between Transformers and SSMs through structured state space duality, suggesting that these architectures can be unified [14]. In the context of recommendation, Mamba-based models have been applied to various domains. Mamba4Rec was the first to explore selective SSMs for efficient sequential recommendation, outperforming RNN and attention baselines in both effectiveness and efficiency [20]. Other works have investigated bidirectional gated Mamba [18], spectral filtering with bidirectional SSMs [16], and applications in knowledge tracing [17]. Furthermore, specific studies have uncovered the capabilities of selective SSMs in handling lifelong sequential recommendation, demonstrating significant reductions in training duration for sequences exceeding 2,000 items [15][19]. While these SSM-based methods offer superior efficiency, they often focus primarily on temporal dynamics and may not fully capture the diverse, category-specific interaction patterns or the nuanced distinction between similar and diverse item relationships that attention mechanisms provide [14][20].

Our work, STAR-Rec, builds upon these foundations by synergistically combining preference-aware attention with state-space modeling. Unlike previous approaches that treat attention and SSMs as competing alternatives [12][14], we propose a sequence-level mixture-of-experts framework. This design allows the model to adaptively route different behavioral patterns to specialized experts, addressing the limitation of single-architecture models in handling both focused category-specific browsing and diverse exploration [5][16]. By unifying the temporal compression capabilities of SSMs with the relational modeling of attention, STAR-Rec aims to resolve the trade-off between computational efficiency and the ability to capture complex, diverse user preferences [10][14].
