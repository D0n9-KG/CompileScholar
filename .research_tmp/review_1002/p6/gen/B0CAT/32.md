**Related Works**

**Representation Similarity Metrics**
Research into the internal dynamics of neural networks has heavily relied on quantitative metrics for comparing learned representations across layers and models. Early foundational work introduced Singular Vector Canonical Correlation Analysis (SVCCA) to measure representational similarity in a manner invariant to affine transformations, enabling the analysis of learning dynamics and intrinsic dimensionality [2]. Subsequent studies refined these approaches by developing Projection Weighted CCA to better distinguish signal from noise in representation comparisons [1]. A critical advancement came with the introduction of Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), which addresses the limitations of CCA-based methods in high-dimensional spaces and provides a robust measure for identifying correspondences between representations from different initializations [3]. More recent applications have extended these dissimilarity measures to language models, revealing insights into activation function asymmetries and generalization properties that are not visible through standard performance metrics [6]. While these tools provide a rigorous framework for measuring representational change, they often require data-dependent analyses, limiting their applicability to post-hoc settings rather than intrinsic, data-oblivious model characterization.

**Layer-Specific Functional Roles in LLMs**
A distinct line of inquiry focuses on identifying which specific layers within Large Language Models (LLMs) are responsible for particular functions or behaviors. Studies have demonstrated that factual associations in autoregressive transformers are localized in middle-layer feed-forward modules, allowing for direct editing of these computations [7]. Similarly, research into truthfulness has shown that internal hidden layer activations can be used to classify the veracity of statements, indicating that specific layers encode truthfulness signals [8]. In the context of knowledge injection, recent work has highlighted the disproportionate importance of shallow layers, observing that removing or expanding these layers significantly impacts performance, whereas deeper layers are less critical for knowledge retention [9]. Furthermore, probing experiments have suggested a "Concept Depth" hierarchy, where simpler concepts are processed in shallow layers and more complex inferential tasks require deeper layers [11]. Other investigations into intermediate representations have found that these layers often yield more informative features for downstream tasks than final layers, exhibiting specific entropy patterns tied to training data [12]. Despite these insights, many of these findings are derived from fine-tuned models or specific task probes, leaving a gap in understanding the intrinsic critical layers of pre-fine-tuned models without relying on task-specific data.

**Structural Dynamics and Practical Applications**
Beyond identifying specific functional roles, researchers have examined the structural evolution of representations during training and fine-tuning. Analyses of fine-tuned transformers have revealed a block diagonal structure in representation similarity, where earlier and later layers form distinct clusters, suggesting that later layers contribute marginally to task performance and can sometimes be pruned [5]. This structural perspective has been applied to practical scenarios such as efficient domain adaptation and security. For instance, layer-specific editing strategies have been proposed to defend LLMs against jailbreak attacks by targeting specific layers [10]. Additionally, understanding how representations vary with width and depth has provided insights into network over-parameterization and convergence dynamics [4]. However, these approaches typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, which limits their utility for identifying intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs. Consequently, there remains a need for data-oblivious methods that can identify layers susceptible to change during fine-tuning based solely on the model's pre-trained representation dynamics.
