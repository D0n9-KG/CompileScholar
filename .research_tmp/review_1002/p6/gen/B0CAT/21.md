**Related Works**

Video Frame Interpolation (VFI) has evolved significantly, encompassing a diverse range of methodological families including kernel-, flow-, hybrid-, phase-, GAN-, Transformer-, Mamba-, and diffusion model-based approaches [1]. Early deep learning methods often relied on explicit motion compensation, such as depth-aware flow projection to handle occlusions and large object motion [2]. More recent advancements focus on efficiency and real-time performance; for instance, RIFE employs intermediate flow estimation to achieve high-speed interpolation without relying on pre-trained optical flow models [3]. To address the computational overhead of multi-frame synthesis, M2M splatting utilizes bidirectional flows to directly warp pixels, minimizing the need for subsequent refinement [4]. Furthermore, hybrid architectures that integrate CNNs with Transformers have been developed to efficiently extract inter-frame motion and appearance via unified attention mechanisms [5]. Despite these advances, VFI methods generally face challenges in handling large motions, occlusions, and non-linear camera movements, which can degrade spatial and temporal coherence in complex scenes [1][2].

Neural rendering techniques, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, have become dominant for 3D scene reconstruction from 2D images. These methods optimize scene geometry and appearance by minimizing photometric discrepancies across multiple input views [9]. However, the quality of reconstruction is inherently limited by the density and coverage of the input views, a limitation that is particularly pronounced in dynamic or complex environments where certain object angles remain unseen [9]. To mitigate this, several studies have explored the use of learned priors to regularize the reconstruction process. For example, DiffusioNeRF utilizes a denoising diffusion model trained on synthetic data to predict gradients of the log-likelihood of color and depth patches, thereby regularizing under-constrained geometry and color fields [9]. Similarly, 3DGS-Enhancer leverages view-consistent 2D diffusion priors to enhance unbounded 3D Gaussian Splatting, addressing artifacts in sparse view settings [10]. While these prior-based methods improve robustness, they often require additional training data or complex optimization procedures, and may not fully capture the specific dynamics of real-world video sequences [9][10].

In the context of autonomous driving and large-scale scene reconstruction, specific neural rendering variants have been tailored to handle the unique challenges of outdoor environments. NeuRAD proposes a robust novel view synthesis method that incorporates extensive sensor modeling, including rolling shutter and beam divergence, to handle dynamic driving data [6]. Lightning NeRF introduces an efficient hybrid scene representation that leverages LiDAR geometry priors to improve novel view synthesis quality and reduce computational overheads in driving scenarios [7]. Additionally, AligNeRF addresses high-resolution reconstruction by integrating convolutional layers with MLPs and employing alignment-aware training strategies to handle misaligned input data caused by moving objects or calibration errors [8]. ViewCrafter extends this line of work by utilizing video diffusion models to synthesize high-fidelity novel views from sparse images, facilitating applications in immersive experiences and text-to-3D generation [11]. Although these methods achieve state-of-the-art performance in their respective domains, they often rely on dense semantic supervision, specific sensor modalities, or iterative view synthesis strategies that may limit their generalizability to arbitrary static and dynamic scenes without extensive domain-specific tuning [6][7][11].
