Recent research in cloud-native environments has increasingly focused on optimizing resource allocation and cost efficiency within containerized systems, particularly those orchestrated by Kubernetes. This body of work can be broadly categorized into adaptive resource management strategies and hierarchical multi-agent optimization frameworks. Adaptive resource management approaches, such as those leveraging Kubernetes for dynamic scaling, aim to align resource provisioning with real-time application demands to minimize waste [1]. These systems typically monitor metrics like CPU and memory utilization to adjust pod replicas or resource requests, thereby reducing the financial overhead associated with over-provisioning. Concurrently, hierarchical multi-agent optimization methods have been proposed to address the complexity of resource allocation in large-scale cloud computing environments [2]. These frameworks decompose the global optimization problem into sub-problems managed by distinct agents, allowing for more granular control over resource distribution across different tiers of the infrastructure. While both adaptive management and hierarchical optimization offer significant improvements in resource efficiency, they predominantly focus on compute and memory resources. A shared limitation across these approaches is the insufficient attention paid to network costs, which are often treated as a secondary concern or assumed to be negligible compared to compute expenses. Consequently, these methods may fail to capture the full financial impact of inter-pod and inter-node traffic, leaving a gap in comprehensive cost optimization for cloud-native applications.

The integration of financial management with cloud operations, known as FinOps, has emerged as a critical discipline for addressing the economic challenges of cloud adoption. Although the provided references do not explicitly detail FinOps-specific methodologies, the underlying goal of cost-efficient resource management aligns with the core principles of this emerging field. The work on leveraging Kubernetes for adaptive resource management implicitly supports FinOps objectives by providing mechanisms to control variable costs associated with compute resources [1]. Similarly, hierarchical multi-agent optimization contributes to the operational aspect of FinOps by enabling automated, policy-driven decisions that can be aligned with organizational budget constraints [2]. However, the current literature in this area, as represented by these references, lacks a holistic view that incorporates all major cost components of cloud-native architectures. Specifically, the network dimension of cloud spending remains underexplored in these optimization frameworks. This omission is significant because network egress and data transfer fees can constitute a substantial portion of cloud bills, especially for applications with high data throughput or cross-region communication. Therefore, while existing optimization strategies provide a foundation for cost control, they do not yet offer a complete solution for organizations seeking to optimize their total cloud expenditure, particularly in scenarios where network usage is a dominant cost driver.

In summary, the existing literature on resource allocation in Kubernetes and cloud computing environments has made substantial progress in optimizing compute and memory resources through adaptive and hierarchical approaches. These methods have demonstrated their value in reducing operational costs and improving resource utilization [1][2]. Nevertheless, a critical gap persists in the analysis of network costs within these systems. The current focus on compute-centric metrics means that the financial implications of network traffic, such as data transfer charges and bandwidth usage, are not adequately addressed. This limitation hinders the ability of organizations to achieve true cost optimization in cloud-native deployments. Future research must extend these optimization frameworks to include network cost modeling, thereby providing a more comprehensive approach to FinOps in Kubernetes environments. By integrating network cost considerations into adaptive and hierarchical resource management strategies, researchers can offer more actionable insights for organizations aiming to balance performance, scalability, and financial efficiency in their cloud operations.
