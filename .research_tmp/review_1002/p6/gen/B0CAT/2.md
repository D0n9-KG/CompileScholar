**Related Works**

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have recently advanced the integration of visual and textual data, with representative approaches including vision-language models for understanding and localization [1], chat-centric video understanding systems [2], and instruction-tuned audio-visual language models [3]. More recent efforts have expanded this scope to include audio and action modalities, such as Unified-IO 2, which unifies diverse modalities into a shared semantic space for autoregressive generation [4]. While these models demonstrate strong capabilities in spatiotemporal reasoning and general multimodal understanding [2][4], they primarily focus on high-level semantic alignment and often lack the fine-grained, time-aligned control necessary for generating specific nonverbal cues like precise facial expressions or body gestures in real-time conversational contexts.

Research on nonverbal communication and emotion recognition has traditionally relied on specialized datasets and models. Key resources include IEMOCAP for interactive emotional dyadic motion capture [6], CMU-MOSEI for multimodal language analysis in the wild [7], and MELD for multi-party emotion recognition in conversations [8]. Recent works have sought to learn emotion representations from uncurated data, such as EmotionCLIP, which extracts visual emotion representations from verbal and nonverbal communication [12]. Additionally, studies have focused on recognizing nonverbal cues for accessibility [11]. Despite these advancements, these approaches are often limited to classification or recognition tasks rather than generative modeling, and they frequently struggle to capture the dynamic, continuous nature of nonverbal behavior in open-ended conversational flows.

The generation of nonverbal cues, particularly facial expressions and body motions, has been addressed through various generative frameworks. Methods like EMOCA focus on emotion-driven monocular face capture and animation to ensure high-fidelity expression reconstruction [15]. Other works target holistic motion generation, such as generating 3D human motion from speech [16] or text-aligned whole-body motion generation using hierarchical VQ-VAEs [18]. Furthermore, models have been developed to generate listener responses, such as facial gestures from speaker text [19] or non-deterministic dyadic facial motion [20]. These approaches typically treat nonverbal generation as a separate task from language generation, often requiring distinct architectures for motion synthesis and text processing, which limits the seamless integration of verbal and nonverbal streams.

Recent efforts have begun to bridge the gap between language models and nonverbal generation, often in the context of embodied agents or social robots. Systems like FurChat utilize LLMs to generate dialogue alongside facial expressions for embodied conversational agents [13], while other works propose augmenting social robots with empathetic non-verbal cues generated by LLMs [14]. Additionally, CHAMPAGNE introduces a generative model of conversations that accounts for visual contexts using large-scale web video data [9], and "Let's Go Real Talk" presents a face-to-face spoken dialogue model that processes and generates audio-visual speech [10]. However, these systems often rely on prompt engineering or separate modules for nonverbal generation, rather than a unified framework that natively understands and generates nonverbal cues as part of the language modeling process.

In contrast to these fragmented approaches, our work introduces MARS, a unified multimodal language model that integrates nonverbal cues directly into the next-token prediction objective. By leveraging the VENUS dataset, which provides time-aligned text, facial expressions, and body language, MARS achieves a cohesive understanding and generation of both verbal and nonverbal languages, addressing the limitations of existing models that treat these modalities in isolation.
