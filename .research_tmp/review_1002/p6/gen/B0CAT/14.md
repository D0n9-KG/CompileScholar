**Related Works**

Video super-resolution (VSR) has evolved through several architectural paradigms, primarily categorized into recurrent, transformer-based, and state-space model approaches. Early recurrent methods, such as the Recurrent Structure-Detail Network [4], leverage hidden states to propagate temporal information, though they often suffer from error accumulation and artifact propagation in real-world scenarios [14]. To address alignment and fusion challenges, methods like EDVR [9] and MuCAN [11] introduced deformable convolutions and multi-correspondence aggregation, respectively, while BasicVSR [2] and its enhanced version BasicVSR++ [3] streamlined the pipeline by focusing on essential components like propagation and alignment. More recently, Transformer-based architectures have gained prominence for their ability to model long-range dependencies. VRT [7] and RVRT [8] utilize parallel frame prediction and recurrent structures, respectively, to balance efficiency and temporal modeling, whereas the Video Super-Resolution Transformer [1] adapts self-attention for spatial-temporal locality. Despite these advancements, traditional attention mechanisms incur high computational costs for long sequences, and recurrent structures remain vulnerable to the propagation of complex degradations in real-world videos [13][14].

To overcome the computational inefficiency of attention mechanisms, recent research has shifted toward State Space Models (SSMs), including structured state spaces [15], simplified S5 layers [16], and the selective Mamba architecture [17]. Mamba introduces content-based selectivity to SSMs, enabling efficient long-sequence modeling without the quadratic complexity of Transformers. This paradigm has been extended to multi-dimensional data via Mamba-ND [18] and to visual representation learning through Vision Mamba (Vim) [19], which employs bidirectional state space blocks to achieve superior efficiency and performance compared to vision Transformers. While these models offer significant computational advantages, their application to video restoration tasks, particularly in handling the specific spatio-temporal coherence required for high-fidelity VSR, remains an emerging area that requires specialized adaptation to mitigate the inherent randomness and potential artifacts of generative processes.

The challenge of real-world VSR is further compounded by the diversity and complexity of in-the-wild degradations, necessitating robust training strategies and self-supervised techniques. Benchmarks like the NTIRE 2019 challenge [10] and subsequent studies [12] have highlighted the difficulty of generalizing to unknown degradation patterns. To address this, RealBasicVSR [13] introduces a pre-cleaning stage and stochastic degradation schemes to balance detail synthesis with artifact suppression, while FastRealVSR [14] proposes a Hidden State Attention module to mitigate artifacts in recurrent frameworks. Concurrently, self-supervised learning methods, such as SimCLR [21], MoCo [22], and SwAV [20], have demonstrated the power of contrastive learning in extracting robust visual representations without explicit labels. These approaches leverage data augmentations and consistency constraints to learn degradation-insensitive features. However, integrating such self-supervised mechanisms into diffusion-based or SSM-based VSR frameworks to specifically guide the generation of high-resolution details while maintaining temporal consistency across frames remains an open problem, as existing methods often struggle to fully decouple content fidelity from the stochastic artifacts introduced by generative models.
