**Related Works**

Graph-based collaborative filtering has emerged as a dominant paradigm for learning user-item embeddings by leveraging the bipartite interaction graph. Early approaches, such as Graph Convolutional Matrix Completion [6] and Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) [7], introduced message passing mechanisms to propagate collaborative signals through the graph structure. Subsequent works refined these architectures; LightGCN [9] simplified the process by removing feature transformations and nonlinear activations, focusing solely on neighborhood aggregation, while Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF) [8] addressed the diversity of user intents by disentangling representations. More broadly, Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have been applied to web-scale recommendation [2] and semi-supervised classification [5], with comprehensive surveys detailing the taxonomy of GNNs [17]. Despite their efficacy, these methods typically rely on high-precision floating-point embeddings, which incur significant memory and computational costs during online inference, limiting their scalability in resource-constrained environments.

To address the efficiency bottleneck, representation binarization and hashing techniques have been developed to compress embeddings into compact binary sequences. Foundational work in similarity search via hashing [10] and discrete collaborative filtering [12] laid the groundwork for efficient retrieval. In the deep learning era, HashNet [11] tackled the ill-posed gradient problem in binary optimization using continuation methods. Specifically for recommendation, CIGAR [15] proposed a candidate generation framework using preference-preserving binary embeddings, while other studies explored learning binary codes with neural collaborative filtering [14] and discrete personalized ranking [13]. Recent advancements include Bi-GCN [19] and Binary Graph Neural Networks [20], which apply binarization directly to GNN parameters and activations to reduce memory footprint. However, these approaches often treat binarization as a post-hoc quantization step or focus primarily on numerical quantization, neglecting the substantial information loss that occurs during the transition from continuous to discrete spaces, which leads to noticeable performance degradation.

To mitigate the information loss inherent in binarization, recent frameworks have introduced reinforcement and distillation mechanisms. BiGeaR [21] proposed multi-faceted quantization reinforcement at pre-, mid-, and post-stages of binarized representation learning to retain informativeness. Knowledge distillation, originally introduced for compressing ensembles [22], has been adapted for graph structures to transfer knowledge from teacher to student models. This includes topology-aware distillation from GCNs [24], holistic knowledge distillation using attributed graphs [26], and free-direction knowledge distillation via reinforcement learning [29]. Furthermore, representation knowledge distillation for GNNs [25] and fine-grained learning behavior-oriented distillation [28] aim to preserve global topology and learning dynamics. In the context of hashing, deep hash distillation [31] and semi-supervised knowledge distillation for cross-modal hashing [30] have been employed to minimize representation discrepancies. While these methods improve robustness, they often operate in isolation from the specific challenges of graph-based collaborative filtering binarization, leaving room for integrated frameworks that explicitly leverage supervisory signals from both real data and latent samples to optimize the binarization process.
