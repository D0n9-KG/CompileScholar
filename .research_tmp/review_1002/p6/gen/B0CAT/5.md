**Related Works**

The evolution of text representation has progressed from static word-level vectors to dynamic, context-aware sentence embeddings. Early foundational work established distributed representations for words and phrases, such as Word2Vec [1] and GloVe [2], which captured syntactic and semantic relationships but suffered from an inherent indifference to word order and context. The advent of the Transformer architecture [3] revolutionized sequence modeling by relying solely on attention mechanisms, enabling superior parallelization and quality. This led to the development of bidirectional pre-trained models like BERT [4], which jointly condition on left and right contexts to produce robust representations. However, directly applying these models to sentence similarity tasks incurs massive computational overhead, as they require processing sentence pairs jointly. To address this, Sentence-BERT (SBERT) [6] introduced siamese network structures to derive independent sentence embeddings, reducing inference time from hours to seconds. Subsequent research focused on optimizing the embedding space geometry; SimCSE [7] demonstrated that simple contrastive learning with dropout as noise could regularize anisotropic spaces, while WhitenedCSE [8] and AoE [11] further refined this by employing whitening and angle-optimization techniques to improve semantic textual similarity. More recently, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) has been explored to generate high-quality embeddings using synthetic data [9]. Despite these advancements, these generic embedding methods produce static representations that do not adapt to specific user intents or downstream task requirements, limiting their flexibility in diverse application scenarios.

To overcome the rigidity of static embeddings, recent research has shifted toward instruction-following text embedding, where the embedding is conditioned on natural language instructions. INSTRUCTOR [12] pioneered this approach by training a single embedder on a multitask mixture of instructions, allowing it to generate task-specific embeddings without further training. Building on this, InBedder [13] proposed an "embed-via-answering" perspective, treating the instruction as a question and encoding the expected answers to capture instruction-specific characteristics. Promptriever [14] extended this paradigm to retrieval, demonstrating that instruction-tuned retrievers can be prompted like language models to follow detailed relevance instructions and improve robustness. Furthermore, GRIT [10] unified generative and representational tasks within a single LLM, showing that instruction tuning can handle both generation and embedding at no performance loss. While these methods significantly enhance the adaptability of embeddings, they share a critical limitation: they typically require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction. This necessitates computationally expensive re-computation, creating a significant bottleneck for real-time applications and large-scale datasets where instructions may vary dynamically.

In contrast to the re-encoding paradigm, our work proposes GSTransform, a lightweight transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed generic embeddings in real time. By leveraging the observation that instruction-relevant information is inherently encoded in generic embeddings, GSTransform avoids the high computational overhead associated with methods like INSTRUCTOR [12], InBedder [13], and Promptriever [14]. Instead of re-encoding, we apply a guided space transformation to align existing embeddings with user instructions, achieving dramatic speedups while maintaining high instruction-following quality.
