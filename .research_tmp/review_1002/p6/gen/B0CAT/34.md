**Related Works**

**Time Series Forecasting Architectures**
Recent advancements in time series forecasting have been dominated by Transformer-based architectures and their efficient variants, including Informer [1], LogSparse Transformer [3], Autoformer [6], FEDformer [7], Pyraformer [2], Crossformer [5], MICN [8], and PatchTST [4]. These models address the quadratic complexity of standard Transformers through mechanisms such as ProbSparse self-attention [1], convolutional self-attention [3], auto-correlation [6], and frequency-enhanced decomposition [7]. More recently, the field has seen a shift towards lightweight and alternative paradigms, such as the 2D-variation modeling in TimesNet [9], the MLP-based TSMixer [11], and the linear models in LTSF-Linear [10]. Additionally, probabilistic forecasting methods like DeepAR [12] have been developed to estimate future probability distributions. Despite their high accuracy, these forecasting-centric approaches often suffer from a fundamental goal mismatch: they optimize for prediction error (e.g., MSE) rather than the downstream decision quality, and they frequently fail to adequately characterize the uncertainty inherent in long-horizon predictions [10][12].

**Predict-then-Optimize and Decision-Focused Learning**
The integration of machine learning with optimization has evolved from traditional Predict-then-Optimize (PtO) frameworks to more sophisticated Predict-and-Optimize (PnO) and Decision-Focused Learning (DFL) paradigms. Early PtO approaches, such as those in prescriptive analytics [16] and fund allocation [18], treat prediction and optimization as separate stages, leading to suboptimal decisions due to the disconnect between prediction loss and decision cost. To address this, the Smart Predict-then-Optimize (SPO) framework introduced a loss function that directly measures decision error, along with its convex surrogate, SPO+ [26]. Theoretical works have since established generalization bounds [17] and risk calibration results for these losses [21]. Concurrently, DFL has emerged as a broader paradigm that trains ML models end-to-end with optimization objectives, utilizing differentiable optimization layers like OptNet [23] and CVXPY-based differentiable convex layers [24]. Recent benchmarks and surveys have systematically evaluated these PtO and PnO methods, highlighting the superior decision quality of PnO over traditional PtO [19][22][20]. However, many existing DFL and PnO methods rely on specific optimization structures or assume deterministic predictions, often neglecting the stochastic nature of time series inputs and the computational overhead of solving optimization problems during training [25].

**Uncertainty Quantification in Forecasting**
To mitigate the risks associated with prediction errors, several studies have focused on quantifying uncertainty in time series forecasts. Conformal prediction methods have been applied to construct distribution-free prediction intervals for time series, including approaches for dynamic time series [14] and general frameworks like EnbPI that avoid data exchangeability assumptions [15]. Earlier works also explored conformal time-series forecasting [13]. While these methods provide statistical guarantees on coverage, they are typically post-hoc procedures that do not integrate uncertainty directly into the decision-making process. Consequently, they do not inherently align the calibration of uncertainty with the specific risk preferences of the downstream allocation problem, leaving a gap between accurate interval estimation and risk-aware fund allocation [13][14][15].
