**Related Works**

The field of automatic sensor calibration has evolved significantly, with early efforts focusing on basic radar and vision fusion for object detection in autonomous vehicles [1][2]. As the complexity of sensor suites increased, the focus shifted toward targetless extrinsic calibration methods that eliminate the need for specialized calibration targets. A comprehensive survey of automatic targetless LiDAR-camera calibration highlights the diversity of existing approaches [3]. Early targetless methods relied on maximizing mutual information between 3D LiDAR and camera data [4] or utilized motion-based techniques to calibrate multimodal sensor arrays [5]. These classical approaches laid the groundwork for modern deep learning-based solutions, which aim to automate the calibration process further by leveraging neural networks to infer extrinsic parameters directly from raw sensor data.

Deep learning has emerged as a dominant paradigm for online self-calibration, particularly for LiDAR-camera pairs. RegNet introduced the first deep convolutional neural network to infer 6-DOF extrinsic calibration, casting feature extraction, matching, and regression into a single real-time capable CNN [6]. Subsequent works refined this approach; CalibNet proposed a self-supervised network using 3D Spatial Transformer Networks to estimate rigid body transformations without direct supervision on calibration parameters [7]. CalibRCNN further incorporated recurrent convolutional neural networks and geometric constraints to improve calibration accuracy [8]. More recently, LCCNet utilized cost volume networks inspired by PWC-Net for feature matching, predicting decalibrated deviations rather than absolute parameters to enhance robustness [9]. While these methods have achieved high precision in LiDAR-camera calibration, they generally rely on the dense point clouds provided by LiDARs, making them less applicable to sparser radar data.

In parallel, research has addressed the specific challenges of radar-camera calibration, where data sparsity and measurement uncertainty pose significant hurdles. Targetless rotational auto-calibration for radar and camera systems was pioneered by methods using coarse and fine convolutional neural networks with boosting-inspired training to handle the high sparsity of traffic radars [11]. To address the limitations of 2D radars, continuous-time approaches have been developed for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration, utilizing radar velocity measurements to determine rigid-body transforms without specialized retroreflectors [12]. Furthermore, spatiotemporal calibration algorithms have been proposed to handle both spatial transforms and temporal misalignments in 3D millimeter-wavelength radar-camera pairs, leveraging ego-velocity measurements for targetless in-situ calibration [13]. Despite these advancements, existing radar-camera calibration methods often struggle with the inherent height uncertainty and data sparsity of radar measurements, particularly in online settings where robust feature matching is critical.

Our work builds upon these foundations by addressing the specific challenge of online automatic geometric calibration for radar and camera systems. Unlike LiDAR-based methods that benefit from dense point clouds [6][7][8][9], or previous radar-camera approaches that may rely on specific motion assumptions or rotational-only calibration [11][12][13], our method introduces a Dual-Perspective representation and a Selective Fusion Mechanism. This approach explicitly handles the trade-off between rich but sensitive height information in frontal views and robust features in bird's-eye views, thereby mitigating the effects of height uncertainty that have limited the performance of prior radar-camera calibration techniques.
