**Related Works**

**Generative Modeling for Protein Conformation Sampling**
Recent advances in deep generative modeling have shifted the paradigm of protein dynamics from traditional molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to data-driven approaches. This family of methods includes diffusion-based frameworks such as EigenFold [4], ConfDiff [7], and Str2Str [5], as well as flow-matching approaches like AlphaFlow and ESMFlow [8]. Additionally, latent-space language models, such as Structure Language Models (SLM) [9], and trajectory-based generative models [10] have been developed to capture conformational ensembles. While these methods offer efficient sampling of structural distributions, they often struggle to incorporate physical prior knowledge effectively. For instance, standard score-based diffusion methods may produce conformations that deviate significantly from the true equilibrium distribution due to the lack of explicit physical constraints [7]. Furthermore, many of these approaches rely heavily on target-specific simulation data or operate in geometric spaces that are inefficient for exploring diverse ensemble modes [5][9].

**Physics-Informed and Energy-Based Generative Models**
To address the physical plausibility of generated structures, a distinct sub-family of approaches integrates statistical mechanics and energy functions into the generative process. This includes Boltzmann Generators, which learn coordinate transformations to sample equilibrium states directly [2], and DiG, which uses deep neural networks to transform simple distributions towards equilibrium conditioned on molecular descriptors [6]. Other methods, such as ConfDiff, explicitly incorporate force-guided networks to align data-based score models with physical forces [7]. Despite these advancements, integrating physical supervision into data-driven models remains challenging. Standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization problems, and existing methods frequently fail to balance conformational states accurately based on their energy differences [7]. Consequently, there is a persistent gap in efficiently calibrating generative models to physical feedback without compromising the diversity or fidelity of the sampled ensembles.

**Alignment and Preference Optimization**
The challenge of aligning generative models with specific objectives or feedback signals has been extensively studied in other domains, offering potential strategies for protein modeling. In natural language processing, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has been used to optimize summarization models for human preferences [11]. Similarly, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) provides a stable, lightweight alternative to RLHF by solving the alignment problem with a simple classification loss [13]. In the context of diffusion models, Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO) enables the direct optimization of models for downstream objectives, such as aesthetic quality or image compressibility, by treating denoising as a multi-step decision-making problem [12]. These alignment techniques demonstrate that generative models can be fine-tuned to maximize complex, non-likelihood-based rewards. However, applying such reinforcement learning or preference-based alignment to protein conformation generation, particularly to incorporate physical energy feedback, remains an open problem. Current protein generative models lack a robust framework for aligning their outputs with physical plausibility metrics in a stable and computationally efficient manner.

**Protein Structure Prediction and Energy Functions**
The foundation for many of these generative approaches lies in high-accuracy single-state structure prediction and the development of robust energy functions. AlphaFold has revolutionized protein structure prediction, achieving near-experimental accuracy for single structures [1]. This success has been leveraged by flow-matching methods like AlphaFlow, which repurpose AlphaFold and ESMFold as base models for ensemble generation [8]. Additionally, the Rosetta all-atom energy function provides a critical physical benchmark for evaluating the plausibility of macromolecular models [14]. While these tools provide accurate static structures or energy estimates, they do not inherently capture the dynamic conformational ensembles required for understanding biological function. The integration of these high-accuracy predictors and energy functions into dynamic, generative frameworks that can sample equilibrium distributions remains a key area for future development.
