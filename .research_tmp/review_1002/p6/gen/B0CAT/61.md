**Related Works**

**Personalized Outfit Recommendation and Generation**
Research in fashion recommendation has evolved from traditional retrieval-based methods to generative approaches that synthesize new outfits. Early works focused on modeling compatibility and user preferences through graph structures and latent embeddings. For instance, Hierarchical Fashion Graph Network (HFGN) unifies compatibility modeling and personalized recommendation by constructing a hierarchical structure over user-outfit interactions [1]. Similarly, Compatibility Family Learning introduces a latent space with prototypes to capture the asymmetric nature of item compatibility [12], while CRAFT leverages adversarial feature transformers to learn non-linear transformations between complementary item manifolds [13]. More recently, the Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model bridges user preferences for individual items and outfits using Transformer architectures to satisfy both compatibility and personalization requirements [6]. Other approaches, such as Personalized Outfit Recommendation with Learnable Anchors [2] and Personalized Capsule Wardrobe Creation [4], further refine how user-specific tastes are integrated into the recommendation pipeline. However, these methods are generally constrained by the existing inventory of fashion products, limiting their ability to address diverse and novel user needs by generating entirely new items [10].

**Generative Fashion Models and Diffusion Architectures**
The advent of generative AI has shifted the paradigm from selecting existing items to creating new fashion content. Diffusion models have emerged as a dominant architecture for high-fidelity image synthesis, with foundational works like Latent Diffusion Models enabling high-resolution image generation [8]. In the context of fashion, DiFashion introduces a generative outfit recommender that uses diffusion models to generate multiple fashion images in parallel, guided by conditions for fidelity, compatibility, and personalization [10]. This approach transcends the limitations of pre-defined outfit recommendation by allowing for the creation of visually compatible outfits tailored to specific users [10]. Additionally, ControlNet demonstrates how spatial conditioning controls can be added to pretrained text-to-image diffusion models, facilitating robust control over generation processes such as pose and depth [9]. While these generative models offer unprecedented creative potential, they often rely on supervised learning paradigms and may lack the diversity required for highly personalized fashion recommendations [10].

**Preference Optimization and Alignment Techniques**
To align generative models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has gained significant traction as a stable and computationally lightweight alternative to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). DPO enables the extraction of optimal policies in closed form using a simple classification loss, eliminating the need for complex reward modeling [22]. This technique has been adapted for diffusion models; Diffusion-DPO re-formulates DPO to account for diffusion likelihoods, aligning models with human comparison data [23]. Similarly, D3PO addresses the high GPU memory requirements of diffusion models by directly fine-tuning them without training a separate reward model, proving to be more cost-effective [24]. In the domain of personalized image generation, PatchDPO extends DPO to the patch level, addressing the issue that generated images are often inconsistent with references only in local regions [20]. Furthermore, JeDi proposes a finetuning-free personalization technique that learns the joint distribution of related text-image pairs, enabling fast personalization at test time [19]. Despite these advances, applying DPO to fashion-specific tasks remains an open challenge, as existing methods often lack task-specific reward functions or comprehensive feedback mechanisms that cover quality, compatibility, and personalization simultaneously [21][25].
