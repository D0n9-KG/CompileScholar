**Related Works**

**Safety Alignment and Vulnerabilities**
Safety alignment techniques, including Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and preference modeling, are standard for mitigating harmful outputs in Large Language Models (LLMs) [1][2][10]. However, these alignments are often brittle. Research demonstrates that safety training can be easily subverted through subversive fine-tuning methods like LoRA [4] or minimal data injection known as Shadow Alignment [5], even when users do not intend to compromise safety [6]. Furthermore, adaptive jailbreaking attacks, such as those leveraging logprobs or random search on suffixes, can achieve high success rates against state-of-the-art aligned models [7][8][9]. These findings highlight a shared limitation: current alignment methods lack robustness against targeted adversarial interventions and fine-tuning attacks.

**Activation Steering and Representation Engineering**
To control model behavior without retraining, researchers employ activation steering and representation engineering (RepE). This family includes Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) [17], Activation Addition (ActAdd) [18], and Conditional Activation Steering (CAST) [19], which modify residual stream activations to induce specific behaviors. Related approaches include Spectral Editing of Activations (SEA) [14] and category-wise safety steering [15], which project representations into directions associated with desired traits. These methods rely on the linear representation hypothesis, which posits that high-level concepts are encoded as directions in activation space [26][28][29]. While effective, these techniques often require predefined positive and negative example pairs to compute steering vectors, limiting their applicability to scenarios where such labeled behavioral contrasts are unavailable or where the target behavior is not explicitly defined in the output space.

**Concept Identification and Erasure**
Identifying and manipulating specific concepts within LLMs involves methods for concept erasure and latent knowledge discovery. Techniques such as Iterative Null-space Projection (INLP) [21] and LEACE [22] remove specific features from embeddings, while causal probing frameworks identify linear subspaces encoding concepts [20]. Unsupervised methods, such as those discovering latent knowledge via logical consistency in activations [16], allow for the identification of directions without supervision. Additionally, mechanistic interpretability tools like sparse autoencoders [31] and bilinear MLP analysis [32] aim to decompose polysemantic neurons into monosemantic features [25][30]. A common challenge in this area is the reliance on auxiliary tasks, predefined templates, or manual analysis to identify the relevant directions, which can be computationally expensive and may not generalize to weakly aligned or adversarially modified models.

**Refusal Mechanisms**
Recent work has focused specifically on the mechanics of refusal. Studies have shown that refusal is mediated by a single direction in the activation space [11] or can be modeled as an affine function [12]. These insights enable white-box jailbreaks or precise control over refusal responses. However, existing methods for identifying these refusal directions often depend on the presence of specific refusal tokens in the output or require manual analysis of model internals. This dependence on output-based signals limits their effectiveness in adversarial settings where refusal tokens may be suppressed or in weakly aligned models where refusal behavior is not clearly defined by standard templates. Our work, COSMIC, addresses this by using cosine similarity metrics to identify steering directions entirely independent of model outputs.
