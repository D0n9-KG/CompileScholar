**Related Works**

Long-tailed recognition (LTR) addresses the challenge of class imbalance in visual recognition, where a few head classes dominate the data distribution while most tail classes are under-represented [1][2][7]. Existing solutions generally fall into three categories: class re-balancing, information augmentation, and module improvement [7]. Class re-balancing strategies include data re-sampling, loss re-weighting based on effective sample sizes [2], and cost-sensitive learning that jointly optimizes class-dependent costs [4]. Information augmentation methods involve decoupling representation learning from classifier training, allowing for the adjustment of decision boundaries without altering feature extraction [5][8]. Module improvement approaches include bilateral-branch networks that separate representation and classifier learning [6] and logit adjustment techniques that modify softmax outputs to account for label frequencies [9]. Despite their effectiveness, these methods often suffer from a trade-off where re-balancing strategies may damage the representational ability of deep features [6], or where decoupled approaches require complex re-training procedures for the classifier [8].

Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for representation learning, with foundational works establishing its efficacy in both unsupervised [14][15][16][22] and supervised settings [17]. Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL) pulls samples of the same class together while pushing apart different classes, achieving state-of-the-art performance on balanced datasets [17]. However, applying SCL to long-tailed data presents significant challenges. Theoretical analyses reveal that SCL tends to bias towards high-frequency classes, increasing the difficulty of imbalanced learning [10][11]. Furthermore, SCL fails to form a regular simplex in the feature space for long-tailed data, leading to poor uniformity and separability for minority classes [12][21]. This bias stems from the fact that SCL maximizes mutual information between latent features and input data rather than ground-truth labels, ignoring class imbalances [13]. Consequently, standard contrastive losses struggle to provide consistent performance gains as the number of views or classes increases in imbalanced scenarios [10].

To mitigate the biases in standard contrastive learning for LTR, several specialized variants have been proposed. Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) introduces class-wise learnable centers to rebalance optimization and adaptively enhance the attraction of same-class samples [10][11]. Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) addresses gradient imbalances by employing class-averaging and class-complement mechanisms to ensure all classes contribute equally to the loss [12]. Probabilistic Contrastive Learning (ProCo) estimates data distributions in the feature space to sample contrastive pairs more effectively, overcoming the need for large batches that cover all categories [18]. Subclass-balancing Contrastive Learning (SBCL) clusters head classes into subclasses to capture semantic substructures and enforce instance-level balance [19]. Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC) improves feature uniformity by guiding features to converge to uniformly distributed targets on a hypersphere [21]. While these methods improve upon standard SCL, they often rely on specific assumptions about data distribution or require complex auxiliary structures. Our work addresses the underlying gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction/repulsion forces identified in SCL, offering a more direct solution to the generalization issues observed in multi-view training for long-tailed recognition.
