**Related Works**

Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) has emerged as a privacy-preserving paradigm for search systems, allowing collaborative model training without sharing raw user data. Early approaches utilized evolution strategies to optimize ranking models in a federated setting [1][2]. Subsequent research introduced differentiable pairwise losses, such as Pairwise Differentiable Gradient Descent (PDGD), to enable unbiased gradient estimation for both linear and non-linear models [4]. These methods were adapted for federated contexts to handle implicit feedback like clicks while preserving user privacy [3]. More recent studies have examined the impact of non-IID data distributions on FOLTR performance [5] and analyzed the security implications of poisoning attacks against these systems [6]. Additionally, efforts have been made to extend online learning to rank to neural architectures by managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off [7]. Our work builds upon these foundational FOLTR methods by specifically addressing the challenge of data removal, a critical requirement that prior FOLTR studies have largely overlooked.

The legal mandate for the "right to be forgotten" has driven the development of machine unlearning techniques, which aim to erase specific data contributions from trained models. In centralized settings, unlearning is often achieved through retraining or gradient-based adjustments. However, in Federated Learning (FL), the distributed nature of data presents unique challenges. Federaser proposed an efficient method for client-level data removal by leveraging historical updates [8]. Other approaches have utilized knowledge distillation to restore model performance after subtracting a client’s accumulated updates, avoiding the need for client participation [9]. Rapid retraining strategies have also been developed to efficiently erase data samples while preserving model utility [10]. Furthermore, methods have been proposed that perform local unlearning at the target client and then use the resulting model to initialize a few rounds of federated learning with remaining clients [11]. While these methods address unlearning in general FL contexts, they do not account for the specific dynamics of online learning to rank. Our paper differs by adapting and evaluating these unlearning strategies specifically within the FOLTR framework, where models are updated continuously based on implicit feedback.

Recent work has extended unlearning concepts to specific application domains, such as on-device recommendation and knowledge graph embedding. Federated Recommendation Unlearning (FRU) employs a log-based rollback mechanism to remove user contributions from federated recommendation models, enhancing both privacy and robustness against malicious clients [12]. Similarly, in the domain of knowledge graphs, FedLU proposes a framework for heterogeneous federated learning that incorporates unlearning via cognitive neuroscience-inspired mechanisms, such as retroactive interference and passive decay [13]. These domain-specific approaches highlight the growing importance of unlearning in federated systems. However, they do not address the unique evaluation challenges of ranking tasks. Our study contributes to this line of work by systematically analyzing unlearning capabilities in FOLTR, focusing on the trade-offs between under-unlearning and over-unlearning.

A critical gap in the existing literature is the lack of comprehensive evaluation metrics for unlearning effectiveness. Previous proposals often relied on single evaluation metrics, which can be controversial and insufficient for assessing whether unlearning has successfully occurred without compromising model utility [11][14]. The recent work on forgetting clients in FOLTR highlights the difficulty of measuring unlearning success in this specific context [14]. To address this limitation, our paper rigorously assesses five unlearning strategies using adapted and newly proposed evaluation metrics. By providing a detailed analysis of both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios, we offer valuable insights into optimizing federated unlearning to balance data privacy and system performance, thereby extending the current state of the art in verifiable unlearning for FOLTR.
