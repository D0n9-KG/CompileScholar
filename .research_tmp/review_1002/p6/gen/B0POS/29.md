**Related Works**

Differential Privacy (DP) has emerged as the de facto standard for protecting individual data in aggregate statistics [1][6]. However, the security of DP relies heavily on the faithful execution of noise generation mechanisms by the server. In central DP models, a malicious server can manipulate results by sampling incorrect noise distributions or generating correlated noise, using the inherent randomness of DP as a smokescreen for nefarious activities [1]. This vulnerability is particularly acute in distributed settings where trust is fragmented. While Crypt$\epsilon$ proposes a framework using two non-colluding untrusted servers to achieve central-model utility without a trusted collector, it relies on secure computation protocols that can be computationally intensive and complex to implement [6]. Our work addresses these adversarial behaviors directly by introducing Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), which incorporates explicit verification mechanisms to ensure faithful noise execution in a client-server-verifier setup, rather than relying solely on non-collusion assumptions.

To mitigate the risk of malicious servers, early research introduced the concept of Verifiable Differential Privacy (VDP). This approach requires the publishing entity to provide a zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) that convinces an efficient verifier that the output is both differentially private and reliable [1]. By mixing private and public randomness, VDP allows for the verification of counting queries without revealing the noise itself. However, existing VDP solutions often suffer from high computational overhead in proof generation. Furthermore, while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving verifiability, they are not strictly necessary for all DP mechanisms. In contrast, our work explores the broader relationship between ZKPs and DP, demonstrating that efficient verification can be achieved without the full generality of ZKPs. We propose the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM), which offers a $4 \times 10^5$ improvement in proof generation efficiency compared to previous state-of-the-art verifiable mechanisms, while maintaining negligible error rates.

In the context of Local Differential Privacy (LDP), malicious data providers can manipulate their outputs before transmission, exploiting the fact that data collectors must estimate distributions from already randomized data [3]. This "plausible deniability" allows attackers to skew estimations by deviating from the agreed-upon randomization protocol. To counter this, verifiable randomization mechanisms have been proposed to allow data collectors to verify the completeness of the randomization process for each provider [3]. Earlier cryptographic techniques for randomized response also aimed to prevent cheating respondents from affecting the tally beyond their own vote [5]. While these methods protect against client-side manipulation, they often incur significant communication costs and verifier overhead. Our work extends this line of research to the distributed setting by developing an improved solution for Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR) under local DP. We achieve a reduction of up to 5000x in communication costs and verifier overhead, providing a more scalable solution for verifying LDP compliance.

The challenge of generating noise in distributed settings has also been addressed through Secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC). Various protocols have been developed for securely sampling discrete Gaussian noise and other distributions to facilitate multi-party DP [8][9]. Recent benchmarking efforts have highlighted the performance disparities among these secure sampling protocols, noting that while they offer utility comparable to the central model, they often lack comprehensive experimental evaluation [10]. Other works, such as DPrio, focus on efficient DP with high utility for specific protocols like Prio [2], and distributed noise generation has been explored to enhance privacy via client-side contributions [7]. However, these approaches often focus on the efficiency of noise generation itself rather than the verifiability of the process against malicious deviations. Our VDDP framework differs by explicitly integrating verification into the DP mechanism, ensuring that the distributed noise generation is not only secure but also provably faithful to the DP protocol, thereby closing the gap between secure sampling and verifiable privacy.
