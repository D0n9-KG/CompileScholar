Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a critical tool in multiple sclerosis (MS) research, offering superior contrast for distinguishing paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) from other lesion types [5]. PRLs, characterized by iron deposition, serve as an emerging biomarker for disease progression and prognosis [5]. While significant effort has been directed toward the detection and segmentation of these lesions to monitor longitudinal changes, the rarity of PRLs presents a substantial challenge due to severe class imbalance between rim and non-rim lesions [5]. Previous work, such as QSMRim-Net, has addressed this imbalance through specialized learning strategies tailored for chronic active MS lesion identification [5]. However, these approaches often rely on existing data distributions that may not fully capture the variability of rare rim lesions. In contrast, our work introduces synthetic QSMs of PRLs to explicitly address this scarcity, demonstrating that the inclusion of such synthetic data improves classifier performance beyond what is achieved by standard imbalance-aware learning alone.

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have become a cornerstone for synthetic data generation in medical imaging, enabling the creation of high-fidelity samples to augment training sets [8]. Conditional GANs (cGANs) extend this framework by allowing the generation of data conditioned on specific labels, such as class identities, which is essential for targeted data augmentation [10]. In the context of neuroimaging, synthetic data generation has been explored to improve generalizability and address data scarcity [1]. For instance, SinGAN-Seg utilizes synthetic training data to enhance medical image segmentation tasks [4]. Earlier work by [7] proposed a U-Net based encoder-decoder to synthesize MS lesions on MRI, aiming to improve supervised detection algorithms by augmenting healthy images with synthetic lesions. While these methods demonstrate the utility of synthetic data, they often focus on general lesion synthesis or specific imaging modalities without specifically targeting the unique contrast characteristics of QSMs. Our approach differs by producing synthetic QSMs specifically designed to capture the paramagnetic rim characteristics, thereby providing a more targeted augmentation strategy for PRL detection.

Addressing class imbalance through oversampling techniques is a well-studied problem in machine learning. DeepSMOTE, for example, fuses deep learning with SMOTE to generate high-quality artificial images for minority classes, avoiding the mode collapse issues often associated with GAN-based oversampling [6]. This method leverages an encoder-decoder framework and a dedicated loss function to balance the training set [6]. Similarly, transfer learning has been widely applied in MRI to improve generalization across different domains and tasks, with systematic reviews highlighting its importance in handling variations in MR images [3]. Theoretical bounds on transfer learning suggest that careful selection of transferred information is crucial for maximizing improvement [2]. While these methods provide robust frameworks for handling imbalance and domain shifts, they do not inherently address the specific challenge of ambiguous labels in rare lesion detection. Our work builds on these foundations by not only generating synthetic minority class samples but also exploiting the projection capability of our trained generative network to handle label noise.

The robustness of generative models to noisy labels is a critical area of research, particularly when dealing with ambiguous clinical cases. Robust Conditional GANs (RCGAN) have been proposed to learn from noisy labeled samples by corrupting labels before feeding them to the discriminator, thereby forcing the generator to produce clean samples [11]. Recent advances in noisy label learning, such as Noisy Prediction Calibration (NPC), use generative models to correct noisy predictions from pre-trained classifiers as a post-processing step [13]. Additionally, modeling label noise through latent space shifts offers a robust means for noisy learning in multilabel classification [12]. GAN inversion techniques also provide insights into the latent space of pretrained models, facilitating image editing and restoration [9]. Although these methods offer powerful tools for handling label noise, they are often applied in general computer vision contexts or as post-hoc corrections. Our paper distinguishes itself by integrating a denoising approach directly into the training process of the generative network, allowing us to train on ambiguous rim cases and substantially increase the effective size of the minority class in a clinically interpretable manner.
