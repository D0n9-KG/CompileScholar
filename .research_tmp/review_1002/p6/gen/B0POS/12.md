Event cameras have emerged as a promising alternative to conventional frame-based cameras due to their asynchronous, per-pixel operation, which provides extremely high temporal resolution and low latency [1]. However, this high temporal fidelity results in massive data streams that impose significant demands on bandwidth, memory, and computational resources, particularly for edge-based applications [7]. To mitigate these challenges, early research focused on spatial and temporal downsampling techniques specifically tailored for event-based visual classification tasks [1]. These initial approaches often employed fixed heuristics or simple threshold-based strategies to reduce the event rate, aiming to balance information retention with data reduction [3]. While effective in specific contexts, such methods generally lack adaptability to varying scene dynamics or task requirements. In contrast, the present work proposes POLED, a probabilistic framework that models event importance through an event-importance probability density function (ePDF), allowing for arbitrary definition and adaptation to different applications rather than relying on static heuristics.

Subsequent studies have explored more sophisticated downsampling methods to improve robustness and efficiency. For instance, EvDownsampling was introduced as a robust method for reducing event camera data, aiming to preserve critical information while lowering the data volume [2]. Similarly, bio-inspired filtering and data reduction algorithms have been developed to mimic biological visual systems, offering a less data-intensive yet information-rich representation of the scene [3]. More recent work has investigated the impact of spatial downscaling on spiking neural networks, comparing various DVS data downscaling methods to determine their efficacy in neuromorphic computing contexts [4]. These approaches often focus on specific hardware constraints or biological analogies, but they may not fully address the need for online, scene-specific adaptation in general-purpose deep learning pipelines. Our approach differs by operating in a purely online setting, estimating event importance on-the-fly from raw event streams, thereby enabling real-time, scene-specific adaptation without the need for offline processing or task-specific architectural changes.

Recent advancements have also drawn inspiration from biological systems to optimize spatio-temporal downsampling. Insect-inspired methods have been proposed to handle event-based input, leveraging the efficient processing strategies observed in insect visual systems to reduce data complexity [5]. Additionally, neuromorphic downsampling techniques have been developed to align event camera output with the processing capabilities of neuromorphic hardware, further emphasizing the importance of efficient data representation [6]. These bio-inspired and neuromorphic approaches highlight the potential for reducing event rates while maintaining performance, yet they often remain tied to specific hardware implementations or biological models. In contrast, our method introduces a zero-shot event downsampling paradigm where downsampled events remain usable for models trained on the original event stream, eliminating the need for task-specific adaptation or retraining.

Furthermore, recent investigations have pushed the boundaries of event subsampling in event-based video classification using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Studies have demonstrated that the number of events per video can be reduced by an order of magnitude with minimal drop in accuracy, revealing the extent of the accuracy-versus-event-rate trade-off [7]. However, these studies also note that lower classification accuracy at high subsampling rates is not solely due to information loss but also stems from increased training instability and sensitivity to hyperparameters in highly subsampled scenarios [7]. This highlights the critical role of intelligent sampling strategies in maintaining model performance under strict event-budget constraints. Our work builds on this line of research by designing a contour-preserving ePDF that prioritizes structurally important events, ensuring that the downsampled data retains the necessary structural integrity for downstream tasks such as object classification, image interpolation, surface normal estimation, and object detection.
