**Related Works**

Early video super-resolution (VSR) methods primarily relied on recurrent architectures to model temporal dependencies. For instance, EDVR introduced enhanced deformable convolutional networks to handle large motions and fuse features effectively [9]. Subsequent works like BasicVSR and its enhanced version, BasicVSR++, focused on essential components such as propagation, alignment, and aggregation to improve efficiency and restoration quality [2][3]. Other approaches, such as the Recurrent Structure-Detail Network, separated structure and detail components to mitigate error accumulation in recurrent units [4]. While these methods established strong baselines for synthetic degradations, they often struggle with the complex, unknown degradations present in real-world videos. In contrast, our work addresses these limitations by incorporating self-supervised learning into a diffusion-based framework, specifically designed to handle real-world noise robustness rather than relying solely on recurrent propagation.

With the rise of Transformer architectures, several studies have adapted them for VSR to capture long-range temporal dependencies. The Video Super-Resolution Transformer (VST) proposed spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention to exploit data locality [1]. More recently, Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) and Recurrent Video Restoration Transformer (RVRT) have explored parallel frame prediction and hybrid recurrent-parallel structures to balance model size and long-range modeling [7][8]. Additionally, methods like MuCAN have leveraged multi-correspondence aggregation to exploit self-similarity across frames [11]. Although these Transformer-based approaches offer powerful global context modeling, they often incur high computational costs and may introduce artifacts due to the randomness inherent in generative processes. Our approach differs by integrating Mamba, a selective state space model, which provides efficient global spatio-temporal attention with lower computational overhead, thereby reinforcing coherence without the prohibitive cost of full self-attention.

State Space Models (SSMs), particularly the Mamba architecture, have emerged as efficient alternatives to Transformers for sequence modeling. Mamba introduces selective mechanisms that allow the model to propagate or forget information based on input content, achieving linear-time complexity [17]. Extensions like Mamba-ND and Vision Mamba have further adapted these models for multi-dimensional and visual data, demonstrating superior efficiency and performance in tasks like image classification and detection [18][19]. Prior SSM variants, such as S4 and S5, laid the groundwork for efficient long-range dependency modeling [15][16]. While these models have shown promise in vision backbones, their application to video super-resolution, particularly for mitigating diffusion-induced artifacts, remains underexplored. Our paper builds on this line of work by employing a Video State-Space block with a 3D Selective Scan module to enhance the diffusion model, ensuring content consistency across adjacent frames at an affordable computational cost.

Real-world VSR presents unique challenges due to diverse and complex degradations. Recent studies have highlighted the tradeoffs in handling severe in-the-wild noise, suggesting that pre-cleaning stages are essential to prevent artifact propagation [13]. Methods like FastRealVSR have proposed Hidden State Attention to mitigate artifacts in recurrent models by aggregating cleaner hidden states [14]. Furthermore, benchmark datasets and decomposition-based learning schemes have been developed to better evaluate and train models on real-world data [12][10]. Self-supervised learning techniques, such as contrastive learning frameworks like SimCLR, MoCo, and SwAV, have proven effective in learning robust visual representations without labeled data [20][21][22]. However, these methods have not been fully integrated into diffusion-based VSR frameworks to guide detail synthesis. Our work distinguishes itself by introducing a self-supervised ControlNet that leverages contrastive learning to extract degradation-insensitive features from low-resolution videos, thereby guiding the diffusion process to reduce artifacts and improve perceptual quality.
