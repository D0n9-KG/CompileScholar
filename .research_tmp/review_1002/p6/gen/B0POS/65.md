Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a paradigm that replaces traditional index-retrieve pipelines with a single neural network, enabling end-to-end optimization of document retrieval [1][14]. Early works like the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) demonstrated that a Transformer can encode corpus information in its parameters to map queries directly to document identifiers [14]. Subsequent research has focused on improving the quality of these identifiers, moving from atomic IDs to semantic or hierarchical structures to better capture document content [4][10][13]. Other studies have addressed the scalability and update challenges of GR, proposing methods for incremental indexing and continual learning to handle dynamic corpora [18][23][24]. Furthermore, recent efforts have sought to bridge the gap between generative retrieval and dense retrieval, analyzing their theoretical equivalence and leveraging dense retrieval techniques to enhance GR performance [12][15][22]. While these works primarily focus on architectural improvements, identifier design, and scalability, our paper differs by providing a theoretical analysis of the inherent limitations of the auto-regressive decoding process itself, specifically examining how constraints and beam search affect generalization capabilities.

A significant body of literature has investigated the robustness and generalization of generative retrieval models, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. Empirical studies have shown that GR models often struggle with query variations, unseen tasks, and corpus expansions compared to dense retrieval baselines [19][20]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed various decoding strategies, such as nonparametric decoding to leverage external memory [21] and planning-ahead mechanisms to guide generation [32]. Additionally, learning-to-rank frameworks have been introduced to align the autoregressive generation objective with final passage ranking targets, addressing the disconnect between intermediate generation and final retrieval performance [6]. Some works have also explored the integration of GR with downstream tasks like question answering to improve overall system reliability [33][34]. Although these approaches aim to improve empirical robustness through architectural or training modifications, our work builds on this line of research by deriving theoretical lower bounds on error, offering a fundamental understanding of why these empirical limitations exist rather than just proposing heuristic fixes.

Theoretical analyses of constrained generation and beam search have been conducted in broader contexts of machine learning and natural language processing. Research on concurrent verifiers has provided generalization bounds for models that modify outputs to satisfy constraints at inference time [25]. Similarly, studies on tractable control for autoregressive language generation have explored how to sample from conditional distributions under lexical constraints, highlighting the intractability of exact sampling for complex constraints [27][28]. In the domain of tree-based models, which share structural similarities with hierarchical document identifiers, theoretical work has analyzed the training-testing discrepancy caused by beam search, proposing concepts like Bayes optimality under beam search to understand performance degradation [30][31]. These theoretical frameworks provide tools for analyzing constrained decoding, but they have not been specifically applied to the generative retrieval setting. Our paper applies and extends these theoretical perspectives to GR, specifically deriving error bounds in terms of KL divergence between ground-truth and model-predicted step-wise marginal distributions, thereby clarifying the impact of corpus-specific constraints and beam search on retrieval accuracy.
