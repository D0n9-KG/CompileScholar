**Related Works**

Long-form narrative summarization presents unique challenges distinct from news or document summarization, primarily due to the need to capture intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence. Early approaches focused on specific narrative structures, such as movie scripts, where graph-based scene extraction was proposed to identify key narrative elements [2]. More recently, researchers have addressed the issue of scene saliency, noting that only a fraction of scenes are critical for understanding the overall narrative [3]. While these methods effectively isolate salient content, they often rely on specific structural assumptions or manual annotation. In contrast, our work introduces a generalizable multi-agent framework that handles diverse narrative forms, including books and TV scripts, without requiring domain-specific structural parsing or fine-tuning.

The computational constraints of Transformer models have historically limited their ability to process long sequences, leading to the development of various efficient attention mechanisms. Models such as BigBird, Longformer, and Reformer introduced sparse or linear attention patterns to reduce quadratic complexity, enabling the processing of longer documents [4][5][6]. Subsequent advancements, including Linformer and LongT5, further optimized self-attention for long-range dependencies [7][8]. However, these architectural modifications often require extensive pretraining or specific hardware configurations. Our approach avoids the need for architectural changes or fine-tuning by leveraging a structured, sequential pipeline that operates within the standard context windows of existing Large Language Models (LLMs).

Recent research has highlighted that even with extended context windows, LLMs struggle to robustly utilize information located in the middle of long inputs, a phenomenon known as the "lost in the middle" effect [10]. To mitigate this, techniques such as Position Interpolation have been developed to extend the effective context window of pretrained models [9]. Other methods, like SLED and Unlimiformer, propose sliding-window encoders or k-nearest-neighbor indexing to handle inputs exceeding standard limits [11][12]. While these methods improve the model's capacity to ingest long texts, they do not inherently solve the issue of maintaining narrative coherence across disjointed chunks. NexusSum addresses this by employing a hierarchical multi-LLM summarization pipeline that explicitly optimizes chunk processing and controls output length, ensuring that thematic coherence is preserved throughout the summarization process.

The emergence of multi-agent frameworks has offered new strategies for managing long-context tasks. Chain-of-Agents (CoA) proposes a collaborative framework where multiple worker agents process segmented portions of text, followed by a manager agent that synthesizes the contributions [14]. This approach mitigates focus issues by assigning each agent a shorter context. Similarly, studies on book-length summarization have explored hierarchical merging of chunk-level summaries and incremental updating of running summaries, identifying common coherence errors in LLM-generated outputs [15]. Evaluation metrics like BooookScore and NarrativeFactScore have been introduced to better assess factual accuracy and coherence in these long-form summaries [15][16]. Our work builds on these multi-agent and hierarchical concepts by introducing Dialogue-to-Description Transformation, a narrative-specific preprocessing method that standardizes character dialogue and descriptive text into a unified format. This innovation, combined with our hierarchical pipeline, establishes a new state-of-the-art in narrative summarization, achieving significant improvements in BERTScore across diverse storytelling domains.
