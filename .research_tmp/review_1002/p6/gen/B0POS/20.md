**Related Works**

Classical approaches to single-pixel imaging (SPI) and compressed sensing rely on iterative optimization algorithms that balance data fidelity with sparsity or structural priors. Early methods, such as gradient projection [1] and iterative hard thresholding [2], established theoretical foundations for recovering signals from undersampled measurements. Subsequent advancements introduced the fast iterative shrinkage-thresholding algorithm (FISTA) [3] and Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) [4], which improved convergence rates and allowed for more complex regularization terms, such as nonlocal low-rank priors [5]. While these classical solvers are robust, they often suffer from slow convergence and limited ability to capture complex natural image statistics compared to modern deep learning techniques. In contrast, the present paper leverages the mathematical rigor of HQS and ADMM frameworks but replaces their handcrafted regularizers with learned components to achieve superior speed and accuracy.

Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods have emerged as a dominant paradigm for integrating deep learning into inverse problems by using off-the-shelf denoisers as implicit regularizers within iterative schemes. The Denoising-based Approximate Message Passing (D-AMP) framework [6] demonstrated that generic denoisers could be effectively embedded into reconstruction loops, offering state-of-the-art performance. Recent works have sought to provide convergence guarantees for these methods; for instance, Gradient Step Denoisers [7] and Proximal Denoisers [8] ensure that the iterative process converges to stationary points of an explicit functional. Furthermore, Stochastic Deep Restoration Priors (ShaRP) [10] extend this by using ensembles of restoration models to better handle structured artifacts. Although PnP methods are flexible across varying compression ratios (CRs), they are typically limited in reconstruction accuracy and computational speed due to the iterative nature of the denoising steps. Our work builds on this line of research by proposing a proximal trajectory loss that trains a deep image restorer to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal regularizer, thereby retaining the flexibility of PnP while overcoming its accuracy and speed limitations.

Deep unrolling networks address the speed and accuracy trade-offs of PnP by transforming truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks. Early non-iterative approaches like ReconNet [11] and scalable CNNs [12] provided fast baselines, but optimization-inspired networks such as ISTA-Net [15] and ADMM-CSNet [16] offered better interpretability by mapping network layers to optimization steps. More recent architectures, including AMP-Net [17], Memory-Augmented Deep Unfolding Networks [18], and COAST [19], have introduced mechanisms to preserve information across stages and handle arbitrary sampling matrices. Transformer-based hybrids, such as CSformer [21] and OCTUF [22], further enhance global context modeling, while specialized networks like HATNet [26] target large-scale SPI. However, most unrolling networks are trained for specific compression ratios, requiring retraining or fine-tuning when the CR changes. Unlike these CR-specific unrolling networks, our proposed proximal unrolling network utilizes a general proximal trajectory loss to train a single model that flexibly handles varying CRs, outperforming previous methods in both reconstruction quality and inference speed.

Finally, the theoretical underpinnings of using deep networks as regularizers have been formalized through the concept of learned proximal operators. The Regularization by Denoising (RED) framework [28] provided an explicit regularization functional derived from denoisers, clarifying the objective being minimized. More recently, Learned Proximal Networks (LPN) [9] proved that specific network architectures can represent exact proximal operators for data-driven nonconvex regularizers, enabling the recovery of log-priors. These works highlight the importance of ensuring that learned components correspond to valid mathematical operators. Our paper aligns with this theoretical direction by explicitly designing the deep image restorer to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer, ensuring that the unrolled HQS/ADMM network is not merely a black-box fit but a principled optimization solver.
