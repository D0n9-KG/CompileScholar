**Related Works**

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has driven significant research into efficient inference and deployment strategies. Early foundational models like GPT-4 [1], PaLM [2], and the LLaMA series [3][4] established state-of-the-art performance but introduced substantial computational and memory overheads. To address these challenges, algorithm-level optimizations have focused on model compression. Quantization techniques, such as LLM.int8() [12] and SmoothQuant [14], reduce memory footprint and accelerate inference by lowering precision, while pruning methods like SparseGPT [16] and Pruner-Zero [15] remove redundant weights. Additionally, knowledge distillation frameworks, including MiniLLM [18] and DistiLLM [17], compress large teacher models into smaller student models to reduce inference costs. Unlike these approaches that primarily target model size reduction, our work, CLONE, focuses on an in-depth algorithm-hardware co-design that integrates real-time energy optimization while maintaining robust generality for edge deployment.

System-level optimizations aim to maximize throughput and manage resources in constrained environments. Serving systems like vLLM [8] and Orca [23] improve throughput through efficient memory management and scheduling. For resource-constrained settings, FlexGen [22] and PowerInfer [5] leverage hybrid CPU-GPU architectures or offloading to run large models on limited hardware. ExeGPT [6] further optimizes distributed inference by finding optimal execution schedules under latency constraints. While these systems effectively manage data flow and scheduling, they often target cloud or server-grade hardware. In contrast, CLONE is specifically designed for off-the-shelf edge devices, balancing latency, energy, and accuracy through a specialized 28nm scalable hardware accelerator system.

Hardware-aware and edge-specific optimizations have also emerged to address the power and latency constraints of mobile and embedded devices. LLM in a flash [9] explores storing parameters in flash memory to overcome DRAM limitations. EdgeBERT [24] pioneered algorithm-hardware co-design for NLP on edge platforms, utilizing dynamic voltage-frequency scaling (DVFS) and early exit mechanisms for energy efficiency. Other works, such as NeuOS [28] and ApproxCaliper [25], explore multi-dimensional optimization and programmable frameworks for neural network acceleration. Building upon the foundational concepts of edge co-design, CLONE extends these principles to the specific challenges of LLMs, integrating real-time energy optimization with a scalable hardware accelerator to achieve significant speedups and energy savings in always-on edge computing settings.

Finally, the broader context of LLM acceleration involves understanding the trade-offs between training and inference efficiency. Recent reviews highlight the growing importance of LLM-hardware co-design to tailor architectures to specific model requirements [10]. While general frameworks like PyTorch [19], TensorFlow [20], and DeepSpeed [21] provide the software infrastructure for training and serving, they do not inherently address the fine-grained energy-latency trade-offs critical for edge deployment. CLONE differentiates itself by providing a holistic solution that quantifies the challenges of edge deployment and implements a co-designed system that achieves up to 11.92x acceleration and 7.36x energy savings, thereby enabling high-quality LLM inference on resource-constrained devices.
