**Related Works**

**Time Series Forecasting Architectures**
Recent advancements in time series forecasting have been dominated by Transformer-based architectures designed to capture long-range dependencies. Informer introduced a ProbSparse self-attention mechanism to reduce computational complexity for long sequences [1]. Subsequent works, such as Autoformer and FEDformer, incorporated decomposition and frequency-enhanced techniques to better model temporal patterns and global trends [6][7]. Other notable models include Pyraformer, which utilizes pyramidal attention [2], LogSparse Transformer for memory efficiency [3], and PatchTST, which employs channel-independent patching to improve multivariate forecasting [4]. Crossformer and MICN further explore cross-dimensional and multi-scale context modeling [5][8]. TimesNet extends temporal variation modeling into 2D space to handle multi-periodicity [9]. However, the effectiveness of Transformers has been questioned; studies indicate that simple linear models can outperform complex Transformer variants due to the permutation-invariant nature of self-attention [10]. Lightweight alternatives like TSMixer have also demonstrated competitive performance with minimal computational overhead [11]. While these models focus on minimizing prediction error, our work differs by treating the forecasting model as a black box, ensuring the framework is agnostic to the specific architecture used for prediction.

**Uncertainty Quantification**
Accurate decision-making requires not just point estimates but also an understanding of prediction uncertainty. DeepAR pioneered probabilistic forecasting using autoregressive recurrent networks to estimate future distributions [12]. More recently, conformal prediction methods have gained traction for providing distribution-free prediction intervals. These approaches, including Conformal Time-series Forecasting and dynamic interval methods, offer theoretical guarantees on coverage without assuming specific data distributions [13][14]. A general framework for constructing such intervals, EnbPI, was developed to be computationally efficient and scalable for sequential prediction [15]. Unlike these methods that focus on generating static or dynamic intervals post-hoc, our RTS-PnO framework integrates adaptive forecasting uncertainty calibration directly into the end-to-end training process, allowing the system to dynamically adjust to the inherent uncertainty of the chosen forecasting model.

**Predict-then-Optimize and Decision-Focused Learning**
The integration of machine learning with optimization has evolved from the traditional Predict-then-Optimize (PtO) paradigm to more sophisticated Predict-and-Optimize (PnO) and Decision-Focused Learning (DFL) approaches. The PtO framework separates prediction and optimization, often leading to goal mismatch where prediction error does not align with decision quality [16]. To address this, the Smart Predict-then-Optimize (SPO) loss was introduced to measure decision error directly, with SPO+ serving as a convex surrogate [26]. Generalization bounds for these losses have been established to ensure out-of-sample performance [17]. Risk bounds and calibration results for SPO+ have further quantified the transfer of excess surrogate risk to true risk [21]. DFL encompasses a broader range of techniques, including gradient-based and gradient-free methods, to train ML models end-to-end with optimization objectives [22]. Differentiable optimization layers, such as OptNet and CVXPY-based layers, enable backpropagation through optimization problems [23][24]. Specific applications in fund allocation, such as PTOFA, have demonstrated the utility of PnO in industrial settings [18]. Benchmarks comparing PtO and PnO methods highlight the trade-offs between these approaches [19][20]. Furthermore, techniques like contrastive losses and solution caching have been proposed to mitigate the computational bottleneck of solving optimization problems during training [25]. Our work builds on this line of research by introducing an objective alignment measurement that facilitates end-to-end training without requiring differentiable optimization layers, thereby maintaining model agnosticism while effectively mitigating the goal mismatch inherent in naive PtO solutions.
