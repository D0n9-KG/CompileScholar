**Related Works**

Calibration is essential for ensuring that model predictions reflect true probabilities, a property critical for downstream tasks such as bidding and risk assessment. Early approaches to calibration focused on post-hoc adjustments of classifier outputs. Platt scaling [2] and isotonic regression [3] are foundational methods that map raw scores to probabilities using parametric or non-parametric functions. More recently, Beta calibration [5] was proposed as a well-founded improvement over logistic calibration for binary classifiers. In the context of modern neural networks, Guo et al. [4] systematically revisited calibration, finding that architecture significantly influences calibration properties, with newer models often exhibiting better calibration than their predecessors. While these methods provide robust theoretical guarantees, they typically rely on fixed functional forms or simple transformations. Our work builds on this foundation by moving beyond fixed forms, proposing an Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) that learns arbitrary monotonic functions, thereby offering greater expressiveness and flexibility than traditional parametric or isotonic calibrators.

In industrial ranking systems, particularly in advertising and e-commerce, calibration is often performed in a field-aware manner to address data sparsity and distribution shifts. Field-aware Calibration [9] introduced a neural post-hoc method that leverages field-specific information to improve reliability. Subsequent works have refined this approach; for instance, AdaCalib [10] employs a doubly-adaptive mechanism to learn isotonic function families guided by posterior statistics, while Confidence-Aware Multi-Field Model Calibration [7] adjusts calibration intensity based on sample confidence to mitigate bias amplification. Additionally, Deep Ensemble Shape Calibration [12] addresses the challenge of multi-field calibration in e-commerce by decomposing the calibration process to handle sparse data more effectively. MBCT [1] further advanced binning methods by incorporating feature awareness to improve individual uncertainty calibration. Although these methods effectively handle field-specific biases, they often rely on predefined transformation structures or ensemble techniques that may limit modeling power. In contrast, our approach utilizes a highly flexible UMNN to capture complex calibration patterns without being constrained by fixed binning schemes or specific parametric families, ensuring superior adaptability to diverse field conditions.

Another significant line of research focuses on the joint optimization of ranking quality and calibration accuracy. Since ranking models prioritize relative order, their absolute values are often miscalibrated. Multi-objective approaches have been developed to balance these goals. Scale Calibration of Deep Ranking Models [13] and Joint Optimization of Ranking and Calibration (JRC) [14] propose methods to align ranking losses with pointwise calibration losses. RCR [15] argues that standard multi-objective combinations are suboptimal and proposes regression-compatible listwise objectives to ensure mutual alignment between ranking and regression components. Similarly, [11] addresses the limitations of aggregating full candidate lists for ranking loss computation in industrial settings, proposing a self-boosted framework for calibrated ranking. Furthermore, [16] extends privileged features distillation to include calibration-compatible listwise objectives, and [17] explores Bayesian approaches for joint optimization. While these methods integrate calibration into the training process, they often face trade-offs between ranking performance and calibration accuracy due to conflicting loss functions. Our method differs by decoupling the calibration process from the primary ranking training, applying a dedicated UMNN calibrator post-hoc. This separation allows the calibrator to focus exclusively on correcting absolute values while preserving the relative order established by the ranking model, avoiding the optimization conflicts inherent in joint training frameworks.
