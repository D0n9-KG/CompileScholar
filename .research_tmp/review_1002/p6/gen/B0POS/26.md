**Related Works**

The fusion of radar and camera sensors has long been a cornerstone of autonomous driving perception, leveraging the complementary strengths of each modality. Early works focused on basic obstacle detection and visualization using millimeter-wave radar data overlaid on image sequences [1]. Subsequent research advanced this by integrating radar and vision for robust object detection in complex autonomous vehicle surroundings, addressing the inherent limitations of single-modality systems [2]. While these studies established the utility of radar-camera fusion, they largely assumed pre-calibrated sensors or focused on detection algorithms rather than the calibration process itself. In contrast, our work addresses the foundational challenge of maintaining accurate sensor alignment during operation, specifically targeting the automatic geometric calibration of radar and camera systems.

A significant body of literature has focused on automatic extrinsic calibration for LiDAR-camera pairs, which serve as a common benchmark for multi-modal registration. RegNet introduced the first deep convolutional neural network to infer 6-DOF extrinsic calibration, casting feature extraction, matching, and regression into a single real-time capable CNN [6]. CalibNet further advanced this field by employing 3D Spatial Transformer Networks for self-supervised extrinsic calibration, eliminating the need for calibration targets and reducing human effort [7]. More recent approaches, such as CalibRCNN, utilize recurrent convolutional neural networks with geometric constraints to refine calibration estimates [8], while LCCNet exploits cost volume networks inspired by PWC-Net for feature matching, achieving high precision in translation and rotation errors [9]. Additionally, motion-based calibration methods have been explored to align multimodal sensor arrays by leveraging vehicle dynamics [5], and online multi-sensor calibration techniques have been developed based on moving object tracking [10]. Although these methods demonstrate high accuracy for LiDAR-camera systems, they rely on the dense point cloud data provided by LiDARs, which is not available in radar-camera configurations. Our paper differs by addressing the specific challenges of radar data sparsity and uncertainty, proposing a method that does not depend on LiDAR density.

Specific research on radar-camera calibration has emerged to address the unique characteristics of radar sensors, such as data sparsity and measurement noise. One pioneering approach proposed the first data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated targets, utilizing a boosting-inspired training algorithm with coarse and fine convolutional neural networks [11]. Another line of work introduced a continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration, utilizing radar velocity measurements to determine the rigid-body transform without requiring specialized retroreflectors [12]. Furthermore, recent advancements have focused on spatiotemporal calibration of 3D millimeter-wavelength radar-camera pairs, leveraging ego-velocity measurements to perform targetless in-situ calibration that accounts for both spatial and temporal misalignments [13]. These methods have made significant strides in handling radar-specific issues but often focus on rotational components or rely on specific motion assumptions. Our work builds on this line of research by introducing a Dual-Perspective representation that explicitly handles height uncertainty through a Selective Fusion Mechanism, thereby achieving comprehensive 6-DOF automatic calibration that outperforms previous radar-camera and even LiDAR-camera techniques.
