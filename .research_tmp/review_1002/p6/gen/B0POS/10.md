**Related Works**

Curriculum learning is a training strategy that orders data from easy to hard to improve model performance and convergence speed [1]. Recent surveys highlight that while curriculum learning offers benefits across various machine learning tasks, determining the optimal pacing function and sample ranking remains a challenge [2]. In the context of language models, prior work has explored predicting future n-grams as a pre-training objective, such as in ProphetNet, which uses future n-gram prediction to enhance sequence-to-sequence models [3]. Additionally, research into the internal representations of transformers, such as Future Lens, demonstrates that hidden states contain sufficient signal to predict subsequent tokens, suggesting that multi-token prediction is a learnable property within standard architectures [4]. Our work builds on these foundations by applying curriculum learning specifically to the Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) objective, addressing the difficulty smaller language models face in adopting this complex pre-training task from the start.

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising alternative to standard next-token prediction (NTP) for improving sample efficiency and downstream performance. The seminal work on MTP demonstrated that training models to predict multiple future tokens using independent output heads leads to better sample efficiency and improved capabilities in code and natural language generation, particularly for larger models [5]. This approach not only enhances generative quality but also facilitates the development of induction heads and algorithmic reasoning [5]. While MTP has shown significant promise, prior observations indicate that smaller language models (SLMs) struggle to effectively learn this objective without specific training strategies. Our paper addresses this gap by proposing a curriculum learning strategy that gradually introduces the complexity of MTP, enabling SLMs to better leverage the objective during pre-training and improve downstream NTP performance.

The benefits of MTP extend beyond pre-training to inference acceleration through self-speculative decoding. Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference by using a draft model to generate tokens that are then verified by the target model [6]. Recent advancements have focused on self-speculative decoding, where the model itself acts as the drafter, eliminating the need for an auxiliary model. Techniques such as LayerSkip enable early exit inference and self-speculative decoding by skipping intermediate layers [7]. Similarly, Kangaroo utilizes a fixed shallow sub-network as a self-draft model with an adapter to bridge representation gaps, optimizing token acceptance rates [8]. Other methods, like Draft & Verify, use layer skipping to generate draft tokens quickly, ensuring lossless output with significant speedups [9]. SWIFT further refines this by adaptively selecting layers to skip on-the-fly, providing a plug-and-play solution for inference acceleration [10]. Additionally, KOALA enhances speculative decoding by transforming the draft head into a multi-layer architecture with adversarial learning to improve prediction accuracy [11], while Medusa adds extra decoding heads to predict multiple tokens in parallel using tree-based attention [12]. Our work differs from these inference-focused approaches by focusing on the pre-training phase; we show that a forward curriculum for MTP not only improves generative quality but also retains the benefits of self-speculative decoding, whereas a reverse curriculum, though improving NTP performance, fails to provide these decoding benefits.
