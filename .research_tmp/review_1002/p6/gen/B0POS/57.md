**Related Works**

Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a paradigm that unifies indexing and retrieval by encoding corpus knowledge into model parameters, eliminating the need for explicit sparse or dense indexes. Early works like GENRE demonstrated that entities could be retrieved by autoregressively generating their unique names, mitigating the memory footprint issues of dense vector storage [1]. This concept was extended to document retrieval with the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), which maps queries directly to document identifiers (docids) using a single Transformer [2]. Subsequent studies, including DynamicRetriever, Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI), and Ultron, further refined this approach by exploring various docid representations and training strategies to improve recall and end-to-end optimization [3][4][5]. More recently, RIPOR addressed scalability challenges, demonstrating that GR models can perform effectively on large-scale benchmarks by optimizing relevance score assignment during sequential decoding [6]. While these works establish the foundational efficacy of GR on static collections, our paper shifts focus to the critical but understudied domain of dynamic corpora, systematically evaluating how these static models generalize when document collections evolve continuously.

A central challenge in GR is the design of document identifiers, which significantly impacts model performance and generalization. Existing approaches have explored diverse docid types, ranging from numeric IDs to text-based identifiers such as titles or substrings. For instance, GenRet proposed learning to tokenize documents into discrete representations to capture semantic information better than fixed rule-based IDs [7]. Similarly, GERE and CorpusBrain utilized generative models to retrieve evidences and support knowledge-intensive tasks by generating document titles or identifiers [8][9]. Other works have investigated the trade-offs between identifier distinctiveness and semantic richness; for example, Multiview Identifiers Enhanced Generative Retrieval combined synthetic, title, and substring identifiers to facilitate holistic ranking [11], while Autoregressive Search Engines used ngrams as identifiers to avoid forcing hierarchical structures [12]. Furthermore, GR$^2$ addressed the limitation of binary relevance by incorporating multi-graded relevance into docid generation [10]. Although these studies optimize docid design for static performance, they rarely assess how different docid granularities affect robustness in dynamic settings. In contrast, our work reveals that fine-grained, text-based docids offer superior generalization to unseen documents compared to numeric-based ones, identifying semantic alignment and lexical diversity as key drivers of this advantage.

Recent efforts have begun to address the limitations of GR in dynamic environments, where new documents are continuously added. DSI++ and CorpusBrain++ explored updating transformer memory and continual pre-training to incorporate new data without full retraining [13][14]. CLEVER specifically tackled continual learning for GR by introducing Incremental Product Quantization to encode new documents efficiently and a memory-augmented mechanism to prevent catastrophic forgetting [15]. Additionally, recent evaluations on the StreamingQA benchmark have highlighted that GR models are more adaptable to evolving knowledge and more efficient in terms of inference and storage compared to dual encoder models [16]. However, these works often focus on specific mechanisms for updating or efficiency metrics without a comprehensive comparative analysis of docid types. Building on these insights, our paper provides a systematic evaluation of representative GR approaches over dynamic corpora and proposes a novel multi-docid design that leverages the efficiency of numeric IDs and the effectiveness of text-based IDs, achieving improved performance without requiring additional retraining.
