**Related Works**

Personalized outfit recommendation and generation have evolved from traditional retrieval-based methods to generative frameworks. Early approaches focused on modeling compatibility and user preferences using graph neural networks and hierarchical structures to predict well-matched items [1][2]. Subsequent works introduced learnable anchors and capsule wardrobe modeling to better capture user-specific coordination preferences [4][5]. In industrial applications, models like POG have bridged outfit generation and recommendation by leveraging Transformer architectures to connect individual item preferences with overall outfit compatibility [6]. More recently, the field has shifted towards generative models that can create novel fashion items rather than just selecting from existing catalogs. Surveys highlight this transition, noting that generative AI offers the potential to transcend the limitations of predefined product sets [3][11]. While these methods have improved the diversity and personalization of recommendations, they often rely on supervised learning paradigms that may limit the model's ability to align with nuanced user preferences without extensive labeled data. In contrast, our work proposes FashionDPO, a framework that fine-tunes generative models using direct preference optimization to enhance alignment with user preferences without relying on task-specific reward functions.

The application of diffusion models in fashion generation has gained significant traction due to their high-fidelity image synthesis capabilities. Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) have been adapted for various fashion tasks, including virtual try-on and outfit composition [8][14][15][16][17]. Specifically, DiFashion introduces a generative outfit recommendation task that uses diffusion models to generate multiple fashion images in parallel, guided by conditions for fidelity, compatibility, and personalization [10]. ControlNet and other architectures have further enabled conditional control over these diffusion processes, allowing for precise spatial and semantic conditioning [9]. Additionally, recent frameworks like Smart Fitting Room integrate matching-aware virtual try-on to ensure that generated or selected items are suitable for the user [18]. Despite these advances, existing generative fashion models often struggle with diversity and may not fully capture the subjective nature of personalization. Our approach builds on these generative foundations by introducing a preference-based fine-tuning mechanism that refines the pre-trained model’s outputs based on multi-expert feedback, thereby addressing the diversity and personalization gaps in current diffusion-based fashion generation.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has emerged as a powerful technique for aligning generative models with human preferences, offering a stable alternative to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Originally developed for large language models, DPO allows for the optimization of policies using pairwise preference data without the need for a separate reward model [22]. This paradigm has been successfully extended to diffusion models, with methods like Diffusion-DPO and D3PO demonstrating that diffusion models can be aligned with human preferences by directly optimizing on comparison data [23][24]. Recent extensions have applied DPO to personalized image generation, such as PatchDPO, which estimates patch-level quality to improve consistency with reference images [20], and JeDi, which enables finetuning-free personalization through joint-image diffusion [19]. Furthermore, step-aware preference optimization has been explored to align preferences with denoising performance at each step [25]. While these methods have shown promise in general image generation and text-to-image tasks, their application to the specific domain of fashion outfit generation remains underexplored. Our paper distinguishes itself by designing a multi-expert feedback generation module that evaluates quality, compatibility, and personalization, providing a comprehensive and objective signal for DPO fine-tuning in the fashion domain.
