**Related Works**

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have become the dominant paradigm for collaborative filtering, evolving from early spectral methods to spatial message-passing architectures. Foundational work introduced scalable semi-supervised learning on graphs [5], which was subsequently adapted for recommendation systems through graph auto-encoders [6] and explicit propagation of user-item interactions [7]. Recent advancements have focused on simplifying these architectures, such as LightGCN, which removes feature transformations and nonlinear activations to enhance efficiency [9], and disentangled models that capture diverse user intents [8]. A comprehensive survey of GNNs highlights their versatility in handling non-Euclidean data [17]. While these methods effectively model high-order connectivity, they typically rely on high-precision floating-point embeddings, which incur significant memory and computational costs during online inference. In contrast, our work focuses on binarizing these graph representations to achieve efficient deployment without sacrificing the structural modeling capabilities of GNNs.

To address the efficiency challenges of large-scale recommendation, representation quantization and hashing techniques have been widely explored. Early approaches utilized locality-sensitive hashing for similarity search [10], while deep learning methods like HashNet addressed the optimization difficulties of binary activations [11]. In the context of recommender systems, discrete collaborative filtering [12] and binary code learning for candidate generation [15] have demonstrated the potential of compact representations for top-N recommendation. More recently, specific efforts have targeted the binarization of GNNs, such as Bi-GCN [19] and general binary GNN strategies [20], which aim to reduce model size and leverage fast bitwise operations. However, many of these approaches treat binarization as a post-hoc quantization step or a separate optimization task, often neglecting the substantial information loss that occurs when continuous embeddings are forced into binary sequences. Our paper differs by explicitly mitigating this information loss at multiple stages of the embedding binarization process, ensuring that the compact binary codes retain sufficient collaborative signal for accurate ranking.

The predecessor to our work, BiGeaR, introduced multi-faceted quantization reinforcement to learn binarized graph representations, addressing the performance degradation associated with naive binarization [21]. This framework demonstrated that retaining representation informativeness is crucial for maintaining accuracy in top-K recommendation tasks. Building upon this foundation, we propose BiGeaR++, which further enhances the framework by incorporating supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples. Specifically, we introduce a fine-grained inference distillation mechanism and an effective embedding sample synthesis approach. These innovations allow the model to leverage both real item data and latent embedding samples, thereby providing richer guidance for the binarization process. Unlike previous methods that rely solely on the original interaction graph or simple quantization losses, our approach actively synthesizes samples to bridge the gap between continuous and binary spaces, resulting in state-of-the-art performance improvements over BiGeaR and other competing methods.

Knowledge distillation has emerged as a powerful technique for transferring information from complex teacher models to efficient student models, originally proposed for neural networks [22]. In the domain of graph learning, distillation methods have been developed to preserve local structural relationships [24], global topology [25], and holistic knowledge [26]. Recent surveys and works have explored various strategies for distilling knowledge in GNNs, including free-direction transfer [29] and fine-grained behavior-oriented approaches [28]. Additionally, distillation has been applied to hashing tasks to mitigate representation discrepancies in cross-modal retrieval [30][31][32]. While these methods effectively compress models or improve retrieval robustness, they are generally not tailored to the specific challenge of binarizing collaborative filtering embeddings. Our work uniquely integrates distillation principles into the binarization pipeline, using pseudo-positive samples to guide the student binary model, thus achieving a seamless combination of efficiency and accuracy that surpasses standard distillation or quantization techniques.
