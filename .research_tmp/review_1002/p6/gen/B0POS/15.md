Brain tumor segmentation has become a critical task in medical imaging, primarily driven by the need for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning for gliomas [3]. The BraTS challenge has served as a standard benchmark for evaluating these algorithms, providing multi-institutional, multi-parametric MRI data to assess the segmentation of histologically distinct tumor sub-regions [2][4]. Early approaches relied on traditional machine learning methods, but the field has rapidly shifted towards deep learning architectures capable of handling the complex heterogeneity of brain tumors [3]. The U-Net architecture, with its encoder-decoder structure and skip connections, established a foundational framework for biomedical image segmentation, which has since been adapted for 3D volumetric data [1]. While these early models laid the groundwork, they often required extensive manual tuning and large datasets to achieve robust performance. Our work builds on this foundation by addressing the specific inefficiencies in current state-of-the-art 3D segmentation pipelines, aiming to reduce training requirements while maintaining high accuracy.

The nnU-Net framework has emerged as a dominant approach in recent years, offering a self-configuring method that automatically determines optimal network configurations for various biomedical image segmentation tasks [10]. In the context of brain tumors, nnU-Net has achieved state-of-the-art results in multiple BraTS challenges, often relying on ensemble strategies and cross-validation to maximize performance [7]. For instance, the winning solution for BraTS 2020 utilized unmodified nnU-Net with specific post-processing and data augmentation tweaks, achieving high Dice scores [7]. Subsequent works, such as the BraTS 2021 winner, further extended nnU-Net by incorporating larger networks and axial attention mechanisms in the decoder to capture more spatial context [8]. Despite these successes, the reliance on extensive training epochs and the underutilization of pre-trained weights remain significant limitations in the current nnU-Net paradigm. In contrast, our paper integrates Axial-Coronal-Sagittal convolutions and pre-trained ImageNet weights directly into the nnU-Net framework, specifically targeting the reduction of trainable parameters and training time.

To address the challenge of limited training data in 3D medical imaging, several studies have explored transfer learning strategies. Med3D proposed a heterogeneous 3D network co-trained on diverse medical datasets to extract general 3D features, demonstrating that pre-training can significantly accelerate convergence compared to training from scratch [13]. Similarly, Models Genesis introduced self-supervised 3D pre-training to overcome the limitations of 2D transfer learning, showing that generic 3D models can outperform both scratch learning and 2D pre-trained models [14]. However, these methods often require large, diverse datasets for pre-training or complex self-supervised setups that may not be readily applicable to specific clinical tasks like brain tumor segmentation. Our approach differs by focusing on the direct transfer of 2D pre-trained weights from ImageNet to the 3D domain, presenting two specific strategies to preserve learned feature relationships without the need for large-scale 3D pre-training datasets.

Recent advancements have also incorporated Transformer architectures to capture long-range dependencies that convolutions may miss. TransBTS was among the first to exploit Transformers in 3D CNNs for brain tumor segmentation, combining local 3D context with global feature modeling [11]. Swin UNETR further advanced this by using Swin Transformers to handle variable tumor sizes and long-range information, achieving strong performance on BraTS datasets [12]. While these Transformer-based models offer powerful global context modeling, they often come with increased computational complexity and parameter counts. Our work diverges from this trend by enhancing the efficiency of convolutional architectures through strategic weight transfer and architectural modifications, rather than increasing model complexity. By leveraging pre-trained encoders from a proxy classification task, we aim to improve segmentation performance for challenging tumor labels while maintaining computational efficiency, offering a practical alternative to heavy Transformer-based ensembles.
