**Related Works**

Early approaches to sequential recommendation primarily relied on Markov Chains and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to capture user behavior dynamics. Markov Chain-based methods assume that the next action depends only on recent history, offering parsimony but limited long-range dependency modeling [8]. RNNs, such as those proposed for session-based recommendations, improved upon this by modeling the entire session sequence, though they often struggled with vanishing gradients and computational inefficiency over long sequences [11]. Subsequent works introduced hierarchical RNNs to personalize session-based models by transferring latent states across sessions, addressing the lack of user identifiers in some domains [6]. While these methods laid the groundwork for sequential modeling, they generally lack the capacity to efficiently handle the varying sequence lengths and diverse interaction patterns inherent in modern large-scale systems. In contrast, STAR-Rec moves beyond these recurrent structures by employing a state-space modeling framework that processes variable-length sequences with linear complexity, thereby overcoming the computational bottlenecks of traditional RNNs.

The advent of Transformer architectures significantly advanced sequential recommendation by enabling the capture of long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms. SASRec introduced self-attention to balance the long-term semantics of RNNs with the local focus of Markov Chains, identifying relevant items from a user’s history [8]. BERT4Rec further extended this by utilizing bidirectional encoder representations and the Cloze task to mitigate the limitations of unidirectional architectures [9]. However, standard attention mechanisms suffer from quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length, posing significant challenges for lifelong user behavior sequences. To address this, recent studies have explored sparse attention [4], frequency-enhanced hybrid attention to capture periodic characteristics [1], and linear attention mechanisms like LinRec to reduce computational costs while preserving learning capabilities [10]. Additionally, dynamic memory-based attention networks have been proposed to segment long sequences and maintain memory blocks for long-term interest preservation [7]. Despite these improvements, pure attention-based models often struggle to efficiently unify the modeling of temporal dynamics with the capture of diverse item relationships. STAR-Rec addresses this by synergistically combining preference-aware attention with state-space modeling, allowing for efficient processing of long sequences while explicitly modeling both similar and diverse item relationships.

Recently, State Space Models (SSMs), particularly the Mamba architecture, have emerged as a promising alternative to Transformers, offering linear-time sequence modeling with selective mechanisms [12]. Theoretical works have demonstrated the close relationship between SSMs and attention mechanisms, showing that they can be unified through structured state space duality [14]. This has spurred a wave of research applying SSMs to sequential recommendation. Mamba4Rec was the first to explore selective SSMs for this domain, demonstrating superior effectiveness and efficiency compared to RNN- and attention-based baselines [20]. Subsequent studies have investigated the capabilities of SSMs in lifelong sequential recommendation, showing that Mamba-based models can significantly reduce training duration while maintaining performance on sequences exceeding 2,000 items [15][19]. Other variants, such as EchoMamba4Rec, have integrated bidirectional processing and spectral filtering to enhance pattern capture [16], while bidirectional gated Mamba architectures have further refined these approaches [18]. Furthermore, SSMs have been applied to related tasks like knowledge tracing to improve efficiency and interpretability [17]. While these works highlight the potential of SSMs, they often treat the model as a monolithic unit. STAR-Rec distinguishes itself by incorporating a sequence-level mixture-of-experts framework that adaptively routes different behavioral patterns to specialized experts, effectively handling both focused category-specific browsing and diverse exploration patterns within a unified SSM-attention architecture.
