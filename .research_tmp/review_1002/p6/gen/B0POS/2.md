**Related Works**

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have recently advanced the integration of visual and textual data. Models such as Qwen-VL [1] and VideoChat [2] focus on video understanding, leveraging spatiotemporal reasoning to answer questions about visual content. Video-LLaMA [3] extends this by incorporating audio-visual instruction tuning, while Unified-IO 2 [4] unifies image, text, audio, and action generation within a single autoregressive framework. GPT-4 [5] represents a significant leap in general-purpose multimodal reasoning. However, these systems primarily treat visual inputs as static or sequential data for comprehension, rather than as dynamic, generative nonverbal cues synchronized with speech. MARS differs by explicitly modeling nonverbal generation as a core output modality, using vector-quantized representations to produce time-aligned facial expressions and body language alongside text.

Research on nonverbal communication has traditionally focused on emotion recognition and dataset curation. Datasets like IEMOCAP [6], CMU-MOSEI [7], and MELD [8] provide rich annotations for audio, visual, and textual modalities, enabling the study of emotion in conversations. More recent works, such as EmotionCLIP [12], explore pre-training paradigms to extract emotion representations from uncurated verbal and nonverbal data. While these resources are vital for understanding affect, they are typically used for classification tasks rather than generative modeling. MARS builds on this foundation by introducing VENUS, a large-scale dataset with time-aligned text, facial expressions, and body language, specifically designed to train a generative model that produces coherent nonverbal cues in response to conversational input.

Several approaches have attempted to incorporate visual context into conversational agents. CHAMPAGNE [9] utilizes large-scale web videos to learn real-world conversation, emphasizing the role of visual context in meaning. Let’s Go Real Talk [10] introduces a face-to-face spoken dialogue model that processes and generates audio-visual speech, marking a step toward avatar chatbots. In the domain of embodied agents, FurChat [13] and systems using LLMs for social robots [14] generate facial expressions and gestures to enhance interaction. These works often rely on prompt engineering or separate modules for nonverbal generation. In contrast, MARS integrates nonverbal understanding and generation within a unified next-token prediction framework, allowing for seamless co-generation of text and nonverbal cues without intermediate text-only bottlenecks.

Recent advancements in motion generation have explored the synthesis of holistic body movements. Works such as [16] generate 3D body motions from speech, using compositional VQ-VAEs for diverse results. HumanTOMATO [18] focuses on text-driven whole-body motion, aligning textual descriptions with facial and body movements. In the context of dyadic interactions, [19] and [20] model listener responses by treating quantized motion elements as language tokens, demonstrating that LLMs can predict nonverbal reactions to speech. While these methods effectively generate motion from text or audio, they often focus on specific aspects like listening responses or isolated motion synthesis. MARS extends this line of work by training a multimodal language model on a comprehensive dataset to generate both text and nonverbal languages simultaneously, achieving a more immersive and context-aware conversational experience.
