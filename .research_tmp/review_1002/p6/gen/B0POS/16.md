Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to overcome the limitations of closed-set detectors that are restricted to pre-defined categories [1]. Early approaches focused on mapping visual features to semantic spaces, often leveraging large-scale image-text datasets to learn transferable representations [2]. The CLIP model, for instance, demonstrated that pre-training on natural language supervision allows for zero-shot transfer to various downstream tasks, including object detection, by aligning image and text embeddings [2]. Subsequent works have refined this alignment to address the domain shift between whole-image and region-level representations. RegionCLIP, for example, extends CLIP to learn region-based visual representations, significantly improving performance on open-vocabulary detection benchmarks by capturing fine-grained alignment between image regions and textual concepts [7]. Similarly, CoDet reformulates region-word alignment as a co-occurring object discovery problem, leveraging visual similarities to align objects with shared concepts in captions, thereby surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods on OV-LVIS [8]. While these methods focus on improving the underlying vision-language alignment for detection, our work, VocAda, operates at the inference stage to refine the user-defined vocabulary itself, ensuring that the detector is guided by categories relevant to the specific image content rather than relying solely on improved feature alignment.

The scale and diversity of detection datasets have also evolved to support broader vocabulary sizes. The COCO dataset introduced complex everyday scenes with 91 object types, establishing a standard for scene understanding [3]. To address the long-tail distribution of categories in natural images, the LVIS dataset was introduced, providing instance segmentation masks for over 1,000 categories and highlighting the challenges of low-sample regimes [4]. More recently, Detic expanded the vocabulary of detectors to tens of thousands of concepts by training detector classifiers on image classification data, demonstrating that large-scale image-level supervision can effectively boost open-vocabulary and long-tail detection performance [6]. These datasets and training strategies have enabled detectors to handle a wider range of classes, yet they do not address the issue of overly broad or mis-specified vocabularies at test time. In contrast, VocAda does not require retraining or access to large-scale labeled data; instead, it dynamically filters the user-provided vocabulary at inference time, making it a plug-and-play solution compatible with existing state-of-the-art detectors trained on datasets like COCO and Objects365.

Recent efforts have also sought to enhance the robustness of open-vocabulary detectors to variations in semantic granularity. SHiNe, for instance, introduces a semantic hierarchy nexus to improve classifier robustness across different vocabulary granularities by integrating super- and sub-categories from class hierarchies into the detection process [9]. This approach addresses the variability in detector performance when dealing with vocabularies of differing specificity. However, SHiNe relies on offline processing of semantic hierarchies to generate classifier vectors. Our proposed VocAda differs by operating entirely at inference time without any training or offline hierarchy construction. By using an image captioner to parse nouns and select relevant classes from the user-defined vocabulary, VocAda automatically tailors the detection task to the visible objects in the image, thereby mitigating the performance degradation caused by irrelevant or overly broad vocabulary specifications.
