**Related Works**

Rehearsal-based methods constitute a prominent class of continual learning (CL) strategies that mitigate catastrophic forgetting by storing and retraining on a subset of past data alongside new inputs. Early approaches, such as Deep Generative Replay, utilized generative models to sample past data for interleaved training with new tasks [5]. Other methods, like iCaRL, combined exemplar storage with classifier and representation learning to handle class-incremental scenarios [7]. While these empirical studies demonstrate the efficacy of concurrent rehearsal, they largely lack rigorous theoretical characterizations of how the interplay between past and new data affects generalization and forgetting. In contrast, our work provides the first comprehensive theoretical analysis of rehearsal-based CL, explicitly characterizing the trade-offs between concurrent and sequential training strategies in overparameterized linear models.

A parallel line of research focuses on regularization and gradient projection techniques to constrain updates and preserve prior knowledge. Elastic Weight Consolidation and its variants regularize weights important for previous tasks [2]. More recent methods, such as Gradient Episodic Memory (GEM) and Gradient Projection Memory (GPM), project gradients onto subspaces orthogonal to those of past tasks to minimize interference [3][8]. Trust Region Gradient Projection (TRGP) further refines this by selecting related old tasks to facilitate forward knowledge transfer [4]. Theoretical analyses of these projection-based methods have begun to emerge, with studies deriving generalization bounds for Orthogonal Gradient Descent in the Neural Tangent Kernel regime [9] and analyzing catastrophic forgetting through NTK overlap matrices [10]. Additionally, regularization-based CL has been studied from a loss approximation viewpoint, linking it to second-order Taylor expansions [11]. However, these works primarily address constraint-based or regularization-based paradigms rather than the specific dynamics of data replay. Our paper differs by focusing specifically on the rehearsal mechanism, providing explicit forms for expected forgetting and generalization error that are not available in these projection-based theoretical frameworks.

Recent theoretical efforts have also examined CL in linear settings, offering insights into the mechanics of forgetting. Studies on linear regression have established connections between CL and alternating projections, providing bounds on forgetting for cyclic and random task orderings [13]. Other works have analyzed the impact of overparameterization on forgetting in random orthogonal transformation tasks [16] and derived general theoretical insights into how task sequence and algorithmic parameters influence forgetting in both underparameterized and overparameterized regimes [15]. Furthermore, recent analyses have provided explicit forms for expected forgetting and generalization error in overparameterized linear models, highlighting the roles of task similarity and ordering [14]. While these studies offer valuable foundational insights into linear CL, they often treat the training process as a monolithic sequential update or focus on specific task orderings without distinguishing between concurrent and sequential rehearsal strategies. Our work builds on these foundations by explicitly comparing concurrent and sequential rehearsal, revealing that sequential rehearsal is superior for dissimilar tasks, and motivating a novel Hybrid Rehearsal method that optimally combines both strategies.
