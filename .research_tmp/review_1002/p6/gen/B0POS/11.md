3D human pose estimation from monocular video remains a challenging task due to depth ambiguity and the need to recover 3D structure from 2D projections [1]. Early approaches relied on geometric constraints, but recent deep learning methods have shifted towards end-to-end learning paradigms [4]. A significant line of research focuses on graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model the skeletal structure. For instance, spatial-temporal GCNs exploit relationships between joints over time [3], while directed graph convolutions explicitly capture the hierarchical nature of the human skeleton [11]. Other methods, such as HPGCN, utilize hierarchical poselets to guide the graph convolution process [22]. While these methods effectively model local skeletal dependencies, they often struggle with long-range interactions between non-linked body parts. In contrast, our paper addresses this limitation by moving beyond standard GCNs to capture long-range dependencies more effectively.

To model long-range dependencies, several studies have incorporated non-local operations or attention mechanisms. Non-local neural networks compute responses as weighted sums of features from all positions, capturing global context [14]. In the domain of pose estimation, transformer-based architectures have gained prominence. GraFormer introduces a graph-oriented transformer to handle the structured nature of pose data [7], while HDFormer leverages high-order directed attention to model complex joint and bone interactions [10]. Furthermore, multi-hypothesis transformers like MHFormer address the ambiguity of 2D-to-3D lifting by learning multiple plausible pose hypotheses [9]. Although these transformer-based methods improve the modeling of global relationships, they often require deeper networks or complex attention mechanisms that increase model size and introduce uncorrelated noise. Our approach differs by proposing a lightweight architecture that efficiently captures these dependencies without excessive computational overhead.

Another critical aspect of 3D pose estimation is the exploitation of temporal and spatial contexts. Methods such as Strided Transformer and MixSTE focus on aggregating information from long sequences of 2D poses to infer a single 3D pose [18][19]. These approaches often use hierarchical structures to reduce sequence redundancy and model spatio-temporal correlations. Additionally, diffusion-based models like DiffPose have emerged to handle the uncertainty and indeterminacy inherent in monocular pose estimation by formulating it as a reverse diffusion process [15][16]. While these methods effectively leverage temporal redundancy, they may not explicitly account for the multi-scale structural hierarchy of the human body. Our work builds on these insights by integrating a pyramid structure that captures correlations across different scales, complementing the context of human sub-structures in a more structured manner.

The concept of multi-scale representation is also prevalent in pose estimation. Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks process graph-structured features across three different scales of skeletal representations to learn both local and global features [23]. Similarly, coarse-to-fine volumetric prediction schemes refine initial estimates through iterative processing [2]. These methods demonstrate the value of hierarchical feature learning. However, existing multi-scale approaches often treat scales independently or concatenate them in ways that do not fully exploit cross-scale correlations. In this paper, we propose a Pyramid Graph Attention (PGA) module that concatenates information from various scales into a compact sequence and computes correlations in parallel. This allows our Pyramid Graph Transformer (PGFormer) to capture pyramid-structured long-range dependencies more effectively than previous multi-scale methods, resulting in a lighter model with superior performance on standard benchmarks.
