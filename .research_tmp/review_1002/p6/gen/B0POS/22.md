**Related Works**

Semantic segmentation has evolved significantly with the advent of deep learning architectures. Early breakthroughs introduced fully convolutional networks for pixel-wise prediction [1], which were subsequently enhanced by atrous convolutions and spatial pyramid pooling to capture multi-scale context [2][3][4]. More recently, transformer-based models have demonstrated superior performance by leveraging global attention mechanisms, such as in SegFormer and SETR [6][7]. While these supervised methods achieve high accuracy, they rely heavily on large-scale annotated datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. In contrast, our work focuses on unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA-SS), aiming to transfer knowledge from labeled source data to unlabeled target data without requiring pixel-level annotations in the target domain.

To address the lack of target annotations, UDA-SS methods typically employ adversarial learning or self-training strategies. Adversarial approaches align feature distributions between domains, as seen in methods that adapt structured output spaces [8][9] or learn texture-invariant representations [10]. Self-training methods, such as CRST and PixMatch, refine pseudo-labels through confidence regularization or pixel-wise consistency [15][20]. Other techniques include bidirectional learning [23], curriculum learning [22], and cross-domain mixed sampling [26]. Recent advances have also incorporated high-resolution training [17] and transformer-based architectures specifically for adaptation [16]. However, these traditional UDA-SS methods generally assume that the category sets of the source and target domains are identical and known a priori. Our paper differs by addressing the more realistic scenario where private classes may exist in the target domain, which causes performance degradation in standard UDA-SS frameworks due to confusion between common and private classes.

Universal Domain Adaptation (UNDA) addresses the challenge of unknown category shifts, where the target domain may contain classes not present in the source. Early works in this area, such as Universal Multi-Source Domain Adaptation, aim to identify common label sets across multiple sources [27]. Subsequent methods have proposed learning to detect open classes [28] or using self-supervised tasks like rotation recognition to separate known and unknown samples [29]. DANCE introduces neighborhood clustering and entropy-based alignment to handle arbitrary category shifts without prior knowledge of the setting [30]. Similarly, OVANet learns a threshold for rejecting unknown samples using one-vs-all classifiers [31]. While these UNDA methods handle category shifts, they often focus on image classification or general feature alignment. Our work builds on this line of research by specifically targeting semantic segmentation, proposing UniMAP to distinguish domain-specific prototypes and match images to enhance the identification of common classes in the presence of private classes.
