**Related Works**

Protein dynamics are fundamental to biological function, yet traditional study relies on computationally expensive molecular dynamics (MD) simulations that often struggle with rare event sampling and long equilibration times [5][7]. To address these limitations, recent advances in generative modeling have emerged as efficient alternatives for conformational sampling. Early approaches like Boltzmann Generators utilized neural networks to learn coordinate transformations of equilibrium distributions, enabling one-shot sampling of condensed matter systems [2]. More recently, diffusion models have become a dominant paradigm for this task. EigenFold introduced a diffusion framework that models protein structures as harmonic oscillators, allowing for the sampling of conformational ensembles and capturing model uncertainty [4]. Similarly, Str2Str proposed a score-based framework for zero-shot conformation sampling that leverages denoising score matching on general crystal structures, avoiding the need for target-specific simulation data [5]. Other methods, such as ConfDiff, have attempted to incorporate physical prior knowledge by using force-guided networks within an SE(3) diffusion model to reduce deviations from the equilibrium distribution [7]. Furthermore, flow matching approaches like AlphaFlow repurpose single-state predictors such as AlphaFold to generate diverse protein ensembles, demonstrating faster convergence to equilibrium properties than MD trajectories [8]. While these methods significantly improve sampling efficiency, they often rely on data-driven objectives that may not fully capture the physical energy landscape. In contrast, our work introduces Energy-based Alignment (EBA), which explicitly aligns generative models with physical feedback to calibrate conformational states based on energy differences, thereby enhancing physical plausibility beyond standard data-driven training.

A parallel line of research focuses on predicting equilibrium distributions and utilizing MD data for generative tasks. Distributional Graphormer (DiG) employs deep neural networks to transform simple distributions into equilibrium distributions conditioned on molecular descriptors, providing estimations of state densities for various molecular systems [6]. Additionally, generative modeling of MD trajectories has been explored to create surrogate models that can perform forward simulation, transition path sampling, and trajectory upsampling from learned data [10]. These approaches highlight the potential of learning from simulation data to bypass the computational costs of explicit MD runs. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven frameworks remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives can lead to intractable optimization problems. Our approach differs by proposing a method that efficiently integrates physical model feedback into the generative process, specifically addressing the challenge of balancing conformational states according to their energy differences without requiring intractable optimization of standard energy objectives.

The integration of external feedback or rewards into generative models has also been explored in other domains, such as natural language processing and image generation. Techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) have been used to align language models with human preferences by training a reward model and fine-tuning the policy [11]. In the context of diffusion models, methods like Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO) pose denoising as a multi-step decision-making problem to optimize for downstream objectives such as aesthetic quality or image compressibility [12]. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) further simplifies this by extracting optimal policies in closed form, eliminating the need for complex reinforcement learning procedures [13]. While these methods demonstrate the efficacy of aligning generative models with specific reward signals, they are typically applied to subjective or task-specific metrics rather than physical laws. Our work builds on this concept of alignment but applies it to the domain of structural biology, using physical energy functions as the supervisory signal to ensure that generated protein ensembles are not only diverse but also physically plausible and energetically consistent.
