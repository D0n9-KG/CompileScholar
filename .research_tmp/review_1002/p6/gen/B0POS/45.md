**Related Works**

Research in opinion mining has traditionally prioritized sentiment polarity classification, aiming to categorize user feedback into positive, negative, or neutral categories to gauge overall product satisfaction. Tools such as Appsent have been developed to facilitate the analysis of app reviews, providing developers with user-friendly interfaces and meaningful analytics to guide software evolution [2]. While these tools effectively address the labor-intensive nature of reading reviews and enhance end-user satisfaction through responsive development, they primarily focus on high-level sentiment aggregation rather than the nuanced emotional states underlying user feedback. Furthermore, empirical analyses of specific app categories, such as mobile learning applications, have examined usage experiences but often lack a granular emotional framework [3]. Our work differs from this line of research by shifting the focus from broad sentiment polarity to fine-grained emotion classification, thereby capturing the specific emotional drivers behind user feedback that standard sentiment tools overlook.

To address the limitations of coarse-grained sentiment analysis, recent studies have begun exploring multilabel and multiclass approaches to capture more complex affective dimensions. For instance, researchers have constructed datasets from Indonesian mobile application reviews that support multilabel sentiment and emotion classification, acknowledging that a single review can contain multiple emotional signals [1]. However, these efforts often rely on existing taxonomies or ad-hoc label sets that may not fully align with established psychological models of emotion. By adapting Plutchik’s emotion taxonomy specifically to the context of app reviews, our study provides a structured annotation framework that bridges the gap between psychological theory and software engineering practice. This approach allows for a more theoretically grounded and consistent identification of emotions compared to previous dataset-centric efforts that lack a unified psychological basis.

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has sparked significant interest in automating complex annotation tasks, including those involving pragmatic and discursive features that are difficult to map to lexical forms. Studies have demonstrated that LLMs, such as GPT-4, can approach human-level accuracy in annotating complex linguistic features like apology components, suggesting their potential for scalable corpus annotation [7]. Similarly, LLMs have been evaluated as substitutes for human experts in annotating political text [4] and as financial data annotators, where their effectiveness and efficiency have been rigorously tested [6]. In the domain of materials science, semi-automated approaches using models like Gemini Pro have been employed to craft annotated outputs, highlighting the versatility of LLMs across diverse domains [5]. While these studies confirm the feasibility of LLM-assisted annotation, they often operate in domains with more structured linguistic cues or specific domain constraints. Our paper builds on this foundation by evaluating the specific feasibility of automating fine-grained emotion annotation in app reviews, assessing not only accuracy but also the cost-effectiveness and agreement with human-labelled data in a context where emotional interpretation is inherently ambiguous.

To mitigate the risks of full automation, recent work has emphasized the importance of human-LLM collaboration in annotation pipelines. Systems like MEGAnno+ have been proposed to facilitate collaborative annotation, leveraging the strengths of both human expertise and LLM efficiency [8]. Other approaches focus on the effective verification of LLM labels, ensuring that human annotators can efficiently validate or correct model outputs [9]. These collaborative frameworks acknowledge that while LLMs can reduce manual effort, human oversight remains critical for maintaining quality in complex annotation tasks. Our study aligns with this collaborative paradigm by documenting the challenges in emotion classification and demonstrating that while LLMs significantly reduce manual effort, full automation remains challenging due to the complexity of emotional interpretation. By providing structured guidelines and an annotated dataset, our work offers a practical pathway for developing hybrid pipelines that balance automated efficiency with human precision.
