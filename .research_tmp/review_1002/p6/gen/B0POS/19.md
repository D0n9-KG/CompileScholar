**Related Works**

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has evolved significantly, moving from early deep learning approaches that generalized speech-to-animation mappings [1] to more sophisticated models that address the ill-posed nature of the task. Early methods like VOCA introduced neural networks to factor identity from facial motion, enabling realistic animations for unseen subjects [10]. However, these deterministic regression-based approaches often suffer from the "regression-to-mean" problem, resulting in over-smoothed and less vivid facial motions [4]. To mitigate this, recent works have explored stochastic generation. FaceDiffuser and 3DiFACE utilize diffusion models to capture the non-deterministic nature of facial cues, allowing for diverse and expressive outputs that better reflect the one-to-many relationship between speech and facial expressions [5][9]. Similarly, CodeTalker casts the problem as a code query task in a discrete motion space, reducing mapping uncertainty and enhancing motion vividness [4]. While these methods improve diversity and realism, they primarily focus on the generative process or motion space representation. In contrast, our work addresses the root cause of feature ambiguity in the audio domain, proposing a semantic decorrelation module that enhances the input features before they are processed by the generative model, thereby improving the precision of lip shape generation for phonetically similar syllables.

A critical component of modern speech-driven animation is the use of self-supervised speech representations. Models such as wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT have demonstrated the ability to learn powerful acoustic and linguistic features from unlabeled audio, outperforming traditional hand-crafted features [14][15]. These pre-trained encoders are widely adopted in recent frameworks like FaceFormer, which uses them to encode long-term audio context for autoregressive mesh prediction [3]. FaceXHuBERT further leverages HuBERT to capture subtle non-lexical cues such as identity and emotion without requiring large lexicons [6]. Other approaches, such as Media2Face, integrate these audio features with multi-modality guidance in a diffusion framework to achieve high-fidelity synthesis [16]. Despite their success, these self-supervised models often exhibit significant coupling for near-homophone syllables, where phonetically similar sounds with distinct lip shapes are mapped to similar feature vectors. This coupling leads to an averaging effect in the generated lip motions. Our paper introduces Wav2Sem, a plug-and-play module that decorrelates these audio encodings by injecting semantic information, specifically targeting the limitation of feature ambiguity in self-supervised spaces.

Beyond basic lip synchronization, recent research has focused on disentangling speech content from emotional and stylistic expressions. Methods like EMOTE and EmoTalk employ decoupled losses or disentangling encoders to separate speech-driven lip movements from emotion-driven facial deformations, allowing for controllable emotional intensity [7][8]. Similarly, Mimic explores the disentanglement of speaking style and semantic content to synthesize subject-specific animations [11]. Earlier works also highlighted the need for expressive control, proposing emotion controllers to adjust facial expressions independently of the speech signal [2]. While these methods successfully isolate emotional and stylistic components, they typically assume that the underlying speech features are sufficiently distinct to drive accurate lip shapes. Our approach complements these efforts by ensuring that the semantic content of the speech is clearly represented in the feature space, thereby providing a more robust foundation for both lip-sync accuracy and the subsequent modeling of expressive attributes.
