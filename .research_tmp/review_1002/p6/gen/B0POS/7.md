**Related Works**

Safety alignment in Large Language Models (LLMs) is primarily achieved through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which trains models to be helpful and harmless [1][2]. However, these alignment mechanisms are often brittle. Research has demonstrated that safety training can be easily subverted through fine-tuning on small datasets of malicious examples [4][5][6] or via adaptive jailbreaking attacks that exploit model vulnerabilities [7][8][9]. These findings highlight the fragility of current alignment strategies and the need for robust, mechanistic understanding of how safety behaviors are encoded within the model [10]. Our work addresses this fragility by moving beyond output-level detection to identify the internal representations that mediate refusal, providing a more robust foundation for steering behavior in adversarial settings.

The linear representation hypothesis posits that high-level concepts are encoded as directions in the activation space of neural networks [26][28][29]. This principle has been foundational to mechanistic interpretability, enabling the identification of specific directions associated with linguistic features or world models [20][24][27]. Recent work has extended this to LLMs, showing that concepts like truthfulness or sentiment can be manipulated by modifying activations along these directions [13][17][18]. Specifically, studies have identified that refusal behavior in chat models is mediated by a single direction in the residual stream [11] or can be modeled as an affine function [12]. While these works establish the existence of such directions, they often rely on predefined templates or manual analysis to identify them. COSMIC differs by introducing an automated framework that uses cosine similarity to identify viable steering directions and target layers without relying on assumptions about specific refusal tokens or manual intervention.

Activation steering methods, such as Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) and Activation Addition (ActAdd), modify intermediate activations to control model outputs [17][18]. These techniques have been applied to various tasks, including detoxification and sentiment shifting, and have been extended to safety-specific contexts, such as category-wise safety steering [15] and conditional activation steering for selective refusal [19]. Other approaches focus on erasing unwanted concepts, such as bias or refusal, using null-space projection [21] or closed-form linear concept erasure [22]. Additionally, spectral editing methods project representations into directions with maximal covariance with positive demonstrations [14]. While these methods effectively steer model behavior, they typically require prior knowledge of the target direction or rely on labeled data pairs. In contrast, COSMIC automates the selection of steering directions and layers, achieving comparable performance without requiring assumptions about the model's refusal behavior or specific output tokens.

Understanding the internal structure of LLMs also involves addressing polysemanticity, where neurons activate for multiple distinct concepts [25][30]. Techniques like sparse autoencoders have been used to decompose activations into more interpretable, monosemantic features [31], while other works explore weight-based interpretability in bilinear MLPs [32] and mathematical frameworks for transformer circuits [33]. These approaches provide deeper insights into how models process information but often focus on general feature extraction rather than specific behavioral control. COSMIC builds on the geometric insights of linear representation but focuses specifically on the practical task of identifying and steering refusal behaviors, offering a targeted solution that is robust across different alignment conditions and model architectures.
