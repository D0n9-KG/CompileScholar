Recent advancements in cloud-native infrastructure have shifted the focus of resource management towards automated and adaptive strategies within containerized environments. Early research in this domain has explored the use of Kubernetes to facilitate dynamic resource allocation, aiming to balance performance requirements with operational efficiency. For instance, [1] investigates methods for leveraging Kubernetes to achieve adaptive and cost-efficient resource management, highlighting the potential of orchestrators to dynamically adjust compute resources based on workload demands. This line of work primarily concentrates on CPU and memory utilization, treating these as the primary drivers of cloud expenditure. However, these approaches often overlook the specific financial implications of network traffic, which can constitute a significant portion of cloud bills for data-intensive applications. In contrast, the present paper extends this scope by specifically investigating the network usage and cost implications of containerised applications, providing actionable insights into network cost optimization that are absent in general resource management frameworks.

Parallel to resource adaptation, there has been growing interest in complex optimization algorithms for cloud computing environments. Research in this area frequently employs multi-agent systems or hierarchical structures to solve the NP-hard problem of resource allocation across distributed nodes. [2] proposes a hierarchical multi-agent optimization framework for resource allocation in cloud computing, demonstrating how decentralized agents can collaboratively improve global efficiency metrics. Such methodologies are effective in minimizing resource contention and maximizing throughput, yet they typically model cost as a secondary constraint or a linear function of compute units. They rarely account for the non-linear and provider-specific pricing models associated with inter-zone or inter-region data transfer. While these optimization techniques offer robust solutions for compute-centric workloads, they do not adequately address the financial nuances of network egress and ingress costs. The current study builds on this foundation by integrating cost modelling with measurement analysis, specifically targeting the financial aspects of network expenditures rather than solely focusing on computational efficiency.

The emerging discipline of FinOps has further emphasized the need to bridge the gap between financial management and cloud operations. While prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems, the specific domain of network cost analysis in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored. Existing literature often treats network costs as a static overhead or a minor component of the total cost of ownership, failing to recognize their variability and impact on overall cloud spend. This gap is critical as modern microservices architectures generate substantial internal and external network traffic. By combining measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modelling, this paper addresses this deficiency. Unlike previous works that focus on compute-centric optimization or general resource allocation, this research provides a dedicated analysis of network cost drivers, offering organizations a clearer understanding of how architectural decisions in Kubernetes affect their financial outcomes.
