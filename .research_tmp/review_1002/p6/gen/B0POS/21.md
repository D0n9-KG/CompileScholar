**Related Works**

Video Frame Interpolation (VFI) has evolved significantly from classical motion compensation to deep learning-based approaches, encompassing kernel-, flow-, GAN-, and Transformer-based architectures [1]. Early deep learning methods addressed challenges such as large motion and occlusion by incorporating depth information to improve flow projection and contextual feature gathering [2]. More recent advancements focus on efficiency and real-time performance; for instance, RIFE utilizes a neural network to estimate intermediate flows end-to-end, achieving state-of-the-art speed and quality without relying on pre-trained optical flow models [3]. To further enhance efficiency for multi-frame interpolation, M2M splatting proposes a fully differentiable framework that estimates bidirectional flows once and fuses overlapping pixels, reducing computational overhead for arbitrary in-between frames [4]. Additionally, recent works have explored explicit extraction of motion and appearance via inter-frame attention, integrating these features into hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures to balance computational complexity with detailed structure preservation [5]. While these methods excel at synthesizing intermediate frames for video processing, they are typically designed for temporal consistency in existing video sequences rather than augmenting sparse multi-view data for 3D reconstruction. In contrast, our work leverages VFI not merely for temporal smoothing but as a data augmentation strategy to generate photorealistic views that enrich the supervision signal for neural rendering, effectively treating the interpolation model as an implicit world prior.

Neural rendering techniques, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, have revolutionized 3D scene reconstruction from 2D images. However, these methods often suffer from under-constrained geometry and appearance when trained on limited input views, leading to artifacts in complex or dynamic scenes [9]. To address this, DiffusioNeRF regularizes NeRF training using a denoising diffusion model to predict gradients of the log-likelihood of color and depth patches, thereby improving reconstruction quality from few views [9]. Similarly, 3DGS-Enhancer enhances unbounded 3D Gaussian Splatting by incorporating view-consistent 2D diffusion priors to refine the representation [10]. In the domain of autonomous driving, where viewpoints are restricted, methods like NeuRAD and Lightning NeRF have been developed to handle dynamic data and utilize geometry priors from LiDAR to improve novel view synthesis and reduce training times [6][7]. Other approaches, such as AligNeRF, focus on high-resolution reconstruction by addressing misalignment and introducing high-frequency aware losses to recover fine details [8]. While these methods improve reconstruction fidelity through specialized priors or architectural modifications, they generally rely on explicit geometric supervision or complex regularization schemes. Our approach differs by using a lightweight video frame interpolation model, PS4PRO, to implicitly model camera movement and 3D geometry, thereby augmenting the dataset with synthetic views that provide additional photo supervision without requiring explicit geometric inputs.

Recent efforts have also explored the use of generative models to synthesize novel views for 3D reconstruction. ViewCrafter, for example, tames video diffusion models to generate high-fidelity novel views from single or sparse images, utilizing point-based representations for coarse 3D clues and iterative view synthesis strategies to extend coverage [11]. This line of work demonstrates the potential of generative priors to overcome the limitations of dense multi-view captures. However, diffusion-based generation can be computationally intensive and may struggle with precise geometric consistency in dynamic scenes. Our method builds on this line of work by proposing PS4PRO, a lightweight interpolation model trained on diverse video datasets. Unlike heavy diffusion models, PS4PRO serves as an efficient implicit prior that enriches photo supervision for both static and dynamic scenes, offering a more computationally feasible path to data augmentation for neural rendering.
