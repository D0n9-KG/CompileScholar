**Related Works**

Sequential recommendation has evolved significantly from early recurrent neural network (RNN) approaches to modern transformer-based architectures. Early methods, such as GRU4Rec, applied RNNs to model session-based user behaviors, addressing the limitations of matrix factorization in short-session contexts [2]. Subsequent works introduced attention mechanisms to capture user intent more effectively; for instance, NARM utilized a hybrid encoder with attention to model sequential behavior and main purpose [4], while STAMP employed short-term attention to prioritize recent items [3]. The paradigm shifted decisively with the introduction of the Transformer architecture [6], which dispensed with recurrence in favor of self-attention. SASRec leveraged self-attention to balance long-term semantics with recent action relevance [7], and BERT4Rec further advanced this by using bidirectional encoder representations and a Cloze task to overcome the limitations of unidirectional modeling [8]. More recent efforts have focused on efficiency and multi-interest modeling, such as low-rank decomposed self-attention [11] and sparse-interest networks that represent users with multiple embedding vectors to capture diverse intentions [10]. Our work builds on these transformer-based foundations but diverges by arguing that existing enhancements often overlook the core self-attention module itself. Instead of adding complex external components, we propose enhancing the self-attention mechanism directly to better learn correlations among behaviors.

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) aims to mitigate data sparsity by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains. Early cross-domain approaches, such as DTCDR, focused on dual-target learning to transfer knowledge between domains [14]. More recent frameworks have explored various strategies for information transfer, including mixed information flow networks that distinguish between behavioral and knowledge transfer [21], and parallel information-sharing networks for shared-account scenarios [20]. Other works have addressed the challenge of negative transfer, where cross-domain information may degrade performance in specific domains. For example, Pacer and Runner propose a cooperative learning framework that adaptively weights prediction losses to control gradient flows and mitigate negative transfer [17]. Similarly, contrastive learning approaches like C^2DSR jointly learn single- and cross-domain preferences by leveraging both intra- and inter-sequence item relationships [16]. Recent methods also employ domain space learning [18] and adaptive multi-task frameworks [19] to handle domain heterogeneity. While these methods explicitly inject domain-specific modules or transfer units, they often treat the self-attention mechanism as a static backbone. In contrast, our approach automates knowledge transfer by formulating cross-domain learning as a multi-objective problem within the self-attention layer, dynamically minimizing cross-domain attention scores to mitigate negative transfer without heavy hyper-parameter tuning.

The integration of multi-objective optimization and Pareto optimality offers a novel perspective for enhancing recommender systems. While multi-task learning frameworks like MDMTRec [19] and generative models like M6-rec [12] have been applied to recommendation, the specific application of Pareto-optimal self-attention to CDSR remains underexplored. Previous works have primarily focused on architectural modifications or explicit transfer modules [15][22], which can introduce significant computational overhead and complexity. Our method, AutoCDSR, differs by introducing a plug-and-play module that optimizes the recommendation task while simultaneously minimizing cross-domain attention scores. This approach not only mitigates negative transfer but also encourages complementary knowledge exchange among auxiliary domains, achieving substantial performance improvements with minimal additional computational cost.
