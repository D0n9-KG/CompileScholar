## Related Works

Neural rendering methods, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), have emerged as a dominant paradigm for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images by modeling continuous functions of geometry and appearance [1][2]. While these methods achieve high-fidelity novel view synthesis, their performance is often constrained by the density and quality of input views, leading to artifacts in complex or dynamic scenes where certain angles are unobserved [2]. To address these limitations, recent research has explored various strategies for enhancing scene representation and data augmentation.

In the domain of autonomous driving, where dynamic scenes and restricted viewpoints pose significant challenges, specialized neural rendering architectures have been developed. NeuRAD proposes a robust novel view synthesis method tailored to dynamic driving data, incorporating extensive sensor modeling for cameras and LiDAR to improve generalizability across datasets [3]. Similarly, Lightning NeRF utilizes an efficient hybrid scene representation that leverages LiDAR geometry priors to significantly reduce computational overheads, achieving a five-fold increase in training speed and a ten-fold improvement in rendering speed compared to state-of-the-art methods [4]. For high-resolution reconstruction, AligNeRF addresses issues of misalignment and overly smooth details by integrating convolutional layers with MLPs and employing a high-frequency aware loss, thereby recovering finer details without substantial additional training costs [1]. Furthermore, DiffusioNeRF regularizes NeRF training using denoising diffusion models to predict gradients of log-likelihoods for RGBD patches, which helps constrain under-determined geometry and color fields, particularly when trained with few input views [2].

Beyond static scene reconstruction, recent advancements have focused on leveraging generative models and video synthesis to expand the range of available views. ViewCrafter utilizes video diffusion models to synthesize high-fidelity novel views from sparse inputs, employing iterative view synthesis and camera trajectory planning to extend 3D clues and coverage [5]. This approach facilitates applications such as real-time rendering via 3D Gaussian Splatting and scene-level text-to-3D generation [5]. Additionally, 3DGS-Enhancer explores the integration of view-consistent 2D diffusion priors to enhance unbounded 3D Gaussian Splatting, further bridging the gap between 2D generative priors and 3D scene representation [6].

Video Frame Interpolation (VFI) serves as a fundamental Low-Level Vision task that synthesizes intermediate frames while maintaining spatial and temporal coherence, evolving from classical motion compensation to deep learning-based approaches including flow-, GAN-, Transformer-, and diffusion model-based methods [7]. Comprehensive surveys such as AceVFI categorize these methodologies into Center-Time and Arbitrary-Time Frame Interpolation, highlighting key challenges such as large motion, occlusion, and lighting variation [7]. Several state-of-the-art VFI methods offer efficient solutions that could serve as data augmentation tools for neural rendering. RIFE (Real-time Intermediate Flow Estimation) employs a neural network to estimate intermediate flows end-to-end, achieving 4–27 times faster performance than methods like SuperSlomo and DAIN while supporting arbitrary-timestep interpolation [8]. Depth-Aware VFI explicitly detects occlusions by exploring depth information, using a depth-aware flow projection layer to synthesize intermediate flows that preferentially sample closer objects, thereby improving quality in scenarios with large object motion [9].

Efficiency in multi-frame interpolation is further improved by methods like Many-to-Many (M2M) Splatting, which estimates multiple bidirectional flows to directly forward warp pixels, reducing computational overhead for interpolating arbitrary numbers of frames [10]. Similarly, EMA-VFI extracts motion and appearance information via inter-frame attention within a hybrid CNN and Transformer architecture, achieving state-of-the-art performance with lighter computational overhead [11]. These VFI techniques, by implicitly modeling camera movement and real-world 3D geometry, provide a promising avenue for enriching photo supervision in 3D reconstruction, effectively augmenting existing datasets for neural rendering methods.
