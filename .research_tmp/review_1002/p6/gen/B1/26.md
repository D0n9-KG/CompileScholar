## Related Works

The integration of radar and camera sensors is critical for robust perception in autonomous vehicles, particularly in adverse weather conditions where radar offers superior penetration capabilities compared to vision systems [1][2]. However, accurate sensor fusion relies on precise extrinsic calibration, which remains a significant challenge due to the physical differences between radar and camera measurement principles, as well as the high sparsity of radar data [3]. While LiDAR-camera calibration has seen substantial progress, radar-camera calibration is more complex due to the lack of dense geometric features and the inherent uncertainty in radar height measurements [3][1].

Early approaches to radar-camera calibration often relied on specialized calibration targets or manual intervention, which limits their applicability in large-scale production systems and dynamic environments [3][2]. To address these limitations, recent research has focused on automatic, targetless calibration methods. For instance, a data-driven method using coarse and fine convolutional neural networks (CNNs) was proposed for rotational radar-camera calibration, employing a boosting-inspired training algorithm to handle residual errors [3]. This approach demonstrated robust registration capabilities despite the lack of public datasets, requiring the recording of custom real-world data [3].

In the domain of 3D radar-camera calibration, continuous-time approaches have been developed to utilize radar velocity measurements, eliminating the need for specialized retroreflectors [1]. This method analyzes observability properties to ensure reliable estimation of the rigid-body transform [1]. Furthermore, spatiotemporal calibration algorithms have been introduced to address both spatial transforms and temporal misalignments, leveraging the radar's ability to measure ego-velocity relative to a fixed reference frame [2]. These targetless methods have shown the ability to match the performance of target-based methods in infrastructure-free environments [2].

Parallel advancements in LiDAR-camera calibration have provided foundational techniques for multimodal sensor registration. RegNet introduced a deep CNN to infer 6-DOF extrinsic calibration between LiDAR and cameras, casting feature extraction, matching, and regression into a single real-time capable network [4]. CalibNet further advanced this field by using a self-supervised deep network with 3D Spatial Transformer Networks to estimate extrinsics without direct supervision, relying instead on geometric and photometric consistency [5]. More recently, LCCNet proposed a cost volume network for online self-calibration, predicting decalibrated deviations to achieve high precision with mean absolute errors of 0.297 cm in translation and 0.017 degrees in rotation [6]. Other methods, such as Calibrcnn, utilize recurrent convolutional neural networks and geometric constraints for calibration [7].

Despite these advancements, the specific challenge of radar height data sparsity and uncertainty in online geometric calibration remains under-addressed. Existing radar-camera methods often struggle with the sensitivity of height information in frontal views, while bird's-eye views offer robustness but lack detail [3][1]. The proposed Dual-Perspective representation and Selective Fusion Mechanism aim to bridge this gap by combining rich frontal features with robust bird's-eye features, explicitly addressing the limitations of previous automatic calibration techniques [3][1][2].
