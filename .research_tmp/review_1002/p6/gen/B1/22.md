## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation (UDA-SS) addresses the challenge of transferring knowledge from labeled source domains to unlabeled target domains, where pixel-wise annotation is prohibitively expensive [1][2][3]. Early approaches focused on closing the inter-domain gap using adversarial learning in the output space, treating segmentation as a structured output problem to align spatial similarities between domains [1][4]. Subsequent methods introduced self-training frameworks, which iteratively generate pseudo-labels for target data to refine the model, though these are susceptible to error propagation from noisy labels [5][6]. To mitigate this, techniques such as confidence-regularized self-training treat pseudo-labels as continuous latent variables, while other approaches utilize prototypical features to denoise labels and compact the target feature space [5][6]. More recent advancements have shifted toward consistency-based training, such as PixMatch, which enforces pixelwise consistency under perturbations, and high-resolution multi-scale training strategies like HRDA, which balance fine detail preservation with long-range context capture [7][8]. Additionally, Transformer-based architectures have been integrated into UDA pipelines, with methods like DAFormer leveraging multi-level context-aware decoders and specific sampling strategies to improve robustness and performance on benchmarks like GTA-to-Cityscapes [9].

Despite these advances, traditional UDA-SS methods typically assume that the category sets of the source and target domains are known or identical, a premise that often fails in real-world scenarios where private classes may exist in the target domain [10][11]. This limitation motivates the study of Universal Domain Adaptation (UNDA), which aims to handle both domain shift and category shift simultaneously [12]. UNDA frameworks must identify common classes while rejecting unknown target classes to avoid negative transfer. Existing UNDA methods employ various strategies for this rejection, such as entropy-based feature alignment and neighborhood clustering in DANCE, which learns target structure in a self-supervised manner [11]. Other approaches, like OVANet, learn adaptive thresholds for rejecting unknown samples by minimizing class entropy and utilizing one-vs-all classifiers trained on source data [12]. Furthermore, methods like UMAN address the more complex setting of universal multi-source domain adaptation, where multiple source domains with potentially different label sets are used to adapt to a target domain with an unknown label set [10]. Some works also explore open-set domain adaptation using self-supervised tasks, such as rotation recognition, to separate known and unknown target samples [13].

Our work, UniDA-SS, extends this line of research by specifically addressing the low-confidence confusion between common and private classes in semantic segmentation. Unlike prior methods that rely on global entropy or fixed thresholds, we propose Domain-Specific Prototype-based Distinction (DSPD) to create finer-grained separation of domain-specific features, and Target-based Image Matching (TIM) to promote effective learning of common classes through batch pairing. This approach builds upon the foundational insights of prototype-based distinction [6] and consistency training [7], but tailors them to the specific challenges of unknown category settings in segmentation tasks.
