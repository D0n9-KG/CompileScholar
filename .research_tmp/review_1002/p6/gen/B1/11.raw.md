## Related Works

3D human pose estimation from monocular images or videos remains a challenging task due to depth ambiguity, self-occlusion, and the inherent complexity of mapping 2D observations to 3D space. Early approaches often relied on two-step pipelines, first localizing 2D joints using Convolutional Networks (ConvNets) and then optimizing for 3D pose \[[Pavlakos](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07828v2)\]. However, recent advancements have shifted toward end-to-end learning paradigms that directly predict 3D joint locations from raw image pixels or 2D sequences \[[Martinez](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098v2)\]. A significant portion of the error in these deep systems stems from limited visual understanding rather than the 2D-to-3D lifting process itself, as demonstrated by simple feed-forward networks that outperform complex end-to-end systems when given ground-truth 2D inputs \[[Martinez](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098v2)\].

To address the structural nature of human skeletons, graph-based methods have emerged as a dominant paradigm. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) represent the skeleton as a graph, capturing spatial relationships between joints \[[Hu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797v2)\]. However, standard undirected graphs fail to explicitly model the hierarchical articulation of the human body. To overcome this, directed graph representations have been proposed, where edges are directed from parent to child joints to reflect kinematic hierarchy \[[Hu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797v2)\]. Furthermore, conditional directed graph convolutions have been introduced to adapt graph topology based on input poses, leveraging non-local dependencies more effectively \[[Hu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797v2)\]. Other graph-based approaches exploit spatial-temporal relationships \[[Cai](https://ref.local/3)\] or employ hierarchical poselet-guided structures to enhance feature representation \[[Wu](https://ref.local/22)\].

The integration of Transformer architectures has further advanced the field by enabling the modeling of long-range dependencies without the computational burden of deep recurrent structures. The original Transformer architecture, based solely on attention mechanisms, has been adapted for pose estimation to capture global context \[[Vaswani](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762v7)\]. Specifically, the Strided Transformer reduces sequence redundancy by replacing fully-connected layers with strided convolutions, allowing for efficient aggregation of long-range temporal information \[[Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304v8)\]. Similarly, MixSTE employs separate temporal and spatial transformer blocks to model joint-specific motions and inter-joint correlations, respectively \[[Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00859v4)\]. High-order Directed Transformers (HDFormer) extend this by incorporating high-order bone and joint relationships to handle occlusions and complex poses, achieving state-of-the-art results with significantly fewer parameters \[[Chen](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01825v2)\].

Recent works have also focused on multi-hypothesis estimation and uncertainty modeling. MHFormer decomposes the task into generating, merging, and aggregating multiple pose hypotheses to resolve depth ambiguity \[[Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707v4)\]. Diffusion-based models, such as DiffPose, formulate pose estimation as a reverse diffusion process to generate multiple plausible poses, addressing the indeterminacy of single-pose predictors \[[Gong](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16940v3)\]\[[Holmquist](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16487v1)\]. Additionally, volumetric prediction methods, like the coarse-to-fine approach, discretize 3D space to predict per-voxel likelihoods, improving performance over direct coordinate regression \[[Pavlakos](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07828v2)\]. Other techniques, such as HEMlets, introduce intermediate heatmap triplets to bridge the gap between 2D observations and 3D interpretations, supporting training with weakly annotated data \[[Zhou](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12032v1)\].

Our work, PGFormer, builds upon these foundations by addressing the limitations of existing methods in modeling long-range dependencies. While prior methods often increase network depth to capture non-linked part dependencies, this can introduce uncorrelated noise and increase model size \[[Chen](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01825v2)\]\[[Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304v8)\]. We propose a Pyramid Graph Attention (PGA) module that captures cross-scale dependencies by concatenating information from various scales into a compact sequence. This pyramid structure allows for the efficient capture of correlations across joints and groups, complementing the context of human sub-structures. By combining PGA with graph convolution modules, PGFormer achieves a lightweight multi-scale architecture that encapsulates human sub-structures into self-attention via pooling, resulting in lower error and smaller model size compared to state-of-the-art methods on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP datasets.
