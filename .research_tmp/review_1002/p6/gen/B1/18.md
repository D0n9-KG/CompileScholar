## Related Works

Industrial Image Anomaly Detection (IAD) has advanced significantly with deep learning, yet deploying these models on resource-constrained embedded devices remains a critical challenge for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) [1]. This section reviews recent developments in anomaly detection architectures, lightweight model optimization, and the integration of foundation models for efficient industrial deployment.

**Anomaly Detection Architectures**
Early approaches to unsupervised anomaly detection often relied on convolutional autoencoders, which threshold pixel-wise reconstruction errors to identify defects [2]. However, these methods struggle with localization inaccuracies and visually altered regions where intensity values remain consistent [2]. To address these limitations, GANomaly introduced a semi-supervised approach using conditional generative adversarial networks to learn data distributions of normal samples, identifying anomalies as outliers in latent space [3]. More recently, PatchCore achieved state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark by combining ImageNet embeddings with a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features, achieving an image-level AUROC of up to 99.6% [4]. While effective, these methods often require significant computational resources. SoftPatch further addressed the issue of noisy training data by employing noise discriminators to generate outlier scores for patch-level denoising before coreset construction, improving robustness in real-world scenarios [5].

**Foundation Models and Few-Shot Learning**
The advent of multimodal foundation models has shifted the paradigm toward general-purpose visual features. CLIP demonstrated that pre-training on image-text pairs enables zero-shot transfer to downstream tasks, matching supervised baselines without dataset-specific training [6]. In the vision-only domain, DINO and DINOv2 have shown that self-supervised learning on Vision Transformers (ViTs) produces robust, all-purpose visual features that surpass previous standards [7][8]. AnomalyDINO leverages DINOv2 for few-shot anomaly detection, achieving state-of-the-art results on MVTec-AD with a training-free, patch-level deep nearest neighbor paradigm [9]. These foundation models provide powerful feature extraction capabilities but often come with high computational costs, necessitating optimization for edge deployment.

**Lightweight Models and Edge Deployment**
To bridge the gap between high-performance models and resource-constrained hardware, recent research has focused on model compression and lightweight architectures. Knowledge distillation allows smaller student models to learn from large teacher models, reducing storage and computational requirements [10]. Pruning and quantization techniques further optimize neural networks by removing redundant parameters and reducing numerical precision, potentially lowering memory footprint by up to 16x [11][12]. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) introduced a promptable segmentation model with impressive zero-shot generalization [13]. Recognizing the need for mobile-friendliness, MobileSAM replaced SAM’s heavyweight ViT-H encoder with a lightweight one using decoupled distillation, resulting in a model 60 times smaller and 5 times faster than concurrent alternatives, with inference times around 10ms per image [14]. STLM further explored SAM-guided lightweight models for anomaly detection, achieving competitive performance with only 16M parameters and 20ms inference time [15]. These advancements in efficient model design are crucial for deploying IAD systems on embedded devices like the NVIDIA Jetson series, enabling real-time quality control in industrial settings [16].
