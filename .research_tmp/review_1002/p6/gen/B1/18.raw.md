## Related Works

Industrial Image Anomaly Detection (IAD) has advanced significantly with deep learning, yet deploying these models on resource-constrained embedded devices remains a critical challenge for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) \[[Liu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514v5)\]. This section reviews recent developments in anomaly detection architectures, lightweight model optimization, and the integration of foundation models for efficient industrial deployment.

**Anomaly Detection Architectures**
Early approaches to unsupervised anomaly detection often relied on convolutional autoencoders, which threshold pixel-wise reconstruction errors to identify defects \[[Bergmann](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02011v3)\]. However, these methods struggle with localization inaccuracies and visually altered regions where intensity values remain consistent \[[Bergmann](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02011v3)\]. To address these limitations, GANomaly introduced a semi-supervised approach using conditional generative adversarial networks to learn data distributions of normal samples, identifying anomalies as outliers in latent space \[[Akcay](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06725v3)\]. More recently, PatchCore achieved state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark by combining ImageNet embeddings with a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features, achieving an image-level AUROC of up to 99.6% \[[Roth](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265v2)\]. While effective, these methods often require significant computational resources. SoftPatch further addressed the issue of noisy training data by employing noise discriminators to generate outlier scores for patch-level denoising before coreset construction, improving robustness in real-world scenarios \[[Jiang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14233v1)\].

**Foundation Models and Few-Shot Learning**
The advent of multimodal foundation models has shifted the paradigm toward general-purpose visual features. CLIP demonstrated that pre-training on image-text pairs enables zero-shot transfer to downstream tasks, matching supervised baselines without dataset-specific training \[[Radford](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020v1)\]. In the vision-only domain, DINO and DINOv2 have shown that self-supervised learning on Vision Transformers (ViTs) produces robust, all-purpose visual features that surpass previous standards \[[Caron](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294v2)\]\[[Maxime Oquab and Timoth{\'e}e Darcet and Th{\'e}o Moutakanni and Huy V. Vo and Marc Szafraniec and Vasil Khalidov and Pierre Fernandez and Daniel HAZIZA and Francisco Massa and Alaaeldin El-Nouby and Mido Assran and Nicolas Ballas and Wojciech Galuba and Russell Howes and Po-Yao Huang and Shang-Wen Li and Ishan Misra and Michael Rabbat and Vasu Sharma and Gabriel Synnaeve and Hu Xu and Herve Jegou and Julien Mairal and Patrick Labatut and Armand Joulin and Piotr Bojanowski](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193v2)\]. AnomalyDINO leverages DINOv2 for few-shot anomaly detection, achieving state-of-the-art results on MVTec-AD with a training-free, patch-level deep nearest neighbor paradigm \[[Damm](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14529v3)\]. These foundation models provide powerful feature extraction capabilities but often come with high computational costs, necessitating optimization for edge deployment.

**Lightweight Models and Edge Deployment**
To bridge the gap between high-performance models and resource-constrained hardware, recent research has focused on model compression and lightweight architectures. Knowledge distillation allows smaller student models to learn from large teacher models, reducing storage and computational requirements \[[Gou](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05525v7)\]. Pruning and quantization techniques further optimize neural networks by removing redundant parameters and reducing numerical precision, potentially lowering memory footprint by up to 16x \[[Vadera](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00241v2)\]\[[Gholami](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13630v3)\]. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) introduced a promptable segmentation model with impressive zero-shot generalization \[[Kirillov](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02643v1)\]. Recognizing the need for mobile-friendliness, MobileSAM replaced SAM’s heavyweight ViT-H encoder with a lightweight one using decoupled distillation, resulting in a model 60 times smaller and 5 times faster than concurrent alternatives, with inference times around 10ms per image \[[Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289v2)\]. STLM further explored SAM-guided lightweight models for anomaly detection, achieving competitive performance with only 16M parameters and 20ms inference time \[[Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19145v2)\]. These advancements in efficient model design are crucial for deploying IAD systems on embedded devices like the NVIDIA Jetson series, enabling real-time quality control in industrial settings \[[Capogrosso](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11932v2)\].
