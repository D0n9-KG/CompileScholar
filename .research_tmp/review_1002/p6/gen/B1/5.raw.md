## Related Works

The evolution of text representation learning has progressed from static word vectors to dynamic, context-aware sentence embeddings. Early approaches like Word2Vec introduced distributed vector representations that capture syntactic and semantic relationships, though they lacked the ability to model word order or idiomatic phrases \[[Tom{\'{a}}s Mikolov and
                  Ilya Sutskever and
                  Kai Chen and
                  Gregory S. Corrado and
                  Jeffrey Dean](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546v1)\]. The introduction of the Transformer architecture, which relies solely on attention mechanisms, significantly improved sequence transduction tasks and established a new paradigm for parallelizable training \[[Ashish Vaswani and
                  Noam Shazeer and
                  Niki Parmar and
                  Jakob Uszkoreit and
                  Llion Jones and
                  Aidan N. Gomez and
                  Lukasz Kaiser and
                  Illia Polosukhin](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762v7)\]. Building on this, BERT introduced deep bidirectional pre-training, achieving state-of-the-art results across numerous natural language processing tasks by jointly conditioning on left and right context \[[Jacob Devlin and
                  Ming{-}Wei Chang and
                  Kenton Lee and
                  Kristina Toutanova](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805v2)\].

However, standard BERT architectures are computationally intensive for similarity search, as they require feeding both sentences into the network for comparison, leading to massive overheads for large-scale retrieval \[[Nils Reimers and
                  Iryna Gurevych](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084v1)\]. To address this, Sentence-BERT (SBERT) modified the BERT network using siamese and triplet structures to derive semantically meaningful sentence embeddings that can be compared via cosine similarity, reducing inference time from hours to seconds while maintaining accuracy \[[Nils Reimers and
                  Iryna Gurevych](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084v1)\]. Further advancements in sentence embedding quality were achieved through contrastive learning. SimCSE demonstrated that simple contrastive objectives, using dropout as data augmentation in unsupervised settings or entailment/contradiction pairs in supervised settings, significantly improve the uniformity and alignment of embedding spaces \[[Tianyu Gao and
                  Xingcheng Yao and
                  Danqi Chen](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821v4)\].

Recent research has focused on making embeddings adaptable to specific user needs through instruction tuning. INSTRUCTOR introduced a method where text inputs are embedded alongside instructions describing the use case, allowing a single model to generate task-specific embeddings without further training \[[Su](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741v3)\]. Similarly, InBedder treats instructions as questions about the input text, fine-tuning language models on abstractive question answering to capture instruction-specific characteristics \[[Letian Peng and
                  Yuwei Zhang and
                  Zilong Wang and
                  Jayanth Srinivasa and
                  Gaowen Liu and
                  Zihan Wang and
                  Jingbo Shang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642v1)\]. Promptriever further advanced this by enabling retrieval models to be prompted like language models, demonstrating strong performance in following detailed relevance instructions and improving robustness to query phrasing \[[Orion Weller and
                  Benjamin Van Durme and
                  Dawn J. Lawrie and
                  Ashwin Paranjape and
                  Yuhao Zhang and
                  Jack Hessel](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11136v1)\]. Other approaches have explored unifying generative and embedding tasks, such as GRIT, which trains large language models to handle both via instruction distinction, and methods leveraging large language models to generate synthetic data for high-quality text embeddings across multiple languages \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00368v3)\]\[[Niklas Muennighoff and
                  Hongjin Su and
                  Liang Wang and
                  Nan Yang and
                  Furu Wei and
                  Tao Yu and
                  Amanpreet Singh and
                  Douwe Kiela](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09906v3)\].

Despite these advancements, existing instruction-following embedding methods often suffer from significant computational overhead, typically requiring the re-encoding of entire corpora for each new instruction. This limitation hinders real-time application in dynamic environments. Our work, GSTransform, addresses this by proposing a lightweight transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed generic embeddings in real time, guided by instruction-focused annotations, thereby achieving dramatic speedups while maintaining high-quality instruction-following performance.
