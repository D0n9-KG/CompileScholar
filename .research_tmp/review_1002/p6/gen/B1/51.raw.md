## Related Works

Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) has emerged as a privacy-preserving paradigm for search systems, enabling the collaborative training of ranking models across decentralized clients without sharing raw data \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09272v2)\]\[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01565v1)\]. In FOLTR, clients participate in a federation to jointly create an effective ranker using implicit click signals, thereby preserving individual user privacy while optimizing search effectiveness \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09272v2)\]\[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01565v1)\]. The foundation of modern FOLTR systems often relies on advances in Online Learning to Rank (OLTR), such as Pairwise Differentiable Gradient Descent (PDGD), which optimizes differentiable models by inferring preferences from user clicks \[[Oosterhuis](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08415v1)\]. This approach has been extended to federated settings as Federated PDGD (FPDGD), which serves as a state-of-the-art baseline for evaluating data distribution challenges, such as non-IID data, in FOLTR systems \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09272v2)\].

The "right to be forgotten," established by data protection legislation such as the GDPR, necessitates that machine learning systems provide mechanisms to remove user data contributions from trained models \[[Liu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320v1)\]\[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410v1)\]. This requirement has driven the development of machine unlearning techniques, which enable data holders to proactively erase their data from models \[[Liu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320v1)\]. In centralized settings, unlearning is relatively straightforward; however, in federated learning (FL), the distributed nature of training and the lack of central access to all training data present significant challenges \[[Liu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320v1)\]\[[Halimi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\]. Existing FL unlearning methods aim to erase a client's contribution by removing the influence of their local data from the global model without requiring full retraining from scratch \[[Halimi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\].

Several strategies have been proposed to achieve efficient federated unlearning. One approach involves subtracting accumulated historical updates from the model and leveraging knowledge distillation to restore performance without using client data \[[Wu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441v1)\]. Another method, inspired by database transaction rollback mechanisms, removes user contributions by rolling back and calibrating historical parameter updates, which is particularly relevant for on-device recommendation systems \[[Yuan](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958v2)\]. To address the storage constraints of keeping all historical updates, techniques such as negative sampling and importance-based update selection have been introduced to store only critical model updates \[[Yuan](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958v2)\]. Additionally, some frameworks propose performing local unlearning at the target client and then using the locally unlearned model as an initialization for a few rounds of federated learning with remaining clients \[[Halimi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\]. In the context of heterogeneous data, frameworks like FedLU combine mutual knowledge distillation with cognitive neuroscience-inspired unlearning methods to handle data drift and erase specific knowledge \[[Zhu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02069v2)\].

Despite these advancements, the specific application of unlearning to FOLTR systems remains a nascent area. Recent work has studied how to remove client contributions in FOLTR by measuring the impact of poisoning attacks before and after the unlearning process to verify effectiveness \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410v1)\]. This approach highlights the need for rigorous evaluation metrics to distinguish between under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410v1)\]. Furthermore, the security of FOLTR systems is a concern, as they are susceptible to data and model poisoning attacks that can compromise search effectiveness \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01565v1)\]. Defense methods against such attacks are being explored to ensure the robustness of federated ranking systems \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01565v1)\]. While previous unlearning proposals often relied on single evaluation metrics, recent studies emphasize the necessity of using multiple metrics for a comprehensive assessment of unlearning capabilities \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410v1)\]. Our work builds upon these foundations by systematically analyzing the effectiveness and efficiency of various federated unlearning strategies within FOLTR, addressing the limitations of prior single-metric evaluations.
