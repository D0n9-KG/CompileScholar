## Related Works

The evolution of text representation learning has progressed from static word vectors to dynamic, context-aware sentence embeddings. Early approaches like Word2Vec introduced distributed vector representations that capture syntactic and semantic relationships, though they lacked the ability to model word order or idiomatic phrases [1]. The introduction of the Transformer architecture, which relies solely on attention mechanisms, significantly improved sequence transduction tasks and established a new paradigm for parallelizable training [2]. Building on this, BERT introduced deep bidirectional pre-training, achieving state-of-the-art results across numerous natural language processing tasks by jointly conditioning on left and right context [3].

However, standard BERT architectures are computationally intensive for similarity search, as they require feeding both sentences into the network for comparison, leading to massive overheads for large-scale retrieval [4]. To address this, Sentence-BERT (SBERT) modified the BERT network using siamese and triplet structures to derive semantically meaningful sentence embeddings that can be compared via cosine similarity, reducing inference time from hours to seconds while maintaining accuracy [4]. Further advancements in sentence embedding quality were achieved through contrastive learning. SimCSE demonstrated that simple contrastive objectives, using dropout as data augmentation in unsupervised settings or entailment/contradiction pairs in supervised settings, significantly improve the uniformity and alignment of embedding spaces [5].

Recent research has focused on making embeddings adaptable to specific user needs through instruction tuning. INSTRUCTOR introduced a method where text inputs are embedded alongside instructions describing the use case, allowing a single model to generate task-specific embeddings without further training [6]. Similarly, InBedder treats instructions as questions about the input text, fine-tuning language models on abstractive question answering to capture instruction-specific characteristics [7]. Promptriever further advanced this by enabling retrieval models to be prompted like language models, demonstrating strong performance in following detailed relevance instructions and improving robustness to query phrasing [8]. Other approaches have explored unifying generative and embedding tasks, such as GRIT, which trains large language models to handle both via instruction distinction, and methods leveraging large language models to generate synthetic data for high-quality text embeddings across multiple languages [9][10].

Despite these advancements, existing instruction-following embedding methods often suffer from significant computational overhead, typically requiring the re-encoding of entire corpora for each new instruction. This limitation hinders real-time application in dynamic environments. Our work, GSTransform, addresses this by proposing a lightweight transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed generic embeddings in real time, guided by instruction-focused annotations, thereby achieving dramatic speedups while maintaining high-quality instruction-following performance.
