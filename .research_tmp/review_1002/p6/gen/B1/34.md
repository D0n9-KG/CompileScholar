## Related Works

The integration of machine learning and optimization for financial decision-making has evolved from traditional two-stage pipelines to end-to-end decision-focused learning frameworks. Early approaches combined machine learning with operations research to prescribe optimal decisions, utilizing data-driven methods to handle conditional stochastic optimization problems where joint probability distributions are unknown [1]. A fundamental paradigm in this domain is the Predict-then-Optimize (PtO) framework, which first predicts unknown parameters of an optimization problem and then solves it using these predictions [2]. However, standard PtO methods often suffer from goal mismatch, as prediction models are typically trained to minimize prediction error rather than the downstream decision cost [3].

To address this mismatch, the Smart Predict-then-Optimize (SPO) framework was introduced, which directly leverages optimization problem structure to design better prediction models via a decision-error-based loss function [3]. Since the SPO loss is non-convex and non-Lipschitz, researchers developed the SPO+ loss, a convex surrogate that is statistically consistent with the SPO loss under mild conditions [4][3]. Generalization bounds for these frameworks have been established, showing that the empirical minimizer of the SPO+ loss achieves low excess true risk with high probability, particularly when the feasible region is a polyhedron or a level set of a strongly convex function [2][4].

In parallel, the Predict-and-Optimize (PnO) paradigm has emerged, aiming to directly optimize towards ultimate decision quality rather than intermediate prediction accuracy [5]. Comprehensive benchmarks indicate that PnO approaches generally outperform traditional PtO methods across various combinatorial optimization problems, although no single design choice is universally superior [5]. Decision-focused learning (DFL) encompasses these techniques, integrating machine learning and constrained optimization to enhance decision quality in uncertain environments [6]. To facilitate the implementation of such end-to-end systems, differentiable optimization layers have been developed, allowing gradients to be backpropagated through optimization solvers [7][8]. For instance, OptNet integrates quadratic programs as differentiable layers in neural networks, while other works enable differentiation through disciplined convex programs within deep learning architectures [7][8]. Furthermore, computational bottlenecks in PnO training, such as the need to recompute optimal solutions, have been mitigated through solution caching schemes and contrastive loss functions [9].

In the specific context of fund allocation, recent work has modeled the matching of funds to customers under constraints using a Predict-Then-Optimize Fund Allocation (PTOFA) framework, which predicts expected revenue and optimizes impression allocation [10]. However, these approaches often rely on specific forecasting models, which may introduce additional uncertainty. In contrast, time series forecasting models like Informer, Autoformer, and PatchTST have significantly improved long-term prediction accuracy by addressing computational complexities and capturing long-range dependencies [11][12][13]. Despite these advances, the integration of such forecasting models into allocation frameworks often ignores the uncertainty inherent in the predictions. Our work addresses these gaps by proposing a risk-aware framework that aligns the forecasting and allocation objectives while adaptively calibrating uncertainty, remaining agnostic to the specific forecasting model used.
