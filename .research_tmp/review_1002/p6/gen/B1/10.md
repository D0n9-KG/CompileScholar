## Related Works

**Multi-Token Prediction and Future Token Anticipation**
Large language models (LLMs) traditionally rely on next-token prediction (NTP) as their primary training objective. Recent research suggests that training models to predict multiple future tokens simultaneously, known as multi-token prediction (MTP), enhances sample efficiency and downstream capabilities without increasing training time [1]. By employing independent output heads on a shared model trunk, MTP improves generative benchmarks, such as coding tasks, and accelerates inference by up to three times [1]. The theoretical basis for this approach is supported by findings that hidden state vectors in transformers encode sufficient information to predict subsequent tokens with high accuracy [2]. Specifically, linear approximation methods on GPT-J-6B demonstrate that individual hidden states can anticipate tokens at positions $t+2$ or later with over 48% accuracy [2]. This capability underpins the effectiveness of MTP in capturing long-range dependencies and improving algorithmic reasoning [1].

**Speculative Decoding and Inference Acceleration**
To mitigate the sequential bottleneck of autoregressive decoding, speculative decoding has emerged as a key paradigm for accelerating LLM inference while preserving output quality [3]. This technique involves a draft model generating multiple tokens in parallel, which are then verified by the target LLM in a single forward pass [4][3]. Early approaches required training separate draft models, which incurred significant computational costs [5]. To address this, self-speculative decoding methods have been developed to eliminate the need for auxiliary models. For instance, Draft & Verify utilizes layer-skipping to create a compact draft model from the target LLM’s intermediate layers, achieving up to 1.99x speedup without additional training [4]. Similarly, SWIFT employs on-the-fly layer skipping to adaptively select layers for drafting, providing a plug-and-play solution that maintains the original distribution of generated text [3]. Other frameworks, such as Medusa, augment LLMs with multiple decoding heads to predict subsequent tokens in parallel, using tree-based attention to verify candidate continuations [6]. Medusa-1, which fine-tunes heads on a frozen backbone, achieves over 2.2x speedup, while Medusa-2, which fine-tunes the entire model, reaches 2.3–3.6x speedup [6]. Additionally, KOALA enhances speculative decoding by transforming single-layer draft heads into multi-layer architectures with adversarial learning, improving draft accuracy and latency speedups by 10.57%–14.09% [7]. Kangaroo further optimizes self-speculative decoding by using a fixed shallow sub-network as a self-draft model with an additional early-exiting mechanism to minimize drafting steps, achieving up to 1.68x speedup with significantly fewer parameters than Medusa-1 [5].

**Curriculum Learning in Machine Learning**
Curriculum learning is a strategy that trains models in a meaningful order, progressing from easy to hard samples, which can improve performance over random shuffling without additional computational costs [8]. This approach has been successfully applied across various machine learning tasks, though its effectiveness depends on defining appropriate ranking criteria and pacing functions [8]. Recent surveys have constructed taxonomies of curriculum learning methods, highlighting how different instantiations tackle the challenges of sample ordering and difficulty progression [8]. In the context of MTP, curriculum learning offers a structured approach to managing the complexity of the pre-training objective, particularly for smaller language models that may struggle with the immediate demands of multi-token prediction.
