## Related Works

Understanding the internal representations of Large Language Models (LLMs) is critical for improving interpretability and robustness. Recent research has focused on comparing neural network representations to understand how features evolve across layers and training dynamics. Early methods, such as Singular Vector Canonical Correlation Analysis (SVCCA), provided tools to compare representations invariant to affine transformations, revealing that networks converge to final representations from the bottom up \[[Maithra Raghu and
                  Justin Gilmer and
                  Jason Yosinski and
                  Jascha Sohl{-}Dickstein](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05806v2)\]. However, Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) and similar statistics face limitations when measuring similarity between representations of higher dimension than the number of data points \[[Simon Kornblith and
                  Mohammad Norouzi and
                  Honglak Lee and
                  Geoffrey E. Hinton](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414v4)\]. To address this, Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) was introduced as a similarity index that reliably identifies correspondences between representations in networks trained from different initializations, overcoming the dimensional constraints of CCA \[[Simon Kornblith and
                  Mohammad Norouzi and
                  Honglak Lee and
                  Geoffrey E. Hinton](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414v4)\].

CKA has since been applied to analyze the structure of fine-tuned Transformers. Studies using CKA to measure representation similarity across layers in task-tuned models have discovered a consistent block diagonal structure, where earlier and later layers form distinct clusters with strong internal similarity but low cross-cluster similarity \[[Phang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08406v2)\]. This suggests that later layers contribute marginally to task performance, allowing for potential pruning without significant degradation \[[Phang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08406v2)\]. Furthermore, representation dissimilarity measures have been utilized to gain insights into the mechanics of language models, identifying asymmetries in activation functions and locating generalization properties that are not visible through standard in-distribution test set performance \[[Davis Brown and
                  Charles Godfrey and
                  Nicholas Konz and
                  Jonathan H. Tu and
                  Henry Kvinge](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14993v1)\].

The role of specific layers in knowledge acquisition and storage has also been extensively investigated. Research indicates that factual associations in autoregressive transformers are localized in mid-layer feed-forward modules, which can be directly edited to update specific facts \[[Kevin Meng and
                  David Bau and
                  Alex Andonian and
                  Yonatan Belinkov](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262v5)\]. In contrast, other studies suggest that shallow layers hold the key to knowledge injection, as removing or expanding these layers causes significant performance dips, whereas deeper layers are less critical for this specific task \[[Tianxiang Chen and
                  Zhentao Tan and
                  Tao Gong and
                  Yue Wu and
                  Qi Chu and
                  Bin Liu and
                  Jieping Ye and
                  Nenghai Yu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02330v1)\]. Additionally, the concept of "Concept Depth" posits that LLMs process concepts of varying complexities in different layers, with simpler factual tasks handled in shallow layers and more complex inferential tasks requiring deeper layers \[[Jin](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07066v7)\]. The quality of intermediate representations has also been examined, with findings suggesting that intermediate layers often yield more informative representations for downstream tasks than final layers, exhibiting bimodal entropy patterns tied to training data \[[Oscar Skean and
                  Md Rifat Arefin and
                  Yann LeCun and
                  Ravid Shwartz{-}Ziv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09563v1)\].

While these studies provide valuable insights, many rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their applicability to post-hoc settings. Our work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via CKA. We demonstrate that layers with significant shifts in representation space are those most affected during fine-tuning, a pattern consistent across tasks. Our spectral analysis reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in top principal components, encoding semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions. These findings are applied to efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense, showing that fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction, while freezing them reduces backdoor attack success rates by up to 40%.
