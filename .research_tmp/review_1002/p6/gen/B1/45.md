## Related Works

Opinion mining and sentiment analysis have become critical components for understanding user feedback in software development. While traditional approaches often focus on broad sentiment polarity, such as positive, negative, or neutral classifications, recent research highlights the need for more granular insights into user emotions. App reviews are increasingly recognized as valuable resources for guiding product improvement and software evolution, although manually analyzing these reviews is labor-intensive for developers [1]. To address this barrier, tools like Appsent have been developed to facilitate the analysis of app reviews by extracting and visualizing important perceptions from end-user feedback, thereby enhancing user satisfaction and product success [1].

Despite the utility of general sentiment tools, fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews remains underexplored. Existing datasets, such as the multilabel multiclass sentiment and emotion dataset derived from Indonesian mobile application reviews, provide foundational data for studying these nuances but often lack comprehensive annotation frameworks for complex emotional states [2]. The challenge lies in moving beyond simple polarity to capture the complexity of emotional interpretation, which requires structured guidelines and robust annotation processes.

The automation of such annotation tasks is a growing area of interest, particularly with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs). LLMs have shown significant potential in various annotation domains, including political text, financial data, and materials science, where they serve as substitutes or assistants for human experts [3][4][5]. In the specific context of linguistic annotation, studies have evaluated the feasibility of using LLMs for pragma-discursive corpus annotation, such as identifying apology components in English. Research comparing GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and human coders found that GPT-4 outperformed GPT-3.5, with accuracy approaching that of human coders, suggesting that LLMs can make annotation processes more efficient and scalable [6].

However, the application of LLMs to fine-grained emotion analysis in app reviews presents unique challenges. While LLMs can significantly reduce manual effort and maintain substantial agreement with human annotators, full automation remains difficult due to the subjective and complex nature of emotional interpretation. Recent work has explored human-LLM collaborative annotation systems, such as MEGAnno+, and methods for effective verification of LLM labels to ensure quality [7][8]. These approaches acknowledge that while LLMs are powerful tools, human oversight is still necessary for complex features that lack direct mapping to lexical forms. Our work builds upon these findings by adapting Plutchik's emotion taxonomy to app reviews, developing a structured annotation framework, and evaluating the cost-effectiveness and agreement of LLM-assisted annotation in this specific domain.
