## Related Works

Sequential recommendation aims to predict a user's next interaction based on their historical behavior, a task critical for modern recommender systems \[[Kang](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781v1)\]. Early approaches relied on Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), with MCs excelling in sparse datasets due to model parsimony and RNNs capturing longer-term semantics in denser settings \[[Kang](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781v1)\]. To address the limitations of unidirectional RNNs, which assume rigidly ordered sequences, BERT4Rec introduced a bidirectional encoder using the Cloze task to jointly condition on left and right contexts, significantly improving sequential representation power \[[Sun](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690v2)\]. Furthermore, SASRec proposed a self-attention based model that balances long-term semantic capture with the efficiency of predicting based on relevant recent actions, outperforming CNN and RNN-based approaches while being more computationally efficient \[[Kang](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781v1)\]. The foundational Transformer architecture, which dispenses with recurrence and convolutions in favor of attention mechanisms, has become the dominant paradigm for sequence transduction and recommendation tasks \[[Vaswani](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762v7)\].

Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) extends this paradigm by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains to mitigate data sparsity and improve prediction accuracy \[[Cao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. A primary challenge in CDSR is the risk of negative transfer, where information from auxiliary domains degrades performance in the target domain due to lack of relevance or differing data sparsity levels \[[Park](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245v2)\]. Existing CDSR frameworks often employ self-attention transformers augmented with explicit domain-specific components, such as domain-aware module blocks, to facilitate knowledge transfer \[[Cao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. For instance, C^2DSR utilizes graph neural networks to mine inter-sequence collaborative relationships and sequential attentive encoders for intra-sequence patterns, employing a contrastive objective to enhance correlation between single- and cross-domain representations \[[Cao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. Similarly, Mixed Information Flow networks incorporate both behavioral and knowledge transfer units to dynamically decide when and how to use cross-domain information \[[Ma](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. The Mixed Attention Network (MAN) addresses the reliance on overlapped users by using local and global attention modules to extract domain-specific and cross-domain information, including item similarity and group-prototype attention \[[Lin](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272v1)\].

To specifically address negative transfer, recent cooperative learning frameworks have emerged. The Pacer and Runner framework estimates the degree of negative transfer for each domain and adaptively weights the prediction loss to control gradient flows, while maximizing mutual information between single-domain and cross-domain representations \[[Park](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245v2)\]. This approach has shown substantial practical benefits, such as a 21.4% increase in click-through rate in industrial deployments \[[Park](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245v2)\]. Other methods, such as SINE, focus on representing users with multiple sparse interest embeddings to capture diverse conceptual prototypes within a single domain, which can be adapted to handle complex user intentions \[[Tan](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09267v1)\]. While these methods improve CDSR performance, many rely on heavy hyper-parameter tuning or additional computational overhead for their transfer modules \[[Park](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245v2)\]\[[Ma](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Our work, AutoCDSR, diverges from these approaches by enhancing the core self-attention mechanism itself. By formulating cross-domain learning as a multi-objective problem that dynamically minimizes cross-domain attention scores, we automate knowledge transfer and mitigate negative transfer without requiring complex auxiliary modules or extensive tuning, offering a plug-and-play solution for existing transformer-based recommenders like SASRec and BERT4Rec \[[Kang](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781v1)\]\[[Sun](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690v2)\].
