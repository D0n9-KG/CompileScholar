## Related Works

The deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) on resource-constrained edge devices presents significant challenges due to the high computational, memory, and power demands of these models [1]. While recent advancements in LLM architecture, such as PaLM and LLaMA, have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in few-shot learning and general reasoning [2][3], their scale often necessitates high-end server-grade accelerators, making direct deployment on edge hardware impractical [4]. Consequently, research has focused on algorithm-hardware co-designs and optimization techniques to enable efficient inference on limited resources.

Several approaches target memory and compute efficiency through model compression and sparsity. Quantization techniques, such as SmoothQuant, enable 8-bit weight and activation quantization for LLMs, achieving up to 1.56x speedup and 2x memory reduction with negligible accuracy loss [5]. Similarly, SparseGPT demonstrates that massive GPT-family models can be pruned to 50-60% sparsity in one-shot without retraining, significantly reducing inference costs [6]. Contextual sparsity methods, like DejaVu, further exploit input-dependent sparsity in attention heads and MLP parameters to reduce inference latency by over 2x compared to state-of-the-art systems [7]. Knowledge distillation frameworks, including MiniLLM and DistiLLM, compress large teacher models into smaller student models to reduce memory footprint while preserving generative capabilities [8][9].

System-level optimizations have also emerged to address the specific constraints of edge and low-power environments. FlexGen enables high-throughput generative inference on a single commodity GPU by aggregating memory from the GPU, CPU, and disk, and compressing weights to 4 bits [10]. PowerInfer introduces a GPU-CPU hybrid inference engine that exploits the power-law distribution of neuron activation, preloading "hot" neurons to the GPU and computing "cold" neurons on the CPU, thereby reducing memory demands and improving speed on consumer-grade hardware [11]. For distributed and server-side inference, systems like vLLM utilize PagedAttention to manage key-value cache memory efficiently, improving throughput by 2-4x [12], while Splitwise separates compute-intensive prompt processing from memory-intensive token generation to optimize cost and power [4]. ExeGPT further optimizes distributed inference by scheduling resources based on latency constraints and workload distributions [13].

Specific to edge deployment, EdgeBERT provides an algorithm-hardware co-design for latency-aware energy optimization in multi-task NLP inference [14]. It employs entropy-based early exit prediction to perform dynamic voltage-frequency scaling (DVFS) at sentence granularity, combined with adaptive attention span, selective pruning, and floating-point quantization [14]. This approach is integrated into a specialized 12nm hardware accelerator featuring fast-switching regulators and embedded non-volatile memories, achieving up to 53x lower energy consumption compared to CUDA adaptations on mobile GPUs [14]. Other works explore hardware-aware optimizations, such as LLM inference on flash memory for devices with limited DRAM [15], and general reviews of LLM acceleration and hardware co-design strategies [1]. These studies collectively highlight the critical need for synergistic algorithm and hardware solutions to balance latency, energy, and accuracy in edge LLM deployments.
