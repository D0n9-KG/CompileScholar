## Related Works

In-context learning (ICL) enables large language models (LLMs) to perform tasks by conditioning on a few input-output pairs without additional training [1][2]. The effectiveness of ICL is heavily dependent on the quality and selection of these demonstrations [1][3]. Early research challenged the necessity of accurate input-label correspondence, suggesting that ICL performance is driven more by the label space, input distribution, and sequence format than by ground-truth accuracy [2]. Subsequent studies quantified this impact, identifying prompt verbosity and model size as key factors in noise resilience [4]. However, while ICL may be robust to noisy labels in text classification, recent work demonstrates that noisy annotations significantly degrade performance in text generation tasks [3].

To address the challenge of noisy demonstrations, several approaches have been proposed. One line of work focuses on demonstration retrieval, such as the Unified Demonstration Retriever (UDR), which uses a multi-task list-wise ranking framework to select relevant examples across diverse tasks [1]. Another approach involves decoding strategies; Contrastive Decoding (CD) has been shown to improve reasoning and open-ended generation by maximizing the likelihood difference between strong and weak models, thereby preventing abstract reasoning errors and repetitive outputs [5][6]. CD has also been adapted to enhance contextual understanding by using adversarial irrelevant passages as negative samples to balance parametric and non-parametric knowledge [7]. Additionally, In-Context Vectors (ICV) offer an alternative to standard ICL by steering latent states, which improves controllability and reduces prompt length [8].

Specific to the problem of noisy labels, recent studies have introduced methods to mitigate performance degradation. One approach proposes a safeguard for ICL with noisy labels, inspired by supervised learning techniques for noisy data [9]. For text generation, Local Perplexity Ranking (LPR) addresses the issue by replacing noisy candidates with their nearest neighbors in semantic space, effectively decoupling matching perplexity from inherent perplexity to prevent the selection of mismatched input-label pairs [3]. This method relies on the assumption that noisy samples yield higher local perplexities than clean counterparts. However, this assumption may break down under high noise ratios, where the distinction between noisy and clean samples becomes blurred due to biases in the annotation process and domain-specific knowledge inherent in LLMs. Our work reexamines this perplexity-based paradigm, introducing a dual debiasing framework that uses synthesized neighbors to correct perplexity estimates. This yields a robust Sample Cleanliness Score that identifies absolute sample cleanliness regardless of the overall corpus noise level, addressing the limitations of existing perplexity-based methods in high-noise environments.
