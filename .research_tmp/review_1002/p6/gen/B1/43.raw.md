## Related Works

Training large language models (LLMs) in distributed environments requires sophisticated hardware and software co-design to manage the complexity of model execution and deployment. The Transformer architecture, which relies solely on attention mechanisms, has become the dominant paradigm for sequence transduction and large-scale language modeling \[[Vaswani](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762v7)\]. To scale these models, researchers have developed various domain-specific architectures and supercomputers. For instance, Google’s TPU v4 utilizes optical circuit switches to dynamically reconfigure interconnect topology, offering significant improvements in scale, availability, and power efficiency compared to traditional Infiniband systems \[[Jouppi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01433v3)\]. Similarly, NVIDIA’s Blackwell architecture represents a major advancement in GPU design for high-performance computing \[[NVIDIA](https://ref.local/1)\].

The training of massive models, such as PaLM with 540 billion parameters \[[Chowdhery](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311v5)\] and Megatron-Turing NLG with 530 billion parameters \[[Smith](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11990v3)\], necessitates advanced parallelization strategies. These systems employ 3D parallelism methodologies, combining data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism to distribute computation across thousands of chips \[[Smith](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11990v3)\]. To automate this process, systems like GSPMD use compiler-based annotations to infer tensor partitioning, achieving high compute utilization on TPU clusters \[[Xu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04663v2)\]. Other frameworks, such as Galvatron, employ decision trees and dynamic programming to automatically select optimal hybrid parallelism strategies for Transformer training on GPUs \[[Miao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13878v1)\].

A critical challenge in LLM training is handling long sequences, where self-attention incurs quadratic memory costs. Sequence parallelism addresses this by splitting input sequences across devices, enabling training with significantly longer contexts than single-device limits \[[Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13120v3)\]. Recent advancements include DeepSpeed-Ulysses, which partitions data along the sequence dimension and uses efficient all-to-all communication to maintain constant communication volume as sequence length increases \[[Jacobs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14509v2)\]. Similarly, Ring Attention distributes long sequences across multiple devices by overlapping key-value block communication with attention computation, allowing for near-infinite context sizes without additional overhead \[[Liu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01889v4)\].

Despite these hardware and algorithmic advances, accurately predicting performance remains difficult due to the heterogeneous nature of modern ML stacks. Profiling tools like NVProf \[[NVIDIA](https://ref.local/3)\], CUPTI \[[NVIDIA](https://ref.local/4)\], and PyTorch Kineto \[[Kineto](https://ref.local/5)\] provide low-level execution traces but often lack predictive capabilities for optimization scenarios. Daydream addresses this by modeling DNN execution with fine-grained dependency graphs derived from CUPTI traces, allowing for the simulation of various optimizations to predict runtime improvements \[[Zhu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03318v1)\]. Other analytical models, such as Amped \[[Moolchandani](https://ref.local/19)\] and Calculon \[[Isaev](https://ref.local/20)\], aim to characterize distributed training performance and facilitate high-level co-design. Simulation frameworks like Astra-sim and Astra-sim 2.0 enable the exploration of hierarchical networks and disaggregated systems for large-model training \[[Rashidi](https://ref.local/23)\]\[[Won](https://ref.local/24)\]. Additionally, toolkits like dpro provide generic performance diagnosis and optimization for distributed DNN training \[[Hu](https://ref.local/25)\], while Apollo focuses on automatic operator fusion to reduce execution overhead \[[Zhao](https://ref.local/22)\]. These works highlight the ongoing need for accurate, trace-driven performance models that can guide optimization efforts in complex, large-scale LLM training environments.
