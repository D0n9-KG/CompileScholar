## Related Works

Understanding the internal representations of Large Language Models (LLMs) is critical for improving interpretability and robustness. Recent research has focused on comparing neural network representations to understand how features evolve across layers and training dynamics. Early methods, such as Singular Vector Canonical Correlation Analysis (SVCCA), provided tools to compare representations invariant to affine transformations, revealing that networks converge to final representations from the bottom up [1]. However, Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) and similar statistics face limitations when measuring similarity between representations of higher dimension than the number of data points [2]. To address this, Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) was introduced as a similarity index that reliably identifies correspondences between representations in networks trained from different initializations, overcoming the dimensional constraints of CCA [2].

CKA has since been applied to analyze the structure of fine-tuned Transformers. Studies using CKA to measure representation similarity across layers in task-tuned models have discovered a consistent block diagonal structure, where earlier and later layers form distinct clusters with strong internal similarity but low cross-cluster similarity [3]. This suggests that later layers contribute marginally to task performance, allowing for potential pruning without significant degradation [3]. Furthermore, representation dissimilarity measures have been utilized to gain insights into the mechanics of language models, identifying asymmetries in activation functions and locating generalization properties that are not visible through standard in-distribution test set performance [4].

The role of specific layers in knowledge acquisition and storage has also been extensively investigated. Research indicates that factual associations in autoregressive transformers are localized in mid-layer feed-forward modules, which can be directly edited to update specific facts [5]. In contrast, other studies suggest that shallow layers hold the key to knowledge injection, as removing or expanding these layers causes significant performance dips, whereas deeper layers are less critical for this specific task [6]. Additionally, the concept of "Concept Depth" posits that LLMs process concepts of varying complexities in different layers, with simpler factual tasks handled in shallow layers and more complex inferential tasks requiring deeper layers [7]. The quality of intermediate representations has also been examined, with findings suggesting that intermediate layers often yield more informative representations for downstream tasks than final layers, exhibiting bimodal entropy patterns tied to training data [8].

While these studies provide valuable insights, many rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their applicability to post-hoc settings. Our work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via CKA. We demonstrate that layers with significant shifts in representation space are those most affected during fine-tuning, a pattern consistent across tasks. Our spectral analysis reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in top principal components, encoding semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions. These findings are applied to efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense, showing that fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction, while freezing them reduces backdoor attack success rates by up to 40%.
