## Related Works

Several approaches target memory and compute efficiency through model compression and sparsity. Quantization techniques, such as SmoothQuant, enable 8-bit weight and activation quantization for LLMs, achieving up to 1.56x speedup and 2x memory reduction with negligible accuracy loss \[[Guangxuan Xiao and
                  Ji Lin and
                  Micka{\"{e}}l Seznec and
                  Hao Wu and
                  Julien Demouth and
                  Song Han](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438v7)\]. Similarly, SparseGPT demonstrates that massive GPT-family models can be pruned to 50-60% sparsity in one-shot without retraining, significantly reducing inference costs \[[Elias Frantar and
                  Dan Alistarh](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00774v3)\]. Contextual sparsity methods, like DejaVu, further exploit input-dependent sparsity in attention heads and MLP parameters to reduce inference latency by over 2x compared to state-of-the-art systems \[[Zichang Liu and
                  Jue Wang and
                  Tri Dao and
                  Tianyi Zhou and
                  Binhang Yuan and
                  Zhao Song and
                  Anshumali Shrivastava and
                  Ce Zhang and
                  Yuandong Tian and
                  Christopher R{\'{e}} and
                  Beidi Chen](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157v1)\]. Knowledge distillation frameworks, including MiniLLM and DistiLLM, compress large teacher models into smaller student models to reduce memory footprint while preserving generative capabilities \[[Jongwoo Ko and
                  Sungnyun Kim and
                  Tianyi Chen and
                  Se{-}Young Yun](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03898v2)\]\[[Yuxian Gu and
                  Li Dong and
                  Furu Wei and
                  Minlie Huang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543v4)\].

System-level optimizations have also emerged to address the specific constraints of edge and low-power environments. FlexGen enables high-throughput generative inference on a single commodity GPU by aggregating memory from the GPU, CPU, and disk, and compressing weights to 4 bits \[[Sheng](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06865v2)\]. PowerInfer introduces a GPU-CPU hybrid inference engine that exploits the power-law distribution of neuron activation, preloading "hot" neurons to the GPU and computing "cold" neurons on the CPU, thereby reducing memory demands and improving speed on consumer-grade hardware \[[Song](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12456v2)\]. For distributed and server-side inference, systems like vLLM utilize PagedAttention to manage key-value cache memory efficiently, improving throughput by 2-4x \[[Woosuk Kwon and
                  Zhuohan Li and
                  Siyuan Zhuang and
                  Ying Sheng and
                  Lianmin Zheng and
                  Cody Hao Yu and
                  Joseph Gonzalez and
                  Hao Zhang and
                  Ion Stoica](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180v1)\], while Splitwise separates compute-intensive prompt processing from memory-intensive token generation to optimize cost and power \[[Pratyush Patel and
                  Esha Choukse and
                  Chaojie Zhang and
                  {\'{I}}{\~{n}}igo Goiri and
                  Aashaka Shah and
                  Saeed Maleki and
                  Ricardo Bianchini](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18677v2)\]. ExeGPT further optimizes distributed inference by scheduling resources based on latency constraints and workload distributions \[[Hyungjun Oh and
                  Kihong Kim and
                  Jaemin Kim and
                  Sungkyun Kim and
                  Junyeol Lee and
                  Du{-}seong Chang and
                  Jiwon Seo](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07947v1)\].

Specific to edge deployment, EdgeBERT provides an algorithm-hardware co-design for latency-aware energy optimization in multi-task NLP inference \[[Tambe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14203v5)\]. It employs entropy-based early exit prediction to perform dynamic voltage-frequency scaling (DVFS) at sentence granularity, combined with adaptive attention span, selective pruning, and floating-point quantization \[[Tambe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14203v5)\]. This approach is integrated into a specialized 12nm hardware accelerator featuring fast-switching regulators and embedded non-volatile memories, achieving up to 53x lower energy consumption compared to CUDA adaptations on mobile GPUs \[[Tambe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14203v5)\]. Other works explore hardware-aware optimizations, such as LLM inference on flash memory for devices with limited DRAM \[[Keivan Alizadeh and
                  Iman Mirzadeh and
                  Dmitry Belenko and
                  Karen Khatamifard and
                  Minsik Cho and
                  Carlo C. Del Mundo and
                  Mohammad Rastegari and
                  Mehrdad Farajtabar](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514v3)\], and general reviews of LLM acceleration and hardware co-design strategies \[[Huang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10903v1)\]. These studies collectively highlight the critical need for synergistic algorithm and hardware solutions to balance latency, energy, and accuracy in edge LLM deployments.
