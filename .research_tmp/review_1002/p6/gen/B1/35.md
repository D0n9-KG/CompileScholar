## Related Works

Protein dynamics are fundamental to biological function, yet traditional computational methods for studying these phenomena, such as molecular dynamics (MD) and Monte Carlo simulations, face significant challenges. These physics-based approaches rely on empirically derived force fields to explore conformational space, but high energy barriers often lead to rare event sampling issues and long equilibration times, resulting in inadequate ensemble sampling without exhaustive computational resources [1][2][3]. While MD remains a powerful technique for studying microscopic phenomena, its high computational cost has driven interest in deep learning-based surrogate models and generative frameworks that can efficiently learn from data [4].

Recent advances in deep generative modeling, particularly diffusion models, have emerged as efficient alternatives for protein structure prediction and conformation sampling. These methods learn distributions over crystallographic structures, enabling the generation of diverse conformations without the need for extensive simulation data during inference [5][1]. For instance, EigenFold utilizes a diffusion process to model protein structures as systems of harmonic oscillators, achieving high accuracy in single-structure prediction while providing a comprehensive view of model uncertainty through sampled ensembles [5]. Similarly, Str2Str proposes a structure-to-structure translation framework that leverages amortized denoising score matching to perform zero-shot conformation sampling, outperforming previous state-of-the-art models in speed and accuracy [1]. Other approaches, such as Distributional Graphormer (DiG), employ deep neural networks to transform simple distributions into equilibrium distributions conditioned on molecular descriptors, facilitating the efficient generation of diverse conformations for various molecular tasks [6].

Despite the success of these data-driven approaches, a critical limitation remains the effective integration of physical supervision. Standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization, causing generated structures to deviate from the true equilibrium distribution [2]. Existing score-based diffusion methods frequently fail to incorporate important physical prior knowledge, resulting in large deviations in sampled conformations [2]. To address this, ConfDiff introduces a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model that incorporates a force-guided network with data-based score models, preserving high fidelity while generating diverse protein conformations [2]. Additionally, AlphaFlow and ESMFlow repurpose highly accurate single-state predictors like AlphaFold and ESMFold under a flow matching framework to generate sequence-conditioned protein ensembles, demonstrating faster convergence to equilibrium properties than replicate MD trajectories [7].

More recently, Structure Language Modeling (SLM) has been introduced to encode protein structures into a compact latent space, enabling efficient and interpretable exploration of diverse ensemble modes with significant speedups over existing methods [3]. Furthermore, generative modeling of molecular dynamics trajectories offers a paradigm for learning flexible multi-task surrogate models, allowing for forward simulation, transition path sampling, and trajectory upsampling from data [4]. These developments highlight a growing trend toward hybridizing deep learning with physical insights to overcome the limitations of purely data-driven or purely physics-based methods. Our work, Energy-based Alignment (EBA), builds upon these foundations by specifically aligning generative models with feedback from physical models to calibrate conformational states based on energy differences, addressing the challenge of physical plausibility in generated ensembles.
