## Related Works

The reconstruction of signals from compressed measurements, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI), has evolved significantly from traditional iterative optimization to deep learning-based frameworks. Early approaches relied on iterative algorithms such as Iterative Hard Thresholding (IHT), which provides near-optimal error guarantees and robustness to noise with linear memory requirements [1]. Similarly, the Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (ISTA) and Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) have been foundational for solving linear inverse problems and $\ell_1$-regularized compressive sensing tasks [2][3]. These classical methods, while theoretically sound, often suffer from slow convergence and limited performance in complex, real-world scenarios.

The advent of deep learning introduced two dominant paradigms for solving these inverse problems: Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods and Deep Unfolding Networks (DUNs). PnP methods integrate off-the-shelf denoisers into iterative optimization schemes, such as half-quadratic splitting, to perform regularization. While flexible, early PnP methods lacked convergence guarantees unless strict assumptions were made about the denoiser [4]. Recent advancements, such as the Gradient Step Denoiser, have addressed this by realizing the denoiser as a gradient descent step on a neural network parameterized functional, ensuring convergence to stationary points of an explicit global functional [4]. Furthermore, Regularization by Denoising (RED) offers a more flexible framework by using the denoising engine to define an explicit image-adaptive regularization functional, guaranteeing convergence to the globally optimal result for problems like deblurring and super-resolution [5]. Other approaches, such as Denoising-based Approximate Message Passing (D-AMP), integrate high-performance denoisers within the AMP framework, achieving state-of-the-art recovery speeds by leveraging Onsager correction terms [6].

In contrast, Deep Unfolding Networks (DUNs) map truncated optimization iterations into trainable neural network stages, offering high interpretability and performance. For instance, ISTA-Net and ADMM-CSNet unroll specific optimization steps for image compressive sensing [7][8]. However, standard DUNs often suffer from information loss between adjacent stages, a problem analogous to short-term memory loss in human cognition. To mitigate this, Memory-Augmented Deep Unfolding Networks (MADUN) introduce High-throughput Short-term Memory and Cross-stage Long-term Memory mechanisms to preserve information across stages [9]. Similarly, Optimization-inspired Cross-attention Transformer (OCT) modules utilize dual cross-attention to enhance information interaction and reduce parameter complexity [10].

Specific to Single-Pixel Imaging, recent works have adapted these frameworks to handle the unique constraints of SPI. HATNet, for example, unfolds the ISTA computation graph into efficient tensor gradient descent and hybrid-attention multiscale denoising modules, specifically designed to handle the Kronecker SPI model and reduce computational overhead [11]. Other architectures, such as CSformer, integrate convolutional and transformer branches to capture both local and global dependencies in compressive sensing [12]. While these methods achieve high accuracy, many require specific training for fixed sampling matrices or compression ratios (CRs), limiting their generalizability. Methods like COAST aim to address this by using random projection augmentation to handle arbitrary sampling matrices with a single model [13].

The current work builds upon these foundations by integrating the flexibility of PnP with the efficiency of unrolling. By proposing a general proximal trajectory loss function, the approach ensures that the learned deep image restorer approximates the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer. This aligns with recent theoretical efforts, such as Learned Proximal Networks (LPN), which provide exact proximal operators for data-driven nonconvex regularizers and promote the recovery of log-priors [14]. Unlike prior CR-specific unrolling networks, this method aims to handle varying compression ratios with a single model, combining the robustness of proximal gradient descent with the speed of end-to-end deep learning.
