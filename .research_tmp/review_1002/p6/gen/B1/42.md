## Background

Modern cloud-native applications increasingly rely on managed cloud services and containerization technologies, such as Kubernetes, to achieve rapid time-to-market and scalable deployments [1]. As the usage of these containerized applications grows, organizations must carefully evaluate various factors, including cost implications, when deciding on a hosting platform [1]. This financial and operational challenge has given rise to an emerging discipline known as FinOps, which combines financial management and cloud operations to optimize costs in cloud-based applications [1].

While prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems, the specific analysis of network costs within Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored [1]. Existing approaches to resource management often focus on leveraging Kubernetes for adaptive and cost-efficient resource management [1]. Additionally, hierarchical multi-agent optimization techniques have been proposed for resource allocation in cloud computing environments [2]. However, these general resource allocation and management strategies do not sufficiently address the distinct financial aspects of network usage.

This paper investigates the network usage and cost implications of containerized applications running on Kubernetes clusters [1]. By utilizing a methodology that combines measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modelling, the study aims to provide organizations with actionable insights into network cost optimization [1]. The findings highlight key considerations for analyzing network expenditures and evaluating the potential cost benefits of deploying applications on cloud providers [1]. Overall, this work contributes to the FinOps discipline by addressing the financial and operational aspects of managing network costs in cloud-native environments [1].
