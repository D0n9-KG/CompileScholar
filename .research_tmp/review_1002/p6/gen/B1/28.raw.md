## Related Works

Active learning (AL) has emerged as a critical strategy to mitigate the high cost of manual annotation in machine learning. By strategically selecting the most informative unlabeled instances for labeling, AL significantly reduces the amount of training data required to achieve specific performance levels. Early work demonstrated that uncertainty sampling could reduce the necessary manual classification effort by up to 500-fold in text categorization tasks \[[Lewis](http://arxiv.org/abs/cmp-lg/9407020v2)\]. In the domain of image classification, AL has been shown to effectively reduce the number of training examples needed for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) by leveraging various uncertainty metrics to identify high-value examples \[[Joshi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825v1)\]. Recent advancements have introduced task-agnostic methods, such as loss prediction modules, which allow deep networks to efficiently select data likely to produce incorrect predictions, outperforming previous methods across diverse tasks like object detection and pose estimation \[[Yoo](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03677v1)\]. Furthermore, core-set selection approaches have been proposed to address the limitations of traditional heuristics in batch settings, significantly outperforming existing methods in image classification by selecting subsets that yield competitive models for the remaining data \[[Sener](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00489v4)\].

Federated Learning (FL) addresses the challenges of training models on decentralized, privacy-sensitive data by allowing multiple clients to collaboratively learn a global model without sharing raw data. This framework is particularly relevant for mobile devices where data is unevenly distributed and non-identically and independently distributed (non-IID) \[[Kone{\v{c}}n{\`y}](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575v1)\]\[[McMahan](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629v4)\]. To enhance communication efficiency and model quality, Active Federated Learning (FAL) integrates AL into the FL paradigm. FAL aims to reduce annotation workloads by having clients collaboratively select informative instances in a distributed manner, thereby improving the performance of global models compared to random sampling or client-level separate AL strategies \[[Jin{-}Hyun Ahn and Kyung Sang Kim and Jeongwan Koh and Quanzheng Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00195v2)\]. Specific implementations, such as Knowledge-Aware Federated Active Learning (KAFAL), address the mismatch between global and local sampling goals by using knowledge-specialized active sampling based on discrepancies between local and global models \[[Yu-Tong Cao and Jingya Wang and Ye Shi and Baosheng Yu and Dacheng Tao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13579v3)\]. Other approaches, like LoGo, integrate global and local-only selector models to handle varying levels of local heterogeneity and global imbalance, consistently outperforming other AL strategies \[[SangMook Kim and Sangmin Bae and Hwanjun Song and Se-Young Yun](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12317v1)\]. Additionally, client selection strategies in FAL can be optimized by conditioning on the current model and local data to reduce training iterations by 20–70% while maintaining accuracy \[[Goetz](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12641v1)\].

Despite these advances, existing FAL methods often struggle with the inherent heterogeneity of data distributions across clients and the resulting fluctuations in model parameters. Research indicates that while FL is robust to data heterogeneity when local training iterations and communication frequency are appropriately chosen, biased sampling from active learning schemes can still lead to challenges in maintaining model accuracy \[[Jia Qian and Lars Kai Hansen and Xenofon Fafoutis and Prayag Tiwari and Hari Mohan Pandey](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05038v2)\]. The proposed CHASe method addresses these gaps by focusing on epistemic variations (EVs) that oscillate around decision boundaries, utilizing techniques for tracking inference inconsistencies, calibrating decision boundaries with alignment loss, and enhancing selection efficiency through data freeze and awaken mechanisms. This approach aims to surpass established baselines in both effectiveness and efficiency across diverse heterogeneous federation settings.
