## Related Works

**Graph Neural Networks for Recommendation**
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a dominant paradigm for collaborative filtering by modeling user-item interactions as bipartite graphs. Early approaches like Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) propagate embeddings on the user-item graph to capture high-order connectivity \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108v2)\]. Subsequent work, LightGCN, simplified this architecture by removing feature transformation and non-linear activations, relying solely on neighborhood aggregation to achieve superior performance with lower computational cost \[[He](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126v4)\]. More recent advancements, such as Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF), address the diversity of user intents by disentangling representations to model distinct interaction motivations \[[Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764v1)\]. These methods establish the foundation for graph-based representation learning, though they typically operate on full-precision floating-point embeddings, which limits their efficiency in large-scale, resource-constrained environments.

**Representation Quantization and Binarization**
To address the memory and computational overhead of high-dimensional embeddings, representation quantization has gained attention. Binarization, which compresses latent features into compact binary sequences, offers significant advantages in memory footprint and inference speed via bitwise operations \[[Chen](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02115v1)\]. The BiGeaR framework introduced multi-faceted quantization reinforcement to mitigate information loss during binarization, achieving substantial performance improvements over state-of-the-art quantization-based recommenders \[[Chen](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02115v1)\]. However, existing quantization methods often focus primarily on numerical optimization, potentially neglecting the nuanced information loss that occurs during the transition from continuous to discrete spaces. This gap motivates the development of enhanced frameworks that explicitly supervise the binarization process to preserve representation informativeness.

**Knowledge Distillation in Graph Learning**
Knowledge Distillation (KD) is a widely used technique to compress large teacher models into smaller, efficient student models. In the context of GNNs, KD aims to transfer structural and topological knowledge from complex architectures to lightweight ones. Early methods, such as Distilling Knowledge from Graph Convolutional Networks, utilize local structure preserving modules to align topological semantics between teacher and student models \[[Yang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10477v4)\]. More recent approaches, like Graph Contrastive Representation Distillation (G-CRD), employ contrastive learning to preserve global topology by aligning node embeddings in a shared representation space \[[Joshi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04964v4)\]. Other strategies, such as FreeKD, use reinforcement learning to facilitate free-direction knowledge transfer between shallower networks, addressing issues of over-smoothing in deep GNNs \[[Feng](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06561v4)\]. While these methods effectively compress model size, their application to the specific challenge of binarized graph representations for recommendation remains an area for further exploration, particularly in integrating distillation mechanisms with binary code generation.

**Hashing and Efficient Retrieval**
Efficient candidate generation is critical for large-scale recommendation systems. Hashing techniques, such as those used in CIGAR, learn preference-preserving binary embeddings to build hash tables for rapid candidate retrieval \[[Kang](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05475v1)\]. Deep learning approaches to hashing, like HashNet, utilize continuation methods to learn exactly binary hash codes from imbalanced similarity data, overcoming optimization difficulties associated with sign activations \[[Cao](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00758v4)\]. These methods demonstrate the viability of binary representations for efficient search, yet their integration with the collaborative filtering signals inherent in GNNs requires careful handling of information loss. The proposed BiGeaR++ framework builds upon these foundations by incorporating supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples and fine-grained inference distillation, thereby enhancing the fidelity of binarized graph representations for top-k recommendation tasks.
