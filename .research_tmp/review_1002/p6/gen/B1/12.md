## Related Works

Event cameras provide high temporal resolution by capturing scene changes asynchronously on a per-pixel basis, but this capability introduces significant challenges regarding bandwidth, memory, and computational resources [1]. To mitigate these demands, recent research has focused on event downsampling and subsampling techniques that reduce data volume while preserving essential information for downstream tasks.

Early approaches to event data reduction have explored various spatial and temporal downsampling strategies. Spatial and temporal downsampling methods have been developed specifically for event-based visual classification to manage data rates [2]. Similarly, EvDownsampling has been proposed as a robust method for downsampling event camera data, aiming to maintain signal integrity during reduction [3]. Bio-inspired filtering and data reduction algorithms have also been introduced to achieve the goal of retaining the same information with less data, leveraging biological principles for efficient processing [4].

The integration of downsampling with neuromorphic computing and spiking neural networks (SNNs) has also been a significant area of investigation. Performance comparisons of DVS data spatial downscaling methods using SNNs have highlighted the interplay between data reduction and neuromorphic processing efficiency [5]. Insect-inspired spatio-temporal downsampling techniques have been applied to event-based inputs, drawing from biological visual systems to optimize data handling [6]. Furthermore, neuromorphic downsampling of event-based camera output has been studied to align data reduction with the operational constraints of neuromorphic hardware [7].

More recent work has quantified the trade-offs between event rate and classification accuracy. Studies using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have demonstrated that the number of events per video can be reduced by an order of magnitude with minimal drop in accuracy across various datasets [1]. This finding suggests that event streams contain significant redundancy. However, research indicates that lower classification accuracy at high subsampling rates is not solely due to information loss; it is also attributable to increased training instability and sensitivity to hyperparameters in highly subsampled scenarios [1]. This instability has been quantified through novel metrics evaluating hyperparameter sensitivity and analyzed via weight gradients to understand the underlying causes [1].

While these prior works establish the feasibility of event subsampling, many rely on fixed heuristics or threshold-based strategies that limit adaptability to specific scenes or tasks. The proposed POLED framework addresses these limitations by introducing a probabilistic approach that models event importance through an event-importance probability density function (ePDF). Unlike fixed methods, POLED operates in a purely online setting, estimating event importance on-the-fly to enable scene-specific adaptation. Additionally, the introduction of zero-shot event downsampling ensures that downsampled events remain usable for models trained on original streams without task-specific adaptation, a capability not explicitly addressed in the heuristic-based methods described in [2]–[7].
