## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation is a critical task in medical imaging, essential for the diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment planning of gliomas, which are the most common primary brain malignancies [1][2][3]. These tumors exhibit significant heterogeneity in shape, extent, and location, with distinct sub-regions such as peritumoral edema, necrotic core, and enhancing tumor, making accurate segmentation challenging [1]. The International Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) challenge has served as a primary benchmark for evaluating computational algorithms on multi-institutional, multi-parametric magnetic resonance imaging (mpMRI) data [1][2]. Recent iterations of the challenge, such as BraTS 2021 and 2022, have expanded scope to include radiogenomic classification and continuous evaluation on diverse datasets, highlighting the need for robust and generalizable models [2][4].

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have established the state-of-the-art in this domain. The U-Net architecture, a foundational encoder-decoder model, remains widely used for biomedical image segmentation [5]. Variants such as the two-stage cascaded U-Net achieved first place in the BraTS 2019 challenge [6], while an autoencoder-regularized semantic segmentation network won the BraTS 2018 challenge by addressing limited training data sizes [3]. More recently, nnU-Net emerged as a self-configuring method that significantly improved segmentation performance by incorporating specific modifications for the BraTS task, winning the BraTS 2020 competition with high Dice scores [7][8]. Building on this success, the BraTS 2021 winning solution extended nnU-Net by utilizing a larger network, group normalization, and axial attention in the decoder [9]. In the BraTS 2022 challenge, an ensemble of DeepSeg, nnU-Net, and DeepSCAN frameworks achieved top rankings, demonstrating the continued dominance of CNN-based ensembles [4].

To address the limitations of standard CNNs in capturing long-range dependencies, Transformer-based architectures have been introduced. TransBTS was among the first to exploit Transformers in 3D CNNs for brain tumor segmentation, combining local 3D context extraction with global feature modeling via self-attention [10]. Similarly, Swin UNETR reformulated 3D semantic segmentation as a sequence-to-sequence prediction problem, utilizing a hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder to capture long-range information effectively, ranking among top approaches in the BraTS 2021 validation phase [11].

Despite these advances, deep learning models in medical imaging often suffer from high training costs and data scarcity. Transfer learning from natural image datasets like ImageNet has been a practical paradigm, though it often requires reformulating 3D tasks into 2D, which can compromise performance by losing anatomical information [12]. Alternative approaches include self-supervised learning frameworks like Models Genesis, which leverage recurrent anatomy in medical images to learn generic 3D representations without manual labeling, outperforming both scratch learning and 2D transfer learning [12]. Additionally, heterogeneous 3D networks like Med3D have been developed to co-train on diverse medical datasets to extract general 3D features, accelerating convergence and improving accuracy in downstream tasks [13]. These methods highlight the potential of leveraging pre-trained weights and efficient architectures to reduce training requirements while maintaining or improving segmentation accuracy, a direction our work further explores by integrating pre-trained weights into the nnU-Net framework.
