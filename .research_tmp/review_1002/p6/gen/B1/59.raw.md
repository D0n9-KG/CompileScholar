## Related Works

The integration of fairness into recommender systems has emerged as a critical research area, driven by the recognition that data-driven algorithms can perpetuate bias and generate unfair outcomes for both users and providers \[[Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13619v6)\]. Early surveys have systematically organized this fragmented literature, highlighting the need for standardized taxonomies of fairness definitions and evaluation techniques \[[Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13619v6)\]\[[Deldjoo](https://ref.local/2)\]. A significant portion of recent research focuses on the multi-stakeholder nature of these systems, acknowledging that recommendation is not solely a user-centric task but a complex interaction involving multiple parties \[[Abdollahpouri](https://ref.local/6)\]\[[Abdollahpouri](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.13158v1)\]. This perspective subsumes traditional reciprocal and group recommendation research, emphasizing that fairness concerns must be addressed across different classes of multi-stakeholder recommender systems \[[Abdollahpouri](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.13158v1)\].

A dominant theme in the literature is the tension between user satisfaction and item-side fairness, particularly in two-sided platforms where producers and customers interact \[[Patro](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764v2)\]\[[Wu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09024v1)\]\[[Biswas](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180v1)\]. Customer-centric designs often maximize individual utility but can lead to an unfair distribution of exposure among producers, potentially harming their economic well-being \[[Patro](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764v2)\]\[[Biswas](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180v1)\]. Conversely, producer-centric approaches may compromise customer experience \[[Patro](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764v2)\]. To address this, several frameworks have been proposed to ensure two-sided fairness. The FairRec algorithm, for instance, maps the recommendation problem to the fair allocation of indivisible goods, guaranteeing Maximin Share (MMS) exposure for producers and Envy-Free up to One item (EF1) fairness for customers \[[Patro](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764v2)\]\[[Biswas](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180v1)\]. Similarly, TFROM designs models that balance provider exposure fairness with the reduction in recommendation quality for customers \[[Wu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09024v1)\]. Other approaches, such as CPFair, integrate consumer and producer fairness constraints into a joint optimization objective to improve both sides without significantly degrading overall recommendation quality \[[Naghiaei](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\].

Beyond two-sided fairness, researchers have explored various algorithmic strategies to balance accuracy and fairness. Policy learning frameworks have been developed to optimize standard ranking metrics like NDCG while satisfying exposure fairness constraints, utilizing stochastic ranking policies to rigorously express fairness specifications \[[Singh](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04056v2)\]. In the domain of re-ranking, the Tax-rank method conceptualizes fair re-ranking as a taxation process, levying taxes based on utility differences between items to ensure continuity and controllability over accuracy loss \[[Xu](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\]. This economic analogy aligns with the current work’s focus on elasticity and tax transfer dynamics. Other methods, such as VerFair, utilize vertical allocation to amortize fair exposure, aiming for a superior balance between exposure fairness and ranking performance compared to prior amortized optimization methods \[[Yang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03046v2)\]. Additionally, the use of generalized Gini welfare functions has been explored to specify normative criteria for fairness, weighting individuals based on their rank to promote equality among items or least-satisfied users \[[Do](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06521v4)\].

Despite these advances, existing evaluations often rely on single fairness metrics, which may hinder a comprehensive assessment of the accuracy-fairness trade-off \[[Chen Xu and Zhirui Deng and Clara Rus and Xiaopeng Ye and Yuanna Liu and Jun Xu and Zhicheng Dou and Ji-Rong Wen and Maarten de Rijke](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11883v1)\]. The FairDiverse toolkit has been introduced to standardize the evaluation of fairness and diversity-aware algorithms across different information retrieval tasks, addressing inconsistencies in experimental setups \[[Chen Xu and Zhirui Deng and Clara Rus and Xiaopeng Ye and Yuanna Liu and Jun Xu and Zhicheng Dou and Ji-Rong Wen and Maarten de Rijke](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11883v1)\]. However, the underlying mechanisms governing the trade-off, particularly the elasticity of utility between item groups, remain under-explored. This gap motivates the development of evaluation frameworks like the Elastic Fairness Curve and algorithms such as ElasticRank, which leverage elasticity calculations to adjust inter-item distances, providing a more nuanced understanding of how item-side fairness constraints translate into user-side accuracy costs.
