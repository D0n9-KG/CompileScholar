## Related Works

The integration of machine learning and optimization for financial decision-making has evolved from traditional two-stage pipelines to end-to-end decision-focused learning frameworks. Early approaches combined machine learning with operations research to prescribe optimal decisions, utilizing data-driven methods to handle conditional stochastic optimization problems where joint probability distributions are unknown \[[Dimitris Bertsimas and
                  Nathan Kallus](http://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5481v4)\]. A fundamental paradigm in this domain is the Predict-then-Optimize (PtO) framework, which first predicts unknown parameters of an optimization problem and then solves it using these predictions \[[Othman El Balghiti and
                  Adam N. Elmachtoub and
                  Paul Grigas and
                  Ambuj Tewari](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11488v3)\]. However, standard PtO methods often suffer from goal mismatch, as prediction models are typically trained to minimize prediction error rather than the downstream decision cost \[[Adam N. Elmachtoub and
                  Paul Grigas](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005v5)\].

To address this mismatch, the Smart Predict-then-Optimize (SPO) framework was introduced, which directly leverages optimization problem structure to design better prediction models via a decision-error-based loss function \[[Adam N. Elmachtoub and
                  Paul Grigas](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005v5)\]. Since the SPO loss is non-convex and non-Lipschitz, researchers developed the SPO+ loss, a convex surrogate that is statistically consistent with the SPO loss under mild conditions \[[Heyuan Liu and
                  Paul Grigas](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08887v2)\]\[[Adam N. Elmachtoub and
                  Paul Grigas](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005v5)\]. Generalization bounds for these frameworks have been established, showing that the empirical minimizer of the SPO+ loss achieves low excess true risk with high probability, particularly when the feasible region is a polyhedron or a level set of a strongly convex function \[[Othman El Balghiti and
                  Adam N. Elmachtoub and
                  Paul Grigas and
                  Ambuj Tewari](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11488v3)\]\[[Heyuan Liu and
                  Paul Grigas](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08887v2)\].

In parallel, the Predict-and-Optimize (PnO) paradigm has emerged, aiming to directly optimize towards ultimate decision quality rather than intermediate prediction accuracy \[[Geng](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07633v5)\]. Comprehensive benchmarks indicate that PnO approaches generally outperform traditional PtO methods across various combinatorial optimization problems, although no single design choice is universally superior \[[Geng](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07633v5)\]. Decision-focused learning (DFL) encompasses these techniques, integrating machine learning and constrained optimization to enhance decision quality in uncertain environments \[[Jayanta Mandi and
                  James Kotary and
                  Senne Berden and
                  Maxime Mulamba and
                  Victor Bucarey and
                  Tias Guns and
                  Ferdinando Fioretto](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13565v4)\]. To facilitate the implementation of such end-to-end systems, differentiable optimization layers have been developed, allowing gradients to be backpropagated through optimization solvers \[[Brandon Amos and
                  J. Zico Kolter](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00443v5)\]\[[Akshay Agrawal and
                  Brandon Amos and
                  Shane T. Barratt and
                  Stephen P. Boyd and
                  Steven Diamond and
                  J. Zico Kolter](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12430v1)\]. For instance, OptNet integrates quadratic programs as differentiable layers in neural networks, while other works enable differentiation through disciplined convex programs within deep learning architectures \[[Brandon Amos and
                  J. Zico Kolter](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00443v5)\]\[[Akshay Agrawal and
                  Brandon Amos and
                  Shane T. Barratt and
                  Stephen P. Boyd and
                  Steven Diamond and
                  J. Zico Kolter](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12430v1)\]. Furthermore, computational bottlenecks in PnO training, such as the need to recompute optimal solutions, have been mitigated through solution caching schemes and contrastive loss functions \[[Maxime Mulamba and
                  Jayanta Mandi and
                  Michelangelo Diligenti and
                  Michele Lombardi and
                  Victor Bucarey and
                  Tias Guns](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05354v2)\].

In the specific context of fund allocation, recent work has modeled the matching of funds to customers under constraints using a Predict-Then-Optimize Fund Allocation (PTOFA) framework, which predicts expected revenue and optimizes impression allocation \[[Tang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03165v1)\]. However, these approaches often rely on specific forecasting models, which may introduce additional uncertainty. In contrast, time series forecasting models like Informer, Autoformer, and PatchTST have significantly improved long-term prediction accuracy by addressing computational complexities and capturing long-range dependencies \[[Haoyi Zhou and
                  Shanghang Zhang and
                  Jieqi Peng and
                  Shuai Zhang and
                  Jianxin Li and
                  Hui Xiong and
                  Wancai Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436v3)\]\[[Yuqi Nie and
                  Nam H. Nguyen and
                  Phanwadee Sinthong and
                  Jayant Kalagnanam](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730v2)\]\[[Haixu Wu and
                  Jiehui Xu and
                  Jianmin Wang and
                  Mingsheng Long](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008v5)\]. Despite these advances, the integration of such forecasting models into allocation frameworks often ignores the uncertainty inherent in the predictions. Our work addresses these gaps by proposing a risk-aware framework that aligns the forecasting and allocation objectives while adaptively calibrating uncertainty, remaining agnostic to the specific forecasting model used.
