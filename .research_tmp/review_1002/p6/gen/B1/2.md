## Related Works

Nonverbal communication, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language, is fundamental to human interaction, conveying intent and emotion beyond verbal content [1]. Despite its importance, most neural conversational models have historically been limited to text-only processing, failing to incorporate these critical nonverbal elements [2]. Recent advancements have sought to bridge this gap by integrating multimodal data into language models and conversational agents.

In the domain of multimodal video and language understanding, several frameworks have emerged to process visual and textual information. VideoChat integrates video foundation models with large language models to excel in spatiotemporal reasoning and causal inference [3]. Similarly, Video-llama presents an instruction-tuned audio-visual language model for comprehensive video understanding [4]. Unified-IO 2 extends this capability by unifying image, text, audio, and action modalities within a single autoregressive framework, achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks [5]. While these models demonstrate strong multimodal understanding, they often focus on general video comprehension rather than the specific generation of nonverbal cues in conversational contexts.

Research on emotion recognition has further highlighted the necessity of multimodal data. Datasets such as MELD provide large-scale multimodal multi-party emotional conversational data, emphasizing the importance of contextual and multimodal information for accurate emotion recognition [6]. Other resources, including IEMOCAP and CMU-MOSEI, have contributed to the analysis of interactive emotional dyadic motion capture and multimodal language in the wild [7][8]. EmotionCLIP introduces a pre-training paradigm to extract visual emotion representations from verbal and nonverbal communication using uncurated data, addressing data scarcity in emotion understanding [9]. These works underscore the value of nonverbal cues in conveying emotional states but primarily focus on recognition tasks rather than generative conversational systems.

The generation of nonverbal responses has also seen significant progress. CHAMPAGNE introduces a generative model of conversations that accounts for visual contexts, trained on a large-scale corpus of video-based dialogues [2]. For face-to-face interactions, recent work has introduced spoken dialogue models that process and generate audio-visual speech, marking steps toward avatar chatbots without intermediate text [10]. In the realm of embodied agents, FurChat utilizes large language models to generate open and closed-domain dialogue along with facial expressions on robotic platforms [11]. Similarly, other research proposes augmenting social robots with empathetic non-verbal cues, such as speech, gestures, and facial expressions, generated via LLMs to enhance interaction authenticity [12].

Specific techniques for generating nonverbal motion from speech or text have been developed to ensure realistic and coherent outputs. EMOCA focuses on emotion-driven monocular face capture, using perceptual emotion consistency loss to ensure 3D facial geometry matches emotional content [13]. For holistic body motion, frameworks like the one proposed for generating 3D human motion from speech synthesize body poses, hand gestures, and facial expressions from audio inputs [14]. HumanTOMATO targets text-driven whole-body motion generation, aligning generated motions with textual descriptions using hierarchical VQ-VAE structures [15]. Furthermore, studies on listener responses have explored generating facial gestures from speaker transcripts using VQ-VAE quantized tokens within transformer-based language models [16][17]. These approaches provide foundational techniques for modeling nonverbal dynamics, which MARS builds upon by unifying text and vector-quantized nonverbal representations within a single next-token prediction framework.
