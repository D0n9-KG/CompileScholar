## Related Works

**Fashion Outfit Recommendation and Generation**
Fashion outfit recommendation has evolved from predicting user preferences for existing items to generating novel, compatible outfits. Early approaches focused on modeling compatibility between items and user preferences, often treating these as separate tasks. The Hierarchical Fashion Graph Network (HFGN) unified fashion compatibility modeling and personalized recommendation by constructing a hierarchical graph structure to aggregate item information into outfit representations \[[Xingchen Li and
                  Xiang Wang and
                  Xiangnan He and
                  Long Chen and
                  Jun Xiao and
                  Tat{-}Seng Chua](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566v1)\]. Similarly, the Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model addressed the dual requirements of compatibility and personalization by connecting user preferences for individual items and outfits using a Transformer architecture, deployed at Alibaba’s iFashion platform \[[Wen Chen and
                  Pipei Huang and
                  Jiaming Xu and
                  Xin Guo and
                  Cheng Guo and
                  Fei Sun and
                  Chao Li and
                  Andreas Pfadler and
                  Huan Zhao and
                  Binqiang Zhao](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866v3)\]. More recently, the advent of AI-generated content has enabled Generative Outfit Recommendation (GOR), which aims to generate fashion images and compose them into visually compatible outfits. The DiFashion model utilizes diffusion models to achieve high fidelity, compatibility, and personalization in parallel image generation, overcoming the limitations of pre-defined outfit pools \[[Yiyan Xu and
                  Wenjie Wang and
                  Fuli Feng and
                  Yunshan Ma and
                  Jizhi Zhang and
                  Xiangnan He](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279v3)\].

**Virtual Try-On and Image Synthesis**
Virtual try-on (VTON) is a critical application of image synthesis in fashion, allowing users to visualize clothing on themselves. Early methods like VITON used coarse-to-fine strategies to transfer clothing items onto person images without 3D information \[[Xintong Han and
                  Zuxuan Wu and
                  Zhe Wu and
                  Ruichi Yu and
                  Larry S. Davis](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08447v4)\]. However, GAN-based methods often struggle with high-resolution details. Diffusion models have emerged as a superior alternative for high-quality synthesis. DCI-VTON leverages a warping module to guide diffusion models, preserving local clothing details during inpainting \[[Junhong Gou and
                  Siyu Sun and
                  Jianfu Zhang and
                  Jianlou Si and
                  Chen Qian and
                  Liqing Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06101v1)\]. StableVITON further advances this by learning semantic correspondence between clothing and the human body within the latent space of pre-trained diffusion models, using zero cross-attention blocks to maintain fidelity \[[Jeongho Kim and
                  Gyojung Gu and
                  Minho Park and
                  Sunghyun Park and
                  Jaegul Choo](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01725v1)\]. The Smart Fitting Room framework integrates matching-aware virtual try-on with retrieval-based recommendation, combining generative and retrieval methods to enhance the shopping experience \[[Mingzhe Yu and
                  Yunshan Ma and
                  Lei Wu and
                  Kai Cheng and
                  Xue Li and
                  Lei Meng and
                  Tat{-}Seng Chua](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16825v2)\].

**Preference Optimization and Alignment**
Aligning generative models with human preferences is traditionally achieved through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which is complex and unstable. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a simpler, more stable alternative by optimizing a policy directly using a classification loss, eliminating the need for a separate reward model \[[Rafael Rafailov and
                  Archit Sharma and
                  Eric Mitchell and
                  Christopher D. Manning and
                  Stefano Ermon and
                  Chelsea Finn](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290v3)\]. This approach has been adapted for diffusion models. Diffusion-DPO re-formulates DPO for diffusion models using the evidence lower bound to derive a differentiable objective, significantly improving visual appeal and prompt alignment in Stable Diffusion XL \[[Bram Wallace and
                  Meihua Dang and
                  Rafael Rafailov and
                  Linqi Zhou and
                  Aaron Lou and
                  Senthil Purushwalkam and
                  Stefano Ermon and
                  Caiming Xiong and
                  Shafiq Joty and
                  Nikhil Naik](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12908v1)\]. D3PO addresses the high GPU memory requirements of diffusion denoising by using the relative scale of objectives as a proxy for human preference, reducing computational overhead while maintaining performance \[[Kai Yang and
                  Jian Tao and
                  Jiafei Lyu and
                  Chunjiang Ge and
                  Jiaxin Chen and
                  Qimai Li and
                  Weihan Shen and
                  Xiaolong Zhu and
                  Xiu Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13231v3)\]. These methods provide the foundational techniques for fine-tuning generative models without task-specific reward functions, a paradigm that FashionDPO applies to the domain of personalized outfit generation.
