## Related Works

**Multi-Token Prediction and Future Token Anticipation**
Large language models (LLMs) traditionally rely on next-token prediction (NTP) as their primary training objective. Recent research suggests that training models to predict multiple future tokens simultaneously, known as multi-token prediction (MTP), enhances sample efficiency and downstream capabilities without increasing training time \[[Fabian Gloeckle and Badr Youbi Idrissi and Baptiste Rozière and David Lopez-Paz and Gabriel Synnaeve](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. By employing independent output heads on a shared model trunk, MTP improves generative benchmarks, such as coding tasks, and accelerates inference by up to three times \[[Fabian Gloeckle and Badr Youbi Idrissi and Baptiste Rozière and David Lopez-Paz and Gabriel Synnaeve](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. The theoretical basis for this approach is supported by findings that hidden state vectors in transformers encode sufficient information to predict subsequent tokens with high accuracy \[[Pal](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04897v1)\]. Specifically, linear approximation methods on GPT-J-6B demonstrate that individual hidden states can anticipate tokens at positions $t+2$ or later with over 48% accuracy \[[Pal](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04897v1)\]. This capability underpins the effectiveness of MTP in capturing long-range dependencies and improving algorithmic reasoning \[[Fabian Gloeckle and Badr Youbi Idrissi and Baptiste Rozière and David Lopez-Paz and Gabriel Synnaeve](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

**Speculative Decoding and Inference Acceleration**
To mitigate the sequential bottleneck of autoregressive decoding, speculative decoding has emerged as a key paradigm for accelerating LLM inference while preserving output quality \[[Heming Xia and Yongqi Li and Jun Zhang and Cunxiao Du and Wenjie Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06916v2)\]. This technique involves a draft model generating multiple tokens in parallel, which are then verified by the target LLM in a single forward pass \[[Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08168v2)\]\[[Heming Xia and Yongqi Li and Jun Zhang and Cunxiao Du and Wenjie Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06916v2)\]. Early approaches required training separate draft models, which incurred significant computational costs \[[Fangcheng Liu and Yehui Tang and Zhenhua Liu and Yunsheng Ni and Kai Han and Yunhe Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18911v1)\]. To address this, self-speculative decoding methods have been developed to eliminate the need for auxiliary models. For instance, Draft & Verify utilizes layer-skipping to create a compact draft model from the target LLM’s intermediate layers, achieving up to 1.99x speedup without additional training \[[Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08168v2)\]. Similarly, SWIFT employs on-the-fly layer skipping to adaptively select layers for drafting, providing a plug-and-play solution that maintains the original distribution of generated text \[[Heming Xia and Yongqi Li and Jun Zhang and Cunxiao Du and Wenjie Li](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06916v2)\]. Other frameworks, such as Medusa, augment LLMs with multiple decoding heads to predict subsequent tokens in parallel, using tree-based attention to verify candidate continuations \[[Tianle Cai and Yuhong Li and Zhengyang Geng and Hongwu Peng and Jason D. Lee and Deming Chen and Tri Dao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774v3)\]. Medusa-1, which fine-tunes heads on a frozen backbone, achieves over 2.2x speedup, while Medusa-2, which fine-tunes the entire model, reaches 2.3–3.6x speedup \[[Tianle Cai and Yuhong Li and Zhengyang Geng and Hongwu Peng and Jason D. Lee and Deming Chen and Tri Dao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774v3)\]. Additionally, KOALA enhances speculative decoding by transforming single-layer draft heads into multi-layer architectures with adversarial learning, improving draft accuracy and latency speedups by 10.57%–14.09% \[[Kaiqi Zhang and Jing Zhao and Rui Chen](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08146v1)\]. Kangaroo further optimizes self-speculative decoding by using a fixed shallow sub-network as a self-draft model with an additional early-exiting mechanism to minimize drafting steps, achieving up to 1.68x speedup with significantly fewer parameters than Medusa-1 \[[Fangcheng Liu and Yehui Tang and Zhenhua Liu and Yunsheng Ni and Kai Han and Yunhe Wang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18911v1)\].

**Curriculum Learning in Machine Learning**
Curriculum learning is a strategy that trains models in a meaningful order, progressing from easy to hard samples, which can improve performance over random shuffling without additional computational costs \[[Petru Soviany and Radu Tudor Ionescu and Paolo Rota and Nicu Sebe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10382v3)\]. This approach has been successfully applied across various machine learning tasks, though its effectiveness depends on defining appropriate ranking criteria and pacing functions \[[Petru Soviany and Radu Tudor Ionescu and Paolo Rota and Nicu Sebe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10382v3)\]. Recent surveys have constructed taxonomies of curriculum learning methods, highlighting how different instantiations tackle the challenges of sample ordering and difficulty progression \[[Petru Soviany and Radu Tudor Ionescu and Paolo Rota and Nicu Sebe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10382v3)\]. In the context of MTP, curriculum learning offers a structured approach to managing the complexity of the pre-training objective, particularly for smaller language models that may struggle with the immediate demands of multi-token prediction.
