## Related Works

The calibration of probabilistic predictions is critical for machine learning systems where accurate absolute values are required for downstream tasks, such as medical diagnosis, meteorological forecasting, and online advertising \[[Huang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348v2)\]. While many classifiers prioritize classification accuracy, calibrated estimates are necessary to reflect true likelihoods \[[Huang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348v2)\]. Early approaches to calibration included scaling and binning methods; however, binning methods are often preferred due to their distribution-free theoretical guarantees \[[Huang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348v2)\]. Despite these advantages, traditional binning schemes have limitations, including reliance solely on original prediction values and the mapping of multiple samples to identical values, which reduces suitability for order-sensitive applications \[[Huang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348v2)\]. To address these issues, Multiple Boosting Calibration Trees (MBCT) was proposed, utilizing tree structures and linear functions within nodes to achieve individual, non-monotonic calibration that improves order accuracy \[[Huang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348v2)\].

In the domain of neural networks, miscalibration remains a significant concern, particularly as newer, more accurate models have historically produced poorly calibrated predictions \[[Guo](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998v2)\]. Recent systematic analyses indicate that architecture is a major determinant of calibration properties, with non-convolutional models among the best calibrated, and that trends like calibration decay with model size are less pronounced in recent architectures \[[Guo](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998v2)\]. For binary classifiers, Beta calibration has been introduced as a well-founded improvement over logistic calibration \[[Kull](https://ref.local/5)\]. In multiclass settings, transforming classifier scores into accurate probability estimates remains an active area of research \[[Zadrozny](https://ref.local/2)\], with specific comparisons made between probabilistic outputs for support vector machines and regularized likelihood methods \[[Platt](https://ref.local/3)\].

In industrial applications, particularly online advertising and personalized ranking, calibration is essential for tasks like click-through rate (CTR) prediction and bidding \[[Kweon](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428v2)\]\[[Zhao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17655v2)\]\[[Pan](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713v3)\]\[[Wei](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07295v2)\]\[[Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010v1)\]\[[Yang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09507v2)\]\[[Sheng](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164v2)\]\[[Bai](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01494v2)\]\[[Gui](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08727v1)\]. Field-aware calibration methods have been developed to adjust model outputs based on specific feature values, addressing mismatches between predicted probabilities and true likelihoods caused by data distribution shifts \[[Zhao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17655v2)\]\[[Pan](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713v3)\]\[[Wei](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07295v2)\]. Neural Calibration, for instance, leverages field-aware information to improve metrics such as negative log-likelihood and Brier score \[[Pan](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713v3)\]. AdaCalib proposes a doubly-adaptive approach that learns isotonic functions guided by posterior statistics to ensure field-appropriate calibration \[[Wei](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07295v2)\]. Confidence-aware multi-field calibration further addresses data sparsity by adaptively adjusting calibration intensity based on sample statistics \[[Zhao](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17655v2)\].

Joint optimization of ranking and calibration has also been explored to balance relative ordering with absolute accuracy. Multi-objective methods often combine pointwise losses for calibration with ranking losses for order, but this can lead to sub-optimal trade-offs \[[Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010v1)\]\[[Sheng](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164v2)\]\[[Bai](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01494v2)\]. The Joint Optimization of Ranking and Calibration (JRC) approach constrains predicted probabilities to be a function of logit subtraction to preserve the interpretation of logits as click probabilities \[[Sheng](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164v2)\]. Similarly, Regression Compatible Ranking (RCR) ensures that ranking and regression components are mutually aligned, improving Pareto frontiers in multi-objective optimization \[[Bai](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01494v2)\]. Other techniques include Self-Boosted Calibrated Ranking (SBCR), which addresses the conflict between conflicting loss functions on a single prediction \[[Zhang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010v1)\], and Calibration-compatible Listwise Distillation (CLID), which preserves calibration ability while distilling ranking knowledge from privileged features \[[Gui](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08727v1)\]. Additionally, Deep Ensemble Shape Calibration (DESC) decomposes multi-field calibration into value and shape components to enhance data utilization in e-commerce advertising \[[Yang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09507v2)\]. Large-scale industrial frameworks like LiRank have also integrated novel calibration techniques into production ranking systems \[[Borisyuk](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06859v2)\].
