## Related Work

**Data-Dependent vs. Data-Oblivious Layer Analysis**
A significant portion of prior research relies on data-dependent analyses to identify critical layers, typically requiring access to specific task datasets or fine-tuning procedures. Studies such as [1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, 12] employ various metrics—including projection weighted canonical correlation analysis, singular vector canonical correlation analysis, causal interventions, and performance degradation tests—that are inherently tied to specific training data or task objectives. Similarly, [5] conducts post-hoc analysis of fine-tuned models to identify layer clusters, while [11] utilizes task-specific probing to determine where concepts are acquired. In contrast, our approach is data-oblivious, focusing on the intrinsic properties of pre-fine-tuned models. This aligns with [6], which also investigates representation dissimilarity in a data-agnostic manner, but extends this line of inquiry by establishing a direct link between intrinsic representation shifts and downstream fine-tuning sensitivity without requiring task-specific data.

**Metrics for Representation Similarity**
The choice of metric for measuring representation dynamics is central to identifying critical layers. Several works have proposed geometry-based metrics to compare neural network representations, such as Projection Weighted Canonical Correlation Analysis (PWCCA) [1] and Singular Vector Canonical Correlation Analysis (SVCCA) [2]. Other approaches rely on distinct methodologies, including causal intervention and neuron activation analysis [7], hidden layer activation classification [8], performance degradation upon layer removal [9], and metrics based on prompt entropy and curvature [12]. While [6] uses general representation dissimilarity measures, our work specifically employs Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) to analyze representation dynamics. This choice is consistent with [3], which introduced CKA as a robust metric for comparing representations, and [5], which applied CKA to fine-tuned Transformers. However, unlike [5], which uses CKA in a post-hoc setting, we apply it to pre-fine-tuned models to identify intrinsic critical layers.

**Application Scope: Interpretability vs. Practical Utility**
Most existing studies on layer analysis are primarily motivated by interpretability, aiming to visualize or understand the internal workings of models. Works such as [1, 2, 3, 6, 11] focus on specific interpretability visualizations or conceptual depth, while [7] localizes factual associations for model editing, and [8] uses internal states for truthfulness detection. Other applications are more narrow in scope, such as model compression and pruning [5], single-task performance optimization [9], or architectural optimization and training guidance [12]. In contrast, our work positions the identification of critical layers as a general-purpose tool for both adaptation and defense. This dual-use perspective is shared by [10], which proposes layer-specific editing for jailbreak defense, but our method broadens the impact by demonstrating utility in efficient domain adaptation as well, thereby moving beyond single-domain applications.

**Spectral Interpretation of Critical Layers**
Prior work has offered various interpretations of why certain layers are critical, often relying on broad dichotomies or structural observations. For instance, [5, 9, 11] often frame their findings in terms of a shallow versus deep layer dichotomy, while [1] and [2] discuss bottom-up convergence and intrinsic dimensionality. Other studies highlight asymmetry in activations [6], mid-layer feed-forward module localization [7], or bimodal entropy patterns in intermediate layers [12]. However, no cited prior work provides a spectral interpretation based on top principal components encoding semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions. Our spectral analysis fills this gap by revealing that the shifts in representation space identified via CKA are driven by changes in the top principal components, offering a mechanistic explanation for the criticality of these layers that distinguishes our approach from purely empirical or black-box selection methods.
