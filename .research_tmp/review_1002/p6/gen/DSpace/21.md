## Related Work

**Source of Photometric Supervision**
Neural rendering methods fundamentally rely on minimizing photometric error to reconstruct 3D scenes, yet the quality of reconstruction is often constrained by the sparsity of input views. A significant body of work, including NeuRAD [6], Lightning NeRF [7], and AligNeRF [8], relies exclusively on real captured input views to supervise the optimization process, limiting their ability to handle complex scenes where certain object angles are never observed. To address data scarcity without generating new views, DiffusioNeRF [9] employs self-supervised consistency losses to regularize the training process. More recently, ViewCrafter [11] introduces synthetic views generated via a video diffusion model to facilitate 3D-GS optimization. In contrast, PS4PRO utilizes synthetic views generated via video frame interpolation as a data augmentation strategy, effectively enriching the photo supervision with intermediate frames that bridge the gaps between sparse input views.

**Role of the Interpolation Model**
The integration of generative models into the rendering pipeline has evolved from standalone tasks to components that guide 3D reconstruction. Traditional video frame interpolation methods, such as those surveyed in [1] and implemented in depth-aware [2], real-time flow estimation [3], and splatting-based [4] approaches, are typically treated as standalone video generation tasks unrelated to 3D reconstruction. While DiffusioNeRF [9] leverages a denoising diffusion model as an implicit scene prior for regularization, and ViewCrafter [11] uses a generative model specifically for novel view synthesis, these approaches do not fully exploit the interpolation model as a geometric guide. PS4PRO distinguishes itself by positioning the interpolation model as an implicit world prior that implicitly models camera movement and real-world 3D geometry, thereby enriching the photo supervision directly during the optimization phase.

**Training Data for the Prior**
The generalization capability of learned priors in neural rendering is heavily dependent on the diversity and realism of their training data. DiffusioNeRF [9] trains its regularization model on synthetic 3D scene datasets, such as those generated by Blender, which may lack the complexity of real-world dynamics. In contrast, PS4PRO is trained on diverse real-world video datasets. This choice allows the model to capture real-world motion and geometry that synthetic data might lack, enabling better generalization to complex and dynamic scenes where accurate reconstruction is most challenging.

**Model Architecture Complexity**
Efficiency is a critical design constraint for integrating auxiliary models into neural rendering pipelines, which already involve computationally expensive explicit representations. Most neural rendering methods, including NeuRAD [6], Lightning NeRF [7], AligNeRF [8], DiffusioNeRF [9], and 3DGS-Enhancer [10], rely on complex explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting or NeRF variants that are computationally intensive. On the other end of the spectrum, some video frame interpolation methods, such as [2, 3, 4], utilize simple optical flow-based warping without deep learning refinement, while others like [5] employ hybrid CNN and Transformer architectures. ViewCrafter [11] utilizes heavyweight general-purpose video diffusion models, which are often too slow for real-time or efficient integration. PS4PRO addresses this gap by proposing a lightweight yet high-quality video frame interpolation model, ensuring that the data augmentation process remains efficient while maintaining the fidelity necessary for photorealistic rendering.
