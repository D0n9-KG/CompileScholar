## Related Work

**Cost Focus Domain**
Prior research in containerized systems has predominantly focused on optimizing compute and storage resources to enhance efficiency and reduce operational overhead. For instance, [1] proposes a Kubernetes-based system for adaptive resource management specifically aimed at reducing costs through dynamic allocation, while [2] presents a hierarchical multi-agent optimization framework designed to improve resource allocation in cloud computing environments. These studies share a common focus on system-level resource efficiency, treating cost reduction as a byproduct of optimized compute and storage utilization. In contrast, this paper addresses a distinct gap by focusing explicitly on network costs, including bandwidth, data transfer, and inter-zone traffic, which remains underexplored in the context of Kubernetes deployments.

**Hosting Platform Comparison**
Existing literature largely investigates optimization strategies within single, homogeneous environments, such as cloud-managed platforms or isolated infrastructure, without providing a direct comparative analysis of heterogeneous hosting options. For example, [2] focuses on resource allocation within cloud computing environments, and [1] targets cost efficiency within a Kubernetes context without explicitly contrasting it against alternative physical infrastructures. This paper distinguishes itself by conducting a direct comparative study between cloud-managed Kubernetes and bare metal Kubernetes, evaluating the specific cost benefits and trade-offs of each platform to provide a comprehensive view of hosting decisions.

**Methodological Approach**
Methodologies in prior work have typically relied on either purely technical performance metrics or theoretical optimization models, often lacking a direct link to financial expenditures. [1] and [2] both employ optimization frameworks and adaptive management techniques to improve system performance, yet they do not integrate empirical financial modelling with measurement analysis to quantify actual monetary impact. This paper advances the methodological landscape by combining measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modelling, thereby providing actionable insights into real-world network expenditures rather than solely technical throughput or latency improvements.

**Disciplinary Lens**
The disciplinary lens of previous studies has been primarily rooted in computer science and systems engineering, focusing on technical efficiency and resource allocation algorithms. Works such as [1] and [2] approach cost reduction through technical optimization of resource management and allocation strategies, respectively, without explicitly framing their contributions within the broader context of financial operations. This paper adopts the emerging FinOps (Financial Operations) discipline, explicitly addressing the financial and operational aspects of managing network costs in cloud-native environments, thereby bridging the gap between technical implementation and financial management.
