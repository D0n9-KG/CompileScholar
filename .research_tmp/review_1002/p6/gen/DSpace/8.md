## Related Work

### Uncertainty-Aware Evaluation Signals
A significant body of literature has recently shifted focus toward uncertainty quantification, recognizing that model confidence provides critical insights beyond binary correctness. In the domain of Large Language Models (LLMs), recent works have developed specific frameworks to measure and benchmark uncertainty, such as semantic entropy for linguistic invariances [26], predictive uncertainty quantification for free-form generation [27], and exploratory studies on risk assessment in NLP and code tasks [20, 30]. These efforts are supported by broader surveys and methods for uncertainty estimation in deep neural networks and NLP systems [18, 19, 21, 23, 25, 29], as well as investigations into the reliability of self-evaluation in language models [28, 31]. While these studies establish the technical foundations for measuring uncertainty, they primarily focus on general reliability or calibration rather than fairness-specific implications. In contrast, our work leverages uncertainty-aware signals specifically to evaluate fairness, moving beyond general uncertainty quantification to address implicit biases in model decisions.

### Fairness Metric Granularity
Prior research on fairness metrics has predominantly relied on group-level statistical parity measures, such as Equalized Odds, to assess disparities across demographic groups [11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 35]. Other approaches have introduced more specialized metrics, including bias correlation between pre-trained and prompted models [4], gender prediction bias in occupational contexts [5], and compositional evaluations across various social groups and tasks [7]. Additionally, some works have explored the trade-offs between debiasing and model reliability [32] or proposed using uncertainty itself as a fairness measure [33, 34, 35]. However, a distinct gap remains in the literature: no cited prior work employs a fine-grained metric that captures internal decision bias through uncertainty, such as our proposed UCerF. While existing metrics evaluate aggregate outcomes or specific bias types, they fail to capture the implicit impact of model confidence on fairness, a dimension that our metric explicitly addresses.

### Evaluation Dataset Characteristics
The datasets used for fairness evaluation vary significantly in scope and specificity. Some benchmarks are general-purpose, covering diverse tasks without a specific focus on fairness [20, 23, 26, 27, 28, 31], while others are tailored to specific tasks like pronoun co-reference resolution [4, 5, 11, 12, 15], question answering [16], or open-ended generation [14]. Comprehensive surveys and collections, such as those documenting over 200 algorithmic fairness datasets [9] or organizing existing data by compositional taxonomies [7], provide broad coverage but often lack the scale or diversity required for modern LLM evaluation. Notably, while one prior work introduced a large-scale gender bias dataset for coreference resolution [17], it does not match the specific characteristics of our new dataset. Our contribution distinguishes itself by introducing a new, diverse, and large-scale gender-occupation dataset with 31,756 samples, designed to address the data size, diversity, and clarity issues present in current benchmarks.

### Target Model Class
The target model class in fairness research has evolved from traditional NLP models to modern Large Language Models. Early studies focused on BERT variants and other traditional NLP architectures [11, 12, 16, 17], while some uncertainty-related works targeted small-scale discriminative models [25, 29] or general deep neural networks [18, 19, 21, 22, 23]. More recently, the focus has shifted to LLMs, with numerous studies evaluating fairness and uncertainty in these large-scale systems [1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 13, 14, 15, 20, 26, 27, 28, 30, 31]. Surveys have also categorized fairness research by model size and training paradigms [3, 6]. Although many recent works target LLMs, they often rely on conventional accuracy-based metrics or general uncertainty measures. Our paper stands out by combining the LLM target class with an uncertainty-aware fairness metric, providing a more nuanced evaluation of how modern LLMs handle fairness in their internal decision-making processes.
