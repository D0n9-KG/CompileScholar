## Related Work

**Handling Class Imbalance**
Addressing the extreme rarity of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) in multiple sclerosis requires strategies to mitigate class imbalance, as standard re-balancing techniques often fail due to the scarcity of real data. Prior approaches have primarily focused on oversampling existing minority class samples, such as DeepSMOTE, which fuses deep learning with SMOTE to generate high-quality artificial images for balancing datasets [6]. In the specific context of neuroimaging, synthetic data generation has been explored to improve model generalizability [1] and to generate synthetic training data for medical image segmentation [4]. Within the domain of multiple sclerosis, imbalance-aware learning methods have been proposed for identifying chronic active lesions on quantitative susceptibility maps (QSM) [5], while transfer learning techniques have been systematically reviewed for their application in magnetic resonance brain imaging [3], supported by theoretical bounds on transfer learning limits [2]. More recently, generative models have been employed to synthesize multiple sclerosis lesions in MRI using encoder-decoder architectures for data augmentation [7]. This paper aligns with the latter approach by utilizing synthetic data generation via GANs to augment the minority class of rim lesions, directly addressing the scarcity of real PRLs to improve classifier performance.

**Label Ambiguity Resolution**
A significant challenge in clinical imaging is the presence of ambiguous or noisy labels, which can hinder the effective training of models on rare lesion types. Most prior work in this domain relies on standard hard-label training without explicit ambiguity handling [10]. Alternative strategies include robust training against random label noise, such as methods that corrupt labels during training to enhance the robustness of conditional GANs [11]. Other approaches focus on post-correction of noisy multilabel predictions by modeling label noise as stochastic shifts in latent space [12], or on post-processing calibration of noisy predictions using generative models to estimate transition matrices [13]. In contrast to these methods, which either ignore label noise or correct it post-hoc, this paper introduces a novel latent projection denoising approach. This method allows the model to train directly on ambiguous rim cases, thereby substantially increasing the effective size of the minority class and better approximating the true rim lesion distribution.

**Input Modality and Output Scope**
The scope of input modalities and output predictions varies significantly across prior literature, ranging from general image processing tasks to specific clinical applications. General image editing and restoration techniques, such as GAN inversion for mapping real images to latent spaces, have been widely surveyed [9]. In the context of generative modeling, conditional GANs have been introduced to generate data conditioned on class labels, typically demonstrated on single-channel datasets like MNIST [10]. Similarly, robust conditional generation from noisy labels has been explored in single-channel image classification and generation tasks [11]. Multilabel classification methods have also been developed to handle complex label structures [12], and general classification frameworks have been applied to various domains [13]. In the specific area of multiple sclerosis, prior work has largely focused on single-contrast MRI analysis for lesion synthesis [7]. This paper extends beyond these single-channel or single-contrast limitations by providing a multi-channel extension that generates accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps, offering a more comprehensive clinical tool for longitudinal monitoring and detailed lesion characterization.

**Generative Model Utility**
The utility of generative models in prior work has typically been confined to specific roles, such as data augmentation or image restoration. Generative models have been used solely for data augmentation in imbalanced datasets [6], for synthesizing multiple sclerosis lesions in MRI [7], and for conditional generation based on class labels [10]. In the realm of image editing, GAN inversion techniques have been employed for restoration tasks by mapping images to latent spaces [9]. Other works have utilized generative models for robust conditional generation from noisy labels [11], uncertainty estimation and prediction correction [12], and estimating transition matrices for prediction calibration [13]. While these studies treat data generation and inference-time correction as separate tasks, this paper proposes a dual-purpose framework. By exploiting the projection capability of the trained GAN, we achieve both data augmentation and inference-time denoising via latent projection, creating a synergistic effect that distinguishes our approach from prior work that addresses these objectives independently.
