## Related Work

**Trace-Driven Performance Modeling**
Prior work on performance modeling for distributed deep learning generally falls into two categories: analytical or simulation-based approaches and trace-driven methods. Analytical models, such as Amped [19], rely on first-principles roofline calculations to estimate performance, while simulation frameworks like Astra-sim [23, 24] perform full re-simulation of the training process to explore software and hardware co-design. Other approaches focus on high-level co-design methodologies [20] or specific optimization techniques like automatic operator fusion [22]. In contrast, trace-driven approaches leverage actual execution data to capture complex system interactions that analytical models often miss. Daydream [21] represents the closest prior work in this dimension, utilizing fine-grained dependency graphs from low-level traces to predict optimization efficacy. However, while Daydream targets general DNN training on heterogeneous stacks, Lumos extends this trace-driven methodology specifically to large-scale LLM training on modern H100 clusters, achieving high accuracy in replaying execution time and estimating new configurations from existing traces.

**Scope of Prediction and Configuration Exploration**
A critical distinction in performance modeling tools is their ability to generalize to new configurations without full re-execution. Simulation-based tools such as Astra-sim [23, 24] typically require a full re-simulation for every new configuration, which can be computationally expensive for exploring vast design spaces. In contrast, analytical models like Amped [19] and trace-driven tools like Daydream [21] are designed to estimate performance for new setups based on existing data, facilitating efficient exploration. Lumos aligns with the latter group by enabling the estimation of performance for new model and deployment configurations from existing traces. This capability allows for rapid iteration and system-level studies without the overhead of repeated full-scale simulations or the potential inaccuracies of purely analytical approximations in complex distributed environments.

**System Scale and Hardware Target**
The scale and hardware target of prior work vary significantly, ranging from single-node setups to massive distributed clusters. Early foundational work, such as the Transformer architecture [6], was demonstrated on single-node or small-scale multi-GPU setups. Subsequent systems have targeted various large-scale environments, including TPU clusters [2, 8, 12] and GPU clusters using frameworks like Megatron-LM [9, 10, 11] and DeepSpeed [17, 18]. While many of these systems operate at large scale, they often focus on specific hardware architectures or parallelization strategies, such as sequence parallelism [16, 17, 18] or automatic parallelism [14, 15]. Lumos is positioned to address the specific communication and synchronization overheads of modern, large-scale LLM training on NVIDIA H100 clusters (up to 512 GPUs), a configuration that shares its scale with recent large-scale GPU training efforts [2, 8, 9, 10, 11, 17, 18, 19, 20, 23, 24] but distinguishes itself by providing a unified performance modeling toolkit for this specific hardware and workload class.

**Granularity of Performance Metrics**
The granularity of performance metrics reported by prior tools differs, impacting their utility for detailed system analysis. Many large-scale training systems and optimization frameworks, such as those described in [2, 12, 15, 17, 18], primarily report aggregate metrics like throughput or FLOPS, which are insufficient for diagnosing fine-grained bottlenecks. Other works focus on specific aspects, such as memory efficiency and batch/sequence length limits [16]. In contrast, trace-driven tools like Daydream [21] provide detailed runtime behaviors and execution time breakdowns. Lumos adopts this high-fidelity approach, capturing detailed runtime behaviors and execution time breakdowns rather than just scalar performance numbers. This granularity is essential for guiding optimization efforts and conducting system-level studies, as it reveals the specific execution behaviors and interactions that aggregate metrics obscure.
