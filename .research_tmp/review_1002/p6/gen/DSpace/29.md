## Related Work

**Trust Model and Execution Fidelity**
Prior research on securing differential privacy (DP) mechanisms has adopted various trust models to address execution fidelity. A significant body of work operates under fully distributed or peer-to-peer local DP settings, where clients are assumed to be the primary actors in noise generation [9, 10]. Other approaches focus on untrusted clients or respondents, employing cryptographic guarantees to ensure that data providers execute agreed-upon protocols without manipulation [3, 5]. In centralized contexts, some frameworks assume a trusted publisher whose output is verifiable by clients [1], while others, such as Crypt$\epsilon$, rely on secure multi-party computation (SMPC) with two non-colluding untrusted servers to achieve central model accuracy without a trusted data collector [6]. In contrast, our work introduces a Client-Server-Verifier setup that explicitly models an untrusted server with verifiable execution, addressing the specific vulnerability where servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols.

**Verification Mechanisms**
The choice of verification mechanism significantly impacts the feasibility of verifiable DP. Several prior studies rely on zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) as a necessary and sufficient condition for ensuring that a publishing entity faithfully generates DP noise without revealing it [1]. Alternative approaches include interactive proof systems for verifying the correctness of differentially private counting queries [4] and verifiable randomization mechanisms designed to prevent manipulation in local DP [3]. Some works employ traditional or quantum cryptographic techniques to guarantee unconditional privacy and prevent cheating in tallies [5], while others, particularly those leveraging SMPC for secure sampling, often operate with black-box trust assumptions regarding the underlying cryptographic primitives [6, 10]. We distinguish our approach by demonstrating that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not necessary. Instead, we propose efficient custom proofs that decouple verifiability from heavy ZKP constructions, enabling more practical deployment.

**Privacy Setting Scope**
Existing literature addresses specific privacy settings, often limiting the scope of theoretical contributions. Centralized differential privacy (CDP) is the focus of works utilizing ZKPs [1], interactive proofs [4], and crypto-assisted frameworks on untrusted servers [6]. Conversely, local differential privacy (LDP) is addressed through verifiable randomization mechanisms [3] and cryptographic randomized response techniques [5]. A separate line of research focuses on the secure sampling of noise, such as discrete Gaussian noise [9] and biased coins [8], or benchmarks the efficiency of secure sampling protocols in distributed settings [10]. Additionally, some works propose distributed noise generation [7] or secure voting with unconditional privacy [5]. Our work defines Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP) as a general framework that encompasses both centralized and local settings, providing a broader theoretical foundation rather than optimizing a specific LDP or CDP protocol.

**Efficiency-Utility Tradeoff**
The practical deployment of verifiable DP is often hindered by the efficiency-utility tradeoff. Prior works that employ heavy ZKPs or high-utility secure computation methods typically incur high communication and computational costs [1, 6, 10]. Other approaches, such as verifiable randomization mechanisms, introduce moderate overhead for full verification [3], while cryptographic techniques aim for efficient and practical constructions for specific privacy guarantees [5]. In contrast, our work prioritizes high-efficiency proof generation with minimal utility loss. We develop the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM), which offers up to a $4 \times 10^5$x improvement in proof generation efficiency with negligible error, and an improved Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR) mechanism that reduces communication costs and verifier overhead by up to 5000x, thereby making verifiable DP practically deployable.
