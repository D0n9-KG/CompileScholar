**Related Works**

**Time Series Forecasting Models**
Recent advancements in time series forecasting have been dominated by Transformer-based architectures, which aim to capture long-range dependencies more effectively than traditional recurrent models. Early efforts addressed the quadratic complexity of standard Transformers through sparse attention mechanisms. Informer introduced a ProbSparse self-attention mechanism to achieve $O(L \log L)$ complexity for long sequence forecasting [1]. Similarly, LogSparse Transformer and Autoformer proposed decomposition-based approaches and auto-correlation mechanisms to handle long-term series with reduced memory costs [3][6]. More recent models have focused on structural innovations to improve efficiency and representation. PatchTST utilizes channel-independent patching to reduce computational load and enhance local semantic retention [4], while FEDformer integrates frequency-enhanced decomposition to capture global trends with linear complexity [7]. Other notable architectures include Pyraformer, which employs pyramidal attention for low-complexity modeling [2], Crossformer, which leverages cross-dimension dependencies [5], and MICN, which models multi-scale local and global contexts [8]. Beyond Transformers, TimesNet transforms 1D time series into 2D tensors to model temporal variations using 2D kernels [9]. However, the efficacy of these complex architectures has been questioned; studies suggest that simple linear models can often outperform sophisticated Transformer-based methods due to the permutation-invariant nature of self-attention [10]. Furthermore, lightweight alternatives like TSMixer demonstrate that MLP-based structures can achieve competitive performance with significantly lower computational requirements [11].

**Uncertainty Quantification**
Accurate forecasting is insufficient for risk-aware decision-making; understanding the uncertainty of predictions is critical. Probabilistic forecasting methods, such as DeepAR, estimate the probability distribution of future values using autoregressive recurrent networks [12]. To provide rigorous statistical guarantees, conformal prediction has been adapted for time series. Recent frameworks offer distribution-free prediction intervals with explicit bounds on coverage gaps, utilizing ensemble predictors to avoid data exchangeability assumptions [13][14][15]. These methods provide the theoretical foundation for calibrating forecasting uncertainty, a key component in our proposed framework.

**Predict-then-Optimize and Decision-Focused Learning**
The integration of machine learning with optimization has evolved from the traditional "Predict-then-Optimize" (PtO) paradigm to "Predict-and-Optimize" (PnO) or Decision-Focused Learning (DFL). Standard PtO approaches suffer from goal mismatch, where prediction errors do not necessarily correlate with decision quality [16]. The Smart Predict-then-Optimize (SPO) framework addresses this by introducing a loss function that measures decision error rather than prediction error [26]. To handle the non-convexity of the SPO loss, the SPO+ loss was derived as a convex surrogate, with subsequent work establishing generalization and risk bounds for this approach [17][21]. DFL extends this by training models end-to-end to optimize downstream decision quality directly [22]. This is often achieved by embedding differentiable optimization layers within neural networks, allowing gradients to flow through the optimization process [23][24]. Recent benchmarks and empirical evaluations have compared various PtO and PnO methods, highlighting the trade-offs between training efficiency and decision quality [19][20]. Specific applications in finance, such as fund allocation, have begun to adopt these frameworks to maximize revenue under constraints [18]. Our work builds upon these foundations by introducing a risk-aware, model-agnostic PnO framework that specifically addresses the uncertainty inherent in time series forecasting for fund allocation.
