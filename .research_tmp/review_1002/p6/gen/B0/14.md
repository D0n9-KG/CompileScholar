**Related Works**

Video Super-Resolution (VSR) aims to reconstruct high-resolution (HR) videos from low-resolution (LR) inputs by exploiting spatial and temporal redundancies. Early approaches primarily relied on recurrent architectures to propagate information across frames. For instance, methods like the Recurrent Structure-Detail Network [4] and EDVR [9] utilize recurrent units or deformable convolutions to handle motion and align features, effectively mitigating error accumulation. To further enhance efficiency and quality, BasicVSR [2] and its successor BasicVSR++ [3] introduced essential components for propagation, alignment, and aggregation, establishing strong baselines for subsequent research. More recently, Transformer-based architectures have gained prominence due to their ability to model long-range dependencies. VRT [7] and RVRT [8] leverage parallel frame prediction and guided deformable attention to balance model size and temporal modeling capability. Additionally, VST [1] adapts Transformers for VSR by incorporating spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention to exploit data locality. Other methods, such as MuCAN [11] and TGAN [5], focus on multi-correspondence aggregation and hierarchical temporal group attention, respectively, to better handle complex motions and diverse frame rates.

Despite these advancements, most existing VSR methods are trained on synthetic degradations, limiting their generalizability to real-world scenarios. Real-world VSR faces significant challenges due to unknown and complex degradations, such as noise, blur, and compression artifacts. Recent studies have highlighted the difficulty in balancing detail synthesis and artifact suppression. RealBasicVSR [13] introduces an image pre-cleaning stage to reduce noise before propagation, addressing the issue where severe in-the-wild degradations are exaggerated through long-term propagation. Similarly, FastRealVSR [14] proposes a Hidden State Attention module to mitigate artifacts by replacing hidden states with cleaner counterparts derived from various filters. These works underscore the critical need for robustness against complex, real-world degradations, which traditional synthetic-data-driven models often fail to address effectively.

To overcome the limitations of attention-based models in terms of computational efficiency and to enhance robustness, recent research has explored State Space Models (SSMs). Mamba [17] introduces selective state spaces that allow for content-based reasoning, offering linear-time complexity for sequence modeling. This architecture has been extended to multi-dimensional data through Mamba-ND [18] and applied to vision tasks via Vision Mamba [19], which demonstrates that bidirectional SSMs can serve as efficient alternatives to self-attention for visual representation learning. These developments suggest that SSMs, particularly with selective mechanisms, can provide a computationally affordable yet powerful framework for modeling spatio-temporal coherence in video tasks, potentially reducing the randomness and artifacts associated with diffusion-based or purely attention-based approaches.

Furthermore, self-supervised learning techniques have emerged as a promising strategy to extract robust features without relying on paired HR-LR data. Contrastive learning frameworks, such as SimCLR [21], MoCo [22], and SwAV [20], have shown that unsupervised representations can capture semantic and structural information effectively. By leveraging contrastive objectives, models can learn degradation-insensitive features that remain consistent across different augmentations or views. Integrating such self-supervised mechanisms into VSR pipelines can help stabilize training and reduce the introduction of complex artifacts, particularly when combined with guidance from HR features. This approach aligns with the goal of developing noise-robust VSR frameworks that maintain content consistency while enhancing perceptual quality.
