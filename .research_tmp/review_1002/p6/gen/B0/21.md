**Related Works**

Neural rendering techniques, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, have revolutionized 3D scene reconstruction from 2D images. These methods optimize scene representations by minimizing photometric discrepancies across multiple input views [6][7]. However, reconstruction quality is inherently bounded by the density and coverage of the input views. In complex or dynamic scenes, unseen angles often lead to under-constrained geometry and appearance, resulting in artifacts [9]. To address this, recent works have explored the integration of generative priors to regularize the optimization process. For instance, DiffusioNeRF utilizes a denoising diffusion model to predict gradients of the log-likelihood for RGBD patches, effectively regularizing scene geometry and color when trained on sparse views [9]. Similarly, 3DGS-Enhancer employs view-consistent 2D diffusion priors to enhance unbounded 3D Gaussian Splatting, improving fidelity in regions with limited supervision [10]. While these methods leverage diffusion models as implicit world priors, they often require significant computational overhead or are tailored to specific scene types, such as autonomous driving environments where sensor modeling is critical [6][7].

Video Frame Interpolation (VFI) offers a complementary perspective by synthesizing intermediate frames between existing ones, thereby increasing the temporal density of the input data. VFI has evolved from classical motion compensation to deep learning-based approaches, including flow-based, GAN-based, and Transformer-based architectures [1]. Early deep learning methods, such as Depth-Aware VFI, explicitly handle occlusions by leveraging depth information to guide flow projection, ensuring that closer objects are sampled preferentially over farther ones [2]. More recent advancements focus on efficiency and robustness. RIFE (Real-time Intermediate Flow Estimation) introduces a neural network for end-to-end intermediate flow estimation, achieving state-of-the-art performance with significantly higher speed compared to prior methods like SuperSlomo and DAIN [3]. RIFE’s ability to support arbitrary-timestep interpolation via temporal encoding makes it particularly suitable for generating diverse view augmentations [3]. Furthermore, the Many-to-Many (M2M) splatting framework addresses the inefficiency of multi-frame interpolation by estimating bidirectional flows once and fusing overlapping pixels, providing robustness to holes and artifacts [4]. Recent works have also explored the separation of motion and appearance information; for example, a novel module using inter-frame attention explicitly extracts these components within a hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture, achieving high efficiency and state-of-the-art results for both fixed and arbitrary timesteps [5].

The intersection of VFI and neural rendering presents a promising avenue for data augmentation. By treating VFI as a means to generate additional training views, one can implicitly model camera movement and real-world 3D geometry. This approach enriches the photo supervision available to neural rendering algorithms, effectively acting as an implicit world prior. Unlike diffusion-based regularization which may struggle with precise geometric consistency in dynamic scenes [9][10], VFI-based augmentation leverages the temporal coherence of video data to provide physically plausible intermediate views. This is particularly beneficial in scenarios where certain angles of objects are never seen in the original sparse captures. Our proposed method, PS4PRO, builds upon these foundations by designing a lightweight yet high-quality VFI model trained on diverse video datasets. By integrating this model into the neural rendering pipeline, we aim to overcome the limitations of sparse input views, enhancing reconstruction performance in both static and dynamic environments. This strategy aligns with broader trends in leveraging video diffusion models for novel view synthesis, such as ViewCrafter, which uses video diffusion priors to generate high-fidelity novel views from sparse inputs [11]. However, our approach specifically targets the efficiency and geometric fidelity required for optimizing explicit 3D representations, offering a distinct advantage in terms of computational cost and integration with existing rendering frameworks.
