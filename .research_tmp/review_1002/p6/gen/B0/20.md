**Related Works**

The reconstruction of images from compressive measurements, including single-pixel imaging (SPI), has traditionally relied on iterative optimization algorithms. Early methods such as Iterative Hard Thresholding (IHT) [2] and the Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (FISTA) [3] established foundational approaches for sparse signal recovery. To handle non-convex regularizers and improve convergence properties, Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) [4] and Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) [27] have been widely adopted. These algorithms decompose the inverse problem into subproblems, where the regularization step is often approximated by a proximal operator. While effective, these classical methods can be computationally intensive and may struggle with complex noise distributions without explicit modeling.

To leverage the power of deep learning, two primary paradigms have emerged: Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods and Deep Unrolling networks. PnP algorithms integrate off-the-shelf denoisers into iterative optimization loops, effectively using the denoiser as an implicit regularizer. The Denoising-based Approximate Message Passing (D-AMP) framework [6] demonstrated that high-performance denoisers could significantly enhance compressed sensing recovery speed and accuracy. More recently, Regularization by Denoising (RED) [28] provided a theoretical framework for using denoisers to define explicit regularization functionals, offering a more principled alternative to standard PnP. However, PnP methods often suffer from slow convergence and lack convergence guarantees unless the denoiser is specifically designed to approximate a proximal operator, as explored in works proposing gradient step denoisers [7] and proximal denoisers [8]. Furthermore, learned proximal networks (LPNs) have been introduced to ensure that the learned operator corresponds to a valid non-convex regularizer, thereby improving the interpretability and convergence of the iterative scheme [9].

In contrast, Deep Unrolling networks transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks. This approach typically offers faster inference and higher reconstruction accuracy compared to PnP methods. Early non-iterative approaches like ReconNet [11] paved the way for optimization-inspired architectures. Subsequent works have unrolled various algorithms, including ISTA [15], ADMM [16], and AMP [17], into deep networks. These unrolling networks, such as ADMM-CSNet [16] and AMP-Net [17], have achieved state-of-the-art performance in compressive sensing. However, a significant limitation of many existing unrolling networks is their dependence on specific sampling matrices or compression ratios (CRs). Retraining or fine-tuning is often required when the CR changes, limiting their flexibility.

To address the issue of varying sampling matrices, several studies have proposed controllable unrolling frameworks. COAST [19] introduced a strategy to handle arbitrary sampling matrices with a single model by promoting training diversity. Similarly, Memory-Augmented Deep Unfolding Networks (MADUN) [18] and Optimization-inspired Cross-attention Transformer (OCT) [22] have focused on preserving information across stages to improve performance and interpretability. Other architectures, such as CSformer [21] and Dual-Scale Transformer for SPI (HATNet) [26], have integrated attention mechanisms to capture global dependencies, particularly beneficial for large-scale SPI. Despite these advancements, most unrolling networks still treat the denoising or regularization module as a black box, lacking a direct link to an explicit regularizer.

Our work bridges the gap between PnP and unrolling approaches by proposing a proximal unrolling framework. Unlike standard unrolling networks that may not guarantee the learned module approximates a proximal operator, we design a Deep Image Restorer (DIR) specifically to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer. This is achieved through a general proximal trajectory (PT) loss function, inspired by the principles of learned proximal networks [9] and stochastic restoration priors [10]. By unrolling HQS and ADMM with this proximal-aware DIR, our method retains the speed and accuracy of unrolling networks while achieving the flexibility of PnP algorithms to handle varying compression ratios without retraining. This approach ensures that the learned network corresponds to a valid optimization process, combining the interpretability of optimization-inspired designs with the robustness of deep learning.
