**Related Works**

The challenge of summarizing long-form narratives, such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been a persistent focus in natural language processing due to the complexity of plotlines and character interactions. Early approaches to narrative summarization often relied on extractive methods or specific structural analyses. For instance, research on novel chapter summarization highlighted the difficulty of aligning reference summaries with chapter text, introducing metrics to handle extreme paraphrasing [1]. In the domain of movie scripts, graph-based scene extraction was proposed to identify key narrative elements [2]. More recently, the concept of scene saliency has been formalized to address the issue that only a fraction of scenes in a script are critical for understanding the overall narrative. By identifying salient scenes before generation, models can produce summaries that more accurately reflect the movie’s information content compared to processing the entire script [3].

A significant portion of the literature addresses the fundamental limitation of Transformer architectures: the quadratic complexity of self-attention with respect to sequence length. To process long documents, several efficient attention mechanisms have been developed. BigBird introduced a sparse attention mechanism that reduces dependency to linear, enabling the handling of sequences up to eight times longer than previous standards [4]. Similarly, Longformer combined local windowed attention with global attention to process documents of thousands of tokens, with its encoder-decoder variant (LED) proving effective for long-document summarization [5]. Other approaches, such as Reformer, utilized locality-sensitive hashing to achieve $O(L \log L)$ complexity, while Linformer approximated self-attention with low-rank matrices to reduce complexity to $O(n)$ [6][8]. LongT5 further optimized text-to-text transformers for long sequences [7]. Beyond architectural changes, Position Interpolation (PI) has been shown to extend the context window of RoPE-based models like LLaMA to 32,768 tokens with minimal fine-tuning, preserving quality on long-context tasks [9].

Despite these advancements, simply extending the context window does not guarantee effective information utilization. Studies have revealed that language models often suffer from "lost in the middle" phenomena, where performance degrades significantly when relevant information is located in the center of long inputs [10]. To mitigate this, sliding-window approaches like SLED partition inputs into overlapping chunks, encoding them with short-text models and fusing information in the decoder, offering a viable strategy for long-text understanding without expensive pretraining [11]. Unlimiformer extends this by offloading cross-attention to a k-nearest-neighbor index, allowing for the processing of inputs up to 500k tokens [12]. For training efficiency, CachED enables end-to-end training of encoder-decoder models on entire documents using gradient caching, avoiding the mismatch between truncated training and full-length testing [13].

In the era of Large Language Models (LLMs), multi-agent frameworks have emerged as a robust solution for long-context tasks. Chain-of-Agents (CoA) leverages multi-agent collaboration to handle long contexts by assigning short segments to worker agents who sequentially communicate, followed by a manager agent that synthesizes the final output [14]. This approach mitigates the focus issues inherent in single-model long-context processing. Specifically for book-length summarization, recent studies have explored hierarchical merging of chunk-level summaries versus incremental updating of a running summary, identifying common coherence errors in LLM-generated summaries [15]. Furthermore, evaluation methodologies are evolving to capture factual accuracy, with frameworks like NarrativeFactScore using Character Knowledge Graphs to assess and refine summaries of long narratives [16]. These developments underscore the shift from purely architectural efficiency to structured, agent-based pipelines for high-quality narrative summarization.
