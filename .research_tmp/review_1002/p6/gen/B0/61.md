**Related Works**

**Fashion Outfit Recommendation and Generation**
Fashion recommendation has evolved from simple item retrieval to complex outfit composition, requiring models to balance compatibility and personalization [3]. Early approaches focused on complementary item recommendation, utilizing visual cues to learn distributions of co-occurring items [13] or embedding items into latent spaces to model compatibility families [12]. Subsequent works addressed the dual requirements of compatibility and user preference by unifying these tasks within hierarchical graph structures [1] or employing learnable anchors to capture personalized coordination preferences [2][5]. Industrial-scale applications, such as the Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model, have demonstrated the efficacy of Transformer architectures in bridging user preferences with outfit generation [6]. More recently, the field has shifted towards generative approaches to overcome the limitations of pre-defined product catalogs. The Generative Outfit Recommendation (GOR) task aims to synthesize new fashion images that are both visually compatible and personalized [10]. While diffusion models have shown promise in this domain [10], existing methods often rely on supervised learning paradigms that may limit diversity and fail to fully align with nuanced user preferences.

**Generative Models and Virtual Try-On**
The advancement of generative AI has significantly impacted fashion technology, particularly in virtual try-on and image synthesis. Early virtual try-on networks, such as VITON, utilized coarse-to-fine strategies to transfer clothing items onto human images [14]. Recent innovations have leveraged latent diffusion models to achieve higher fidelity and detail preservation [8]. For instance, StableVITON learns semantic correspondence in the latent space to maintain clothing details while utilizing the robust generative capabilities of pre-trained models [17]. Other approaches, like Diffusion-based Collaborative Local-Flow Global-Parsing [16] and GP-VTON [15], focus on warping modules and flow-based techniques to enhance realism. Furthermore, ControlNet has enabled the addition of spatial conditioning controls to text-to-image diffusion models, facilitating more precise generation [9]. In the context of personalized generation, methods like JeDi and PatchDPO have explored finetuning-free personalization by learning joint distributions or patch-level preferences, respectively [19][20]. However, these general-purpose techniques have not been specifically adapted to the unique constraints of fashion outfit compatibility and multi-item coherence.

**Preference Optimization and Alignment**
Aligning generative models with human preferences is a critical challenge. Traditional Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is often complex and unstable, requiring the training of a separate reward model [22]. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a simpler alternative by directly optimizing a policy using a classification loss, eliminating the need for explicit reward modeling [22]. This approach has been successfully adapted to diffusion models. Diffusion-DPO re-formulates DPO to account for the likelihood notion in diffusion processes, using the evidence lower bound to derive a differentiable objective [23]. Similarly, D3PO addresses the high GPU memory requirements of diffusion denoising by directly fine-tuning models without a reward model, proving to be more cost-effective [24]. Recent extensions, such as Step-aware Preference Optimization, further refine this process by aligning preferences with denoising performance at each step [25]. While these methods have been applied to text-to-image generation and human image synthesis [21], their application to fashion outfit generation remains underexplored. Our work, FashionDPO, extends these principles to the fashion domain, utilizing a multi-expert feedback module to generate comprehensive preferences for quality, compatibility, and personalization, thereby refining pre-trained fashion models without task-specific reward functions.
