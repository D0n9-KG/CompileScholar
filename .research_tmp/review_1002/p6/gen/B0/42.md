The rapid adoption of cloud-native architectures has necessitated a shift in how organizations manage infrastructure, moving from static provisioning to dynamic, containerized deployments. Kubernetes has emerged as the de facto standard for orchestrating these containerized workloads, enabling scalable and resilient application delivery [1]. As the complexity of these systems grows, the focus of research has increasingly turned toward optimizing the underlying resource management to ensure both performance and efficiency. Early efforts in this domain concentrated on leveraging Kubernetes' native capabilities to achieve adaptive resource allocation, where systems dynamically adjust compute and memory resources based on real-time workload demands [1]. This approach aims to prevent over-provisioning, which leads to wasted capital expenditure, while simultaneously avoiding under-provisioning that could degrade service levels. By integrating feedback loops between the application layer and the orchestration layer, these adaptive mechanisms strive to maintain an optimal balance between resource utilization and operational stability [1].

Building upon the foundation of single-cluster optimization, subsequent research has expanded the scope to include more complex, multi-agent frameworks for resource allocation in broader cloud computing environments. Hierarchical multi-agent optimization represents a significant advancement in this area, addressing the challenges of coordinating resources across distributed systems where multiple stakeholders or subsystems interact [2]. In such architectures, agents operate at different levels of the hierarchy, with higher-level agents making strategic decisions regarding resource distribution while lower-level agents handle tactical, real-time adjustments [2]. This hierarchical structure allows for more granular control over resource allocation, enabling the system to respond to varying workload patterns with greater precision. The use of multi-agent systems introduces a level of autonomy and adaptability that is particularly suited to the dynamic nature of cloud environments, where resource availability and demand can fluctuate rapidly [2]. These methods have demonstrated improved efficiency in resource utilization by minimizing conflicts and optimizing the overall allocation strategy across the cloud infrastructure [2].

Despite these advancements in compute and memory resource optimization, the financial implications of network usage in Kubernetes clusters remain a relatively underexplored area. The emergence of FinOps, a discipline that combines financial management with cloud operations, highlights the critical need for organizations to understand the total cost of ownership of their cloud-native applications. While prior work has extensively covered system-level strategies for improving resource efficiency, the specific cost drivers associated with network traffic, data transfer, and inter-service communication within Kubernetes environments have not received comparable attention. Network costs can constitute a significant portion of cloud expenditures, particularly for applications with high data throughput or those deployed across multiple regions or availability zones. Understanding these costs requires a methodology that goes beyond simple resource monitoring, incorporating detailed measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modelling to provide actionable insights. By addressing this gap, recent studies aim to equip organizations with the tools necessary to evaluate the potential cost benefits of different deployment strategies on various cloud providers. This focus on network cost optimization is essential for the maturation of FinOps practices, ensuring that financial management is integrated into the operational aspects of managing cloud-native environments. The integration of these financial considerations with existing resource optimization techniques, such as those described in adaptive Kubernetes management [1] and hierarchical multi-agent systems [2], represents a promising direction for future research in cloud cost efficiency.
