**Related Works**

**Protein Structure Prediction and Conformational Sampling**
The accurate prediction of protein structures has been revolutionized by deep learning, with AlphaFold achieving high accuracy in single-state structure prediction [1]. However, biological function often depends on dynamic structural ensembles rather than static conformations. Traditional methods for studying these dynamics, such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, are computationally expensive and suffer from rare event sampling issues, leading to inadequate exploration of conformational space [5][7]. To address these limitations, recent research has shifted toward generative modeling approaches that learn distributions over protein structures. EigenFold introduced a diffusion-based framework that models protein structure as a system of harmonic oscillators, enabling the sampling of conformational ensembles and capturing model uncertainty [4]. Similarly, Str2Str proposed a score-based framework for zero-shot conformation sampling that leverages general crystal structures without relying on target-specific simulation data, thereby improving generalizability [5]. Other approaches, such as Structure Language Models (SLM), encode protein structures into latent spaces for efficient conditional language modeling, offering a more interpretable exploration of ensemble modes compared to 3D geometric diffusion processes [9]. Furthermore, flow matching techniques have been applied to repurpose single-state predictors like AlphaFold and ESMFold, allowing for the generation of diverse protein ensembles that capture conformational flexibility and higher-order observables [8].

**Integrating Physical Principles into Generative Models**
While data-driven generative models offer efficiency, they often lack the physical rigor required to ensure the plausibility of generated structures. Standard energy-based objectives can lead to intractable optimization problems when integrating physical supervision. Early efforts to bridge this gap include Boltzmann Generators, which combine deep learning with statistical mechanics to generate unbiased equilibrium samples of many-body systems, including proteins, by learning coordinate transformations of configurational distributions [2]. More recently, ConfDiff proposed a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model that incorporates a force-guided network with data-based score models. This approach aims to overcome the limitations of existing score-based methods that fail to properly incorporate physical prior knowledge, thereby reducing deviations from the equilibrium distribution [7]. Additionally, Distributional Graphormer (DiG) employs deep neural networks to transform simple distributions towards equilibrium distributions, inspired by thermodynamic annealing, to predict the equilibrium distribution of molecular systems [6]. These methods highlight the growing trend of using physical feedback to guide generative processes, ensuring that sampled conformations are not only diverse but also physically consistent.

**Alignment and Optimization of Generative Models**
The challenge of aligning generative models with specific objectives, such as physical plausibility or human preferences, has been addressed in various domains. In natural language processing, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has been used to fine-tune models to optimize for human preferences, as demonstrated in summarization tasks [11]. This concept has been extended to diffusion models, where Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO) treats denoising as a multi-step decision-making problem, enabling the optimization of models for downstream objectives like aesthetic quality or image compressibility [12]. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) further simplifies this process by allowing the extraction of optimal policies in closed form, eliminating the need for complex reinforcement learning procedures [13]. In the context of molecular dynamics, generative models have also been used to learn surrogate models of MD trajectories, enabling tasks such as forward simulation and transition path sampling [10]. Our work, Energy-based Alignment (EBA), builds upon these advancements by specifically aligning generative models with feedback from physical models, such as the Rosetta all-atom energy function [14], to calibrate conformational states based on energy differences. This approach aims to enhance the physical plausibility of generated protein ensembles, addressing the gap between data-driven efficiency and physical accuracy.
