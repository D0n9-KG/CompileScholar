**Related Works**

The detection and characterization of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) in multiple sclerosis (MS) have emerged as a critical area of research due to their prognostic value. Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) provides the necessary contrast to distinguish PRLs from non-rim lesions, which are often associated with chronic active disease and neurodegeneration [5]. However, the clinical utility of QSM-based PRL detection is hindered by significant class imbalance, as PRLs are rare compared to other lesion types. Addressing this imbalance is essential for developing robust classifiers that can monitor longitudinal changes in MS patients [5].

To mitigate data scarcity and class imbalance in medical imaging, synthetic data generation has become a prominent strategy. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been foundational in this domain, originally proposed as a framework for estimating generative models through an adversarial process between a generator and a discriminator [8]. Conditional GANs (cGANs) extend this framework by conditioning the generation process on specific labels, allowing for the targeted synthesis of data with desired characteristics [10]. In the context of neuroimaging, synthetic data has been shown to enhance the generalizability of learning-based models [1]. Specifically for MS, previous work has utilized encoder-decoder architectures, such as U-NETs, to synthesize lesions on MRI images to augment training sets and improve supervised detection algorithms [7]. More recently, SinGAN-Seg has explored synthetic training data generation specifically for medical image segmentation tasks, further validating the efficacy of generative approaches in overcoming data limitations [4].

Beyond simple synthesis, addressing imbalanced data often involves sophisticated oversampling techniques. DeepSMOTE, for instance, fuses deep learning with SMOTE to generate high-quality artificial images for minority classes, avoiding the mode collapse issues often associated with standard GAN-based oversampling [6]. While these methods focus on instance resampling, the quality of the training labels remains a critical factor. In clinical settings, label noise is inevitable due to the subjective nature of expert annotation, particularly for ambiguous cases like PRLs. Robust Conditional GANs (RCGAN) have been proposed to handle noisy labels by corrupting the label of generated samples before feeding them to the discriminator, thereby forcing the generator to produce samples with clean labels [11]. This approach demonstrates that generative models can be made robust to label noise, which is crucial when training on datasets where rim lesion boundaries may be ambiguous.

The correction of noisy predictions and labels has also been addressed through post-processing and latent space modeling. Recent methods propose correcting noisy multilabel predictions by modeling label noise as stochastic shifts in the latent space, allowing for robust learning even when labels are corrupted [12]. Similarly, Noisy Prediction Calibration (NPC) utilizes generative models to estimate a transition matrix that corrects noisy predictions from pre-trained classifiers, offering a post-processing solution that does not require retraining the classifier [13]. These techniques highlight the growing importance of integrating noise-robustness into the training pipeline, particularly for rare disease biomarkers where annotation consistency is challenging.

Transfer learning also plays a role in enhancing model performance in MRI analysis. Systematic reviews indicate that transfer learning is beneficial for re-utilizing models trained on related tasks to improve generalization in new domains, such as brain tumor segmentation or dementia classification [3]. However, theoretical limits suggest that the success of transfer learning depends heavily on the dependence between transferred information and the target problem, implying that domain-specific synthetic data generation, as proposed in this work, may be necessary to bridge gaps that generic transfer learning cannot address [2]. By combining synthetic PRL generation with denoising approaches for ambiguous labels, our work aims to overcome both the scarcity and the noise inherent in PRL datasets, thereby improving the clinical interpretability and detection accuracy of these critical biomarkers.
