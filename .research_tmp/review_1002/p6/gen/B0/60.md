**Related Works**

The calibration of machine learning models has been extensively studied to ensure that predicted probabilities align with true likelihoods. Early approaches focused on post-hoc adjustments for binary classifiers, such as Platt scaling and isotonic regression, which map raw scores to calibrated probabilities [2][3]. More recently, research has highlighted that modern neural networks often exhibit miscalibration, with architecture playing a significant role in determining calibration properties [4]. To improve upon simple parametric methods, Beta calibration was proposed as a well-founded alternative to logistic calibration for binary classifiers [5]. In the context of ranking systems, where absolute probability accuracy is crucial for downstream tasks like bidding, several specialized calibration techniques have emerged. For instance, Gaussian and Gamma calibration methods were introduced to estimate calibrated preference probabilities in personalized ranking without affecting recommendation performance [6]. Furthermore, field-aware calibration methods have been developed to address miscalibration across specific feature subsets, such as user or item IDs, which is critical for reliable probabilistic predictions in online advertising [9]. These methods often employ neural networks to learn calibration functions that leverage field-specific information [9].

To handle the complexities of industrial-scale ranking, recent works have explored more sophisticated calibration frameworks. MBCT (Multiple Boosting Calibration Trees) addresses the limitations of traditional binning methods by using feature-aware binning to provide individual uncertainty calibration, which is particularly useful for order-sensitive applications [1]. In e-commerce advertising, where data sparsity across multiple fields poses a challenge, Deep Ensemble Shape Calibration (DESC) decomposes multi-field calibration to improve data utilization and interpretability [12]. Similarly, AdaCalib proposes a doubly-adaptive approach that learns isotonic function families guided by posterior statistics, ensuring that the calibration is appropriate for specific field values [10]. Confidence-aware multi-field calibration has also been introduced to adaptively adjust calibration intensity based on sample confidence, thereby mitigating bias amplification caused by limited data for certain field values [7]. These approaches demonstrate the importance of tailoring calibration to the specific structural and data distributional characteristics of the ranking task.

Another significant line of research focuses on the joint optimization of ranking and calibration, rather than treating calibration as a purely post-hoc step. Multi-objective methods that combine pointwise losses for calibration with ranking losses for order preservation have become standard in calibrated ranking [11]. However, these methods often face conflicts between the two objectives. To address this, JRC (Joint Optimization of Ranking and Calibration) proposes a contextualized hybrid model that constrains predicted probabilities to be a function of logit subtraction, consolidating the interpretation of logits [14]. Similarly, RCR (Regression Compatible Ranking) argues that ranking and regression objectives are not necessarily compatible and proposes a framework where the two components are mutually aligned to improve the Pareto frontier [15]. In the domain of CTR prediction, calibration-compatible listwise distillation has been used to transfer ranking ability from teacher models to student models while preserving calibration [16]. Additionally, Bayesian approaches have been leveraged for the joint optimization of ranking and calibration to handle binary preferences more effectively [17].

Despite these advancements, many existing calibration methods rely on predefined transformation functions, such as piece-wise linear or isotonic functions, which may lack the expressiveness required for complex scenarios. For example, LiRank at LinkedIn details various calibration techniques used in production but does not specifically address the flexibility limitations of fixed-form calibrators [8]. Our work builds upon these foundations by proposing an Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) as a calibrator. Unlike fixed-form functions, UMNN can learn arbitrary monotonic functions, offering greater flexibility while preserving the relative order of predictions. We further introduce a Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) to optimize this highly flexible network, ensuring that the calibration process does not excessively distort the original predictions. This approach aims to bridge the gap between the expressiveness of neural calibrators and the stability required for large-scale online ranking systems.
