The proliferation of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly accelerated research and development, yet the management and retrieval of these assets remain challenging. Platforms like Hugging Face have become central hubs for sharing models and datasets, but they primarily rely on unstructured or semi-structured metadata, which limits the ability to perform complex queries and advanced analyses [3]. To address these limitations, recent research has increasingly turned to Knowledge Graphs (KGs) as a means to impose structured representations on ML ecosystems. By capturing explicit relationships between entities, KGs enable more sophisticated information retrieval (IR) tasks, such as tracing the evolution of models and recommending relevant resources based on semantic connections rather than simple keyword matching.

One prominent application of KGs in this domain is method and library recommendation. Early efforts focused on enhancing recommendation systems by incorporating domain-specific knowledge. For instance, DEKR introduced a description-enhanced knowledge graph specifically designed for machine learning method recommendation, aiming to bridge the gap between user queries and available algorithms [1]. Similarly, subsequent work has explored task-oriented approaches, where KGs are utilized to recommend specific ML or deep learning libraries based on the functional requirements of a given task [2]. These studies demonstrate that structured knowledge can significantly improve the precision and relevance of recommendations in the ML tooling space. However, these approaches often focus on the selection of libraries or methods in isolation, rather than managing the broader ecosystem of models, datasets, and their interdependencies.

Beyond method recommendation, the integration of KGs with code repositories and academic artifacts has also been explored. The paper2repo framework, for example, addresses the challenge of recommending relevant GitHub repositories for academic papers by mapping embeddings of papers and repositories into a shared space [3]. This cross-platform recommendation system utilizes graph convolutional networks to learn similarities without relying on human labeling, highlighting the potential of graph-based methods to link disparate information sources. While paper2repo focuses on the link between academic literature and code, it does not explicitly model the internal structure of ML resources such as model architectures or dataset provenance. In a related vein, GRETA proposed a graph-based approach for tag assignment for GitHub repositories, demonstrating that graph structures can be leveraged to automatically categorize and organize code assets [4]. These works underscore the utility of graph-based techniques in organizing and retrieving software artifacts, but they generally do not address the specific semantic nuances of ML resources like model weights, training data, and inference pipelines.

Despite these advancements, there remains a gap in large-scale, domain-specific knowledge graphs dedicated to the open-source ML community. Existing platforms often lack the explicit structured representations necessary to support advanced IR tasks such as tracing model lineage or performing fine-grained resource classification. The construction of HuggingKG addresses this void by building a comprehensive graph from the Hugging Face community, capturing both domain-specific relations and rich textual attributes. This structured representation serves as the foundation for HuggingBench, a multi-task benchmark designed to evaluate IR capabilities in this specific context. By providing a large-scale, publicly available resource, this work aims to advance research in open-source resource sharing and management, offering a robust infrastructure for future studies in ML resource recommendation, classification, and evolution tracing.
