**Related Works**

**Graph Neural Networks for Collaborative Filtering**
Modern recommender systems have increasingly shifted from matrix factorization to deep learning architectures, particularly Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), to capture complex user-item interactions. Early approaches like Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) replaced inner products with neural networks to model interactions [4]. Subsequently, GNNs were introduced to explicitly encode collaborative signals by propagating embeddings on the user-item bipartite graph. Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) demonstrated that high-order connectivity significantly enhances embedding quality [7]. To address the diversity of user intents, Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF) modeled distinct intent distributions to refine interaction graphs [8]. More recently, LightGCN simplified the GCN architecture by removing feature transformation and nonlinear activation, focusing solely on neighborhood aggregation, which proved more effective for collaborative filtering [9]. These advancements highlight the critical role of graph structure in learning vectorized embeddings for user-item matching [17].

**Representation Binarization and Quantization**
To enable efficient online inference and reduce memory overhead, representation binarization has emerged as a promising technique. This approach embeds latent features into compact binary sequences, facilitating fast bitwise operations. Early work in hashing, such as HashNet, addressed the ill-posed gradient problem in learning binary codes by using a continuation method [11]. In the context of recommendation, Discrete Collaborative Filtering and related methods explored discrete representations for fast ranking [12][13]. CIGAR proposed a candidate generation framework using preference-preserving binary embeddings to accelerate Top-N recommendation [15]. Specifically for graph-based recommenders, BiGeaR introduced multi-faceted quantization reinforcement to mitigate information loss during the binarization of graph representations [21]. Other studies have explored binary graph convolutional networks [19] and general strategies for binarizing GNNs to reduce model size and energy consumption [20]. However, existing approaches often focus primarily on numerical quantization, neglecting the significant information loss that leads to performance degradation [21].

**Knowledge Distillation for Graphs**
Knowledge distillation (KD) is a powerful paradigm for transferring knowledge from a larger teacher model to a smaller student model, which is particularly relevant for mitigating the performance drop caused by binarization. The foundational concept of distilling knowledge from ensembles into single models was established by Hinton et al. [22]. In the domain of GNNs, recent surveys have categorized KD methods based on what, who, and how to distill [23]. Early graph-specific KD methods, such as Distilling Knowledge from Graph Convolutional Networks, proposed local structure preserving modules to transfer topological semantics [24]. Subsequent works have explored preserving global topology through contrastive learning [25] and distilling holistic knowledge that integrates individual and relational information [26]. Other approaches have focused on free-direction knowledge transfer [29] and fine-grained learning behavior-oriented distillation [28]. While these methods effectively compress GNNs, their application to the specific challenge of binarized graph representations for collaborative filtering, particularly through the lens of pseudo-positive supervision and embedding sample synthesis, remains an area for further exploration. Our work, BiGeaR++, builds upon these insights by integrating fine-grained inference distillation and embedding sample synthesis to enhance binarized graph representations.
