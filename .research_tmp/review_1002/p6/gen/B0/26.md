**Related Works**

The calibration of heterogeneous sensor pairs is a prerequisite for robust perception in autonomous driving. Early approaches to radar-camera integration focused on manual or target-based methods, such as visualizing millimeter-wave radar data on image sequences [1] and fusing radar and vision for object detection [2]. However, these methods often require dedicated calibration targets or significant human intervention, limiting their applicability for online, automatic calibration during system operation.

In the domain of LiDAR-camera calibration, significant progress has been made in developing targetless, automatic methods. A comprehensive survey of these techniques highlights the shift from classical geometric approaches to data-driven solutions [3]. Early automatic methods, such as maximizing mutual information for extrinsic calibration [4], laid the groundwork for subsequent deep learning-based approaches. RegNet was among the first to employ a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) to infer 6-DOF extrinsic calibration between LiDAR and camera, casting feature extraction, matching, and regression into a single real-time capable network [6]. Following this, CalibNet introduced a self-supervised deep network using 3D Spatial Transformer Networks to estimate rigid body transformations without direct supervision on calibration parameters [7]. Further advancements include CalibRCNN, which incorporates recurrent convolutional neural networks and geometric constraints to improve calibration accuracy [8], and LCCNet, which utilizes cost volume networks inspired by PWC-Net for feature matching, achieving high precision in translation and rotation errors [9]. These LiDAR-camera methods have established state-of-the-art benchmarks, but they generally rely on the dense point clouds provided by LiDARs, which are not available in radar-camera systems due to the inherent sparsity of radar data.

Radar-camera calibration presents unique challenges due to the significant data sparsity and measurement uncertainty in radar height data. Traditional motion-based calibration methods for multimodal sensor arrays [5] and online multi-sensor calibration based on moving object tracking [10] have been explored, but they often struggle with the specific noise characteristics of radar. Targetless rotational auto-calibration of radar and camera has been proposed using coarse and fine CNNs with a boosting-inspired training algorithm to handle the sparsity of traffic radars [11]. Additionally, continuous-time approaches for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration have been developed, utilizing radar velocity measurements to avoid the need for specialized retroreflectors [12]. More recently, spatiotemporal calibration of 3D millimetre-wavelength radar-camera pairs has been addressed through targetless algorithms that leverage ego-velocity measurements to ensure reliable long-term operation [13].

Despite these advancements, existing radar-camera calibration methods often treat the problem in isolation or rely on single-perspective feature representations that are sensitive to height uncertainty. Our work distinguishes itself by proposing the first online automatic geometric calibration method that explicitly addresses data sparsity and height uncertainty through a Dual-Perspective representation. By fusing features from both frontal and bird's-eye views via a Selective Fusion Mechanism, our approach mitigates the sensitivity of frontal view height information while leveraging the robustness of bird's-eye view features. Furthermore, the incorporation of a Multi-Modal Cross-Attention Mechanism and a Noise-Resistant Matcher during training enhances the robustness of location correspondence matching, setting a new benchmark for radar-camera auto-calibration.
