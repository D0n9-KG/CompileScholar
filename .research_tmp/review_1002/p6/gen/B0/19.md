**Related Works**

Speech-driven 3D facial animation aims to synthesize realistic and expressive facial motions from audio inputs. Early approaches, such as VOCA, introduced neural networks to factor identity from facial motion, enabling the generation of realistic speaking styles for unseen subjects [10]. Subsequent research has focused on enhancing the realism and expressivity of these animations. For instance, methods have been developed to generate expressive facial animations with controllable emotions, addressing the limitations of earlier models that produced satisfactory lip movements but lacked dramatic emotional flexibility [2]. More recently, the field has shifted towards leveraging deep learning architectures to handle the complexity of human face geometry and the scarcity of 3D audio-visual data.

A significant trend in recent literature is the adoption of pre-trained self-supervised speech models as audio encoders. These models, such as wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT, learn powerful representations from speech audio alone, capturing both acoustic and linguistic features [14][15]. FaceFormer, for example, integrates self-supervised pre-trained speech representations into a Transformer-based autoregressive model to encode long-term audio context, thereby improving lip movement accuracy [3]. Similarly, FaceDiffuser employs the pre-trained HuBERT model to encode audio inputs within a diffusion framework, aiming to generate non-deterministic facial animations that better reflect the inherent variability of human expressions [5]. FaceXHuBERT further utilizes HuBERT to capture personalized and subtle cues, such as identity and emotion, without relying on large lexicons, demonstrating robustness to background noise [6].

Despite the success of these self-supervised encoders, they often struggle with the "averaging effect" caused by phonetically similar syllables. In self-supervised feature spaces, near-homophone syllables, which may have distinct lip shapes, tend to exhibit significant coupling. This coupling leads to over-smoothed or averaged lip motions in the generated animations. To mitigate the regression-to-mean problem inherent in deterministic regression tasks, CodeTalker casts speech-driven facial animation as a code query task in a discrete motion prior space, promoting vividness by reducing cross-modal mapping uncertainty [4]. Similarly, 3DiFACE proposes a lightweight audio-conditioned diffusion model to address the one-to-many relationship between speech and facial expressions, allowing for stochastic generation and motion editing [9].

Beyond lip synchronization, recent works have focused on disentangling different components of facial motion, such as emotion, identity, and speaking style. EMOTE generates 3D talking-head avatars that maintain lip-sync while enabling explicit control over emotion through decoupled losses for speech and emotion [7]. EmoTalk proposes an end-to-end neural network to disentangle emotions from speech content, using an emotion disentangling encoder to generate rich 3D facial expressions [8]. Mimic explores the coupled information between speaking style and semantic content, introducing a disentanglement method to encode arbitrary-subject speaking styles for more realistic synthesis [11]. Additionally, Media2Face utilizes a diffusion model in a generalized expression latent space to accept multi-modality guidances, broadening the scope of expressiveness and style adaptability [16].

While these methods have advanced the state of the art in expressivity and style control, they often rely on the raw features from self-supervised models that may not sufficiently distinguish between phonetically similar but visually distinct syllables. Our work addresses this specific limitation by proposing Wav2Sem, a plug-and-play semantic decorrelation module. By extracting semantic features corresponding to the entire audio sequence, Wav2Sem decorrelates audio encodings within the feature space, thereby alleviating the averaging effect and enhancing the precision and naturalness of facial animations. This approach complements existing architectures by improving the quality of the audio features fed into the animation generation pipeline.
