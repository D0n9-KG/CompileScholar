**Related Works**

**Multi-Token Prediction Objectives**
The standard pre-training objective for large language models (LLMs) is next-token prediction (NTP), where the model predicts the immediate subsequent token [5]. Recently, Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising alternative, wherein models are trained to predict the next $k$ tokens simultaneously using multiple independent prediction heads [5]. This approach has been shown to improve sample efficiency and downstream performance, particularly in generative tasks such as coding, with gains becoming more pronounced in larger model architectures [5]. Prior work has also explored predicting future n-grams for sequence-to-sequence pre-training [3]. Furthermore, research into the internal representations of transformers suggests that hidden states contain sufficient signal to anticipate subsequent tokens, supporting the feasibility of multi-step prediction from a single forward pass [4]. However, while MTP offers significant benefits for large models, prior studies indicate that smaller language models (SLMs) often struggle to effectively leverage this objective, leading to suboptimal performance compared to standard NTP training.

**Curriculum Learning Strategies**
Curriculum learning is a training paradigm that orders data from easy to hard, which can improve model performance without additional computational costs [1][2]. This strategy has been successfully applied across various machine learning tasks to enhance convergence and generalization [2]. In the context of language modeling, we propose applying curriculum learning to the MTP objective. Specifically, we explore two variants: a forward curriculum that gradually increases the complexity of the prediction task from NTP to MTP, and a reverse curriculum that does the opposite. This approach aims to mitigate the difficulties SLMs face when directly optimizing for multi-token predictions, allowing them to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training.

**Self-Speculative Decoding and Inference Acceleration**
A key advantage of MTP is its potential to accelerate inference through self-speculative decoding. Traditional speculative decoding relies on a separate draft model to propose tokens, which are then verified by the target LLM [6]. However, training and maintaining a separate draft model can be costly and complex. To address this, recent works have introduced self-speculative decoding methods that utilize the target model itself for drafting. For instance, LayerSkip enables early exit inference and self-speculative decoding by skipping intermediate layers [7]. Similarly, SWIFT proposes an on-the-fly approach that adaptively selects layers to skip, requiring no additional training or auxiliary models [10]. Kangaroo employs a fixed shallow sub-network as a self-draft model, using an adapter to bridge representation gaps, and introduces an additional early exiting mechanism to optimize drafting steps [8]. Another method, Draft & Verify, uses layer skipping to generate draft tokens quickly, ensuring lossless acceleration without extra memory footprint [9]. Additionally, Medusa augments LLM inference by adding extra decoding heads to predict multiple subsequent tokens in parallel, using a tree-based attention mechanism for verification [12]. KOALA further enhances this by transforming the draft head into a multi-layer architecture with adversarial learning to improve prediction accuracy [11]. Our work connects these inference acceleration techniques with pre-training objectives, demonstrating that curriculum learning for MTP can retain the benefits of self-speculative decoding while improving downstream NTP performance and output quality for SLMs.
