**Related Works**

Long-tailed recognition addresses the challenge of class imbalance in real-world datasets, where a few head classes dominate the data while most tail classes are under-represented [7]. Early approaches focused on data-level strategies, such as oversampling and undersampling, to mitigate the effects of imbalance on convolutional neural networks [1]. Subsequent work introduced algorithmic adjustments, including cost-sensitive learning that jointly optimizes class-dependent costs and network parameters [4], and class-balanced loss functions that re-weight samples based on their effective number to account for diminishing returns in data overlap [2]. A significant paradigm shift occurred with the decoupling of representation learning from classifier training. This approach revealed that data imbalance primarily affects the classifier rather than the feature extractor, allowing for robust representations learned via natural sampling [5]. Further advancements in this direction include the Bilateral-Branch Network (BBN), which separates representation and classifier learning into distinct branches to prevent re-balancing strategies from damaging feature quality [6], and methods employing Stochastic Weight Averaging to enhance the generalization of feature extractors [8]. Additionally, logit adjustment techniques have been proposed to correct the bias towards dominant labels by enforcing larger margins for rare classes [9].

Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful framework for representation learning, initially gaining prominence in self-supervised settings through methods like MoCo, SimCLR, BYOL, and SwAV [14][15][16][22]. Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL) extends this by leveraging label information to pull same-class samples together and push different-class samples apart, achieving state-of-the-art performance on balanced datasets [17]. However, applying SCL to long-tailed data presents unique challenges. Theoretical analyses indicate that standard SCL tends to bias towards high-frequency classes, failing to form the ideal geometric configuration of a regular simplex in the feature space [12]. This bias results in poor uniformity and separability for minority classes [21]. To address these issues, several variants of contrastive learning have been developed for long-tailed recognition. Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) introduces learnable class-wise centers to rebalance optimization and enhance hard example learning [10], a concept further generalized in GPaCo to improve robustness and generalization [11]. Other approaches include Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL), which employs class-averaging and class-complement to correct optimization behavior [12], and Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC), which forces features to converge to uniformly distributed targets on a hypersphere to improve uniformity [21]. Probabilistic Contrastive Learning (ProCo) estimates class distributions in the feature space to sample contrastive pairs effectively, overcoming the limitation of small batch sizes in imbalanced settings [18]. Furthermore, Subclass-balancing Contrastive Learning (SBCL) clusters head classes into subclasses to preserve semantic substructures while enforcing balance [19].

Despite these advancements, the interaction between multi-view training and contrastive learning in long-tailed scenarios remains under-explored. While multi-view training is known to boost performance by reducing variance from data augmentation [23], it does not consistently enhance generalization in contrastive settings as the number of views increases. Recent work has highlighted that the fundamental limitation of contrastive methods in long-tailed tasks lies in their attempt to maximize mutual information between latent features and input data rather than ground-truth labels [13]. Our work builds upon these insights by identifying specific gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction/repulsion gradients in SCL. We propose Aligned Contrastive Learning (ACL) to eliminate these issues, offering a unified solution that addresses both the geometric and optimization challenges inherent in long-tailed contrastive learning.
