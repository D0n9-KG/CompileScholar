**Related Works**

Sequential recommendation has evolved significantly from early recurrent neural network (RNN) approaches to sophisticated Transformer-based architectures. Early works applied RNNs to model session-based interactions, demonstrating that modeling the entire session yields more accurate recommendations than item-to-item methods [11]. Subsequent research introduced hierarchical RNNs to personalize session-based recommendations by transferring latent hidden states across user sessions [6]. The advent of self-attention mechanisms marked a pivotal shift, with SASRec balancing long-term semantics with sparse prediction by identifying relevant items in a user’s history [8]. BERT4Rec further advanced this paradigm by employing bidirectional encoder representations and a Cloze task to overcome the limitations of unidirectional architectures [9]. More recently, attention mechanisms have been refined to address specific data characteristics; for instance, FEARec shifts the perspective to the frequency domain to capture high-frequency information and periodicity that standard self-attention often misses [1]. Other variants, such as STRec, utilize sparse transformers to enhance efficiency, while multi-behavior transformers incorporate diverse user actions into the recommendation process [4][5].

Despite the success of Transformers, their quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length poses significant challenges for long-term and lifelong user behavior modeling. To mitigate this, LinRec proposed a linear attention mechanism that preserves the learning capabilities of dot-product attention while achieving linear complexity [10]. Parallel to these efforts, state-space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising alternative, offering near-linear scalability. Mamba introduced selective SSMs that address the content-based reasoning weaknesses of previous subquadratic models by allowing parameters to be functions of the input [12]. Theoretical connections between Transformers and SSMs have been further elucidated through the State Space Duality framework, which unifies these architectures via structured semiseparable matrices [14]. A comprehensive survey of Mamba-based models highlights their rapid adoption across diverse domains as a cornerstone for efficient foundation models [13].

The application of SSMs to sequential recommendation has gained substantial traction recently. Mamba4Rec was the first to explore selective SSMs for this domain, demonstrating that Mamba blocks can effectively tackle the effectiveness-efficiency dilemma, outperforming both RNN- and attention-based baselines [20]. RecMamba further investigated the capabilities of selective SSMs in lifelong sequential recommendation, showing that Mamba achieves performance comparable to representative models while significantly reducing training duration [15][19]. Other recent works have extended these foundations; EchoMamba4Rec harmonizes bidirectional SSMs with spectral filtering to capture complex patterns and dependencies in user interaction data [16]. Similarly, Bidirectional Gated Mamba has been proposed to enhance sequential recommendation performance [18]. While these models efficiently handle long sequences, they often focus primarily on temporal dynamics or general efficiency.

In contrast, STAR-Rec synergistically combines preference-aware attention and state-space modeling within a sequence-level mixture-of-experts framework. Unlike prior works that treat attention and SSMs as separate alternatives [10][12], our approach theoretically unifies them, leveraging SSMs for temporal state compression and attention for capturing both similar and diverse item relationships. Furthermore, while existing Mamba-based recommenders focus on sequence efficiency [20][15], STAR-Rec incorporates a mixture-of-experts component to adaptively route different behavioral patterns, such as focused category-specific browsing versus diverse exploration, to specialized experts. This design allows STAR-Rec to address the limitations of current models in handling sequence length variations and diverse interaction patterns more effectively.
