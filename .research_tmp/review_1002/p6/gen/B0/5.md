**Related Works**

The evolution of text representation has progressed from static word vectors to contextualized sentence embeddings. Early methods like Word2Vec [1] and GloVe [2] established the foundation for distributed representations, capturing syntactic and semantic relationships. However, these static vectors lack context sensitivity. The introduction of the Transformer architecture [3] enabled the development of deep bidirectional models such as BERT [4], which significantly improved language understanding by conditioning on both left and right contexts. To address the computational inefficiency of using BERT for pairwise similarity tasks, Sentence-BERT (SBERT) [6] introduced siamese network structures to derive independent sentence embeddings, reducing inference time from hours to seconds. Further advancements in embedding quality were achieved through contrastive learning frameworks. SimCSE [7] demonstrated that simple contrastive objectives with dropout as noise could regularize the anisotropic embedding space, while WhitenedCSE [8] and AoE [11] further optimized embedding geometry for semantic textual similarity. More recently, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) has enhanced embedding quality; for instance, methods leveraging synthetic data generated by LLMs have achieved state-of-the-art results on benchmarks like MTEB without extensive labeled data [9].

A significant shift in this domain is the move toward instruction-following text embeddings, where embeddings are dynamically adapted to specific user instructions or task descriptions. INSTRUCTOR [12] pioneered this approach by training a single embedder on a multitask mixture of instructions, allowing it to generate tailored embeddings for diverse downstream tasks without further training. This method demonstrated robustness to instruction variations and superior performance across numerous unseen tasks. Building on this, InBedder [13] proposed a novel perspective that treats instructions as questions about the input text. By fine-tuning language models on abstractive question answering tasks, InBedder generates embeddings based on the expected answers to these instructions, thereby capturing specific textual characteristics more effectively. This "embed-via-answering" strategy has shown improved instruction-following capabilities and interpretability compared to prior methods.

Another line of research focuses on unifying generation and embedding capabilities within a single model. GRIT [10] introduced Generative Representational Instruction Tuning, training LLMs to handle both generative and embedding tasks by distinguishing them through instructions. This unification allows models like GritLM to achieve state-of-the-art performance on both MTEB and generative tasks, offering benefits such as accelerated Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Similarly, Promptriever [14] explores the potential of instruction-tuned retrieval models that can be prompted like language models. By curating an instance-level instruction training set, Promptriever achieves strong performance on standard retrieval tasks while following detailed relevance instructions. It demonstrates increased robustness to lexical choices and the ability to perform hyperparameter search via prompting, highlighting the growing trend of aligning information retrieval techniques with natural language prompting interfaces.

Despite these advancements, existing instruction-following embedding methods often suffer from high computational overhead, as they typically require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction. Our work, GSTransform, addresses this limitation by proposing a lightweight transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed generic embeddings in real time, guided by a small amount of instruction-focused annotation, thereby achieving significant speedups without sacrificing embedding quality.
