Brain tumor segmentation is a critical task in medical imaging, essential for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment planning of gliomas [3]. The heterogeneity of gliomas, characterized by varying intensity profiles across multi-parametric MRI scans, presents significant challenges for automated segmentation [3]. To address these challenges, the BraTS challenge has served as a premier benchmark for evaluating computational algorithms on multi-institutional data [2]. Recent iterations of the challenge, such as BraTS 2021, have expanded the scope to include not only the segmentation of histologically distinct sub-regions but also the classification of molecular characteristics like MGMT promoter methylation status [4]. This dual focus highlights the growing importance of integrating segmentation with other clinical prediction tasks [4].

Early approaches to brain tumor segmentation relied on traditional machine learning methods, which struggled with the complex spatial structures of tumors [3]. The advent of deep learning, particularly the U-Net architecture, revolutionized biomedical image segmentation by leveraging encoder-decoder structures with skip connections [1]. In the context of 3D MRI, semantic segmentation networks have been enhanced with regularization techniques, such as variational auto-encoders, to mitigate the effects of limited training data sizes [5]. A significant milestone was achieved with the Two-Stage Cascaded U-Net, which improved segmentation accuracy by processing images at multiple resolutions [6].

More recently, the nnU-Net framework has emerged as a state-of-the-art baseline for biomedical image segmentation due to its self-configuring nature [10]. Applied to the BraTS 2020 challenge, nnU-Net achieved top performance by incorporating specific modifications such as region-based training and aggressive data augmentation [7]. Subsequent works have further extended nnU-Net for brain tumor segmentation by experimenting with larger networks, group normalization, and axial attention mechanisms in the decoder [8]. These enhancements have consistently yielded improvements in quantitative metrics, solidifying nnU-Net’s position as a dominant architecture in the field [7][8]. Additionally, ensemble methods combining multiple deep learning frameworks, such as nnU-Net and DeepSeg, have demonstrated superior performance on recent BraTS challenges, often outperforming single-model approaches [9].

Beyond convolutional architectures, Transformer-based models have gained traction for their ability to capture long-range dependencies. TransBTS was among the first to exploit Transformers in 3D CNNs for brain tumor segmentation, combining local 3D context with global feature modeling [11]. Similarly, Swin UNETR leveraged Swin Transformers to address the limitations of convolutional kernels in modeling long-range information, achieving competitive results on BraTS datasets [12]. These architectures offer promising alternatives to pure CNNs by better handling the variable sizes and complex shapes of tumors [11][12].

Despite these advancements, current state-of-the-art methods like nnU-Net often require extensive training and underutilize pre-trained weights from large-scale datasets. Transfer learning from natural images, such as ImageNet, has been established as a practical paradigm for medical image analysis [14]. However, applying 2D pre-trained weights to 3D tasks is non-trivial, as it risks losing rich 3D anatomical information [14]. Previous efforts, such as Med3D, have attempted to bridge this gap by co-training heterogeneous 3D networks on diverse medical datasets to extract general 3D features [13]. While Med3D accelerated training convergence compared to training from scratch, it did not fully leverage the power of ImageNet pre-training in a 3D context [13]. Other approaches, like Models Genesis, have used self-supervised learning to create generic 3D models that outperform 2D transfer learning methods [14]. Nevertheless, the specific challenge of efficiently transferring 2D ImageNet weights to 3D medical segmentation while preserving feature relationships remains an area for further exploration, particularly in reducing training epochs and parameter counts for clinical deployment.
