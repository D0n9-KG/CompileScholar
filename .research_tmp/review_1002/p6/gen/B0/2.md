**Related Works**

The integration of nonverbal cues into conversational AI has emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to bridge the gap between text-based interactions and fully immersive human-like communication. Early efforts in this domain focused on recognizing and generating specific nonverbal elements, such as facial expressions and body gestures, often in isolation or within narrow contexts. For instance, [11] explored the recognition of nonverbal communication cues to enhance accessibility, while [12] introduced EmotionCLIP, a pre-training paradigm that extracts visual emotion representations from both verbal and nonverbal communication using uncurated data. These works highlight the importance of nonverbal signals in conveying emotion and intent, yet they typically do not offer a unified framework for generating these cues alongside natural language text.

Recent advancements in multimodal large language models (LLMs) have expanded the scope of nonverbal understanding. Models like Qwen-VL [1] and VideoChat [2] have demonstrated strong capabilities in video understanding, spatiotemporal reasoning, and causal inference by integrating video foundation models with LLMs. Similarly, Video-LLaMA [3] and Unified-IO 2 [4] have pushed the boundaries of autoregressive multimodal modeling, unifying diverse modalities such as vision, language, audio, and action into a shared semantic space. While these models excel at understanding and generating text and visual content, they generally lack the specific focus on the fine-grained, time-aligned generation of nonverbal cues like facial expressions and body language that are essential for natural conversational dynamics.

In the realm of embodied conversational agents, several studies have attempted to incorporate nonverbal behaviors into LLM-driven systems. FurChat [13] deployed an LLM-based system on a Furhat robot to generate dialogue and facial expressions, demonstrating the potential for engaging open and closed-domain conversations. Similarly, [14] proposed augmenting social robots with empathetic non-verbal cues (SAFE: Speech, Action, Facial expression, Emotion) generated by LLMs, showing that context-aware nonverbal responses can lead to more authentic interactions. However, these approaches often rely on prompt engineering or separate modules for nonverbal generation, rather than a unified next-token prediction objective that seamlessly integrates text and nonverbal representations.

The generation of holistic human motion from text or speech has also seen significant progress. HumanTOMATO [18] introduced a framework for text-driven whole-body motion generation, using a hierarchical VQ-VAE to model fine-grained body and hand motions aligned with textual descriptions. [16] addressed the generation of 3D holistic body motions from speech, employing a compositional VQ-VAE for body and hand motions and an autoencoder for face motions. In the context of dyadic interactions, [19] and [20] explored modeling listener responses, treating quantized motion elements as additional language tokens for autoregressive prediction. These works provide foundational techniques for motion generation and nonverbal response modeling, but they are often limited to specific tasks like listener response or speech-driven motion, rather than general conversational generation.

Datasets for multimodal conversation have evolved to support these advancements. Traditional datasets like IEMOCAP [6], CMU-MOSEI [7], and MELD [8] have provided valuable resources for emotion recognition in conversations, offering annotations for audio, visual, and textual modalities. More recently, CHAMPAGNE [9] introduced YTD-18M, a large-scale corpus of video-based dialogues, and [10] presented MultiDialog, a multimodal spoken dialogue corpus with parallel audio-visual recordings. While these datasets are crucial for training models in real-world conversational contexts, they often lack the specific time-aligned annotations for nonverbal cues required for unified multimodal generation. Our work, MARS, builds upon these foundations by introducing VENUS, a large-scale dataset with time-aligned text, facial expressions, and body language, and a unified framework for generating both text and nonverbal cues.
