3D human pose estimation from monocular images or videos remains a challenging task due to the inherent ambiguity of lifting 2D joint coordinates to 3D space [4]. Early approaches often relied on two-stage pipelines, where a ConvNet predicts 2D joints followed by an optimization step to recover 3D pose [2]. While effective, these methods can suffer from error accumulation. Subsequent research shifted towards end-to-end learning, with simple feed-forward networks demonstrating that lifting ground-truth 2D poses can achieve remarkably low error rates [4]. To further refine these estimates, methods like HEMlets introduced intermediate part-centric heatmap triplets to better represent relative depth information, significantly improving performance on benchmarks like Human3.6M [21].

A significant portion of recent literature focuses on modeling the structural dependencies among body joints using graph-based architectures. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have been widely adopted to capture spatial relationships, with variants such as Semantic Graph Convolutional Networks [5] and Modulated Graph Convolutional Networks [6] enhancing the representation of joint interactions. However, standard undirected graphs often fail to explicitly capture the hierarchical and articulated nature of the human skeleton. To address this, Conditional Directed Graph Convolution [11] represents the skeleton as a directed graph, leveraging parent-child relationships to model non-local dependencies more effectively. Similarly, HPGCN utilizes hierarchical poselet-guided structures to refine pose estimation [22]. These methods highlight the importance of structural priors, yet they often struggle with long-range dependencies that extend beyond immediate skeletal neighbors.

The advent of Transformer architectures has provided a powerful mechanism for capturing long-range dependencies, originally introduced in the context of sequence transduction [20]. In the domain of pose estimation, GraFormer adapted the Transformer for graph-oriented tasks, effectively modeling global joint interactions [7]. HDFormer further advanced this by incorporating high-order directed attention to handle overlapping joints and rapid pose changes, achieving state-of-the-art results with reduced computational costs [10]. Other approaches, such as MHFormer, address the multi-hypothesis nature of pose estimation by learning spatio-temporal representations of multiple plausible poses [9]. While these Transformer-based methods excel at global context modeling, they often require deeper networks to capture dependencies between non-linked parts, which can introduce uncorrelated noise and increase model size.

To mitigate the computational burden and improve efficiency, several studies have explored multi-scale and temporal strategies. Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks process graph-structured features across multiple scales to learn both local and global representations [23]. In the temporal domain, Strided Transformer and MixSTE have been proposed to efficiently aggregate long-range information from video sequences, reducing redundancy while maintaining accuracy [18][19]. Additionally, non-local operations have been integrated into various architectures to compute responses as weighted sums of features at all positions, offering a generic solution for long-range dependency capture [14]. More recently, diffusion models like DiffPose have been explored to handle uncertainty and indeterminacy by formulating pose estimation as a reverse diffusion process [15][16].

Despite these advancements, existing methods often treat long-range dependencies as a simple extension of pairwise interactions, neglecting the modulation of joints by broader body part groups. Our work addresses this by proposing a Pyramid Graph Attention (PGA) module within the PGFormer architecture. Unlike previous approaches that increase network depth to learn dependencies, our method utilizes a pyramid structure to capture cross-scale correlations in parallel. This allows for the efficient modeling of long-range dependencies across both individual joints and body part groups, resulting in a lightweight architecture that achieves lower error and smaller model size compared to state-of-the-art methods [10][23].
