## Related Work

**Representation Similarity Metrics**
Quantifying the similarity of internal representations is fundamental to understanding neural network dynamics, with recent advances focusing on robust, affine-invariant metrics that overcome the limitations of traditional linear approaches. Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) and its variants have been widely used to analyze how representations evolve, revealing that generalizing and wider networks tend to converge to more similar representations than memorizing or narrower ones [1]. To address computational and dimensional constraints, Singular Vector Canonical Correlation Analysis (SVCCA) was proposed as a fast, affine-invariant tool for measuring intrinsic dimensionality and probing learning dynamics [2]. However, when the number of data points is smaller than the representation dimension, canonical correlation methods can become unreliable; Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) was introduced to provide a more robust similarity index that reliably identifies correspondences between representations in such high-dimensional settings [3]. In this work, we leverage CKA to perform a data-oblivious analysis of representation dynamics, allowing us to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned models without relying on task-specific fine-tuning data.

**Layer-wise Representation Analysis in LLMs**
A growing body of literature investigates how feature representations evolve across the depth and width of large language models, seeking to characterize the functional roles of different layers. Early studies examined whether wide and deep networks learn the same features, establishing foundational insights into how architectural changes affect representation learning [4]. More recently, researchers have utilized representation dissimilarity measures to uncover internal mechanics, such as activation function asymmetries and generalization properties, while also evaluating how feature variations shift with increased model capacity [6]. In the context of Transformers, CKA has been employed to demonstrate that fine-tuned models exhibit block diagonal similarity patterns, suggesting that later layers contribute marginally to task performance and can potentially be discarded [5]. Furthermore, the concept of "Concept Depth" has been introduced to show that LLMs process simpler concepts in shallow layers and more complex concepts in deeper layers [11], while other investigations have found that intermediate layers often yield more informative data for downstream tasks than final layers, revealing distinct architectural differences in representation evolution [12]. Our work extends this line of inquiry by identifying data-oblivious critical layers through spectral analysis, showing that these layers correspond to significant shifts in the top principal components that encode semantic transitions, thereby providing a model-agnostic framework for understanding layer-specific functionality.

**Locating and Editing Knowledge in LLMs**
Understanding where and how knowledge is stored within LLMs has enabled targeted interventions for model editing and knowledge injection. Research has demonstrated that factual associations in models like GPT are localized in mid-layer feed-forward modules, leading to the development of Rank-One Model Editing (ROME) for direct and specific weight modifications [7]. Conversely, other studies have highlighted the importance of shallow layers for knowledge integration; for instance, the S strategy selectively enhances shallow layers while pruning deep ones to improve knowledge injection efficiency, resulting in models like Llama Slayer-8B [9]. While these approaches typically rely on data-dependent analyses to locate specific knowledge or behaviors, our approach identifies critical layers based on intrinsic representation dynamics prior to fine-tuning. We show that the layers identified through our data-oblivious spectral analysis are the same layers most affected during fine-tuning, offering a complementary perspective to data-driven localization methods by revealing the inherent structural vulnerabilities and strengths of the model architecture.

**Internal States for Truthfulness and Safety**
The internal states of LLMs have been increasingly explored for enhancing model safety and reliability, particularly in detecting falsehoods and defending against adversarial attacks. For example, classifiers trained on hidden layer activations have been shown to detect the truthfulness of statements with higher reliability than methods based solely on sentence probabilities [8]. Similarly, layer-specific editing techniques have been employed to defend against jailbreak attacks by modifying specific layers to mitigate harmful outputs [10]. These studies underscore the utility of manipulating or monitoring specific layers to improve model behavior. Building on this, we apply our identified critical layers to practical safety and adaptation scenarios. We demonstrate that fine-tuning these critical layers leads to greater loss reduction in domain adaptation, while freezing them during fine-tuning significantly reduces backdoor attack success rates, thereby validating the practical importance of the intrinsic critical layers identified through our spectral analysis.
