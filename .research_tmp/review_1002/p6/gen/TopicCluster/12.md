## Related Work

**Bio-inspired and Neuromorphic Downsampling**
Early approaches to event data reduction have drawn inspiration from biological vision systems and neuromorphic computing principles, aiming to mimic the efficient information processing found in natural organisms. For instance, [3] develops a bioinspired filtering algorithm that minimizes data volume while preserving essential information, while [5] applies insect-inspired strategies to perform spatio-temporal downsampling of event-based inputs. Similarly, [6] implements neuromorphic techniques to downsample event camera output, leveraging hardware-efficient processing paradigms. These methods share a common goal of reducing data redundancy through biologically motivated heuristics; however, they often rely on fixed biological priors that may not generalize across diverse scene dynamics. In contrast, our work moves beyond these fixed bio-inspired heuristics by introducing a probabilistic framework that adapts to scene-specific characteristics in an online setting.

**General Event Data Downsampling Methods**
Beyond bio-inspired strategies, several general-purpose methods have been proposed to manage the high bandwidth and storage demands of event cameras. [1] proposes specific spatial and temporal downsampling techniques optimized for event-based visual classification tasks, focusing on structural reduction of the event stream. Complementing this, [2] introduces EvDownsampling, a robust technique designed to reduce the volume of event camera data broadly. While these approaches provide effective baseline reductions, they typically employ static or heuristic-based thresholds that do not account for the varying importance of events within a dynamic scene. Our approach differs by modeling event importance through an event-importance probability density function (ePDF), allowing for a more flexible and adaptive downsampling strategy that can be tailored to specific application requirements.

**Downsampling for Spiking Neural Networks**
The integration of event data with Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) has necessitated specific downsampling strategies to align with the temporal and spatial constraints of neuromorphic hardware. [4] evaluates and compares the performance of various DVS data spatial downscaling methods when used with SNNs, highlighting the sensitivity of these networks to data reduction techniques. This line of work emphasizes the compatibility of downsampling with spiking architectures, often prioritizing hardware efficiency over general-purpose visual task performance. Our work, while compatible with various downstream models, focuses on a purely online, probabilistic estimation of event importance that is agnostic to the specific neural architecture, thereby offering a more generalizable solution for event stream management.

**Impact of Subsampling on CNN Classification**
Recent studies have investigated the effects of aggressive subsampling on conventional deep learning models, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). [7] pushes the boundaries of event subsampling in video classification, demonstrating that event rates can be reduced by an order of magnitude with minimal accuracy loss, while also analyzing the resulting training instability. This research underscores the potential for significant data reduction without compromising performance in classification tasks. However, it primarily focuses on the feasibility of reduction rather than the intelligent selection of which events to retain. Our work builds upon this insight by providing a principled, contour-preserving ePDF that actively prioritizes structurally important events, ensuring that the remaining data retains high utility for a broader range of tasks beyond simple classification.
