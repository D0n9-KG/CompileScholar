## Related Work

**BraTS Challenge Benchmarks and Datasets**
The evaluation of brain tumor segmentation algorithms has been standardized through the Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) challenge, which provides a consistent multimodal MRI benchmark for assessing algorithmic performance [2]. Subsequent studies have systematically reviewed the progression of machine learning techniques within this framework, analyzing the evolution of methods for segmentation, progression assessment, and survival prediction from the early challenges through 2018 [3]. More recently, the scope of the benchmark has expanded to include radiogenomic classification, as exemplified by the RSNA-ASNR-MICCAI BraTS 2021 benchmark, which evaluates algorithms on both segmentation and MGMT promoter methylation status prediction [4]. This paper utilizes these established benchmarks to validate our proposed efficient segmentation framework, ensuring that our results are comparable to the state-of-the-art within the standard BraTS evaluation protocol.

**Winning Solutions and Architectures for BraTS Segmentation**
Historically, top-performing solutions in the BraTS challenges have relied on deep convolutional architectures, often employing complex ensembles or specific regularization techniques to handle limited data. For instance, the BraTS 2018 winner utilized autoencoder regularization to enhance semantic segmentation performance [5], while the 2019 first-place solution introduced a two-stage cascaded U-Net to refine segmentation boundaries [6]. The introduction of the nnU-Net framework marked a significant shift, with its application and BraTS-specific modifications securing first place in the 2020 challenge [7]. This momentum continued in 2021, where extensions to nnU-Net incorporating group normalization and axial attention achieved top results [8], and in 2022, where an ensemble of DeepSeg, nnU-Net, and DeepSCAN frameworks dominated the challenge [9]. While these approaches demonstrate high accuracy, they often require extensive training resources or complex ensembles; our work addresses this by integrating axial-coronal-sagittal convolutions and pre-trained weights directly into the nnU-Net framework to improve efficiency without sacrificing performance.

**Transformer-Based Models for Brain Tumor Segmentation**
Recent advancements have seen the integration of Transformer architectures into brain tumor segmentation to capture long-range dependencies that are difficult for standard CNNs to model. TransBTS, for example, proposes an encoder-decoder network that combines 3D CNNs for local feature extraction with Transformers for global context modeling [11]. Similarly, Swin UNETR reformulates 3D semantic segmentation as a sequence-to-sequence prediction problem, utilizing a hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder to effectively capture long-range spatial relationships [12]. Although these transformer-based models offer powerful global context capabilities, they often come with increased computational costs. Our approach complements this line of work by focusing on efficient convolutional strategies and transfer learning, providing a lightweight alternative that achieves comparable performance with reduced training requirements.

**General Deep Learning Frameworks and Transfer Learning for Medical Imaging**
The foundation of modern medical image segmentation is largely built upon the U-Net architecture, which introduced a symmetric encoder-decoder structure specifically designed for biomedical image segmentation [1]. To address the challenge of manual hyperparameter tuning, nnU-Net was developed as a self-configuring method that automatically adapts to new datasets, significantly simplifying the deployment of deep learning models in medical imaging [10]. Furthermore, transfer learning has emerged as a critical strategy to mitigate data scarcity in medical domains. Med3D, for instance, leverages a heterogeneous 3D network pre-trained on a diverse multi-domain dataset to accelerate convergence and improve accuracy for various 3D tasks [13]. More recently, Models Genesis introduced generic, self-supervised 3D models trained without manual labels, demonstrating that preserving rich 3D anatomical information can outperform traditional 2D transfer learning and scratch training [14]. Building on these insights, our paper specifically investigates the transfer of 2D ImageNet pre-trained weights into the 3D nnU-Net framework, proposing strategies to preserve learned feature representations for efficient brain tumor segmentation.
