## Related Work

### Graph Neural Networks for Collaborative Filtering
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a dominant paradigm for collaborative filtering, leveraging the inherent bipartite structure of user-item interactions to capture higher-order collaborative signals. Early foundational work introduced graph convolutional neural networks for web-scale recommender systems, demonstrating the scalability of spectral-based approaches [2, 5]. Subsequent research refined these architectures by explicitly propagating embeddings on the user-item graph to encode collaborative signals, as seen in Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) [7], and by simplifying the message-passing mechanism to remove feature transformations and nonlinear activations, as proposed in LightGCN [9]. To address the complexity of user preferences, Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF) was introduced to model diverse user intents by disentangling representations [8], while Graph Convolutional Matrix Completion (GC-MC) formulated matrix completion as link prediction on bipartite graphs using differentiable message passing [6]. A comprehensive survey further categorizes these advancements, highlighting the versatility of GNNs in recommendation tasks [17]. Our work builds upon these GNN-based collaborative filtering frameworks by addressing the specific challenge of representation efficiency, moving beyond standard vectorized embeddings to explore binarized representations that retain the structural benefits of graph propagation while significantly reducing computational overhead.

### Binary and Discrete Representations for Efficient Recommendation
To mitigate the high memory and computational costs associated with high-dimensional vector embeddings, recent research has focused on learning binary or discrete representations for efficient recommendation. Hashing techniques have been widely adopted for similarity search in high-dimensional spaces [10], with deep learning approaches like HashNet using continuation methods to learn exactly binary hash codes [11]. In the context of collaborative filtering, discrete representations have been explored to enable fast personalized ranking from implicit feedback [12, 13]. More recently, the integration of neural collaborative filtering with binary code learning has been proposed to create efficient recommendation systems [14], and frameworks like CIGAR have utilized preference-preserving binary embeddings for large-scale Top-N candidate generation [15]. The application of GNNs to learn hash functions has further improved efficiency in recommender systems [16], while architectures such as Bi-GCN and general binary GNN strategies have been developed to reduce memory and energy consumption in graph-based learning [19, 20]. Specifically, the BiGeaR framework introduced multi-faceted quantization reinforcement to learn binarized graph representations for Top-K recommendation [21], and other studies have investigated low-loss 1-bit quantization of user-item representations [18]. Unlike these prior works that primarily focus on numerical quantization, our study explicitly mitigates the information loss inherent in binarization by leveraging supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples, thereby enhancing the fidelity of the binary codes.

### Knowledge Distillation for Graph Neural Networks
Knowledge distillation (KD) has become a critical technique for compressing and accelerating GNNs, transferring knowledge from complex teacher models to lightweight student models. The foundational concept of distilling knowledge from neural networks has been extended to graph domains, where surveys systematically review how, what, and who knowledge is distilled to improve GNN efficiency [22, 23]. Early dedicated approaches proposed distilling knowledge from pre-trained GCNs using local structure preserving modules to transfer topological semantics [24]. Subsequent methods have focused on representation alignment, such as Graph Contrastive Representation Distillation (G-CRD), which uses contrastive learning to align student and teacher node embeddings to preserve global graph topology [25]. Other frameworks have aggregated individual and relational knowledge via attributed graphs [26], enhanced graph structure augmentation through distillation [27], or focused on fine-grained learning behaviors to guide student training [28]. More recently, FreeKD proposed a reinforcement learning-based framework for free-direction knowledge exchange between shallower GNNs, eliminating the need for a deep teacher model [29]. In this paper, we adapt the principles of knowledge distillation to the specific context of binarized representation learning, introducing a fine-grained inference distillation mechanism that transfers knowledge from real item data and latent embedding samples to the binary code generator, thereby compensating for quantization errors.

### Deep Learning and Ranking Foundations for Recommendation
The development of modern recommender systems is grounded in deep learning architectures and probabilistic ranking objectives. The application of deep neural networks to video recommendations, as demonstrated in YouTube’s system, established the viability of deep models for large-scale ranking tasks [1]. Prior to the rise of GNNs, Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) replaced the inner product in matrix factorization with a multi-layer perceptron to model non-linear user-item interactions [4]. A fundamental contribution to the field was the introduction of the Bayesian Personalized Ranking (BPR) criterion, which provides a stochastic gradient descent algorithm to directly optimize recommender models for personalized ranking from implicit feedback [3]. These foundational works have been further extended to specific domains such as cross-modal retrieval, where semi-supervised knowledge distillation has been applied to improve cross-modal hashing [30], and deep hash distillation has been used to transfer hash knowledge from weakly to strongly transformed samples for image retrieval [31]. Additionally, teacher-student learning frameworks have been utilized to develop efficient hierarchical message aggregation hashing for cross-modal retrieval [32]. Our work integrates these foundational ranking objectives with advanced graph-based binarization techniques, ensuring that the efficient binary representations are optimized for accurate personalized ranking while maintaining the robustness established by deep learning and distillation methodologies.
