## Related Work

**Efficient Long-Sequence Transformer Architectures**
A significant body of research has focused on modifying the core attention mechanisms of Transformers to mitigate the quadratic computational complexity associated with long sequences, thereby enabling the processing of extended inputs [4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12]. Early approaches introduced sparse or linear attention variants to reduce memory and time costs; for instance, BigBird employs a sparse attention mechanism that combines global, local, and random attention to achieve linear complexity [4], while Longformer utilizes a combination of local windowed and global attention to efficiently handle long documents [5]. Other architectures have pursued different efficiency gains, such as the Reformer, which uses locality-sensitive hashing for attention and reversible residual layers to lower memory usage [6], and Linformer, which approximates self-attention via low-rank matrix decomposition to achieve linear complexity in both time and space [8]. More recent developments include LongT5, an efficient text-to-text Transformer specifically designed for long sequences [7], and Unlimiformer, which wraps existing encoder-decoder models to handle unlimited-length inputs by offloading cross-attention to a k-nearest-neighbor index [12]. While these architectural modifications provide foundational capabilities for long-sequence processing, they often require specific model implementations or extensive pre-training adjustments. In contrast, NexusSum leverages standard LLMs without requiring architectural changes or fine-tuning, instead relying on a structured multi-agent pipeline to manage long-form narratives.

**Long-Context Processing Strategies and Limitations**
Beyond architectural changes, recent strategies have addressed the practical limitations of long-context processing in Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly regarding context window extension and information retrieval within long inputs [9, 10, 11, 13, 14]. Position Interpolation (PI) has been proposed to extend the context window of RoPE-based LLMs to 32,768 tokens with minimal fine-tuning by linearly down-scaling position indices [9]. However, studies such as "Lost in the Middle" reveal that LLMs often suffer from performance degradation when relevant information is located in the middle of the input, indicating that simply extending the context window does not guarantee effective utilization of all tokens [10]. To address this, SLED enables short-text pretrained models to process long sequences by partitioning inputs into overlapping chunks and fusing information in the decoder [11], while CachED proposes a gradient caching technique that allows end-to-end training on full-length documents without truncation [13]. Furthermore, Chain-of-Agents (CoA) introduces a multi-agent framework where worker agents sequentially process segmented portions of long texts, with a manager agent synthesizing the results to mitigate focus issues [14]. Unlike these methods that focus on extending context windows or training on full documents, NexusSum specifically targets narrative structure, employing a hierarchical multi-LLM summarization pipeline that optimizes chunk processing and controls output length to maintain coherence across complex plotlines.

**Narrative and Book Summarization Tasks**
The task of summarizing long-form narratives, including novels and movie scripts, presents unique challenges due to the need to capture intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence [1, 2, 3]. Recent work has explored specific formulations of this task, such as the novel chapter summarization task introduced in [1], which proposes a new metric for aligning reference summary sentences with chapter sentences to create gold-standard extractive summaries. In the domain of film, [2] formulates movie script summarization as a graph-based scene extraction problem, while [3] proposes a two-stage abstractive approach that first identifies salient scenes using a human-annotated dataset before generating the summary. These studies highlight the importance of structural elements, such as scenes and chapters, in narrative summarization. However, many existing approaches rely on specific dataset annotations or extractive techniques that may not fully capture the abstractive nature of high-quality summaries. NexusSum addresses this by introducing Dialogue-to-Description Transformation, a narrative-specific preprocessing method that standardizes character dialogue and descriptive text into a unified format, thereby improving coherence in the final abstractive summary without relying on manual scene annotation.

**Evaluation of Long-Document Summarization**
Evaluating the quality of long-document summaries, particularly for narratives, remains a challenging area due to the difficulty of assessing factual consistency and coherence over extended spans [15, 16, 17]. Traditional metrics often fail to capture the nuanced errors present in long-form summaries. To address this, BooookScore was introduced as an automatic metric and systematic study for evaluating the coherence of LLM-generated book-length summaries by identifying common error types and measuring the proportion of error-free sentences [15]. Similarly, NarrativeFactScore proposes an Agent-as-a-Judge framework that utilizes a Character Knowledge Graph to evaluate and refine the factual consistency of LLM-generated summaries of long narratives [16]. These evaluation frameworks underscore the need for metrics that go beyond surface-level similarity to assess deeper structural and factual integrity. While [17] represents broader contributions to machine learning and neural networks, the specific advancements in narrative evaluation by [15] and [16] provide the necessary benchmarks for assessing the effectiveness of NexusSum. Our work contributes to this line of research by demonstrating that a structured multi-agent pipeline can significantly improve BERTScore (F1) and narrative coherence, thereby offering a scalable approach for generating high-quality summaries that meet these rigorous evaluation standards.
