## Related Work

**Surveys and Toolkits for Fairness in Recommendation and Retrieval**
Comprehensive overviews of the fairness landscape in recommender systems and information retrieval have been established to systematize foundational concepts, methodologies, and applications [1, 2]. These surveys highlight the critical need for standardized evaluation protocols to address data and algorithmic biases effectively. To facilitate rigorous and reproducible research, specialized toolkits such as FairDiverse have been developed to standardize the assessment of fairness and diversity algorithms across various base models and retrieval tasks [3]. While these resources provide essential infrastructure for benchmarking, they often focus on static metrics without deeply analyzing the dynamic mechanisms governing the trade-off between accuracy and fairness. This paper builds upon this foundation by introducing the Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve), a novel evaluation framework that moves beyond static metrics to analyze algorithm performance across varying elasticity levels, thereby offering a more comprehensive assessment of fair re-ranking algorithms.

**Two-Sided and Multi-Stakeholder Fairness in Recommendation**
Recent research has increasingly recognized that fairness in recommendation systems must account for multiple stakeholders, including both consumers and producers, rather than focusing solely on user-side metrics [6, 7]. This shift has led to the development of two-sided fairness frameworks that aim to balance the interests of all parties involved in the platform ecosystem. For instance, FairRec maps the recommendation problem to the fair allocation of indivisible goods to ensure Maxi-Min Share exposure for producers and Envy-Free up to One Item fairness for customers [5, 14]. Similarly, TFROM theoretically analyzes and practically balances the trade-offs between recommendation quality, customer fairness, and provider exposure fairness [13], while CPFair integrates fairness constraints for both consumers and producers into a joint optimization objective to improve two-sided fairness without compromising recommendation quality [12]. Although these approaches successfully model the interdependencies between stakeholders, they often treat the accuracy-fairness trade-off as a static constraint. In contrast, this work elucidates the underlying economic mechanisms of this trade-off through the lens of elasticity, providing a deeper theoretical understanding of how item-side fairness constraints propagate to user-side accuracy loss.

**Fair Re-ranking and Exposure Allocation Methods**
A significant body of literature focuses on algorithmic techniques for fair re-ranking and exposure allocation to mitigate bias in the final list of recommendations. Methods such as P-MMF ensure provider max-min fairness in re-ranking processes [4], while VerFair utilizes prior knowledge of consumers to achieve a better balance between exposure fairness and ranking performance [10]. Furthermore, policy learning approaches like Fair-PG-Rank optimize utility metrics while satisfying fairness of exposure constraints by searching the space of stochastic ranking policies via a policy-gradient approach [9]. A particularly relevant line of work is Tax-rank, which introduces a taxation perspective for fair re-ranking, using optimal transport to ensure continuity and controllability over accuracy loss [8]. While Tax-rank draws an initial analogy to economic taxation, it does not fully formalize the transfer mechanism using elasticity. This paper extends this economic analogy by rigorously defining the elasticity of utility between item groups, which governs the extent to which item-side fairness costs are transferred to user-side accuracy, and proposes ElasticRank to leverage this insight for efficient re-ranking.

**Theoretical Foundations and Economic Perspectives on Fairness**
Theoretical underpinnings for fairness in ranking and classification have been explored through various mathematical and economic lenses. For example, generalized Gini welfare functions have been used to specify normative fairness criteria in recommender systems, with non-smooth optimization techniques employed to resolve their non-differentiability [11]. Additionally, axiomatic approaches based on fair division principles have been developed to ensure envy-freeness and dominance over uniform rankings without relying on arbitrary link functions [17]. Broader economic theories regarding the distribution and redistribution of income [16] and flexible approaches to fair classification using constraints [15] also provide context for understanding resource allocation in algorithmic systems. These works establish the normative and mathematical bases for fairness but often lack a direct mechanistic link to the practical accuracy-fairness trade-off observed in re-ranking. By introducing the concept of elasticity from economics, this paper bridges this gap, offering a unified theoretical framework that explains how fairness constraints in re-ranking function as a commodity tax transfer process, ultimately guiding the design of more effective fair re-ranking algorithms.
