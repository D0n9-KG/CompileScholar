## Related Work

**Optimal Taxation Theory and Policy Design**
The theoretical foundations of income taxation have long been dominated by models seeking to balance equity and efficiency, ranging from early explorations of optimum income taxation [4] to modern frameworks that incorporate production efficiency [5] and labor supply elasticities [6, 7]. Recent advancements have extended these theories to address complex macroeconomic conditions, such as unemployment and wage responses, through sufficient statistics approaches [8], while synthesizing basic research into concrete policy recommendations for progressive systems [3]. However, these traditional models often assume rational, homogeneous agents and static parameters, limiting their ability to capture the dynamic heterogeneity of real-world taxpayer behavior. In contrast, our work moves beyond these static theoretical bounds by integrating large language models with agent-based modeling to simulate heterogeneous, potentially irrational agents, thereby enabling a more adaptive and realistic design of fiscal policy.

**Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) Analysis**
Empirical literature has extensively examined the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) as a primary tool for mitigating income inequality, providing both comprehensive overviews of the program’s structure [2] and rigorous evaluations of its effectiveness in redistributing income [1]. While these studies highlight the EITC’s role in supporting low-income households, they typically operate within fixed policy parameters and do not account for the broader, dynamic interactions between tax policy and aggregate economic outcomes. Our research complements this line of inquiry by situating the EITC within a broader, adaptive tax framework; rather than treating tax credits as static interventions, we model them as part of an iterative optimization process that responds to evolving household behaviors and macroeconomic conditions.

**AI and Machine Learning for Economic Policy and Simulation**
The application of artificial intelligence and machine learning to economics has expanded significantly, with recent works reviewing the trajectory of agent-based modeling (ABM) in financial and economic contexts [11] and examining the broader impact of ML techniques on economic analysis [10]. Notably, the "AI Economist" framework introduced a two-level deep reinforcement learning approach to discover dynamic tax policies that improve the equality-productivity trade-off in simulated environments [9]. While these studies demonstrate the potential of AI in policy discovery, they often rely on numerical optimization or reinforcement learning that may lack the interpretability and contextual reasoning capabilities of modern language models. TaxAgent distinguishes itself by leveraging the reasoning and generative capabilities of LLMs within an ABM framework, allowing for more nuanced, explainable, and adaptive policy adjustments compared to purely numerical or reinforcement-based approaches.

**Large Language Models in Finance**
The integration of large language models (LLMs) into financial applications has emerged as a promising frontier, with recent surveys detailing the progress, prospects, and challenges associated with deploying these models in financial domains [12]. Although LLMs have shown promise in tasks such as sentiment analysis, report generation, and trading strategy formulation, their application to macroeconomic policy design, particularly in the context of fiscal policy and taxation, remains underexplored. This paper addresses this gap by proposing TaxAgent, a novel framework that utilizes LLMs not merely for financial data processing, but as the core decision-making engine for designing and optimizing adaptive tax policies in a simulated macroeconomic environment.
