## Related Work

**Graph Convolutional Networks for 3D Pose Estimation**
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a dominant paradigm for 3D human pose estimation, leveraging the skeletal topology to model spatial dependencies among joints. Early approaches focused on optimizing network structures and weight sharing strategies to enhance feature propagation across the graph [12, 13]. Subsequent works introduced more sophisticated graph architectures to capture complex kinematic relationships, such as semantic graph convolutions that incorporate anatomical semantics [5], modulated graph convolutions that adaptively adjust message passing based on input features [6], and conditional directed graph convolutions that utilize pose-conditioned topologies to represent hierarchical joint dependencies [11]. Furthermore, spatial-temporal relationships have been exploited to improve estimation accuracy by modeling both spatial joint connections and temporal dynamics [3]. To address the need for multi-scale representation, Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks process skeletal features at varying scales to learn both local and global structures [23], while Hierarchical Poselet-Guided GCNs leverage hierarchical structures and poselet guidance to better model skeletal relationships [22]. While these methods effectively capture local and hierarchical dependencies, they often struggle with long-range interactions between non-linked body parts without significantly increasing model depth or introducing uncorrelated noise. Our work addresses this limitation by proposing a pyramid structure that captures cross-scale dependencies, complementing the local context provided by standard graph convolutions.

**Transformer Architectures for 3D Pose Estimation**
The success of the Transformer architecture in natural language processing [20] has spurred its adoption in 3D pose estimation, offering a powerful mechanism for modeling long-range dependencies through self-attention. Recent advancements include graph-oriented transformers like GraFormer, which integrates graph structures into the attention mechanism to respect skeletal topology [7]. To handle the complexity of joint and bone relationships, HDFormer introduces high-order directed attention mechanisms for efficient and accurate estimation [10]. In the domain of video-based pose estimation, temporal contexts are crucial; the Strided Transformer reduces sequence redundancy and aggregates long-range dependencies using strided convolutions [18], while MixSTE alternates between temporal and spatial transformer blocks to capture joint-specific motions and inter-joint correlations [19]. Additionally, MHFormer addresses depth ambiguity and self-occlusion by decomposing the estimation process into stages of generating, merging, and aggregating multiple pose hypotheses [9]. Although these transformer-based methods excel at capturing global context, they often suffer from high computational costs and large model sizes due to the quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms over all joints. In contrast, our PGFormer employs a lightweight multi-scale design that encapsulates human sub-structures into self-attention via pooling, achieving lower error and smaller model size compared to these state-of-the-art approaches.

**Single-Image 2D-to-3D Lifting and Baselines**
A significant body of research focuses on the direct lifting of 2D joint locations to 3D positions from single images, establishing fundamental baselines for the task. Early deterministic approaches determined 3D postures from single views using geometric constraints [1]. A simple yet effective feed-forward baseline demonstrated that a substantial portion of 3D pose estimation error originates from 2D visual analysis rather than the 2D-to-3D mapping itself [4]. To improve accuracy, coarse-to-fine volumetric prediction schemes have been proposed, using ConvNets to estimate per-voxel joint likelihoods [2]. More recently, HEMlets Pose introduced Part-Centric Heatmap Triplets as an intermediate representation to bridge the gap between 2D observations and 3D interpretations [21]. Additionally, learning pose grammar models that enforce kinematic and symmetry constraints via hierarchical Bi-directional RNNs has been explored to encode human body configuration [17]. These methods provide essential benchmarks for evaluating the effectiveness of more complex architectures. Our approach builds upon these foundations by enhancing the modeling of long-range dependencies within the lifting process, thereby reducing the error attributable to the 2D-to-3D mapping stage.

**Probabilistic and Uncertainty-Aware Pose Estimation**
Given the inherent ambiguity and indeterminacy of monocular 3D pose estimation, recent works have shifted towards probabilistic and uncertainty-aware frameworks. Non-local neural networks have been utilized as generic building blocks to compute responses as weighted sums of features at all positions, effectively capturing long-range dependencies that contribute to robust feature representation [14]. To explicitly handle uncertainty and occlusion, DiffPose formulates 3D pose estimation as a reverse diffusion process, utilizing pose-specific initialization and Gaussian Mixture Model-based forward diffusion to generate reliable poses [15]. Similarly, another variant of DiffPose employs a conditional diffusion model to predict multiple 3D pose hypotheses from 2D joint candidate samples, directly addressing the multi-modal nature of the posterior distribution [16]. Furthermore, exploiting temporal correlations has been shown to improve estimation reliability by leveraging the consistency of motion over time [8]. While these probabilistic methods offer robustness to ambiguity, they often come with increased computational overhead. Our work complements this line of research by focusing on the structural modeling of long-range dependencies through a pyramid graph attention mechanism, which provides a deterministic yet highly accurate and lightweight solution that can be integrated with or compared against probabilistic baselines.
