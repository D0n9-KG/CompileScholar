## Related Work

### Video Super-Resolution and Restoration Architectures
Early approaches to video super-resolution (VSR) primarily relied on recurrent architectures and explicit motion compensation to handle temporal dependencies. For instance, EDVR introduced a pyramid, cascading, and deformable alignment module to achieve state-of-the-art performance in video restoration tasks [9], while subsequent works like the Recurrent Structure-Detail Network divided inputs into structure and detail components to improve robustness against appearance changes [4]. To further enhance efficiency and quality, BasicVSR identified essential components for propagation and alignment, which were later refined in BasicVSR++ to improve these mechanisms [2, 3]. Alternative strategies have explored dynamic upsampling filters to bypass explicit motion compensation [6] and multi-correspondence aggregation to exploit similar patterns across frames and scales without relying solely on optical flow [11]. More recently, Transformer-based architectures have gained prominence for their ability to model long-range dependencies. The Video Super-Resolution Transformer (VSR-Transformer) adapted spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention for data locality [1], while VRT and RVRT introduced temporal mutual self-attention and guided deformable attention, respectively, to balance model size and effectiveness in parallel frame prediction [7, 8]. Additionally, hierarchical temporal group attention has been employed to handle large motions by integrating different frame rates [5]. These advancements have been validated on standard benchmarks such as the NTIRE 2019 challenge dataset [10]. Our work builds upon these architectural insights by integrating a global spatio-temporal attention mechanism using the Video State-Space block, which reinforces coherence at an affordable computational cost compared to traditional attention mechanisms.

### Real-World Video Super-Resolution Challenges
Real-world VSR presents distinct challenges due to complex, unknown degradations that differ from synthetic benchmarks. To address this, researchers have introduced specific datasets and learning schemes, such as the decomposition-based learning scheme proposed for real-world VSR [12]. The tradeoffs in real-world VSR have been investigated through models like RealBasicVSR, which incorporates an image pre-cleaning stage to mitigate artifact propagation and uses stochastic degradation schemes to reduce training time [13]. Furthermore, FastRealVSR has been proposed to mitigate artifacts in recurrent models by employing a Hidden State Attention module with selective cross-attention, thereby achieving faster inference speeds [14]. These studies highlight the difficulty of maintaining content consistency and avoiding complex degradations in high-resolution videos. In contrast to these methods that primarily focus on degradation modeling or artifact mitigation in recurrent pipelines, our paper proposes a noise-robust framework that incorporates self-supervised learning and Mamba into pre-trained latent diffusion models to specifically address the inherent randomness and artifact generation of diffusion-based approaches.

### State Space Models and Efficient Sequence Modeling
State Space Models (SSMs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to Transformers for efficient sequence modeling, offering linear-time complexity. The foundation was laid by methods for efficiently modeling long sequences with structured state spaces [15], followed by the S5 layer, which simplified SSMs to match the computational efficiency of S4 while achieving state-of-the-art performance on long-range tasks [16]. Mamba further advanced this field by introducing selective state spaces where SSM parameters are input-dependent, enabling state-of-the-art performance across modalities with a hardware-aware parallel algorithm [17]. This capability has been extended to multi-dimensional data structures in Mamba-nd [18]. In the vision domain, Vision Mamba (Vim) demonstrated that bidirectional state space models can serve as generic backbones, achieving higher performance and significantly improved computation and memory efficiency compared to vision transformers [19]. Leveraging these advancements, our work integrates the Video State-Space block with a 3D Selective Scan module to enhance the diffusion model, ensuring content consistency across adjacent frames with reduced computational overhead.

### Self-Supervised and Contrastive Visual Representation Learning
Self-supervised learning has become a cornerstone for extracting robust visual representations without labeled data. SimCLR established a simple contrastive learning framework relying on data augmentation composition and large batch sizes to achieve state-of-the-art performance [21]. To overcome the limitations of large batch sizes, Momentum Contrast (MoCo) introduced a dynamic dictionary built with a queue and a moving-averaged encoder, facilitating efficient contrastive learning and closing the gap with supervised pre-training [22]. Alternatively, SwAV proposed an unsupervised learning algorithm that learns visual features by clustering data and enforcing consistency between cluster assignments of different augmentations, avoiding the need for pairwise feature comparisons [20]. These methods have proven effective in capturing invariant features from visual data. In this paper, we introduce a self-supervised ControlNet that leverages contrastive learning principles to extract degradation-insensitive features from LR videos, using HR features as guidance to further reduce artifacts in generated details.
