## Related Work

**Conversational Recommendation Systems**
Conversational recommendation systems (CRSs) have evolved significantly to address the challenge of capturing complex user preferences through multi-turn dialogue. Early and foundational approaches focus on structural and probabilistic modeling to manage the interaction dynamics. For instance, [1] proposes an estimation-action-reflection framework to facilitate deep interaction between conversational and recommender components, while [2] introduces Conversational Path Reasoning (CPR), which models the task as interactive path reasoning on a graph to explicitly utilize user-preferred attributes and prune irrelevant candidates. To handle specific data challenges, [3] presents Conversational Thompson Sampling (ConTS), which unifies attributes and items in the same arm space to manage the exploration-exploitation trade-off for cold-start users, and [6] proposes LOT-CRS, a framework that simulates balanced datasets and employs pre-training with retrieval-augmented fine-tuning to alleviate the long-tail problem. More recent advancements leverage the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and prompt engineering to enhance system performance. [4] develops a unified CRS approach via knowledge-enhanced prompt learning, while [7] broadens the model's perspective through demonstration-augmented prompt learning. Furthermore, [8] investigates the potential of LLMs as zero-shot conversational recommenders, and [9] explores methods to unleash the retrieval potential of LLMs specifically within the CRS context. Additionally, [5] improves CRSs by employing counterfactual data simulation techniques to generate more robust training data. In contrast to these methods that primarily focus on the recommender side or data augmentation, our work introduces a generative reward model based simulated user (GRSU) to actively engage in the dialogue, providing both coarse-grained scoring and fine-grained attribute-based critiques to refine the recommendation process through automatic multi-turn interaction.

**LLM Verification and Reward Modeling**
The integration of verification and reward modeling has emerged as a critical technique for enhancing the reasoning and decision-making capabilities of Large Language Models. Traditional approaches often rely on discriminative models or simple scoring mechanisms; for example, [10] introduces the GSM8K dataset and proposes training verifiers to select the best among multiple candidate solutions for multi-step mathematical reasoning, thereby improving LLM performance through best-of-N selection. More recently, the paradigm has shifted towards generative reward models that leverage the generative nature of LLMs for more nuanced evaluation. [11] proposes GenRM, a hybrid algorithm that unifies RLHF and RLAIF by training an LLM on self-generated reasoning traces to produce synthetic preference labels that align closely with human judgments. Similarly, [12] introduces generative verifiers trained via next-token prediction, which improve LLM reasoning performance in best-of-N selection by leveraging chain-of-thought reasoning and test-time compute. While these works focus on verifying reasoning steps or selecting optimal responses in general reasoning tasks, our paper adapts this generative reward modeling paradigm to the domain of conversational recommendation. We design a unified simulated user that utilizes generative item scoring and attribute-based item critique as feedback actions, effectively bridging the gap between reward modeling and interactive preference clarification in CRSs.
