## Related Work

### Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) Methods and Analysis
Recent advancements in Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) have focused on balancing search effectiveness with user privacy in decentralized environments. Early approaches introduced evolution strategies to facilitate collaborative training across distributed data sources, aiming to optimize ranking models without centralizing sensitive user data [1]. Subsequent work has rigorously evaluated the reproducibility of these evolution-based methods, highlighting the importance of consistent experimental protocols in verifying their efficacy [2]. To further enhance privacy guarantees while maintaining performance, researchers have developed specific frameworks that integrate privacy-preserving mechanisms directly into the FOLTR pipeline [3]. The robustness of these systems under realistic data conditions has also been scrutinized; for instance, studies have investigated whether non-IID data distributions pose a significant threat to the performance of Federated Pairwise Differentiable Gradient Descent (FPDGD), identifying critical gaps in handling data bias [5]. Furthermore, the security landscape of FOLTR has been analyzed through the lens of adversarial threats, with recent work examining the impact of untargeted data and model poisoning attacks on search effectiveness and evaluating the efficacy of corresponding defense mechanisms [6]. This paper builds upon this foundation by extending the analysis of FOLTR systems to the specific challenge of unlearning, addressing the need for verifiable data removal capabilities within these established frameworks.

### Online Learning to Rank with Neural Models
The integration of neural networks into online learning to rank has enabled more sophisticated modeling of user preferences from implicit feedback. A key contribution in this area is the introduction of Pairwise Differentiable Gradient Descent (PDGD), an unbiased method that optimizes non-linear models by inferring preferences directly from user clicks, thereby eliminating the need for interleaving techniques [4]. Complementing this, other research has focused on the theoretical underpinnings of learning neural ranking models online, proposing methods that manage the explore-exploit trade-off through neural tangent kernel convergence analysis [7]. These neural-based approaches provide the foundational model architectures and optimization techniques that are often adapted for federated settings. Our work leverages these neural online learning paradigms as the baseline for evaluating unlearning strategies, ensuring that the privacy mechanisms assessed are compatible with state-of-the-art ranking model architectures.

### Federated Unlearning and Right to be Forgotten
The "right to be forgotten" has driven significant research into machine unlearning, particularly within federated learning (FL) systems where data remains local. Various strategies have been proposed to efficiently remove client contributions from global models. Some methods focus on rapid retraining, allowing data holders to erase their influence while keeping training data local [10]. Others, such as Federaser, enable efficient client-level data removal by leveraging specific model structures [8]. Knowledge distillation has also been employed to restore model performance after subtracting historical updates, thereby eliminating a client's contribution without requiring their data or participation [9]. Alternative approaches include performing local unlearning at the target client followed by limited rounds of federated aggregation with remaining clients [11], or rolling back and calibrating historical parameter updates with negative sampling to reduce storage overhead on resource-constrained devices [12]. In more complex domains, such as heterogeneous knowledge graph embedding, frameworks like FedLU utilize mutual knowledge distillation and neuroscience-inspired methods to erase specific knowledge from both local and global models [13]. Specifically within the FOLTR domain, recent work has proposed methods to remove client contributions without full retraining, using poisoning attack simulations to measure unlearning effectiveness [14]. Unlike these prior studies, which often rely on single evaluation metrics, our paper systematically analyzes under- and over-unlearning scenarios using multiple adapted and newly proposed metrics to provide a more comprehensive and verifiable assessment of unlearning strategies.

### Federated Learning Security and Poisoning Attacks
Security in federated learning is a critical concern, particularly regarding adversarial manipulations of the global model. Research in this area has focused on optimizing both attacks and defenses to understand the vulnerabilities of Byzantine clients. For example, recent work has specifically addressed the manipulation of Byzantine clients to optimize model poisoning attacks and evaluate the robustness of defense mechanisms against such threats [15]. Understanding these attack vectors is essential for designing secure unlearning protocols, as unlearning mechanisms must not only remove data but also resist or mitigate potential adversarial exploitation during the unlearning process. Our study considers the security implications of unlearning in the context of these known vulnerabilities, ensuring that the proposed evaluation metrics can detect not only successful unlearning but also potential security regressions or adversarial manipulations.
