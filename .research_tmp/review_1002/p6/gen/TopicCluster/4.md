## Related Work

**In-Context Learning: Demonstration Quality, Noise, and Biases**
Recent research has extensively investigated the factors influencing the efficacy of in-context learning (ICL), with a particular focus on the quality of demonstrations and the robustness of models against noise. Early studies challenged the necessity of ground-truth labels, arguing that ICL performance is primarily driven by the demonstration of label space, input distribution, and sequence format rather than strict label accuracy [5]. However, subsequent work has re-examined this view, introducing metrics to quantify the impact of ground-truth labels and identifying factors such as prompt verbosity and model size that influence noise resilience [1]. To address the specific challenge of corrupted data, several methods have been proposed to safeguard against performance degradation caused by noisy labels in task demonstrations [6]. A prominent paradigm for handling noise in text generation involves Local Perplexity Ranking (LPR), which assumes that noisy samples exhibit higher local perplexities than clean ones, allowing for the replacement of noisy candidates with cleaner nearest neighbors [7]. While effective in certain regimes, this perplexity-based approach may suffer from biases inherent in the annotation process and the domain-specific knowledge of large language models. Furthermore, broader issues of label biases have been addressed through typologies and domain-context calibration methods that use random in-domain words to estimate and mitigate these biases [10], as well as contextual calibration procedures that employ content-free test inputs to stabilize few-shot performance [11]. In contrast to these approaches, which often rely on relative ranking or general calibration, our work introduces a dual debiasing framework that explicitly corrects perplexity estimates using synthesized neighbors to derive a robust Sample Cleanliness Score, thereby uncovering absolute sample cleanliness regardless of the overall corpus noise level.

**In-Context Learning: Demonstration Selection and Representation**
Beyond the quality of individual demonstrations, significant effort has been directed toward optimizing the selection and representation of in-context examples to enhance efficiency and transferability. One line of work focuses on retrieval, such as the Unified Demonstration Retriever (UDR), which employs a multi-task list-wise ranking framework to retrieve relevant demonstrations for a wide range of tasks using a single model, thereby reducing deployment costs and improving transferability [3]. Another distinct approach recasts ICL as a problem of latent space steering; In-Context Vectors (ICV) shift latent states instead of adding prompt demonstrations, making LLM adaptation more effective, controllable, and computationally efficient [4]. While these methods optimize how demonstrations are chosen or represented to maximize utility, they generally assume that the underlying demonstration corpus is of high quality. Our work complements these selection and representation strategies by first ensuring the reliability of the source data through robust noise detection, providing a cleaner foundation upon which advanced retrieval or steering techniques can be applied.

**Contrastive Decoding for Reasoning and Generation**
Contrastive decoding has emerged as a powerful training-free technique to improve the quality of text generation and reasoning by leveraging the likelihood differences between models. In the domain of open-ended text generation, Contrastive Decoding maximizes the likelihood difference between a large expert model and a small amateur model, subject to a plausibility constraint, to optimize output quality [8]. This principle has been extended to reasoning tasks, where maximizing the likelihood difference between strong and weak models has been shown to significantly improve reasoning performance compared to greedy decoding [2]. These methods demonstrate that contrasting model outputs can effectively suppress low-quality or unreasoned generations. Our work draws inspiration from this contrastive principle but applies it to the pre-processing stage of ICL; rather than contrasting model outputs during generation, we use synthesized neighbors to contrast and correct perplexity estimates, thereby debiasing the evaluation of demonstration cleanliness before the ICL process begins.

**Contrastive Decoding for Contextual Grounding**
Recent advancements have also explored contrastive decoding to enhance the model's reliance on input context over its parametric knowledge. One such method integrates contrastive decoding with adversarial irrelevant passages to improve contextual understanding in large language models, effectively forcing the model to ground its responses in the provided context rather than relying on internal biases [9]. This approach highlights the utility of contrastive mechanisms in mitigating specific types of model bias, particularly those related to context adherence. Similarly, our dual debiasing framework aims to mitigate biases in the ICL pipeline, specifically targeting the biases in perplexity estimation caused by annotation noise and domain-specific knowledge. By explicitly correcting these biases, our method ensures that the ICL process is grounded in genuinely clean demonstrations, analogous to how contrastive decoding grounds generation in relevant context.
