## Related Work

**Deep Learning for LiDAR-Camera Calibration**
Recent advancements in deep learning have significantly advanced the field of automatic targetless calibration, particularly for LiDAR-camera systems, by leveraging neural networks to infer extrinsic parameters directly from sensor data [6, 7, 8, 9]. Early approaches such as RegNet introduced deep convolutional neural networks to estimate 6-DOF extrinsic calibration in real-time without human interaction [6], while CalibNet utilized self-supervised learning with 3D Spatial Transformer Networks to maximize geometric and photometric consistency [7]. Subsequent works have refined these architectures; for instance, CalibRCNN incorporates recurrent convolutional neural networks combined with geometric constraints to improve calibration stability [8], and LCCNet employs cost volume networks for feature matching and iterative refinement to achieve high-precision online self-calibration [9]. While these methods have established robust benchmarks for LiDAR-camera alignment, they primarily rely on the dense point cloud data provided by LiDAR, which is not directly applicable to the sparse and noisy nature of radar data. In contrast, our work adapts these deep learning principles to the radar-camera domain, specifically addressing the unique challenges of radar sparsity through a dual-perspective representation and selective fusion mechanism.

**Radar-Camera Calibration**
Calibrating radar and camera systems presents distinct challenges due to the inherent sparsity of radar returns and significant measurement uncertainties, particularly in height estimation [11, 12, 13]. Existing targetless methods have attempted to address these issues using various strategies; for example, one approach utilizes a coarse-to-fine convolutional neural network for rotational auto-calibration in intelligent transportation systems [11]. Other methods leverage the continuous-time nature of radar velocity measurements to determine extrinsic transforms without specialized retroreflectors [12], or employ spatiotemporal algorithms that use ego-velocity data to correct for spatial and temporal misalignments in real-world environments [13]. Although these approaches eliminate the need for physical targets, they often struggle with the high uncertainty in radar height data, which can degrade calibration accuracy. Our proposed RC-AutoCalib addresses this limitation by explicitly modeling height uncertainty through a bird's-eye view representation and a selective fusion mechanism, thereby achieving more robust geometric calibration than previous radar-specific methods.

**General and Motion-Based Sensor Calibration**
Beyond specific sensor pairs, a broad category of research focuses on general automatic targetless calibration and motion-based techniques for multimodal sensor arrays [3, 4, 5, 10]. Surveys of this field highlight the diversity of methods available for aligning sensors without physical targets [3]. Specific algorithms include maximizing mutual information for extrinsic calibration of 3D LiDAR and cameras [4], and utilizing motion data to calibrate multimodal sensor arrays [5]. Additionally, online calibration methods have been developed that leverage the tracking of moving objects to determine sensor alignment in dynamic environments [10]. These general frameworks provide foundational insights into extrinsic parameter estimation; however, they are often designed for sensors with higher data density or rely on specific motion assumptions that may not hold for sparse radar data. Our work builds upon these general principles but introduces specialized mechanisms to handle the specific noise and sparsity characteristics of millimeter-wave radar, ensuring reliable calibration during system operation.

**Sensor Fusion for Object Detection**
The ultimate goal of accurate sensor calibration is to enable effective sensor fusion for downstream tasks such as object detection in autonomous driving [1, 2]. Early works demonstrated the potential of fusing millimeter-wave radar with vision to detect obstacles and visualize results on image sequences [1]. More recent studies have focused on integrating radar and vision data to detect objects in the surroundings of autonomous vehicles, highlighting the importance of precise extrinsic parameters for successful fusion [2]. While these fusion systems benefit from calibrated sensors, they typically assume that calibration is performed offline or with high-precision targets. By providing an end-to-end online automatic calibration network, our work ensures that the geometric alignment required for such fusion tasks remains accurate in real-time, thereby supporting more robust object detection pipelines in dynamic driving scenarios.
