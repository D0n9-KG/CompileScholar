## Related Work

**Kubernetes-based Resource Management**
Recent research has focused on enhancing the operational efficiency of container orchestration platforms by integrating adaptive mechanisms directly into the Kubernetes ecosystem. For instance, [1] proposes a method that leverages Kubernetes to manage resources in a manner that dynamically adapts to changing workload conditions while simultaneously minimizing overall costs. This approach highlights the potential of native orchestration features to drive financial efficiency without requiring external, complex control loops. In contrast to this single-platform adaptive strategy, our work shifts the focus from internal resource adaptation to a broader comparative analysis of network cost implications across different hosting infrastructures, specifically examining how these costs manifest when Kubernetes is deployed on cloud providers versus bare metal environments.

**Multi-agent Optimization for Cloud Resources**
Another significant line of inquiry involves the application of advanced optimization frameworks to improve resource allocation in cloud computing environments. Notably, [2] develops a hierarchical multi-agent optimization framework designed to enhance the efficiency of resource allocation by coordinating multiple agents to make distributed decisions. This multi-agent approach addresses the complexity of cloud resource management by decomposing the optimization problem into manageable hierarchical levels. While such frameworks offer sophisticated solutions for general resource allocation, they often abstract away the specific financial metrics associated with network traffic. Our paper complements this line of work by providing a granular, cost-centric perspective on network expenditures, thereby offering actionable insights for FinOps practices that may not be fully captured by general-purpose multi-agent allocation strategies.
