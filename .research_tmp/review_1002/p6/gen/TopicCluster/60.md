## Related Work

**Field-Aware and Multi-Field Calibration for Advertising**
In the domain of online advertising, the heterogeneity of user behaviors and item attributes necessitates calibration methods that account for specific field characteristics rather than applying global transformations. Recent works have introduced field-aware mechanisms to enhance the reliability of probabilistic predictions by leveraging granular statistical information. For instance, Neural Calibration utilizes field-aware information to improve prediction reliability and introduces a field-level calibration error metric [9], while AdaCalib employs a doubly-adaptive approach that learns isotonic functions guided by posterior statistics to achieve fine-grained field-level calibration [10]. To address data sparsity and complex distribution shifts, other methods decompose the calibration process into distinct components; specifically, Deep Ensemble Shape Calibration (DESC) separates multi-field calibration into value and shape components using basis functions to better utilize data in e-commerce settings [12], and confidence-aware multi-field calibration adaptively adjusts the intensity of calibration based on sample statistics to mitigate sparsity issues [7]. In contrast to these approaches, which often rely on predefined functional forms or specific decomposition strategies, our method employs an Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) to learn arbitrary monotonic functions, thereby offering greater expressiveness and flexibility without sacrificing the order-preserving property essential for ranking tasks.

**Joint Optimization of Ranking and Calibration**
A significant line of research focuses on the simultaneous optimization of ranking accuracy and probability calibration, aiming to resolve the inherent conflict between relative ordering and absolute value accuracy. Several frameworks have been proposed to decouple or align these objectives; for example, the Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR) decouples conflicting losses to avoid full candidate list aggregation during training [11], while JRC proposes a joint optimization approach that preserves the interpretation of logits as click-through rates to maintain calibration while improving ranking ability [14]. Other methods align ranking and regression objectives through regression-compatible listwise objectives [15] or employ calibration-compatible listwise distillation for privileged features to preserve both ranking performance and model calibration [16]. Furthermore, Bayesian approaches have been explored to move beyond binary preference signals for joint optimization [17]. Unlike these methods, which often integrate calibration constraints directly into the ranking loss or use specific distillation techniques, our approach treats calibration as a post-hoc or separate module using a highly flexible UMNN, optimized with a novel Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) that ensures the ideal calibration state without excessively distorting the original predictions.

**General Probability Calibration Methods and Analysis**
General probability calibration methods aim to transform classifier scores into accurate probability estimates, with extensive analysis of their effectiveness across various model architectures. Early and foundational work includes techniques for generating probabilistic outputs from support vector machines and comparisons with regularized likelihood methods [3], as well as methods for transforming classifier scores into accurate multiclass probability estimates [2]. More recent advancements include beta calibration, which offers a well-founded and easily implemented improvement over logistic calibration for binary classifiers [5]. Additionally, empirical studies have revisited the calibration of modern neural networks, finding that recent state-of-the-art image classification models, particularly non-convolutional architectures, exhibit strong calibration properties [4]. While these methods provide robust baselines for general classification tasks, they often rely on fixed parametric forms or assume specific distributional properties. Our work extends this line of research by introducing an unconstrained monotonic neural network that can learn arbitrary monotonic functions, thereby overcoming the limited expressiveness of fixed-form transformations like piece-wise linear or logistic functions in complex ranking scenarios.

**Industrial Ranking Systems and Calibration Applications**
In industrial settings, the deployment of calibrated ranking models is critical for ensuring that output scores serve as reliable probabilistic estimates for downstream decision-making processes. Large-scale industrial frameworks, such as LiRank at LinkedIn, integrate state-of-the-art architectures, novel calibration techniques, and model compression for production deployment, highlighting the practical importance of calibration in real-world systems [8]. Specific techniques have been developed to obtain calibrated preference probabilities from personalized ranking models, such as Gaussian and Gamma calibration methods combined with unbiased empirical risk minimization [6]. Furthermore, the scale calibration of deep ranking models has been addressed to ensure that output scores are not only ranked correctly but also represent reliable probabilities [13]. Our paper contributes to this industrial application by demonstrating that the proposed UMNN-based calibration, when deployed in Kuaishou's large-scale online video ranking system, translates calibration improvements into enhanced business metrics, thereby validating the practical utility of unconstrained monotonic calibration in high-stakes industrial environments.
