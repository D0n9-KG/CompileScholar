## Related Work

**Verifiable Differential Privacy and Manipulation Prevention**
Recent literature has increasingly focused on ensuring the faithful execution of differential privacy mechanisms, particularly in settings where the server or data provider may be untrusted. Early efforts introduced the concept of Verifiable Differential Privacy, utilizing zero-knowledge proofs to certify that published statistics are both differentially private and generated without revealing the underlying noise [1]. This line of work was extended to interactive settings, where proof systems verify the correctness of differentially private counting queries [4]. In the context of local differential privacy, verifiable randomization mechanisms have been proposed to prevent manipulation attacks by allowing data collectors to verify that providers have correctly executed the agreed-upon randomization process [3]. While these works establish foundational frameworks for verifying DP compliance, they often rely on general-purpose proof systems that can be computationally expensive. Our work, VDDP, builds upon these foundations by providing a more general framework for the client-server-verifier setup and introducing efficient mechanisms that specifically address the trade-off between proof generation efficiency and utility, offering significant improvements over previous state-of-the-art verifiable mechanisms.

**Secure Multi-Party Computation for Distributed Differential Privacy**
An alternative approach to ensuring privacy in distributed settings involves leveraging secure multi-party computation (MPC) to generate noise or process data without a single trusted party. For instance, Cryptε employs two non-colluding untrusted servers to execute differential privacy programs on encrypted data, thereby achieving central-model accuracy without requiring a trusted data collector [6]. Similarly, research has explored privacy mechanisms based on distributed noise generation to protect data ownership and integrity in multi-party environments [7]. Recent benchmarking studies have categorized these secure sampling protocols, extended them for Byzantine resilience, and conducted extensive efficiency evaluations to compare their practical viability [10]. Although MPC-based approaches provide strong security guarantees, they often incur high communication and computational overheads. In contrast, our VDDP framework aims to reduce these overheads by integrating verification directly into the noise generation process, achieving a more efficient balance between security and performance compared to pure MPC solutions.

**Cryptographic Protocols for Secure Noise Sampling**
A critical component of distributed differential privacy is the secure generation of noise, which requires cryptographic protocols to ensure that the noise distribution is correct and independent. Several works have addressed this by developing cryptographic methods for securely sampling biased coins, with specific applications to implementing differential privacy mechanisms [8]. Furthermore, specialized protocols have been proposed for securely sampling discrete Gaussian noise to enable multi-party differential privacy, ensuring that the statistical properties required for DP are maintained across parties [9]. These protocols are essential for preventing servers from sampling incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise. Our work leverages and improves upon these cryptographic primitives, specifically designing the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM) to achieve up to a $4 \times 10^5$x improvement in proof generation efficiency while maintaining rigorous privacy guarantees, thereby addressing the efficiency bottlenecks identified in prior secure sampling literature.

**Cryptographic Randomized Response and Local DP Security**
In the domain of local differential privacy, cryptographic techniques are employed to ensure that respondents cannot cheat or manipulate the aggregate results. Cryptographic randomized response techniques have been developed to guarantee unconditional privacy and prevent cheating respondents from influencing the poll tally beyond their own vote [5]. Building on this, DPrio proposes an efficient method for achieving differential privacy with high utility specifically tailored for the Prio protocol, optimizing the communication costs associated with secure aggregation [2]. These works highlight the importance of efficient, cryptographically secure protocols for local DP. Our contribution extends this line of research by providing an improved solution to Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR), a special case of VDDP, which achieves a reduction of up to 5000x in communication costs and verifier overhead compared to existing cryptographic randomized response methods.
