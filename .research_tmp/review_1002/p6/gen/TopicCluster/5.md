## Related Work

**Foundational Word and Sentence Embeddings**
The evolution of text representation has progressed from static word-level vectors to dynamic sentence-level embeddings. Early foundational work focused on learning distributed representations for words and phrases, with models such as GloVe leveraging global co-occurrence statistics [2] and extensions to the continuous Skip-gram model incorporating subsampling and negative sampling to capture idiomatic meanings [1]. The advent of deep learning shifted the paradigm toward context-aware sentence representations, exemplified by the Universal Sentence Encoder, which generates fixed-length vectors for downstream tasks [5]. To address the computational inefficiency of encoding sentences individually, Sentence-BERT (SBERT) introduced siamese and triplet network structures to derive semantically meaningful embeddings that enable efficient similarity search [6]. While these methods established the baseline for static semantic representation, they lack the adaptability to dynamically adjust to specific user instructions, a limitation that our work addresses by transforming these pre-computed embeddings rather than re-encoding the corpus.

**Transformer Architectures and Pre-training**
The architectural backbone of modern natural language processing is the Transformer, which relies solely on attention mechanisms to eliminate recurrence and improve parallelization [3]. This architecture underpins large-scale pre-training models such as BERT, which learns deep bidirectional representations from unlabeled text to achieve state-of-the-art results across various NLP tasks [4]. These models have become the standard for generating high-quality contextual representations. However, standard pre-trained models like BERT are typically optimized for general-purpose understanding and do not inherently support instruction-conditioned embedding generation. Our framework builds upon the robust feature extraction capabilities of such Transformer-based architectures but decouples the encoding process from the instruction adaptation step, allowing for the reuse of static embeddings generated by these foundational models.

**Contrastive Learning and Embedding Optimization**
Recent advancements in embedding quality have been driven by contrastive learning strategies and optimization techniques. SimCSE demonstrated that simple contrastive learning, using dropout as noise in unsupervised settings or NLI pairs in supervised ones, significantly improves sentence embedding quality [7]. Further refinements include WhitenedCSE, which applies whitening transformations to contrastive objectives to enhance uniformity [8], and Angle-optimized Embeddings (AoE), which specifically targets improvements in semantic textual similarity [11]. Additionally, recent methods have leveraged large language models to generate high-quality embeddings by fine-tuning on synthetic data, achieving state-of-the-art results without complex multi-stage pipelines [9]. While these approaches optimize the geometric properties of the embedding space for general semantic similarity, they generally produce static representations. In contrast, GSTransform focuses on the dynamic alignment of these optimized embeddings with specific instructions, ensuring that the inherent instruction-relevant information is effectively utilized without the need for re-training the base encoder.

**Instruction-Tuned and Unified Embedding Models**
A recent line of research focuses on making text embeddings responsive to user instructions, enabling a single model to handle diverse tasks. INSTRUCTOR introduced a single text embedder that incorporates task instructions into the input to generate task-specific embeddings, achieving state-of-the-art performance without further training [12]. Promptriever extended this concept by demonstrating that retrieval models can be prompted like language models to follow detailed relevance instructions and perform hyperparameter search [14]. Other approaches, such as InBedder, treat instructions as questions and fine-tune language models on abstractive question answering to capture user-specified characteristics [13]. Furthermore, GRIT proposed training a single large language model to handle both generative and embedding tasks simultaneously, accelerating Retrieval-Augmented Generation [10]. Despite their effectiveness, these instruction-tuned models typically require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction, leading to significant computational overhead. GSTransform differs from these methods by proposing a lightweight, real-time transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed generic embeddings, thereby achieving dramatic speedups while maintaining competitive instruction-following quality.
