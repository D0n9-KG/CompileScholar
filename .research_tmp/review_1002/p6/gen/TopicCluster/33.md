## Related Work

**Gradient Projection and Orthogonal Subspace Methods**
A prominent line of research addresses catastrophic forgetting by constraining updates to directions that do not interfere with previously learned knowledge, typically through gradient projection or orthogonal subspace methods. Early approaches such as Gradient Projection Memory [3] mitigate forgetting by projecting gradients onto the orthogonal complement of subspaces deemed important for past tasks, while TRGP [4] extends this by using a trust region to select related old tasks and applying scaled weight projection to facilitate forward knowledge transfer. More recent theoretical works have formalized these mechanisms; for instance, [9] provides generalization guarantees for Orthogonal Gradient Descent in the Neural Tangent Kernel regime, proving its robustness to forgetting, and [10] introduces the NTK overlap matrix to quantify task similarity and proposes a PCA-based variant of orthogonal gradient descent. In contrast to these methods, which rely on geometric constraints to preserve past knowledge, our work focuses on the temporal ordering of data utilization within rehearsal-based strategies, offering a complementary perspective on how to balance retention and adaptation.

**Replay and Memory-Based Continual Learning**
Replay-based methods constitute another major category of continual learning techniques, which store a subset of past data or generate synthetic samples to train alongside new data. Gradient Episodic Memory (GEM) [8] constrains gradient updates based on a small memory of past examples to allow beneficial knowledge transfer while preventing forgetting, a strategy refined in A-GEM [6] for improved efficiency. Other approaches include iCaRL [7], which enables class-incremental learning by simultaneously optimizing classifiers and representations without requiring all previous data, and Deep Generative Replay [5], which alleviates forgetting by synthesizing training data for previous tasks using a deep generative model. While these methods have demonstrated superior empirical performance by leveraging stored data, they largely operate as heuristic strategies without a comprehensive theoretical characterization of how the specific scheduling of past data affects forgetting. Our paper provides the first theoretical analysis of such rehearsal strategies, distinguishing between concurrent and sequential rehearsal to explain when and why revisiting past data is beneficial.

**Regularization and Weight Consolidation Approaches**
Regularization-based methods aim to prevent forgetting by penalizing changes to weights that are critical for previous tasks, often through weight consolidation or lateral connections. Progressive Neural Networks [1] use lateral connections to transfer knowledge between tasks while isolating new task parameters to prevent interference, whereas Elastic Weight Consolidation [2] selectively slows down learning on weights important for previously learned tasks. From a theoretical standpoint, [11] unifies the analysis of such regularization-based algorithms by formulating them as a second-order Taylor approximation of the loss function, providing insights into their optimization and generalization properties. Unlike these approaches, which modify the loss function or architecture to protect past knowledge, our work investigates the optimization dynamics of standard rehearsal without additional regularization terms, revealing that the order of training on past and new data is a critical factor in determining forgetting and generalization performance.

**Theoretical Analysis of Forgetting in Linear and Overparameterized Models**
Recent theoretical efforts have sought to understand the mechanisms of catastrophic forgetting in simplified models, particularly linear and overparameterized systems. Early analyses focused on linear classification and regression, showing that weak regularization in separable data reduces to a sequential max-margin problem [12] and deriving exact expressions for forgetting in overparameterized linear regression [13]. Further studies have revealed that task ordering and step size selection significantly impact forgetting, with training tasks having larger eigenvalues later increasing forgetting [15], and that overparameterization itself can reduce catastrophic forgetting in random orthogonal transformation tasks [16]. More comprehensive frameworks have emerged to characterize expected forgetting and generalization error in overparameterized linear models, explaining the roles of task similarity and ordering [14], and analyzing the impact of buffer size and model capacity in replay-based continual learning [17]. While these works provide valuable insights into the factors influencing forgetting, they have not explicitly compared the distinct effects of concurrent versus sequential rehearsal strategies. Our paper builds upon this foundation by providing a unified theoretical analysis that explicitly characterizes the performance of concurrent and sequential rehearsal, leading to the proposal of a hybrid method that optimizes for task similarity.
