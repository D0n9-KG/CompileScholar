## Related Work

**Single-Domain Sequential and Session-Based Recommendation**
The foundation of modern sequential recommendation lies in the ability to model user behavior sequences, a task initially approached through recurrent neural networks (RNNs) that capture temporal dependencies in user sessions [1, 2]. To address the limitations of standard RNNs in capturing long-term dependencies and specific user intents, subsequent works introduced attention mechanisms; for instance, STAMP employs short-term attention and memory to prioritize relevant items [3], while NARM utilizes a hybrid encoder to distinguish between sequential behavior and the user's primary purpose within a session [4]. Parallel to these sequence modeling advancements, learning from implicit feedback has been significantly advanced by Bayesian Personalized Ranking (BPR), which provides a robust optimization criterion for ranking tasks [5]. The paradigm shifted decisively with the introduction of self-attention, where SASRec demonstrated that self-attentive models could effectively balance long-term semantic capture with computational efficiency [7]. This was further extended by BERT4Rec, which leveraged bidirectional Transformer encoders and masked language modeling to overcome the unidirectionality constraints of previous architectures [8]. More recent innovations have focused on efficiency and generative capabilities, such as low-rank decomposed self-attention for lighter models [11], sparse-interest networks that infer diverse user intentions from large concept pools [10], and the application of generative pretrained language models as open-ended recommenders [12]. Our work builds upon these single-domain foundations, specifically targeting the self-attention mechanisms established by SASRec and BERT4Rec, to enhance their applicability in cross-domain settings without altering their core architectural integrity.

**Transformer Architectures and Attention Mechanisms**
The Transformer architecture, originally proposed for machine translation, revolutionized sequence modeling by relying solely on attention mechanisms to eliminate recurrence and convolutions, thereby achieving superior quality and parallelizability [6]. As sequence lengths increased in various domains, the quadratic complexity of standard self-attention became a bottleneck, leading to the development of variants like Longformer, which employs a linearly scaling attention mechanism that combines local windowed and global attention to process long documents efficiently [9]. These architectural advancements highlight the versatility and power of attention-based models. In this paper, we revisit the core self-attention module within the context of sequential recommendation, arguing that rather than adding complex external components, the inherent capabilities of the attention mechanism can be optimized through multi-objective learning to better handle cross-domain correlations.

**Cross-Domain and Multi-Domain Sequential Recommendation**
Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) aims to leverage user interactions across multiple domains to improve prediction accuracy, a challenge complicated by data sparsity and domain shifts. Early frameworks such as DTCDR addressed dual-target cross-domain recommendation [14], while other methods focused on learning and transferring user embeddings to facilitate knowledge sharing [15]. More recent approaches have introduced sophisticated mechanisms to manage information flow between domains. For example, C^2DSR utilizes contrastive learning to jointly model single- and cross-domain preferences [16], and Pacer and Runner employs a cooperative learning framework that adaptively weights losses to mitigate negative transfer [17]. Other works have explored domain space learning for multi-domain scenarios [18], adaptive multi-task frameworks like MDMTRec [19], and parallel information-sharing networks for shared-account scenarios [20]. Additionally, mixed information flow networks have been proposed to simultaneously model behavioral and knowledge transfer [21], and mixed attention networks have been used to extract domain-specific and cross-domain information without relying heavily on overlapped users [22]. Unlike these methods, which often introduce explicit domain-aware modules or complex transfer structures, our approach, AutoCDSR, focuses on enhancing the existing self-attention mechanism via Pareto-optimal optimization. By formulating cross-domain learning as a multi-objective problem that dynamically minimizes cross-domain attention scores, we automate knowledge transfer and mitigate negative transfer with minimal computational overhead, offering a plug-and-play solution that complements rather than replaces the core transformer architecture.

**Relational and Collective Learning**
Relational learning techniques have been applied to recommendation systems to leverage structural information across different data sources. Specifically, collective matrix factorization has been utilized to perform relational learning by sharing latent factors across multiple matrices, thereby improving recommendation quality through the exploitation of inter-domain relationships [13]. While our work does not directly employ matrix factorization, it shares the underlying goal of leveraging cross-domain correlations. However, instead of relying on explicit relational structures or shared latent spaces, we derive these correlations implicitly through the optimization of self-attention weights within a sequential recommendation framework.
