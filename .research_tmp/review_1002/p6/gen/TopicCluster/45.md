## Related Work

**LLM-Assisted and Collaborative Text Annotation**
Recent advancements in natural language processing have sparked significant interest in leveraging large language models (LLMs) to streamline and augment the text annotation process, which is traditionally labor-intensive and prone to inter-annotator disagreement. Several studies have investigated the viability of LLMs as standalone or semi-automated annotators across diverse domains, including political text [4], materials science [5], and financial data [6]. While these works demonstrate that LLMs can achieve high agreement with human experts in specific contexts, they often focus on single-domain applications or fully automated pipelines. In contrast, other research emphasizes the synergistic potential of human-LLM collaboration, proposing systems like MEGAnno+ [8] and frameworks for effective verification of LLM-generated labels [9] to enhance data quality. Additionally, specific assessments of LLM-assisted annotation in nuanced linguistic tasks, such as pragmatics and discourse analysis of apologies, have compared models like GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 against human coders [7]. Our work extends this line of inquiry by applying these collaborative and automated annotation strategies specifically to the domain of fine-grained emotion extraction in mobile app reviews, addressing the unique challenges of emotional interpretation in this context.

**App Review Analysis and Tools**
The analysis of mobile application reviews has become a critical area of research for understanding user feedback and guiding software development. Existing efforts have primarily focused on developing datasets and tools that facilitate the extraction of user perceptions. For instance, Appsent was introduced as a mobile analytics tool designed to extract and visualize user perceptions from app reviews to aid in software improvement [2]. Similarly, recent contributions have expanded the scope of analysis to include multilabel multiclass sentiment and emotion datasets derived from specific linguistic contexts, such as Indonesian mobile application reviews [1]. However, most of these works concentrate on broad sentiment polarity or general sentiment classification rather than the nuanced, fine-grained emotion categories required for deeper psychological insights. This paper builds upon these foundational datasets and tools by adapting Plutchik’s emotion taxonomy to the app review domain, providing a structured annotation framework and dataset that specifically targets the complexities of fine-grained emotion analysis.

**Mobile Learning App Usage Experience**
While general app review analysis is well-studied, specific empirical investigations into the usage experience of particular app categories, such as mobile learning applications, offer valuable context for understanding user interactions. Recent empirical analyses have examined the usage experience of mobile learning apps, highlighting the specific challenges and satisfaction factors inherent to educational software [3]. These studies provide a domain-specific backdrop for understanding how users express their experiences in textual feedback. Our work relates to this line of research by acknowledging the specific nature of app usage experiences and applying fine-grained emotion extraction techniques to capture the subtle emotional nuances that standard sentiment analysis might overlook, thereby enriching the understanding of user feedback in mobile application contexts.
