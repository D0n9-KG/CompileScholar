## Related Work

**Safety Alignment and Jailbreak Attacks**
The alignment of Large Language Models (LLMs) with human values is primarily achieved through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which fine-tunes models to be helpful and harmless [1, 2]. However, this alignment is often fragile; recent studies demonstrate that safety training can be efficiently undone via low-rank adaptation [4] or subverted through "Shadow Alignment" using minimal malicious examples [5]. Furthermore, even benign fine-tuning can inadvertently compromise safety [6], while adaptive jailbreaking techniques—ranging from simple logprob exploitation [7] to universal adversarial attacks [8] and black-box query optimization [9]—have shown high success rates against leading models [3]. These vulnerabilities underscore the broader ethical and social risks associated with LLM deployment [10]. In contrast to these output-level attacks and training-based defenses, our work, COSMIC, operates entirely within the activation space, providing a robust mechanism for identifying refusal directions that remains effective even in adversarial settings where traditional output-based detection fails.

**Inference-Time Activation Steering and Control**
A growing body of literature explores inference-time interventions to control LLM behavior by manipulating internal activations. Early approaches such as Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) [17] and Activation Engineering (ActAdd) [18] introduced the concept of adding steering vectors derived from prompt pairs to residual streams to influence properties like sentiment or hallucination. More recent methods have refined this paradigm: Representation Engineering (RepE) offers a top-down framework for monitoring high-level cognitive phenomena [13], while Spectral Editing of Activations (SEA) projects representations to mitigate bias and untruthfulness [14]. Specific to safety, Conditional Activation Steering (CAST) enables rule-based refusal [19], and category-wise steering vectors allow for fine-grained safety control [15]. Notably, prior work has established that refusal behavior is mediated by a single one-dimensional subspace [11] or can be modeled as an affine function [12]. While these methods often rely on predefined templates or specific model assumptions, COSMIC generalizes this approach by using cosine similarity to automatically identify viable steering directions and target layers without requiring assumptions about specific refusal tokens or manual analysis.

**Linear Representation Geometry and Concept Erasure**
The geometric structure of LLM representations has been extensively studied, revealing that high-level concepts are often encoded in low-dimensional linear subspaces. This "linear representation hypothesis" has been formalized through causal inner products [26] and demonstrated in self-supervised models [28] and contextualized word embeddings [29, 27]. Building on this geometry, various techniques have been developed for concept erasure and bias mitigation, including Iterative Null-space Projection (INLP) [21], LEACE for closed-form linear erasure [22], and geometric debiasing of word embeddings [24]. Additionally, unsupervised methods have been proposed to discover latent knowledge by identifying directions that satisfy logical consistency [16], and geometric causal probing frameworks have been developed to identify linear concept subspaces for precise interventions [20]. COSMIC leverages these geometric insights but diverges from erasure techniques by focusing on the identification of steering directions for behavioral control rather than the removal of specific concepts, thereby preserving model utility while enhancing safety.

**Mechanistic Interpretability and Feature Decomposition**
Mechanistic interpretability seeks to understand the internal computations of LLMs by decomposing their features. The phenomenon of superposition, where sparse features are stored in overlapping directions, has been modeled theoretically [25] and addressed practically through dictionary learning [30] and sparse autoencoders [31], which aim to recover monosemantic features for finer-grained causal analysis. Beyond feature decomposition, weight-based interpretability has been advanced by analyzing bilinear MLPs to reveal low-rank structures [32] and by proposing mathematical frameworks for analyzing Transformer circuits [33]. While these methods provide deep insights into model internals, they often require extensive computational resources or specific architectural assumptions. COSMIC complements this line of work by offering a lightweight, automated framework for direction selection that does not rely on full feature decomposition or circuit tracing, making it applicable to a wider range of alignment conditions and model architectures.
