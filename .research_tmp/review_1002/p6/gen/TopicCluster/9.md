## Related Work

**Transfer Learning in Medical Imaging**
Transfer learning has emerged as a critical strategy for addressing data scarcity in medical imaging, yet its efficacy is bounded by the degree of domain shift between source and target distributions. Theoretical analyses have established that the potential improvement from transferring information between domains is strictly bounded by the probabilistic change in the algorithm, providing a formal limit to what can be achieved through standard transfer techniques [2]. In the specific context of magnetic resonance brain imaging, a systematic review of 129 studies highlights the diverse applications and strategies employed to leverage pre-trained models, while also identifying significant gaps in current methodologies [3]. While these works define the theoretical and practical landscape of transfer learning, our approach diverges by not relying on external source domains but instead synthesizing data within the target domain to mitigate class imbalance, thereby circumventing the domain shift limitations identified in [2, 3].

**Synthetic Data Generation for Medical Imaging**
The generation of synthetic data has become a standard technique to enhance the generalizability and performance of learning-based neuroimaging models, particularly in scenarios with limited annotated data. Recent investigations have demonstrated that incorporating synthetic samples can significantly improve model robustness across different scanners and populations [1]. In the domain of segmentation, methods such as SinGAN-Seg have been proposed to generate synthetic training data specifically to improve the accuracy of medical image segmentation tasks [4]. Furthermore, in the context of multiple sclerosis, encoder-decoder architectures like U-NETs have been utilized to synthesize lesions on MRI images, serving as a data augmentation tool to improve supervised lesion detection [7]. Our work extends this line of research by focusing specifically on the synthesis of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) in quantitative susceptibility maps, addressing the unique challenge of rare lesion types where standard augmentation may not suffice [1, 4, 7].

**Generative Adversarial Networks and Latent Space Inversion**
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) provide a powerful framework for estimating generative models by training a generator and discriminator in a minimax game, enabling the creation of high-fidelity synthetic data [8]. To control the generation process, conditional GANs allow for the synthesis of data conditioned on specific labels, such as class identities, which is essential for targeted data augmentation [10]. Beyond generation, the latent space of GANs offers capabilities for inversion, where an input image can be mapped to a latent code, facilitating tasks such as image restoration and manipulation [9]. We exploit this projection capability of our trained generative network to perform latent space inversion, which serves as the foundation for our novel denoising approach. Unlike standard applications of GANs for pure synthesis [8, 10] or inversion for editing [9], we utilize the latent projection to refine ambiguous labels and increase the minority class representation in a clinically interpretable manner.

**Handling Imbalanced and Noisy Data in Machine Learning**
Class imbalance and label noise are persistent challenges in medical machine learning, often leading to biased models that underperform on minority classes. Various approaches have been developed to address these issues, including imbalance-aware deep learning networks specifically designed for identifying chronic active multiple sclerosis lesions on quantitative susceptibility maps [5]. On the data augmentation front, DeepSMOTE fuses deep learning with SMOTE to generate high-quality synthetic images for balancing datasets without the need for a discriminator [6]. Regarding label noise, robust conditional GAN architectures have been proposed to learn generators from samples with noisy labels [11], while other methods model label noise as stochastic shifts in the latent space to correct noisy multilabel predictions [12]. Additionally, post-processing schemes such as Noisy Prediction Calibration (NPC) use generative models to correct noisy predictions from pre-trained classifiers into true labels [13]. Our proposed method integrates these concepts by using a generative model not only for synthesis but also for "label denoising," effectively transforming ambiguous rim cases into confident minority class samples, thereby combining the benefits of synthetic data generation and noise correction [5, 6, 11, 12, 13].
