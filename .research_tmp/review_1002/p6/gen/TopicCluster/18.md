## Related Work

### Industrial Anomaly Detection Methods and Datasets
Industrial anomaly detection has evolved significantly with the advent of deep learning, necessitating comprehensive reviews to categorize the diverse array of neural network architectures, supervision levels, and evaluation metrics employed in the field [1]. A cornerstone of this progress is the MVTec AD dataset, which provides a comprehensive real-world benchmark specifically designed for unsupervised anomaly detection tasks [2]. Early approaches often relied on generative models or autoencoders; for instance, GANomaly introduced a semi-supervised framework using conditional generative adversarial networks to learn the distribution of normal samples [4], while other methods improved unsupervised defect segmentation by incorporating structural similarity-based perceptual loss functions into convolutional autoencoders to overcome the limitations of pixel-wise reconstruction errors [3]. More recent advancements have shifted towards memory-based and feature-based methods that leverage pre-trained backbones. PatchCore achieved state-of-the-art performance by utilizing a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features from ImageNet embeddings [6], and SoftPatch further extended this paradigm by employing noise discriminators to handle noisy real-world data and soften detection boundaries [7]. Additionally, training-free approaches like AnomalyDINO have demonstrated that adapting DINOv2 features can yield strong few-shot results without fine-tuning [5]. In the context of efficiency, STLM proposed a SAM-guided two-stream lightweight model that balances mobile-friendliness with the robust generalization capabilities of Segment Anything [8]. In contrast to these methods, which often prioritize accuracy at the cost of computational heaviness or rely on complex memory banks, KairosAD leverages the promptable segmentation capabilities of MobileSAM to achieve a superior balance between detection accuracy and deployment feasibility on resource-constrained embedded devices.

### Multimodal and Self-Supervised Vision Foundation Models
The landscape of computer vision has been fundamentally reshaped by the emergence of foundation models, which have transitioned from specialist architectures to general-purpose assistants capable of handling a wide variety of downstream tasks [9]. This shift is underpinned by the ability to learn transferable visual representations through large-scale pre-training. For example, CLIP demonstrated that pre-training on image-text pairs enables zero-shot transfer to downstream tasks without dataset-specific training [10]. In the domain of segmentation, the Segment Anything project introduced a promptable model and a massive dataset of over one billion masks, establishing a new standard for zero-shot image segmentation [11]. Concurrently, self-supervised learning methods have proven highly effective in extracting robust visual features. The DINO method investigated the unique properties of self-supervised Vision Transformers, such as explicit semantic segmentation information and strong k-NN classification capabilities [12], while DINOv2 further showed that scaling self-supervised pre-training with curated data and large models produces all-purpose visual features that surpass existing benchmarks without supervision [13]. While these foundation models provide powerful feature extractors and segmentation tools, their original implementations are often too computationally intensive for edge deployment. KairosAD builds upon this line of work by specifically integrating the MobileSAM variant, which retains the core segmentation logic of these foundation models while optimizing them for the stringent resource constraints of industrial embedded systems.

### Model Compression and Efficient Inference Techniques
Deploying deep learning models on resource-constrained devices requires sophisticated model compression and efficient inference techniques. The field of Tiny Machine Learning (TML) has been extensively surveyed to cover workflows, learning algorithms, and hardware/software features tailored for IoT devices [15]. Several key compression strategies are widely adopted to reduce model size and latency. Pruning methods, which remove redundant weights or neurons, have been categorized into magnitude-based, clustering, and sensitivity analysis approaches to facilitate comparison across different architectures [16]. Quantization is another critical technique, where numerical values in neural network computations are reduced to lower precision to decrease memory footprint and latency while maintaining accuracy [17]. Furthermore, knowledge distillation has emerged as a powerful paradigm, where a smaller student model is trained to mimic the behavior of a larger teacher model, with comprehensive surveys detailing various knowledge categories, training schemes, and applications [18]. Specifically for segmentation tasks, MobileSAM introduced a lightweight version of the Segment Anything Model using decoupled distillation to achieve mobile-friendly inference speeds while maintaining performance comparable to the original model [14]. KairosAD aligns with these efficiency-focused trends by adopting MobileSAM as its backbone, thereby inheriting the benefits of distillation and lightweight design to ensure real-time performance on embedded hardware without sacrificing detection quality.

### Transformer Architectures and Attention Mechanisms
The architectural foundation of modern vision models, including those used in anomaly detection, is largely built upon Transformer architectures and attention mechanisms. The seminal work "Attention Is All You Need" proposed the Transformer, a sequence transduction model based solely on attention mechanisms that eliminates recurrence and convolutions to improve parallelization and performance [21]. This architecture has since been extensively adapted for computer vision, with comprehensive surveys covering its applications in recognition, generative modeling, and multi-modal tasks [22]. The versatility of these models is further highlighted by general surveys on attention mechanisms, which provide a taxonomy and evaluation measures for various structural characterizations in deep learning [20]. Moreover, the design of these architectures is increasingly driven by automation, with Neural Architecture Search (NAS) offering systematic solutions to address challenges in automated architecture design, evolving from early algorithms to modern, efficient search spaces [19]. KairosAD utilizes these underlying Transformer principles within the MobileSAM framework, benefiting from the parallelization and long-range dependency modeling capabilities of attention mechanisms, while specifically optimizing the architectural components to meet the latency and memory requirements of industrial embedded devices.
