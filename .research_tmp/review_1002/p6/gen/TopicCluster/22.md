## Related Work

### Supervised Semantic Segmentation Architectures
The foundation of modern semantic segmentation relies on architectures that effectively capture multi-scale context and spatial details. Early breakthroughs, such as Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs), established the paradigm of pixel-to-pixel prediction by removing fully connected layers and fusing deep semantic features with shallow appearance cues [1]. Subsequent works enhanced this by incorporating global context; ParseNet augmented local features with global average pooling [3], while the Pyramid Scene Parsing Network (PSPNet) utilized a pyramid pooling module to aggregate context from different regions [4]. The DeepLab series further refined feature resolution and field of view through atrous convolution and spatial pyramid pooling, often combined with CRFs for boundary refinement [2]. More recently, attention mechanisms and Transformers have emerged as powerful alternatives. Point-wise spatial attention networks have been applied to improve scene parsing [5], while Transformer-based models like SETR reframe segmentation as a sequence-to-sequence task to model global context without convolutional downsampling [7]. SegFormer offers an efficient design by combining a hierarchical Transformer encoder with a lightweight MLP decoder [6]. Our work builds upon these robust supervised backbones, leveraging their capacity to extract rich features as the basis for our domain adaptation framework.

### Unsupervised Domain Adaptation for Semantic Segmentation
Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation aims to bridge the distribution gap between labeled source and unlabeled target domains. Early approaches focused on adversarial learning to align feature distributions, such as adapting structured output spaces [8] and learning discriminative patch representations in clustered spaces [12]. Other methods addressed specific domain shifts, such as texture invariance through style transfer and self-training [10], or cross-modality gaps in medical imaging via synergistic image and feature adaptation [13]. To handle intra-domain variations, self-supervised strategies have been proposed to minimize gaps between easy and hard target splits [11]. More recent advancements have shifted towards self-training and consistency regularization. PixMatch enforces pixelwise consistency between predictions on target images and their perturbed versions [15], while Confidence Regularized Self-Training (CRST) treats pseudo-labels as continuous latent variables to reduce error propagation [20]. Curriculum-based approaches, such as PyCDA, construct self-motivated pyramids of target properties to improve generalization [22], and bidirectional learning frameworks alternate between image translation and segmentation adaptation [23]. Addressing class imbalance and label noise, recent works employ class-balanced self-training [18], maximum squares loss [19], uncertainty-aware pseudo-label refinement [24], and prototypical pseudo-label denoising [25]. Additionally, methods like DACS utilize cross-domain mixed sampling [26], while others incorporate auxiliary tasks like self-supervised depth estimation [21] or separated semantic features [14]. Transformer-based architectures have also been adapted for UDA, with DAFormer introducing specific training strategies like rare class sampling [16] and HRDA combining high- and low-resolution crops for context-aware adaptation [17]. While these methods achieve strong performance, they typically assume that the category sets of the source and target domains are identical or known, a limitation that our work addresses by handling unknown category shifts.

### Universal and Open-Set Domain Adaptation
To address the unrealistic assumption of known category settings, recent research has explored Universal Domain Adaptation (UDA) and Open-Set Domain Adaptation (OSDA), where the target domain may contain private classes not present in the source. The Universal Multi-Source Domain Adaptation (UMDA) setting was introduced to handle multiple source domains with different label sets and an unknown target label set, using prediction margins to estimate class reliability [27]. In the context of open-set adaptation, methods have been proposed to detect open classes [28] and utilize self-supervised tasks, such as image rotation, to separate known and unknown target samples [29]. The DANCE framework handles arbitrary category shifts by combining self-supervised neighborhood clustering for target structure learning with entropy-based feature alignment and rejection [30]. Similarly, OVANet employs a one-vs-all network to learn a dynamic threshold for rejecting unknown classes based on minimum inter-class distances in the source domain [31]. These works highlight the importance of distinguishing between common and private classes. Our proposed UniDA-SS framework extends this line of work by specifically targeting the low-confidence scores of common classes in the target domain, utilizing domain-specific prototypes and image matching to robustly identify and adapt common classes without prior knowledge of the category settings.
