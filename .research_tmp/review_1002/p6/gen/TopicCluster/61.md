## Related Work

**Fashion Outfit Recommendation and Generation**
The field of fashion recommendation has evolved from traditional retrieval-based systems to sophisticated generative frameworks that model complex user-item interactions. Early and foundational approaches focus on personalized recommendation by modeling the relationships among users, items, and outfits; for instance, Hierarchical Fashion Graph Network (HFGN) unifies compatibility modeling and personalization via embedding propagation on a hierarchical graph [1], while other methods employ learnable anchors to enhance the recommendation process [2] or jointly model garment attributes and user preferences for capsule wardrobe creation [4]. A comprehensive survey of these computational technologies categorizes various sub-tasks and reviews state-of-the-art methods, highlighting the shift towards more nuanced preference learning [3]. More recent works have transitioned from recommending existing items to generating new outfits, such as models that learn user coordination preferences for tailored suggestions [5] or the Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model, which uses a Transformer architecture to bridge user preferences for individual items and outfits at an industrial scale [6]. Furthermore, bundle recommendation has been advanced by multi-view contrastive learning frameworks like MultiCBR, which capture heterogeneous relations among users, bundles, and items [7]. Recent efforts have also proposed novel frameworks that explicitly transition from traditional recommendation to generation paradigms [11]. In the realm of generative outfit recommendation, DiFashion utilizes diffusion models to generate multiple high-fidelity, compatible, and personalized fashion images in parallel [10]. Our work, FashionDPO, builds upon this generative paradigm by addressing the limitations of diversity and reliance on supervised learning in existing generative models. Unlike previous approaches that often require task-specific reward functions or extensive supervised data, we propose a fine-tuning framework using Direct Preference Optimization to refine pre-trained models with automatically generated feedback, thereby enhancing alignment with personalized preferences without the need for explicit reward modeling.

**Item Compatibility and Complementary Recommendation**
A critical component of fashion recommendation is the accurate modeling of item compatibility and the generation of complementary products. Existing methods have explored various strategies to capture these relationships, such as Compatibility Family Learning, which embeds items into latent vectors and projects them into compatible prototypes to learn item compatibility for both recommendation and generation tasks in an end-to-end manner [12]. Another prominent approach is CRAFT, which employs an adversarial feature transformer to learn non-linear transformations between the visual manifolds of complementary item categories, enabling the generation of diverse and visually grounded recommendations [13]. While these methods effectively capture static compatibility or visual complementarity, they often operate within a recommendation or generation pipeline that may not fully account for the dynamic, personalized nature of user preferences. In contrast, our approach integrates compatibility as one of three key evaluation perspectives (alongside quality and personalization) within a multi-expert feedback generation module. By incorporating compatibility signals into the preference optimization process, FashionDPO ensures that the generated outfits not only look aesthetically pleasing but also adhere to established fashion compatibility principles, thereby bridging the gap between item-level compatibility modeling and holistic outfit generation.

**Virtual Try-On and Image Synthesis**
Virtual try-on and image synthesis technologies have seen significant advancements, moving from early coarse-to-fine strategies to high-fidelity diffusion-based methods. VITON introduced an image-based virtual try-on network that uses a coarse-to-fine strategy to transfer clothing items onto person images without 3D information [14]. Subsequent works have improved generalizability and quality, such as GP-VTON, which utilizes collaborative local-flow and global-parsing learning for general-purpose virtual try-on [15]. The advent of diffusion models has further revolutionized this field; for example, DCI-VTON proposes a diffusion-based conditional inpainting approach that employs a warping module to preserve local clothing details [16], while StableVITON learns semantic correspondence between clothing and human bodies in the latent space of a pre-trained diffusion model to achieve high-fidelity results [17]. Additionally, the Smart Fitting Room framework combines retrieval-based fashion recommendation with generative virtual try-on to provide a personalized shopping experience that considers clothing-person matching [18]. These advancements in image synthesis and try-on provide the foundational generative capabilities that our work leverages. However, while these models excel at realistic image synthesis, they often lack the specific alignment mechanisms required for personalized outfit generation. FashionDPO complements these synthesis techniques by focusing on the preference alignment of the generated outfits, ensuring that the high-fidelity images produced by such models are also semantically and personally relevant to the user.

**Diffusion Model Alignment and Personalization via Preference Optimization**
Aligning diffusion models with human preferences and personalizing generation has become a central topic in generative AI. The foundation for this lies in the development of Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) for efficient high-resolution image synthesis [8] and architectures like ControlNet, which add spatial conditioning controls to text-to-image diffusion models [9]. To align these models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) was introduced as a stable and computationally lightweight method that eliminates the need for complex reinforcement learning from human feedback [22]. This concept has been adapted for diffusion models in several ways: Diffusion-DPO aligns text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences by adapting DPO to account for diffusion likelihood [23], while D3PO directly fine-tunes diffusion models using human feedback without training a separate reward model, addressing high GPU memory requirements [24]. Furthermore, step-aware preference optimization has been proposed to align preference signals with denoising performance at each individual step of the generation process [25]. In the domain of personalized generation, JeDi presents a finetuning-free technique that learns the joint distribution of related text-image pairs for fast personalization [19], and PatchDPO introduces an additional training stage using patch-level quality estimation to improve consistency with reference images [20]. Additionally, DPO with AI feedback has been applied to enhance human image generation models [21]. Our work, FashionDPO, extends this line of research by applying Direct Preference Optimization specifically to the domain of fashion outfit generation. Unlike general-purpose alignment methods, we design a multi-expert feedback generation module that provides comprehensive and objective feedback across quality, compatibility, and personalization. This allows us to fine-tune fashion generative models using automatically generated feedback, offering a general fine-tuning approach that refines pre-trained models without the need for task-specific reward functions, thereby enhancing the model's ability to align with users' personalized preferences.
