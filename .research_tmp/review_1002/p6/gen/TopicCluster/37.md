## Related Work

**Narrow-Precision Data Formats and Representation**
Recent advancements in low-precision computing have focused on developing data formats that balance numerical accuracy with hardware efficiency. Early efforts, such as the whitepaper on quantizing deep convolutional networks, established foundational techniques for reducing bit-widths to facilitate efficient inference in standard architectures [2]. Subsequent work introduced learned quantization methods, exemplified by LQ-Nets, which optimize quantization parameters to maintain high accuracy in compact deep neural networks [1]. More recently, the introduction of Block Data Representations (BDR) with shared microexponents (MX) has demonstrated superior performance in large-scale models by leveraging a new narrow-precision format that outperforms existing standards [3]. In contrast to these general-purpose or CNN-centric approaches, our work specifically adopts the Microscaling Integer (MXInt) format to address the unique arithmetic challenges of Vision Transformers, enabling the mapping of complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm onto efficient hardware.

**Hardware-Aware and Automated Quantization**
To bridge the gap between algorithmic quantization and physical implementation, several frameworks have emerged that consider hardware constraints during the quantization process. HAQ utilizes reinforcement learning to automatically determine optimal mixed-precision bitwidths tailored to specific hardware accelerators, thereby maximizing performance within resource constraints [4]. Similarly, GOBO focuses on attention-based NLP models, proposing a technique to compress BERT parameters to 3 bits without retraining, accompanied by hardware implementations designed to minimize latency and energy consumption [5]. While these methods effectively optimize bit-widths for specific model families, they often rely on standard integer or floating-point representations that may not fully exploit the structural benefits of microscaling formats. Our approach differs by integrating MXInt-specific hardware optimizations, allowing for a more granular trade-off between accuracy, performance, and utilization specifically for ViT architectures.

**Model Compression via Pruning and Quantization**
Beyond quantization, model compression techniques such as pruning have been applied to Vision Transformers to reduce computational overhead. HeatViT, for instance, develops a hardware-efficient framework that combines adaptive token pruning with 8-bit quantization to accelerate inference on embedded FPGAs [6]. Although such methods effectively reduce the number of tokens processed, they typically retain standard data formats for the remaining operations, which can limit the potential for further hardware acceleration of complex arithmetic. Our work complements these compression strategies by focusing on the datapath design itself; by refining the datapath for MXInt, we achieve significant speedups over both Float16 and related quantization-only approaches, even when applied to full ViT models without relying solely on token reduction.
