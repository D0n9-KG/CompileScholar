## Related Work

**Generative Models for Protein Conformation Sampling**
Recent advances in generative modeling have significantly accelerated the exploration of protein conformational landscapes, moving beyond single-structure prediction to ensemble generation. Early approaches such as EigenFold utilized diffusion models to sample distributions of structures directly from sequences, capturing inherent flexibility [4]. Subsequent methods have diversified the architectural landscape; Str2Str introduced a score-based framework for zero-shot structure-to-structure translation, eliminating the need for target-specific simulation data [5], while Structure Language Models (SLM) encoded structures into discrete latent spaces for efficient conditional generation [9]. More recently, flow matching techniques have been applied to fine-tune single-state predictors like AlphaFold into ensemble generators, as seen in AlphaFlow and ESMFlow [8]. Additionally, Distributional Graphormer (DiG) has been proposed to predict equilibrium distributions by transforming simple distributions using molecular descriptors [6]. While these data-driven methods offer high efficiency, they often lack explicit physical constraints, leading to potential deviations from thermodynamic principles. Our work addresses this gap by introducing Energy-based Alignment (EBA), which calibrates these generative models to better reflect physical energy differences, thereby improving the physical plausibility of the generated ensembles.

**Physics-Informed Generative Models and Equilibrium Sampling**
Integrating physical laws into generative frameworks is critical for ensuring that sampled conformations adhere to thermodynamic equilibrium. Boltzmann Generators represent a significant step in this direction, employing neural networks to learn coordinate transformations that produce unbiased, one-shot equilibrium samples for many-body systems [2]. Similarly, ConfDiff incorporates physical prior knowledge through force-guided SE(3) diffusion, enhancing the fidelity and diversity of generated conformations [7]. Other approaches have explored the synergy between generative models and traditional physics; for instance, a combined approach utilizing diffusion models and force fields has been developed for coarse-grained molecular dynamics [3]. Furthermore, generative modeling paradigms for molecular dynamics trajectories have been proposed to learn flexible surrogate models capable of forward simulation and transition path sampling [10]. Despite these advancements, effectively integrating physical supervision into standard energy-based objectives remains challenging due to intractable optimization issues. In contrast to these methods, which often rely on complex physical priors or surrogate dynamics, our EBA method provides a direct alignment mechanism that efficiently balances conformational states based on energy feedback, offering a more straightforward path to physically consistent ensemble generation.

**Reinforcement Learning and Preference Optimization for Generative Models**
Aligning generative models with specific objectives or preferences has been a major focus in machine learning, particularly through reinforcement learning (RL) and preference optimization. In the domain of natural language processing, it has been demonstrated that training models to optimize for human preferences via RL with a reward model significantly improves output quality over supervised methods [11]. This paradigm has been extended to diffusion models, where Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO) allows for direct optimization of models for downstream objectives such as image quality [12]. Alternatively, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a computationally lightweight and stable alternative, aligning models using a simple classification loss derived from preference data without the complexity of traditional RL [13]. These techniques have primarily been applied to text and image generation. We adapt the principles of preference alignment to the domain of protein structure generation, utilizing physical energy feedback as a proxy for preference to guide the generative process, thereby ensuring that the model prioritizes physically stable conformations.

**Foundational Protein Structure Prediction and Energy Functions**
The development of modern generative protein models is deeply rooted in foundational advances in structure prediction and energy function design. AlphaFold revolutionized the field by achieving highly accurate protein structure prediction, establishing a benchmark for single-state accuracy that subsequent ensemble methods often build upon [1]. Complementing these predictive capabilities, the Rosetta all-atom energy function has long served as a standard for macromolecular modeling and design, providing the physical scoring metrics necessary to evaluate structural plausibility [14]. While AlphaFold and Rosetta have traditionally been used for static structure prediction and design, respectively, our work bridges these foundational tools with modern generative techniques. By leveraging the accuracy of predictors like AlphaFold and the physical rigor of energy functions like Rosetta, our EBA method enhances the generation of dynamic protein ensembles, ensuring that the resulting structures are not only diverse but also physically robust.
