## Related Work

**Large Language Model Architectures and Foundation Models**
The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has been driven by scaling laws and the development of both proprietary and open-source foundation models. Proprietary systems such as GPT-4 [1] and PaLM [2] have demonstrated that scaling parameter counts and training data significantly enhances few-shot learning and reasoning capabilities, with PaLM specifically leveraging TPUs to showcase the benefits of massive scale. In contrast, the open-source community has focused on accessibility and efficiency, exemplified by LLaMA [3], which achieved competitive performance with larger proprietary models using exclusively public datasets, and Llama 2 [4], which further optimized these models for dialogue use cases with improved safety and helpfulness. While these foundational architectures establish the state-of-the-art in generative quality, their sheer size poses significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. CLONE addresses this gap by providing a co-design approach that adapts these powerful foundation models for efficient, latency-aware inference at the edge, rather than relying solely on the raw computational power assumed by cloud-centric deployments.

**LLM Inference Serving Systems and Resource Management**
To manage the high computational and memory demands of LLMs, recent research has focused on optimizing inference serving systems and resource allocation. PowerInfer [5] exploits the power-law distribution of neuron activations to accelerate serving on consumer-grade hardware, while ExeGPT [6] introduces constraint-aware resource scheduling to optimize throughput under strict latency constraints. Architectural innovations include Splitwise [7], which separates compute-intensive prompt processing from memory-intensive token generation across different hardware, and vLLM [8], which utilizes PagedAttention to manage KV cache memory efficiently, thereby increasing serving throughput. For environments with limited memory, LLM in a flash [9] enables inference by storing parameters in flash memory and optimizing data transfer, and FlexGen [22] aggregates memory from GPU, CPU, and disk to enable high-throughput generation on a single GPU. Additionally, Orca [23] provides a distributed serving system specifically designed for transformer-based generative models. Although these systems significantly improve throughput and memory efficiency in datacenter or high-end GPU settings, they often do not fully address the stringent energy and latency constraints of always-on edge devices. CLONE complements this line of work by integrating real-time energy optimization and hardware specialization to meet the unique power and latency requirements of edge computing.

**Model Compression, Quantization, and Distillation**
Model compression techniques are essential for reducing the footprint of LLMs, with methods spanning quantization, pruning, and distillation. Early work on quantized neural networks [11] laid the groundwork for low-precision training, which has been advanced by LLM.int8() [12] for efficient 8-bit matrix multiplication and SmoothQuant [14], which migrates quantization difficulty from activations to weights to enable accurate post-training quantization. To exploit sparsity, SparseGPT [16] achieves high sparsity in one-shot without retraining, Pruner-Zero [15] evolves symbolic pruning metrics from scratch, and DejaVu [13] exploits contextual sparsity to skip unnecessary computations during inference. In the domain of knowledge distillation, MiniLLM [18] utilizes reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence to improve generation quality in smaller models, while DistiLLM [17] introduces a skew KLD loss and adaptive off-policy approach to achieve significant speedups. While these algorithms effectively reduce model size and computational cost, they are often applied in isolation or for cloud-based inference. CLONE integrates these compression strategies within a broader algorithm-hardware co-design framework, ensuring that the compressed models are optimized not just for size, but for the specific energy and latency profiles of edge accelerators.

**ML Frameworks, Hardware Co-Design, and System Optimization**
The deployment of AI models is further supported by robust machine learning frameworks and specialized hardware co-design techniques. General-purpose frameworks such as PyTorch [19], TensorFlow [20], and DeepSpeed [21] provide the foundational infrastructure for building and training large-scale models. However, achieving optimal performance on edge devices requires deeper system-level optimizations. Recent reviews highlight the importance of combining algorithm-level techniques with hardware co-design and compiler approaches [10]. Specific hardware-aware strategies include EdgeBERT [24], which uses entropy-based early exit and dynamic voltage-frequency scaling (DVFS) for energy-efficient NLP inference, and ApproxCaliper [25], a programmable framework for application-aware neural network optimization. The efficacy of DVFS is further explored in studies on variation-aware scaling [26] and system-level analysis of fast, per-core DVFS using on-chip switching regulators [27]. Furthermore, NeuOS [28] presents a multi-dimensional optimization framework for achieving latency predictability in DNN-driven autonomous systems. CLONE builds upon these insights by specializing in a 28nm scalable hardware accelerator system that synergistically integrates real-time energy optimization with model-level algorithms, thereby achieving superior latency and energy efficiency compared to general-purpose frameworks or isolated optimization techniques.
