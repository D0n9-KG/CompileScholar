## Related Work

**Non-autoregressive TTS Architectures**
The shift from autoregressive to non-autoregressive (NAR) architectures has fundamentally altered the landscape of text-to-speech synthesis by enabling parallel generation of mel-spectrograms, thereby significantly improving inference speed and robustness [2, 3]. While FastSpeech pioneered this feed-forward Transformer approach to decouple duration prediction from acoustic modeling, FastSpeech 2 further refined the paradigm by directly training on ground-truth targets and incorporating explicit pitch, energy, and duration as conditional inputs, thus eliminating the need for teacher-student distillation pipelines [2, 3]. These architectures provide a stable and controllable foundation for speech synthesis; however, their reliance on fixed conditional inputs during training often limits the flexibility for post-hoc adjustments. Our work builds upon these NAR foundations by proposing a method that manipulates internal representations at inference time, allowing for dynamic control without the architectural constraints or retraining requirements inherent in these baseline models.

**Speaking Rate and Speed Control**
Controlling the temporal dynamics of speech, particularly speaking rate, remains a critical challenge in TTS systems, with existing solutions ranging from explicit conditioning to direct representation manipulation. Some approaches integrate speaking rate factors directly into the duration predictor, enabling implicit speed control within non-autoregressive frameworks [5]. In contrast, other methods bypass explicit duration modeling by directly manipulating the output embeddings of the encoder in end-to-end models to adjust the temporal flow of the generated speech [9]. While these techniques offer effective mechanisms for speed adjustment, they typically require specific architectural modifications or access to specific intermediate layers during the forward pass. Our proposed Counterfactual Activation Editing extends this line of work by offering a model-agnostic approach that adjusts prosodic features, including rate, through counterfactual interventions on internal activations, thereby providing a more generalized solution for inference-time refinement.

**Prosody and Style Modeling and Transfer**
The ability to model, control, and transfer prosodic and stylistic attributes is essential for generating expressive and natural speech, with recent advances focusing on both supervised and unsupervised learning strategies. Unsupervised methods, such as Global Style Tokens (GSTs), learn a bank of embeddings to capture acoustic expressiveness without explicit labels, facilitating style transfer in end-to-end synthesis [6]. Similarly, extensions to the Tacotron architecture have enabled the learning of latent prosody spaces from reference audio, allowing for end-to-end prosody transfer across different speakers and texts [7]. On the other hand, techniques like Ctrl-P focus on the temporal control of prosodic variation, offering finer-grained manipulation of prosodic contours [4]. Additionally, earlier work in unit-selection synthesis explored methods for generating expressive prosody, highlighting the long-standing interest in this domain [1]. Although these methods successfully capture and transfer style, they often depend on specialized modules or additional training data. Our approach differs by leveraging counterfactual activation editing to achieve post-hoc prosody control, eliminating the need for dedicated style modules or extensive retraining while preserving the synthesis quality of the pre-trained model.

**Hierarchical Latent Control and Correction**
Beyond basic prosody and speed, recent research has explored hierarchical and corrective frameworks to address finer-grained control and error mitigation in speech synthesis. Hierarchical generative models based on variational autoencoder frameworks have been proposed to enable fine-grained, disentangled control over latent speech attributes, such as speaking style, accent, and noise conditions [8]. In the domain of error correction, the Speech Audio Corrector utilizes speech from non-target speakers to perform one-off corrections of mispronunciations in grapheme-input TTS systems, addressing the limitations of traditional grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries in low-resource settings [10]. These works demonstrate the potential of latent space manipulation and external audio cues for enhancing synthesis quality and accuracy. Our method aligns with this trajectory by targeting internal representations for both prosodic adjustment and mispronunciation correction, offering a unified, model-agnostic framework that operates at inference time without the need for external reference audio or complex hierarchical architectures.
