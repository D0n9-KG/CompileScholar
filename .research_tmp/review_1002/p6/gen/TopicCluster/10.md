## Related Work

**Curriculum Learning**
Curriculum learning is a training paradigm that improves model performance by ordering data from easy to hard instances, thereby guiding the optimization process without incurring additional computational costs [1]. Subsequent surveys have established a comprehensive taxonomy of these methods, categorizing various strategies for ranking samples to enhance machine learning outcomes [2]. While prior work has primarily focused on data ordering for classification and regression tasks, our work extends this principle to the pre-training objective itself. Specifically, we propose a curriculum for multi-token prediction (MTP) that gradually adjusts the complexity of the prediction target, rather than merely reordering the input data, to address the specific challenges smaller language models face with complex objectives.

**Multi-token Prediction and Pre-training**
Recent advancements in pre-training objectives have moved beyond standard next-token prediction (NTP) to incorporate future context. ProphetNet introduced a sequence-to-sequence pre-training method that predicts future n-grams to improve model performance [3]. Concurrently, research has investigated the internal representations of transformers, demonstrating that single hidden states contain sufficient information to anticipate subsequent tokens, as visualized by the "Future Lens" tool [4]. Most notably, recent work has demonstrated that training large language models to predict multiple future tokens simultaneously via multi-token prediction (MTP) significantly improves sample efficiency, downstream capabilities, and inference speed compared to NTP [5]. However, these benefits have been observed primarily in large models, with smaller language models (SLMs) struggling to leverage the MTP objective effectively. Our paper addresses this gap by introducing a curriculum learning strategy specifically designed to enable SLMs to benefit from MTP, thereby improving both downstream NTP performance and generative quality.

**Self-Speculative Decoding via Layer Skipping**
To accelerate inference, several methods have proposed self-speculative decoding techniques that utilize the target model’s own layers for drafting. LayerSkip enables early exit inference and self-speculative decoding by allowing the model to exit at intermediate layers [7]. Building on this, Kangaroo proposes a lossless framework that uses a shallow sub-network as a draft model with an additional early exiting mechanism [8]. Similarly, Draft & Verify accelerates inference by using layer-skipping to generate draft tokens that are subsequently verified by the original model, ensuring lossless output without extra training [9]. More recently, SWIFT introduced an on-the-fly approach that adaptively skips intermediate layers to serve as a draft model, achieving plug-and-play acceleration without auxiliary models [10]. These methods rely on the structural redundancy of deep networks to speed up generation. Our work complements these approaches by showing that a forward curriculum for MTP training retains the benefits of self-speculative decoding, whereas a reverse curriculum, despite improving NTP performance, fails to provide such acceleration benefits.

**Speculative Decoding with Auxiliary Heads**
Another line of research focuses on accelerating inference by adding auxiliary prediction heads to the model. Blockwise parallel decoding predicts multiple time steps in parallel and backtracks to the longest validated prefix to accelerate generation in autoregressive models [6]. Medusa develops an inference acceleration framework that adds multiple decoding heads to an LLM to predict several subsequent tokens in parallel using tree-based attention, reducing sequential decoding steps [12]. Furthermore, KOALA enhances speculative decoding by employing a multi-layer draft head architecture combined with adversarial learning to improve token prediction accuracy and latency speedup [11]. These methods typically require additional training or architectural modifications to the inference pipeline. In contrast, our approach integrates the multi-token prediction objective directly into the pre-training phase via a curriculum, aiming to improve the base model's capabilities and inference efficiency without necessarily relying on post-hoc auxiliary head training for the primary performance gains.
