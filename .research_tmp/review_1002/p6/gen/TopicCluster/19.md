## Related Work

**Speech-Driven 3D Facial Animation Synthesis**
Recent advancements in speech-driven 3D facial animation have shifted from traditional kinematic models to deep learning-based architectures that capture complex audio-visual correlations. Early approaches such as VOCA [10] established the foundation for generating identity-specific, realistic 3D animations by learning from 4D face datasets, enabling the control of speaking styles without retargeting. Subsequent works have increasingly leveraged self-supervised speech representations to enhance feature extraction; for instance, FaceFormer [3] employs Transformer-based autoregressive modeling with biased attention to encode long-term audio context, while FaceXHuBERT [6] utilizes HuBERT embeddings alongside binary emotion and identity conditions to capture subtle, personalized speech cues in a text-less manner. To address the inherent ambiguity in cross-modal mapping, CodeTalker [4] reformulates the task as a discrete code query problem within a learned codebook, reducing uncertainty and promoting motion vividness. More recently, diffusion-based models have emerged to handle the one-to-many nature of speech-to-face mapping. FaceDiffuser [5] introduces a non-deterministic diffusion framework trained on both vertex and blendshape data, while 3DiFACE [9] proposes a lightweight audio-conditioned diffusion model that facilitates stochastic generation and motion editing. Furthermore, Media2Face [16] extends this paradigm by incorporating multi-modality guidance from audio, text, and images to generate high-fidelity co-speech animations using a generalized neural parametric facial asset. Generalized deep learning approaches [1] have also contributed to the robustness of these systems. In contrast to these methods, which often rely on standard self-supervised encoders that may suffer from feature coupling among phonetically similar syllables, our work introduces Wav2Sem, a plug-and-play module that explicitly decorrelates audio encodings to mitigate the averaging effect in lip shape generation.

**Emotion and Style Disentanglement in Facial Animation**
A significant line of research focuses on disentangling semantic content from affective or stylistic attributes to achieve more controllable and expressive facial animations. EMOTE [7] proposes a model with explicit emotion control by employing decoupled losses for speech and emotion, along with a content-emotion exchange mechanism to generate 3D talking-head avatars. Similarly, EmoTalk [8] presents an end-to-end neural network that utilizes an emotion disentangling encoder to separate emotion from content, contributing a large-scale 3D emotional talking face dataset to support this task. Beyond emotion, Mimic [11] addresses the disentanglement of subject-specific speaking styles from semantic content, aiming to synthesize more realistic animations by isolating stylistic variations. Another approach involves using controllable emotion controllers to adjust the type and intensity of emotions in generated animations [2]. While these methods successfully decouple high-level attributes like emotion or style, they typically operate on the assumption that the underlying audio features are sufficiently distinct for the target content. Our work complements this line of research by addressing a lower-level issue: the coupling of near-homophone syllables in the feature space. By decorrelating these specific audio encodings, Wav2Sem provides a more precise semantic foundation that can be integrated with existing disentanglement frameworks to further enhance the naturalness and precision of expressive animations.

**Self-Supervised Speech Representation Learning**
The efficacy of speech-driven facial animation is heavily dependent on the quality of the audio encoder, with self-supervised learning (SSL) emerging as a dominant paradigm for extracting robust speech representations. Deep Speech [13] pioneered the end-to-end deep learning approach for speech recognition, demonstrating that robust functions could be learned directly from audio without hand-designed components. This was further advanced by wav2vec 2.0 [14], which introduced a framework for learning powerful speech representations by masking latent inputs and solving a contrastive task, significantly reducing the need for labeled data. HuBERT [15] subsequently proposed a masked prediction approach using offline clustering to provide aligned target labels, matching or improving upon the performance of wav2vec 2.0. These models have become the standard backbones for many recent facial animation systems, such as FaceFormer [3] and FaceDiffuser [5]. However, our analysis reveals that while these SSL models excel at general speech understanding, their feature spaces often exhibit significant coupling for phonetically similar syllables with distinct lip shapes. Wav2Sem is designed to post-process these standard SSL features, leveraging additional semantic information to decorrelate the encodings and thereby overcoming the limitations inherent in the raw self-supervised representations.

**Lip Sync, Cross-Modal Retrieval, and Holistic Gesture Generation**
Beyond 3D facial animation, related work in video lip-syncing, cross-modal retrieval, and holistic gesture generation provides valuable insights into audio-visual alignment. Wav2Lip [12] addresses the challenge of lip-syncing arbitrary identities in unconstrained videos by learning from a powerful lip-sync discriminator, establishing rigorous benchmarks for synchronization accuracy. In the domain of retrieval, joint embeddings derived from text and audio data have been utilized for cross-modal audio search [17]. For broader motion generation, methods that integrate audio, text, and visual information facilitate the generation of talking faces [18]. Furthermore, EMAGE [19] extends the scope to holistic full-body co-speech gesture generation, employing a Masked Audio Gesture Transformer and a new mesh-level dataset to model expressive gestures from audio and masked inputs. While these works focus on 2D video synchronization or full-body motion, they share the common challenge of aligning audio semantics with visual motion. Our approach is distinct in its focus on 3D facial animation and its specific mechanism for resolving semantic ambiguity in the audio feature space, which is critical for the precise articulation required in high-fidelity 3D rendering.
