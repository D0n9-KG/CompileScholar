## Related Work

**Video Frame Interpolation**
Video frame interpolation (VFI) aims to synthesize intermediate frames between two given inputs, a task that has evolved from traditional optical flow-based methods to sophisticated deep learning architectures. Recent surveys have systematically organized the vast landscape of VFI methodologies, highlighting the progression from simple pixel-wise predictions to complex feature-based synthesis [1]. To address challenges such as large motion and occlusions, several approaches incorporate explicit geometric cues; for instance, depth-aware methods utilize hierarchical features to detect occlusions and improve synthesis quality [2]. In contrast, efficiency-focused architectures like RIFE estimate intermediate flows end-to-end to achieve real-time performance with state-of-the-art accuracy [3], while others employ bidirectional flow estimation and pixel fusion techniques, such as Many-to-Many splatting, to reduce computational overhead and handle artifacts [4]. More recently, hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures have been proposed to explicitly extract motion and appearance information via inter-frame attention, balancing efficiency with high-quality reconstruction [5]. Our work leverages these advancements by training a lightweight VFI model, PS4PRO, not merely for video generation, but as an implicit world prior to augment photo supervision in 3D reconstruction.

**Neural Radiance Fields for Autonomous Driving**
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have been extensively applied to autonomous driving scenarios, where robust novel view synthesis is critical for perception and planning. These methods often integrate multi-sensor data to overcome the limitations of camera-only inputs. For example, NeuRAD introduces a robust novel view synthesis framework that incorporates extensive sensor modeling for both cameras and LiDAR, utilizing a simple network design to enhance generalizability [6]. Similarly, Lightning NeRF employs an efficient hybrid scene representation that leverages LiDAR geometry priors to significantly improve synthesis quality while accelerating both training and rendering speeds [7]. While these methods focus on leveraging explicit geometric sensors for driving-specific tasks, our approach complements this line of work by using video frame interpolation as a data augmentation strategy to enrich the photo supervision, thereby improving reconstruction performance in complex driving environments without necessarily requiring additional sensor modalities.

**Neural Radiance Fields and 3D Reconstruction**
The field of neural rendering and 3D reconstruction has seen rapid advancements in improving fidelity and handling sparse view settings. High-fidelity reconstruction methods, such as AligNeRF, combine MLPs with convolutional layers and employ alignment-aware training strategies to recover high-frequency details in high-resolution scenes [8]. To address the inherent ill-posedness of reconstruction from limited views, regularization techniques have been introduced; DiffusioNeRF, for instance, uses a denoising diffusion model trained on RGBD patches to predict gradients that refine geometry and color in few-view scenarios [9]. Furthermore, recent works have integrated 2D diffusion priors into 3D Gaussian Splatting, such as 3DGS-Enhancer, to ensure view consistency and enhance reconstruction quality in unbounded scenes [10]. Our method aligns with this trend of leveraging 2D priors for 3D tasks but specifically utilizes a video frame interpolation model to generate synthetic views, effectively augmenting the training data to mitigate the uncertainty in geometry and appearance across views.

**Diffusion-Based Novel View Synthesis**
Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful tool for novel view synthesis, capable of generating high-fidelity images from sparse inputs. ViewCrafter, for example, tames video diffusion models to synthesize high-fidelity novel views from single or sparse images by leveraging point-based 3D representations and an iterative strategy to extend view coverage [11]. This approach supports applications ranging from real-time rendering to text-to-3D generation. While diffusion-based methods offer strong generative capabilities, they can be computationally intensive. In contrast, our proposed PS4PRO utilizes a lightweight video frame interpolation model to provide implicit world priors, offering a more efficient pathway for augmenting photo supervision in neural rendering pipelines compared to heavy diffusion-based synthesis.
