## Related Work

**Transformer Architectures for Time Series Forecasting**
Recent advancements in time series forecasting have been dominated by Transformer-based architectures designed to capture long-range dependencies and complex temporal patterns. Early efforts focused on improving computational efficiency for long sequences, with Informer introducing ProbSparse self-attention and self-attention distilling to reduce complexity [1], and Pyraformer employing low-complexity pyramidal attention mechanisms for long-range modeling [2]. Subsequent works have explored various structural enhancements to address specific limitations of standard Transformers; for instance, LogSparse Transformer utilizes convolutional self-attention to enhance locality and break memory bottlenecks [3], while Autoformer leverages series periodicity through an Auto-Correlation mechanism within a decomposition architecture [6]. More recent innovations have shifted towards capturing multi-dimensional and multi-scale dependencies, such as Crossformer’s focus on cross-dimension dependencies for multivariate series [5], FEDformer’s integration of frequency-enhanced decomposition with Fourier-based attention [7], and MICN’s approach to multi-scale local and global context modeling [8]. Additionally, PatchTST has demonstrated the efficacy of segmenting series into subseries-level patches with channel-independence for long-term forecasting [4], and TimesNet has proposed a task-general backbone that transforms 1D series into 2D tensors to model complex temporal variations [9]. While these models achieve state-of-the-art accuracy in point forecasting, they typically operate as standalone predictors without direct alignment to downstream decision-making objectives, a gap that our RTS-PnO framework addresses by integrating such models into a risk-aware allocation pipeline.

**Non-Transformer and Probabilistic Time Series Models**
In contrast to the prevalence of Transformer architectures, several studies have questioned their necessity and explored alternative modeling paradigms. Notably, recent work has demonstrated that simple linear models can outperform sophisticated Transformer-based approaches for long-term forecasting due to the permutation-invariant nature of self-attention [10]. Similarly, TSMixer introduces a lightweight MLP-Mixer architecture that achieves superior performance with minimal computing usage by incorporating online reconciliation heads and hybrid channel modeling [11]. Beyond deterministic point forecasts, probabilistic approaches are critical for capturing uncertainty in financial applications. DeepAR, for example, proposes a methodology for probabilistic forecasting using autoregressive recurrent networks trained on large numbers of related time series to produce accurate distributional forecasts [12]. Our work builds upon these insights by remaining agnostic to the underlying forecasting model, allowing it to leverage the strengths of both efficient non-Transformer models and probabilistic frameworks while explicitly calibrating their uncertainty for risk-aware decision-making.

**Conformal Prediction and Uncertainty Quantification**
Accurate quantification of predictive uncertainty is essential for risk-aware financial decisions, and conformal prediction has emerged as a robust, distribution-free method for this purpose. Recent developments include general frameworks and algorithms like EnbPI, which construct prediction intervals for time series without requiring data exchangeability or model retraining [15]. Other approaches focus specifically on the dynamics of time series, such as methods for constructing conformal prediction intervals for dynamic time-series [14] and broader techniques for conformal time-series forecasting [13]. These methods provide theoretical guarantees on coverage probability, which is crucial for managing financial risk. In our RTS-PnO framework, we incorporate adaptive forecasting uncertainty calibration inspired by these conformal prediction techniques to ensure that the predicted uncertainty is well-calibrated and directly usable for risk-aware fund allocation, thereby bridging the gap between statistical uncertainty quantification and practical optimization.

**Predict-then-Optimize and Decision-Focused Learning**
The integration of machine learning predictions with optimization decisions has been extensively studied under the paradigms of Predict-then-Optimize (PtO) and Decision-Focused Learning (DFL). The Smart "Predict, then Optimize" (SPO) framework and its convex surrogate, the SPO+ loss, were introduced to train prediction models that directly minimize the decision error of downstream optimization problems [26], with subsequent work deriving generalization bounds [17] and developing risk bounds and calibration results for the SPO+ loss [21]. A comprehensive review of decision-focused learning has analyzed both gradient-based and gradient-free techniques, evaluating various methods across multiple problems [22]. To facilitate end-to-end training, differentiable optimization layers such as OptNet [23] and differentiable convex optimization layers [24] have been developed to integrate optimization constraints directly into neural network architectures. Furthermore, surrogate loss functions based on Noise Contrastive Estimation and solution caching schemes have been proposed to reduce computational costs in predict-and-optimize training [25]. Empirical evaluations have compared cost-sensitive learning strategies for PtO versus PnO approaches [20], and modular frameworks have been developed to benchmark these methods across combinatorial optimization problems [19]. In the specific domain of finance, frameworks combining machine learning and operations research for prescriptive analytics [16] and Predict-Then-Optimize frameworks for online fund recommendation [18] have been proposed. However, many existing methods suffer from goal mismatch or fail to adequately account for the uncertainty introduced by state-of-the-art forecasting models. Our RTS-PnO framework addresses these limitations by providing an end-to-end training mechanism with objective alignment measurement and adaptive uncertainty calibration, ensuring that the forecasting model is optimized specifically for the downstream fund allocation task rather than generic prediction accuracy.
