## Related Work

**Classical Iterative Optimization Algorithms for Inverse Problems**
Traditional approaches to solving inverse problems in imaging rely heavily on iterative optimization techniques that balance data fidelity with regularization constraints. Early methods employed gradient projection and thresholding strategies to recover sparse signals from compressed measurements, with foundational work demonstrating the robustness and near-optimal error guarantees of iterative hard thresholding [1, 2]. To improve convergence rates and handle linear inverse problems more efficiently, the fast iterative shrinkage-thresholding algorithm (FISTA) was introduced, significantly reducing the number of iterations required for convergence [3]. For problems involving non-smooth $\ell_1$-norm regularization, Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) algorithms have been widely adopted due to their ability to decouple complex subproblems [4]. Beyond standard sparsity, more sophisticated regularization schemes, such as nonlocal low-rank regularization, have been developed to exploit structural redundancies in natural images for enhanced signal recovery [5]. Furthermore, half-quadratic regularization has been applied to nonlinear image recovery tasks, providing a flexible framework for handling non-convex optimization landscapes [27]. While these classical methods provide strong theoretical guarantees, they often suffer from slow convergence speeds and limited flexibility in adapting to varying compression ratios without extensive parameter tuning, motivating the integration of learned components into these iterative frameworks.

**Plug-and-Play and Denoiser-Based Regularization Frameworks**
Plug-and-Play (PnP) algorithms represent a paradigm shift by replacing explicit regularization terms with off-the-shelf deep denoisers, thereby leveraging the implicit priors learned from large datasets. The Denoising-based Approximate Message Passing (D-AMP) framework pioneered this approach by integrating generic denoisers into the message passing algorithm, achieving state-of-the-art performance with high computational efficiency [6]. However, standard PnP methods often lack convergence guarantees, particularly when using non-convex regularizers. To address this, several studies have proposed convergent variants; for instance, one approach implements the denoiser as a gradient descent step on a neural network functional within a half-quadratic splitting scheme, ensuring convergence to stationary points [7]. Similarly, proximal denoiser approaches have been developed to guarantee convergence for PnP optimization with nonconvex regularization [8]. The Learned Proximal Network (LPN) framework takes this further by providing exact proximal operators for data-driven nonconvex regularizers, enabling the recovery of the log-prior of the true data distribution [9]. More recently, Stochastic Deep Restoration Priors (ShaRP) have been introduced to leverage ensembles of deep restoration models, improving the handling of structured artifacts and enabling self-supervised training for imaging inverse problems [10]. Additionally, Regularization by Denoising (RED) offers a framework that uses denoising algorithms to define an explicit image-adaptive regularization functional, bridging the gap between implicit PnP priors and explicit optimization [28]. Although PnP methods offer flexibility across different compression ratios, they are generally limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed compared to fully unrolled networks, as the iterative process must be run to convergence or near-convergence at test time.

**Deep Unfolding and Optimization-Inspired Networks for Compressive Sensing**
Deep unfolding, or unrolling, transforms truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving superior accuracy and faster inference than PnP methods. Early interpretable networks, such as ISTA-Net, unrolled the Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm to create a deep network for image compressive sensing [15]. Similarly, ADMM-CSNet unrolled the ADMM algorithm, allowing for the learning of both data fidelity and regularization parameters [16]. In the domain of denoising-based unfolding, AMP-Net integrated denoising steps into the Approximate Message Passing framework, while Learned D-AMP provided a principled neural network approach for compressive image recovery [14, 17]. To address information loss across stages, Memory-Augmented Deep Unfolding Networks (MADUN) introduced high-throughput short-term and cross-stage long-term memory mechanisms [18]. Recent advancements have focused on enhancing the representation capabilities of unrolled networks through attention mechanisms and transformers. For example, Saunet utilizes spatial attention, while OCTUF employs a lightweight cross-attention transformer with inertia-supplied and projection-guided mechanisms to reduce parameter complexity [22, 23]. Other architectures, such as Dr2-net, leverage deep residual connections, and CPP-Net incorporates multi-scale feature fusion into the CP-PPA unfolding framework [13, 25]. Furthermore, DGUNet integrates gradient estimation and inter-stage information pathways into Proximal Gradient Descent to handle complex degradations [20]. In the specific context of single-pixel imaging, HATNet utilizes a hybrid-attention Transformer and a Kronecker SPI model to improve imaging quality and computational efficiency [26]. UFC-Net has also been proposed to unroll fixed-point continuous networks for deep compressive sensing [24]. Despite their high performance, most existing unrolling networks are trained for specific compression ratios, requiring fine-tuning or retraining when the sampling budget changes, which limits their practical flexibility.

**End-to-End and Hybrid Deep Learning Architectures for Image Reconstruction**
In contrast to optimization-inspired unrolling, end-to-end and hybrid architectures aim to learn the mapping from measurements to images directly or through flexible hybrid structures. ReconNet introduced a non-iterative convolutional neural network that rapidly reconstructs images by feeding intermediate reconstructions into an off-the-shelf denoiser, bypassing explicit iterative optimization [11]. Scalable convolutional neural networks have also been developed to handle image compressed sensing with varying input sizes [12]. More recently, hybrid architectures have emerged that combine the strengths of different deep learning paradigms. CSformer, for instance, bridges convolution and transformer architectures, using CNNs for local spatial details and Transformers for global context to enhance representation learning in compressive sensing [21]. Similarly, COAST proposes a controllable arbitrary-sampling network that enables a single model to handle various and unseen sampling matrices through random projection augmentation and dynamic feature modulation, addressing the flexibility issue inherent in many CR-specific models [19]. While these end-to-end and hybrid approaches offer significant speed advantages and some degree of flexibility, they often lack the interpretability and strong theoretical grounding of optimization-inspired methods. This paper aims to bridge the gap between these two lines of work by proposing a proximal unrolling framework that retains the interpretability and accuracy of unrolled optimization algorithms while achieving the flexibility of PnP and hybrid methods to handle varying compression ratios with a single model.
