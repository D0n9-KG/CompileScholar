## Related Work

**Transformer-based Sequential Recommendation**
Transformer architectures have become the dominant paradigm in sequential recommendation due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies and parallelize training. Early foundational work, such as SASRec, introduced self-attention mechanisms to balance long-term semantic capture with sparse action selection, outperforming traditional Markov Chain and RNN-based approaches [8]. Subsequent studies expanded this framework by incorporating bidirectional context; BERT4Rec, for instance, utilizes a Cloze task to predict masked items, leveraging bidirectional encoder representations to enhance sequence modeling [9]. To address the computational bottlenecks of standard attention, several efficiency-oriented variants have been proposed. STRec employs sparse attention mechanisms to improve both efficiency and effectiveness [4], while LinRec introduces a linear attention mechanism that reduces computational complexity for long-term sequences without sacrificing learning capabilities [10]. Beyond efficiency, recent efforts have focused on enriching the attention mechanism with additional signal types or user-specific adaptations. FEARec enhances self-attention by integrating frequency-domain analysis to capture periodic and multi-scale characteristics [1], SSE-PT utilizes a personalized transformer architecture to model user-specific sequential patterns [3], and DMAN segments long behavior sequences into sub-sequences with dynamic memory blocks to efficiently capture long-term interests [7]. Furthermore, to handle the complexity of real-world user interactions, multi-behavior sequential transformers have been developed to model diverse user behaviors within a unified framework [5]. In contrast to these approaches, which primarily rely on quadratic or linearized attention mechanisms, our work proposes STAR-Rec, which synergistically combines preference-aware attention with state-space modeling to better handle length variance and pattern diversity.

**RNN-based Session and Sequential Recommendation**
Prior to the widespread adoption of Transformers, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were the standard for modeling sequential user behaviors, particularly in session-based recommendation tasks. Early applications of RNNs with ranking loss functions demonstrated the ability to model short user sessions effectively, establishing a baseline for sequential modeling [11]. To address the limitation of session isolation, hierarchical RNN architectures were proposed to personalize recommendations by transferring and evolving latent hidden states across different user sessions, thereby incorporating cross-session information [6]. While these RNN-based methods provided a strong foundation for sequential modeling, they suffer from inherent limitations in parallelization and capturing long-range dependencies compared to modern attention-based and state-space models. Our work builds upon the sequential modeling objectives of these RNN-based approaches but replaces the recurrent structure with a more efficient and expressive hybrid architecture that better accommodates variable sequence lengths.

**Mamba and State Space Models for Sequential Recommendation**
Recently, State Space Models (SSMs), particularly the Mamba architecture, have emerged as a promising alternative to Transformers for sequential recommendation due to their linear-time complexity and efficient handling of long sequences. Mamba4Rec was the first to explore selective state space models for this domain, demonstrating superior effectiveness and efficiency compared to RNN- and attention-based baselines [20]. Subsequent research has investigated the capabilities of SSMs in lifelong sequential recommendation, showing that Mamba can handle long user sequences with significantly reduced training time and memory costs while maintaining competitive performance [15, 19]. To further enhance the modeling capacity of SSMs, various architectural improvements have been introduced. Bidirectional gated Mamba architectures have been proposed to capture context from both directions [18], and EchoMamba4Rec combines bidirectional Mamba modules with frequency-domain spectral filtering to capture complex interaction patterns [16]. Additionally, Mamba4KT has been applied to knowledge tracing, demonstrating that Mamba-based models can achieve comparable accuracy to state-of-the-art models with improved resource utilization [17]. While these works highlight the efficiency and potential of SSMs, they often treat SSMs as a direct replacement for attention. In contrast, STAR-Rec theoretically unifies SSMs and attention mechanisms, leveraging SSMs for temporal dynamics and attention for diverse item relationships within a mixture-of-experts framework.

**Foundations, Surveys, and Extensions of Mamba/SSMs**
The rapid adoption of Mamba and SSMs in recommendation systems is underpinned by significant theoretical advancements and comprehensive surveys of the broader deep learning landscape. The original Mamba paper introduced a selective state space model architecture that achieves linear-time sequence modeling with high inference throughput, eliminating the need for attention or MLP blocks [12]. A crucial theoretical breakthrough was established by the work demonstrating that Transformers and SSMs are duals of each other, leading to the design of Mamba-2, which offers faster inference while remaining competitive with Transformers [14]. This theoretical connection has been further explored in comprehensive surveys that cover the architectural advancements, adaptation techniques, and diverse applications of Mamba-based models [13]. More broadly, surveys on deep learning recommender systems provide the necessary context for understanding the evolution from traditional methods to modern deep architectures, offering new perspectives on the field [2]. Our work builds on these foundational insights, specifically utilizing the theoretical duality between attention and SSMs to justify the hybrid design of STAR-Rec, which aims to capture the strengths of both paradigms.
