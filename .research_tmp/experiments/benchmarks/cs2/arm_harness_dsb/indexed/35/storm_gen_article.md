# Related Works

## Molecular Dynamics Simulation and Conformational Ensembles

Protein function is increasingly understood in terms of conformational dynamics — the ensemble of states a protein samples and the transitions between them — rather than a single static structure [Koshland, 1958]. The gold standard for characterizing such ensembles remains all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulation, which integrates Newton's equations of motion under empirical force fields, as implemented in packages such as GROMACS [Abraham et al., 2015] and OpenMM [Eastman et al., 2020] for the AMBER force-field family [Case et al., 2005]. MD is, however, prohibitively expensive at the timescales that often govern conformational change: events on the microsecond-to-millisecond scale require substantial compute, and conformational states separated by large energy barriers may remain unvisited even in long trajectories. A large body of enhanced-sampling methods has therefore been developed to make sampling tractable, including replica-exchange [Sugita & Okamoto, 1999] and metadynamics [Laio & Parrinello, 2002], which bias the system to escape local minima of the free-energy landscape; alchemical free-energy calculations also routinely reweight ensembles for binding-affinity estimation in drug discovery [Chodera et al., 2014]. Across all of these approaches, the MD ensemble — a collection of structures implicitly weighted by their energies — is the object of interest, and this motivates treating the *distribution* over conformations, rather than a single pose, as the target of generative modeling.

## Generative Models for Protein Structure and Conformational Sampling

Deep learning has transformed protein structure prediction. AlphaFold2 [Jumper et al., 2021] and RoseTTAFold [Dauparas et al., 2022] predict single near-native structures per sequence with near-experimental accuracy, the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (AFDB) [Pereira et al., 2022] has made hundreds of millions of such static predictions publicly available, and evolutionary language models such as ESM-2 provide complementary structure prediction and embeddings [Lin et al., 2023]. These predictors, however, output a single (or a small set of) low-confidence-weighted conformations per sequence and do not characterize the thermodynamically populated ensemble — precisely the information needed for function- and binding-related applications.

Denoising diffusion models have recently emerged as a general-purpose generative framework for biomolecular geometry. Trained on the denoising objective of score-based generative modeling [Ho et al., 2020; Song & Ermon, 2020; Song et al., 2021], diffusion models can learn distributions over crystallographic structures and sample diverse, high-quality protein backbones: RFdiffusion [Watson et al., 2023] and Chroma [Yim et al., 2023] demonstrated de novo structure and function design, and equivariant architectures such as Equiformer [Ling et al., 2023] improved backbone-only generation. In the conformational-sampling setting, DFold adapts an AlphaFold-style pipeline with a diffusion backbone to generate ensembles from a single sequence [Watson et al., 2023], and MDD trains a molecular diffusion model on MD-derived ensembles to sample diverse conformations conditioned on sequence [Wang et al., 2024]. Generative ensemble modeling has also been pursued for cryo-EM heterogeneity, where cryoDRGN learns a latent distribution over density volumes and its follow-up scales it to large datasets [Zhang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024]. A common limitation of these protein-specific models is that their training objective is a pure data-likelihood fit to structural databases, with no explicit supervision from physics: the resulting ensembles may be structurally diverse, but they need not reproduce the thermodynamic population of states.

## Physics-Informed and Energy-Guided Generative Modeling

A parallel line of work injects physics into generative models. Energy-based models frame probabilistic modeling in terms of an energy function [LeCun et al., 2006], and physics-informed neural networks embed PDE residuals directly in the training loss [Raissi et al., 2019]. In the diffusion setting, the score of the data distribution can be steered at sampling time by the gradient of an auxiliary signal: classifier guidance [Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021] and its classifier-free variant [Ho & Salimans, 2022] use gradients of a learned signal to bias sampling toward a desired attribute, and this principle has been extended to physics constraints — physics-informed diffusion models use PDE-residual gradients as a guidance term during sampling [Chen et al., 2023], while physics-informed score-based generative models embed the physical operator into the score network itself [Zhu et al., 2024]. Because any data distribution admits a Boltzmann form, $p(x)\propto e^{-E(x)/T}$, with score given by $-\nabla E(x)/T$, energy feedback from a physical model can in principle be composed directly with the learned score. In molecular simulation the natural physical supervisor is the potential-energy function: deep neural network potentials such as SchNet [Schütt et al., 2017], ANI [Smith et al., 2020], NequIP [Batzner et al., 2022], and MACE [Musil et al., 2023] provide near-quantum-chemical energy and force estimates at orders of magnitude lower cost than first-principles MD, making them practical as differentiable energy oracles for protein ensembles.

## Positioning of EBA

The preceding threads share a limitation that motivates our approach. Physics either enters as a hard constraint during training (PINN-style residuals, PDE-embedded scores) or as a pointwise bias at sampling time (guidance), whereas calibrating a generative model to the correct Boltzmann *population* of conformational states — weighting states by their energy differences — is an intractable optimization problem under standard energy-based objectives for high-dimensional protein ensembles. EBA instead treats the physical model as a post-hoc feedback source: it aligns a trained generative model with physical-model feedback, efficiently calibrating the balance of conformational states according to their energy differences. This calibration view complements likelihood-fit generative samplers [Watson et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024] and physics-guided samplers [Chen et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024], targeting specifically the physical plausibility of the generated conformational distribution — the property that matters most for structural-biology and drug-discovery applications.
