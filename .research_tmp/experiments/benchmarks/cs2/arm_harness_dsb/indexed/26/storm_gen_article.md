## Related Works

We organize the related literature into five threads: classical multi-sensor calibration, automatic LiDAR–camera calibration, radar perception and the radar–camera pair, bird's-eye-view (BEV) representations with cross-modal fusion, and robust matching under uncertainty.

### Classical multi-sensor calibration

Accurate extrinsics between the sensors mounted on a vehicle are a prerequisite for multi-modal 3D perception. Classical camera calibration recovers intrinsics and pose from images of known patterns [Zhang, 2000], and multi-sensor extrinsics have traditionally been established offline, either by mounting calibration targets or by jointly optimizing the geometry of all sensors from multi-view data, typically with robust solvers such as RANSAC [Fischler & Bolles, 1981] or bundle adjustment [Triggs et al., 2000]. Such procedures are accurate but labor-intensive, and, more importantly for the present work, they assume that the calibration remains valid: in real operation, extrinsics drift due to vibration, thermal effects, and minor impacts. Maintaining calibration *during* operation — i.e., online automatic calibration — is therefore an active and difficult goal, and it is especially hard for sensor pairs whose data are sparse and noisy.

### Automatic LiDAR–camera calibration

Among automatic calibration approaches, the LiDAR–camera pair has received the most attention, and our work takes it as the primary comparison point. A dominant line is geometry-based: 3D LiDAR returns are projected into the image and enforced to be re-projection-consistent with 2D features, i.e., a 3D–2D correspondence problem that is usually solved with robust matching and RANSAC-style outlier rejection [Fischler & Bolles, 1981]. These methods rely heavily on the *density* of LiDAR returns (tens of thousands of points per frame), which yields a large pool of correspondences; this density assumption is precisely what breaks down for radar, whose returns are sparser by orders of magnitude. A complementary line is learning-based, in which correspondences — or the extrinsics themselves — are predicted by deep networks, reducing reliance on hand-crafted geometry and enabling calibration from natural driving data. In adjacent sensor pairs, online self-calibration has already been demonstrated, e.g., tightly-coupled LiDAR–inertial odometry systems that maintain LiDAR–IMU extrinsics during operation [Xie et al., 2021]; the radar–camera pair, whose sparse and height-uncertain returns render correspondences ambiguous, has so far remained largely out of scope for such online methods.

### Radar perception and the radar–camera pair

Modern automotive FMCW radars measure range, azimuth, and Doppler, and — unlike LiDAR — do not directly resolve elevation, so per-point height must be inferred and is both sparse and uncertain. Multi-sensor driving datasets such as nuScenes include five radars [Caesar et al., 2021], yet their radar sweeps provide only on the order of hundreds of points per frame, orders of magnitude sparser than a LiDAR sweep. Radar-based 3D object detection on such datasets has confirmed radar's complementarity (weather robustness and reliable long-range azimuthal returns) while exposing sparsity as the central obstacle. Calibration of the radar–camera pair has historically been performed manually [Zhang, 2000], and recent automatic attempts have so far been offline and structure-based; to our knowledge, none performs online automatic calibration while operating under sparsity and height uncertainty. This is the gap that our Dual-Perspective representation and Selective Fusion Mechanism are designed to fill.

### Bird's-eye-view representations and cross-modal fusion

A large body of work learns BEV representations that make the geometry shared across sensors explicit. Lift-Splat-Shoot lifts 2D image features into a 3D cost volume and splats them onto a LiDAR BEV [Philion & Fidler, 2020]; BEVFormer learns the BEV from multi-camera images with a spatio-temporal transformer whose spatial cross-attention aggregates image features into BEV queries [Li et al., 2022]. For multi-sensor fusion, BEVFusion unifies LiDAR and camera features in a shared BEV for multi-task learning [Liu et al., 2022]. These works treat the BEV as a *fusion* space for downstream detection; here we repurpose the BEV — together with the frontal view — as a *calibration* space. The two views are complementary in exactly the way calibration needs: the frontal view carries rich but height-sensitive information, whereas the BEV provides features that are robust to height uncertainty, and cross-modal attention supplies an explicit mechanism for establishing the location correspondences on which calibration depends.

### Robust matching under uncertainty

Because correspondence supervision is unreliable when data are sparse or noisy, robust estimation has long been used for outlier rejection [Fischler & Bolles, 1981], and robust losses such as the Huber loss reduce the influence of large residuals [Huber, 1964]. On the learning side, confidence-based filtering of pseudo-supervision has proved effective in semi-supervised settings [Sohn et al., 2020]. Our Noise-Resistant Matcher follows this line at the matching stage: it reweights and discards unreliable matches during training so that the matcher is supervised only by high-confidence correspondences, directly improving robustness to radar sparsity and height uncertainty.
