# Problem Statement and Motivation

Accurate sensor calibration is a fundamental prerequisite for the multi-sensor perception systems employed in autonomous vehicles, as the reliability of downstream tasks such as object detection and navigation depends heavily on the precise alignment of data from different modalities [1]. While cameras provide rich semantic and structural information, they are susceptible to adverse weather conditions; conversely, radar sensors, particularly 4D radar, have emerged as critical components due to their ability to withstand such conditions and offer enhanced capabilities in elevation measurement and higher resolution compared to traditional 3D radar [2][3]. However, the integration of these sensors presents significant challenges. Radar point clouds are inherently sparse with low azimuth and elevation resolution, lacking the semantic and structural details found in camera images, which generally results in lower detection performance when processed in isolation [2].
A major obstacle in achieving robust multi-modal perception is the difficulty of establishing accurate extrinsic calibration between cameras and radars. Existing calibration methods often rely on separate targets optimized for either visual or radar modalities, which complicates the establishment of correspondence between the two sensor streams [3]. Furthermore, the sparsity of radar data makes manual labeling labor-intensive and unreliable, while the inherent sparsity of radar data also poses a challenge for accurate extrinsic calibration, as traditional methods may struggle to provide sufficient constraints for precise alignment [4][3]. The deployment of intelligent vehicles in complex urban environments further exacerbates these issues, requiring reliable and precise positioning and perception solutions that can adapt to varying driving contexts and sensor qualities [5].
To address these limitations, there is a growing need for unified frameworks that can handle both calibration and data annotation efficiently. Current approaches often fail to provide a seamless pipeline for 4D radar-camera calibration and auto-labeling, leaving a gap in the development of large-scale annotated datasets required for training radar-based perception algorithms [3]. Therefore, developing an end-to-end automatic calibration network that can directly process raw sensor data, such as the range-Doppler spectrum of radar and camera images, without relying on complex intermediate signal processing or manual intervention, is essential for advancing the state-of-the-art in autonomous driving perception [2][4].

# Methodology

The methodology for automatic radar-camera calibration addresses the challenge of aligning heterogeneous sensor data, which is critical for robust vehicle perception in intelligent transportation systems. Traditional approaches often rely on dedicated calibration targets or manual interventions, which are impractical for real-time or online applications due to the differing physical measurement principles of radar and cameras and the high sparsity of traffic radar data [6]. To overcome these limitations, recent methodologies have shifted towards data-driven and deep learning-based frameworks that enable automatic registration without external references.
A prominent approach involves the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to perform automatic rotational radar-camera calibration. This method employs a two-stage architecture consisting of a coarse network and a fine network [6]. The coarse network provides an initial estimate of the calibration parameters, while the fine network is trained on the residual error of the coarse network using a boosting-inspired training algorithm [6]. This hierarchical structure allows the system to achieve precise and robust sensor registration, a capability demonstrated through the use of real-world recorded data, as public datasets combining synchronized radar and camera measurements are scarce [6].
In the context of multi-object tracking (MOT), calibration is often integrated directly into the tracking framework to facilitate the fusion of radar and camera data. One such methodology positions radar in a crucial role rather than a supplementary one, leveraging its ability to provide accurate range and depth information in a 3D world coordinate system [7]. By utilizing common features between radar and camera data, these frameworks enable online calibration that autonomously associates detections from both sensors [7]. This approach simplifies the integration of detection results and allows for the accurate derivation of real-world positions of detected objects, thereby enhancing tracking efficiency and minimizing manual interventions [7].
Furthermore, deep neural network architectures have been developed to infer dense depth from images and sparse point clouds, which inherently relies on accurate calibration parameters. In these systems, the calibration of the camera, which may differ from the training configuration, is fed as an input to the network along with the sparse point cloud and a single image [8]. A specialized "Calibrated Backprojection Layer" is employed to backproject each pixel in the image to three-dimensional space using the calibration matrix and a depth feature descriptor [8]. The resulting 3D positional encoding is then concatenated with image descriptors and previous layer outputs to yield the input for the next encoder layer, ultimately producing a dense depth map via a decoder that exploits skip-connections [8].
Beyond specific network architectures, comprehensive toolboxes such as OpenCalib have been proposed to provide a rich set of sensor calibration techniques for autonomous vehicles [1]. These tools support various application scenarios, including road-scene-based manual and automatic calibration, production-line calibration, and online calibration [1]. The methodology emphasizes that while sensor accuracy is fundamental, it is insufficient without real-time reliability, necessitating robust calibration frameworks that can maintain precision across diverse operational conditions [1]. Additionally, for stereo vision systems, novel methods represent camera model equations as neural networks within a multi-task learning framework to predict both intrinsic (principal point offset, focal length) and extrinsic (baseline, pitch, translation) properties from image pairs [9]. This approach reconstructs 3D points using the camera model neural network, offering a unique alternative to traditional comprehensive solutions for camera calibration [9].

# Experimental Setup

The experimental evaluation of the RC-AutoCalib network is designed to validate its effectiveness in automatic radar-camera calibration, addressing challenges such as data scarcity and the need for robust parameter estimation. To mitigate the issue of limited training data, which is common in specialized sensor calibration tasks, the study employs a transfer-learning approach. This strategy allows the deep network to be optimized by leveraging pre-trained models, thereby addressing the practical difficulty of obtaining large-scale datasets comprising hundreds of subjects or scenarios scanned under a common protocol for calibration-specific tasks [10].
The core of the calibration process involves predicting both intrinsic and extrinsic camera properties from image pairs. The experimental framework utilizes a multi-task learning approach where camera model equations are represented as a neural network. Specifically, the method employs a camera projection loss (CPL) to estimate desired parameters by reconstructing 3D points using the camera model neural network and minimizing the reconstruction error [9]. This approach contrasts with traditional methods that build comprehensive solutions, instead focusing on the mathematical representation of the camera model within the learning framework to ensure accurate estimation of principal point offset, focal length, baseline, pitch, and translation [9].
To ensure the robustness of the calibration parameters against drift and unobservable directions in the parameter space, the experimental setup incorporates mechanisms for graceful handling of these corner cases. This includes an information-theoretic based segment selection for constant-time estimation and an observability-aware parameter update through a rank-revealing decomposition of the Fisher information matrix [11]. These components are critical for maintaining competitive accuracy while ensuring the algorithm can handle dynamic changes in calibration parameters over time [11].
Furthermore, the evaluation considers the impact of noise on the calibration process. Drawing from principles used in signal processing, the setup may involve adaptive filtering techniques to manage noise characteristics that vary across the sensor swath. For instance, algorithms based on Goldstein filtering combined with quality-guided graphs, such as residue density and pseudo-coherence coefficients, are utilized to determine filtering parameters and remove phase noise in the frequency domain [12]. This ensures that the input data for the calibration network is of high quality, which is essential for the accurate inference of camera poses and 3D geometry from uncalibrated sparse-view images [13]. The overall goal is to demonstrate that the proposed end-to-end network can deliver state-of-the-art performance in pose estimation and geometry reconstruction while maintaining high inference efficiency [13].

# Results and Analysis

The proposed RC-AutoCalib network demonstrates significant improvements in calibration accuracy by addressing the inherent data sparsity and measurement uncertainty of radar height data. By employing a Dual-Perspective representation that integrates features from both frontal and bird's-eye views, the method effectively mitigates the sensitivity of frontal view height information while leveraging the robustness of bird's-eye view features against height uncertainty [14]. This approach is further enhanced by a Selective Fusion Mechanism and a Multi-Modal Cross-Attention Mechanism, which explicitly identify and fuse reliable features to reduce the impact of height uncertainty and establish precise location correspondences across modalities [14].
Experimental results indicate that the network achieves superior calibration accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art methods for multi-sensor extrinsic calibration. In extensive experiments utilizing the View-of-Delft and Dual-Radar datasets, the proposed method reduced both median translational and rotational calibration errors by at least 50% relative to previous approaches [4]. These results highlight the effectiveness of the end-to-end deep learning framework in handling the joint calibration of camera, lidar, and 4D radar sensors, particularly in scenarios where radar data sparsity poses a significant challenge [4].
The performance of the calibration network is also supported by advancements in handling sensor noise and signal quality. While the primary focus is on geometric calibration, the robustness of the system benefits from techniques that manage signal degradation, such as adaptive phase filtering algorithms that account for the gradual decrease in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) from near to far range [12]. Furthermore, the integration of multi-modal cross-attention mechanisms allows the network to perceive difficulties in information flow, similar to how noise injection in architecture search can prevent overshooting in optimization, thereby ensuring convergence to accurate calibration parameters [15].
In comparison to other self-calibration algorithms, such as FastCal, which offers low time complexity and graceful handling of drift and unobservable directions, the RC-AutoCalib approach provides a specialized solution for the specific challenges of radar-camera alignment [11]. The ability to perform online automatic calibration during system operation represents a critical advancement, as it ensures that the sensors maintain the necessary precision and reliability required for real-time autonomous vehicle perception [1]. The domain transfer capabilities of the network have also been examined, suggesting its potential applicability across different operational environments and sensor configurations [4].

# Discussion

The development of RC-AutoCalib addresses critical challenges in sensor calibration for autonomous vehicles, particularly the need for real-time precision and reliability in dynamic application scenarios. While accurate sensor calibration is a prerequisite for multi-sensor perception, the inherent accuracy of individual sensors is insufficient without robust calibration methods that can adapt to real-time conditions [1]. This is especially true in urban environments, where the complexity of the surroundings fosters the need for novel systems that provide reliable and precise positioning and navigation solutions [5].
Existing approaches to sensor calibration often face limitations in computational efficiency and the handling of specific edge cases. For instance, traditional self-calibration algorithms may struggle with drift in calibration parameters and unobservable directions in the parameter space [11]. The proposed RC-AutoCalib framework aligns with the broader trend of developing efficient, low-complexity solutions that can gracefully manage these corner cases, potentially offering performance improvements comparable to or exceeding state-of-the-art methods like FastCal, which emphasizes constant-time estimation and observability-aware parameter updates [11]. Furthermore, the integration of such automatic calibration techniques complements other advanced navigation systems, such as context-aided Unscented Kalman filters, which rely on continuous estimation and correction of inertial navigation system drift errors to maintain accuracy in urban conditions [5].
The significance of robust automatic calibration extends beyond mere technical precision to the broader context of safe driver interaction with vehicle automation. As Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS), such as Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC) and Lane Keeping Assist (LKA), become more prevalent, the reliability of the underlying sensor data is paramount for safety-critical operations [16]. Inconsistencies in system comprehension and potential misuse can be mitigated by ensuring that the sensor data feeding these systems is consistently calibrated and accurate [16]. By providing a reliable end-to-end radar-camera calibration network, RC-AutoCalib contributes to the foundational infrastructure required for these systems to function correctly, thereby supporting the goal of reducing misuse and enhancing road safety through correct knowledge and application of automation features [16].
In the broader landscape of neural architecture and optimization, the challenges of convergence and performance stability, as seen in differentiable architecture search methods like DARTS, highlight the importance of robust optimization strategies in complex systems [15]. While NoisyDARTS proposes injecting unbiased random noise to smooth the loss landscape and prevent overshooting in skip connections [15], the principles of stability and convergence are equally relevant in the iterative processes of sensor calibration. Ensuring that calibration algorithms converge to the right solution area without being overly influenced by transient noise or drift is essential for maintaining the integrity of the perception pipeline in autonomous driving scenarios [15][11].
