# Theoretical Framework

The theoretical framework for risk-aware fund allocation based on time-series forecasting addresses the inherent challenges of financial decision-making under uncertainty, specifically focusing on the alignment between predictive models and optimization objectives. Traditional approaches, such as "prediction-only" or "Predict-then-Optimize" strategies, often suffer from goal mismatch, where the objective of the forecasting model does not align with the ultimate goal of fund allocation. Furthermore, the integration of state-of-the-art time-series forecasting models introduces additional uncertainty into predicted results, which can compromise the robustness of downstream decisions [1]. To mitigate these issues, modern frameworks emphasize the need for well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, as ensuring robustness guarantees in high-dimensional settings requires that uncertainty is not only quantified but also valued in the context of downstream decision-making [2].
A central component of this framework is the integration of conformal prediction (CP) to provide statistically guaranteed uncertainty quantification. While data-driven surrogate models offer quick approximations to complex systems, they typically lack the uncertainty quantification necessary for reliability in safety-critical or high-stakes financial applications [3]. Bayesian methods, although providing uncertainty estimates, often lack statistical guarantees and struggle with computational costs in high-dimensional spatio-temporal problems [3]. In contrast, conformal prediction offers a model-agnostic approach that provides statistically guaranteed marginal coverage with near-zero computational cost [3]. This method has been extended to handle selective prediction, where prediction sets are constructed with valid coverage conditional on a data-driven selection mechanism. Recent advancements, such as PErmutation-based Mondrian Conformal Inference (PEMI), address the challenges of online settings where data arrives sequentially and selection mechanisms are asymmetric, thereby broadening the applicability of conformal guarantees to dynamic financial environments [4].
The framework further incorporates adaptive decision support systems designed to model and forecast nonlinear, nonstationary processes in finance. These systems are built on principles of adaptive model structure estimation and the optimization of parameter estimation procedures, explicitly identifying and accounting for uncertainties inherent in data collection, model construction, and forecasting [5]. By treating uncertainty as a negative influence factor on computational performance, these adaptive systems aim to refine signal generation and asset class definitions, ensuring that the investment process remains cohesive and responsive to new data [5][6]. The shift from batch processing to online processing within these frameworks allows components to produce outputs that are automatically and sequentially updated, facilitating a more integrated approach to investment workflows [6].
To address the specific risks associated with financial time-series forecasting, such as regime shifts and shocks, the framework utilizes uncertainty-gated generative modeling. This approach treats uncertainty as an internal control signal that gates representation, propagation, and generation processes, thereby curbing miscalibration and improving risk-sensitive forecasting [7]. Additionally, the framework considers the problem of learning with abstention, particularly in multi-horizon forecasting scenarios where mispredictions can have high costs. By allowing models to abstain from predictions when the risk of error is elevated, the framework leverages the structured and correlated nature of multi-horizon forecasts to enhance trust and reduce the impact of overconfident point estimates [8]. These theoretical elements collectively support a risk-aware allocation strategy that balances predictive accuracy with rigorous uncertainty management.

# Methodology

The methodology for risk-aware fund allocation based on time-series forecasting integrates advanced machine learning techniques with optimization frameworks to address the challenges of non-stationary financial data and decision-making under uncertainty. A central component of this approach is the development of adaptive decision support systems designed to model and forecast nonlinear, nonstationary processes in economic and financial domains. These systems employ adaptive model structure estimation and optimization of parameter estimation procedures, while explicitly identifying and accounting for uncertainties inherent in data collection, model construction, and forecasting procedures[5]. To enhance the robustness of these models, recent methodologies utilize dynamic calibration datasets that are not necessarily based on the most recent data, but rather on cluster analysis of fundamental market regime indicators and structural time series breakpoint analyses. This approach challenges conventional principles that prioritize recent data, recognizing that in episodic and recurrent market dynamics, the most relevant calibration data may correspond to historical regimes similar to current conditions[9].
To bridge the gap between forecasting and decision-making, frameworks such as Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) have been introduced to resolve the goal mismatch inherent in naive "predict-then-optimize" approaches. The RTS-PnO framework features end-to-end training with objective alignment measurement, adaptive forecasting uncertainty calibration, and agnosticism towards specific forecasting models, thereby reducing the additional uncertainty introduced by state-of-the-art time series models[1]. Furthermore, the integration of uncertainty quantification is critical for robust decision-making. Methods such as Uncertainty-Gated Generative Modeling (UGGM) treat uncertainty as an internal control signal that gates representation, propagation, and generation processes, significantly improving risk-sensitive forecasting and reducing mean squared error in high-stakes financial environments[7]. Additionally, end-to-end frameworks have been developed to learn uncertainty sets for conditional robust optimization, informed by downstream decision-making losses and providing robustness and calibration guarantees through conformal prediction[2].
In the context of portfolio management, deep reinforcement learning frameworks like DeepAries offer a novel approach to dynamic asset allocation. Unlike traditional methods that rely on fixed rebalancing intervals, DeepAries jointly optimizes the timing and allocation of rebalancing decisions using a Transformer-based state encoder and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This allows for the adaptive selection of optimal rebalancing intervals and portfolio weights, effectively reducing unnecessary transaction costs while maximizing risk-adjusted returns[10]. Complementary to this, integrated online frameworks for investment workflows replace batch processing with online processing, allowing components to produce outputs that are automatically and sequentially updated as new data arrives. This cohesive approach accelerates the investment process and enables the optimization of component decisions with direct reference to investment objectives, while also clarifying the extent of backtest overfitting through resampling methods[6].
For specific asset classes, such as stocks, efficient price forecasting is essential for calculating return and risk criteria in portfolio optimization. Techniques such as grey fuzzy methods have been employed to predict stock prices with high efficiency, followed by the application of metaheuristic algorithms like Invasive Weed Optimization (IWO) to solve portfolio optimization models based on cardinality constraints and investment income per share[11]. In multi-horizon forecasting scenarios, where predictions are made for consecutive time steps, learning with abstention frameworks allows models to abstain from offering predictions when the risk of misprediction is elevated. This approach addresses the structured and correlated nature of multi-horizon forecasts, providing a richer set of abstention strategies compared to single-prediction models[8]. Finally, to ensure reliable probabilistic reports in distributed or multi-agent systems, strictly proper decision scoring rules can be used to assess accuracy, enabling agents to learn to solve contextual bandit problems jointly and aggregate distributed information efficiently for decision-making[12].

# Experimental Evaluation

Experimental evaluations in the field of time-series forecasting for fund allocation and risk management have focused on comparing the predictive accuracy of various modeling techniques, assessing the robustness of these models against structural instability, and analyzing the economic implications of different allocation strategies.

## Model Comparison and Forecasting Accuracy

Several studies have compared frequentist and Bayesian model averaging schemes to determine their effectiveness in handling parameter uncertainty, model uncertainty, and time-varying model weights. In simulation experiments characterized by low predictability, structural instability, and fat tails—traits typical of financial-economic time series—specific predictive schemes have been shown to outperform other averaging methods, particularly when the correlation among individual forecasts is low and the underlying data generating process is subject to structural location shifts [13]. Additionally, the development of adaptive decision support systems for modeling nonlinear nonstationary processes has involved the optimization of model parameter estimation procedures and the identification of uncertainties inherent in data collection and forecasting, aiming to mitigate negative influences on computational performance [5].
In the context of specific asset classes, novel architectures have been proposed to address the challenges of high-frequency fluctuations and correlated market factors. For instance, EXFormer, a Transformer-based architecture designed for forecasting daily exchange rate returns, utilizes a multi-scale trend-aware self-attention mechanism and a dynamic variable selector to assign time-varying importance weights to exogenous covariates, providing pre-hoc interpretability while preserving long-range dependencies [14]. Similarly, in electricity forecasting, a dynamic calibration dataset approach based on cluster analysis and structural time series breakpoint analyses has been developed to challenge conventional principles that prioritize recent data, instead selecting the most relevant dataset based on the episodic nature of market dynamics [9].

## Machine Learning in Portfolio Allocation

The application of machine learning to portfolio allocation has demonstrated significant economic and statistical gains. By implementing optimal portfolio rules for time-varying expected returns and volatility using Random Forest models, researchers have achieved substantial improvements over buy-and-hold strategies in terms of utility, risk-adjusted returns, and maximum drawdowns [15]. One model is employed to forecast the sign probabilities of excess returns, while the second constructs optimized volatility estimates, establishing a unifying framework for machine learning applied to both return- and volatility-timing [15].
Furthermore, integrated online frameworks for investment workflows have been proposed to replace batch processing, allowing components to produce outputs that are automatically updated as new data arrives. These frameworks, when used in conjunction with resampling methods, help optimize component decisions with direct reference to investment objectives while clarifying the extent of backtest overfitting [6]. In the domain of multi-horizon time-series forecasting, where mispredictions can have high costs, learning with abstention frameworks has been formalized to allow models to abstain from predictions when the risk of error is elevated, addressing the structured and correlated nature of multi-horizon forecasts [8].

## Robustness and Adversarial Vulnerabilities

The robustness of deep learning models in time-series forecasting has been scrutinized, particularly regarding their vulnerability to adversarial attacks. While previous works have generally studied the deterioration of model performance due to adversarial inputs, recent research proposes a novel formulation of directional, amplitudinal, and temporal targeted adversarial attacks that create specific impacts on the amplitude and direction of output predictions [16]. This highlights the need for security considerations in critical applications ranging from medical devices to the security domain.

## Economic and Behavioral Implications

Beyond pure predictive accuracy, experimental evaluations have also examined the broader economic and behavioral implications of forecasting and allocation strategies. In a segmented market model that includes state-dependent asset market decisions and access to credit, the availability of credit has been found to substantially change dynamics, allowing agents to smooth consumption and reduce movements in velocity, thereby making prices more flexible and dampening liquidity effects [17].
Behavioral experiments involving retail investors have revealed that the time frame of price charts displayed to investors influences risk-taking behavior, with shorter time frames associated with varying levels of risk-taking, although the time frame does not affect average risk-taking [18]. Additionally, the mechanics of rebalancing strategies have been analyzed, noting that mechanical rebalancing, such as monthly or quarterly reallocation, can lead to substantially larger drawdowns than buy-and-hold strategies during crises when markets are trending, as it involves selling winning asset classes and buying losers [19]. Finally, in fund management contexts, capacity analysis has highlighted that the central issue is the ability of a fund to access available alpha before it erodes, which is often dictated by the size of funds under management and holding or trading constraints, rather than just the magnitude of alpha or execution costs [20].

# Related Work and Context

The integration of machine learning and statistical methods into financial decision-making has evolved significantly, moving from isolated forecasting tasks to holistic, risk-aware allocation frameworks. Early approaches often treated prediction and optimization as separate stages, a "Predict-then-Optimize" paradigm that frequently suffers from goal mismatch, where the objective of the forecasting model does not align with the ultimate investment goal [1]. To address this, recent research has proposed end-to-end frameworks that jointly train forecasting and allocation components. For instance, the Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework introduces objective alignment measurement and adaptive uncertainty calibration, allowing for fund allocation without prior assumptions on the specific forecasting models used [1]. Similarly, integrated online frameworks have been proposed to replace batch processing with sequential updates, enabling investment workflows to adapt dynamically as new data arrives and optimizing component decisions with direct reference to investment objectives [6].
A critical component of modern financial modeling is the quantification and management of uncertainty, particularly in the presence of regime shifts and market shocks. Point-accurate models that are overconfident can be dangerous in high-stakes environments; consequently, methods such as Uncertainty-Gated Generative Modeling (UGGM) have been developed to treat uncertainty as an internal control signal for representation, propagation, and generation [7]. This approach has demonstrated significant improvements in risk-sensitive forecasting, such as a 63.5% reduction in Mean Squared Error (MSE) on the NYISO dataset [7]. Furthermore, conformal prediction has emerged as a robust method for providing formal coverage guarantees to black-box machine learning models, although challenges remain when facing subpopulation shifts where test environments differ from calibration data [21]. In the context of multi-horizon forecasting, where mispredictions can have high costs, learning with abstention frameworks allows models to withhold predictions when the risk of error is elevated, addressing the structured and correlated nature of multi-step forecasts [8].
Generative models and probabilistic forecasting techniques have also advanced the state of the art in capturing complex dependencies within financial time series. Diffusion models, such as Diffolio, utilize hierarchical attention architectures and correlation-guided regularizers to better reflect cross-sectional correlations in multivariate financial data, enhancing both forecast accuracy and portfolio construction [22]. Similarly, Diffusion Transformers for Time Series (DiTS) frame endogenous and exogenous variates as distinct modalities to capture inter- and intra-variate dependencies, addressing the limitations of single-stream Transformer backbones in covariate-aware forecasting [23]. These probabilistic approaches are essential for constructing efficient portfolios that account for complex cross-sectional dependencies, moving beyond simple point estimates to provide a richer distribution of potential outcomes [22].
Beyond specific forecasting architectures, the broader context of investment management involves the aggregation of distributed information and the optimization of growth strategies within constrained environments. In multi-agent systems, decision markets and strictly proper scoring rules have been used to elicit and aggregate distributed information from self-interested agents, allowing for efficient joint learning in contextual bandit problems [12]. From a theoretical perspective, the trade-off between risk and return is influenced by the geometry of the explored space; studies on growth models over general graphs reveal that while fruitful strategies often lead to risky positions, the spatial properties of the environment can alleviate this tension [24]. Additionally, machine learning techniques, such as Random Forests, have been applied to reward-risk timing, providing substantial improvements over buy-and-hold strategies in utility, risk-adjusted returns, and maximum drawdowns by forecasting sign probabilities of excess returns and optimizing volatility estimates [15]. These diverse methodologies collectively underscore the importance of timing, uncertainty quantification, and integrated optimization in modern fund allocation.

# Discussion

The integration of time-series forecasting into fund allocation strategies highlights a critical interplay between predictive accuracy, risk management, and the structural constraints of financial markets. A central theme in recent literature is the trade-off between risk and return, which is not merely a function of asset selection but is deeply influenced by the geometry of the explored space and the environment's space-time properties. Research indicates that while fruitful investment strategies often lead to risky positions, this tension can be alleviated by understanding how the environment shapes optimal growth and population distribution, a dynamic observed in both biological and inanimate systems such as goods and money [24]. This perspective suggests that fund allocation models must account for the structural properties of the market environment to optimize returns while managing uncertainty.
Furthermore, the temporal dimension of forecasting is paramount, particularly in multi-horizon settings where predictions are made for consecutive time steps. Traditional abstention strategies, which allow models to withhold predictions when uncertainty is high, are often ill-suited for these structured, correlated forecasting tasks [8]. In the context of fund management, where mispredictions can have high costs and erode trust, developing frameworks that respect the structured nature of multi-horizon forecasts is essential for robust decision-making [8]. This aligns with the broader challenge of handling nonstationary processes in economics and finance, where adaptive decision support systems must account for uncertainties inherent in data collection and model construction to mitigate negative influences on computational performance [5].
The architecture of forecasting models also plays a significant role in capturing the complex dependencies within financial time series. Recent advancements in generative modeling, such as Diffusion Transformers for Time Series (DiTS), propose treating endogenous and exogenous variates as distinct modalities to better capture inter-variate and intra-variate dependencies [23]. Similarly, Foundation Models (FMs) are increasingly being applied to process complex, high-dimensional time-series data, with specific attention to spatial-temporal correlations and the intermittent nature of data streams [25]. These architectural innovations are crucial for improving the accuracy of forecasts that underpin allocation decisions, particularly in domains where data is distributed and proprietary, requiring mechanisms to aggregate information from self-interested agents [12].
However, the practical implementation of these advanced forecasting techniques is constrained by the capacity of the fund to deploy capital. Capacity analysis reveals that the central issue is the fund's ability to access available alpha before it erodes, a limit often dictated by the size of funds under management and trading constraints [20]. While the magnitude of alpha and execution costs play secondary roles in determining capacity, they significantly impact the potential level of net alpha [20]. Consequently, even the most sophisticated time-series forecasting models must be integrated within an investment workflow that accounts for these physical and market-based limits.
To address the fragmentation of the investment process, there is a growing argument for replacing batch processing with online processing frameworks. This approach allows components of the investment workflow to produce outputs that are automatically and sequentially updated as new data arrives, thereby accelerating the process and making it more cohesive [6]. Such integrated frameworks can be enhanced with resampling methods to optimize component decisions with direct reference to investment objectives, while also clarifying the extent of backtest overfitting [6]. This shift towards online, adaptive systems is consistent with the need for rigorous yet efficient dataset curation, where pre-trained AI models can be leveraged to generate cost-effective, high-quality labels with guaranteed accuracy bounds [26]. Ultimately, the convergence of advanced time-series architectures, adaptive decision support, and online processing frameworks offers a pathway to more robust, risk-aware fund allocation strategies that are both theoretically sound and practically viable.
