# Overview

The deployment of Vision Transformers (ViTs) on edge devices and resource-constrained environments presents significant challenges due to strict limitations on memory, compute, and latency [1]. While ViTs offer advantages such as design simplicity and state-of-the-art performance through their self-attention mechanisms, their computational and memory costs increase quadratically with image resolution, making them difficult to employ in real-world applications with limited hardware capabilities [2]. To address these issues, various optimization strategies have been developed, including lightweight multi-stage pipelines that combine activation profiling, memory-aware pruning, and quantization to reduce model footprints without requiring costly retraining [1]. Additionally, algorithm-architecture co-designs, such as TorR, have been proposed to replace dense alignment with hyperdimensional associative reasoning, enabling efficient deployment on power-limited edge devices [3].
A critical aspect of optimizing these models for efficient inference involves the refinement of the datapath, particularly in the context of emerging Microscaling (MX) standards. The MX standard enables narrow bit-widths for inference while maintaining large dynamic ranges for training, which is essential for next-generation neural processing units (NPUs) that support both training and inference operations [4]. However, existing MX multiply-accumulate (MAC) designs face a critical trade-off: integer accumulation requires expensive conversions from narrow floating-point products, whereas FP32 accumulation suffers from quantization losses and costly normalization [4]. To overcome these limitations, hybrid precision-scalable reduction trees have been proposed to combine the benefits of both approaches, enabling efficient mixed-precision accumulation with controlled accuracy relaxation [4].
Furthermore, low-precision accelerators are being designed to meet stringent area-power-latency constraints for edge vision platforms. For instance, the TREA accelerator integrates a dual-precision (4/8-bit) SIMD multiply-accumulate unit based on most-significant-digit-first shift-and-add computation, which eliminates conventional multiplier overhead and reduces accumulator bit-width [5]. This architecture supports high-throughput operations and is co-designed with structured hardware-aware reductive pruning strategies to maintain full MAC utilization while achieving significant sparsity [5]. In parallel, research into multi-format quantization-aware training (QAT) allows a single model to be robust across multiple quantization formats, facilitating practical deployment by converting high-precision representations into lower-precision formats without re-training [6]. These advancements in datapath refinement and precision management are crucial for making foundation models more efficient, affordable, and accessible [7].

# Methodology

The methodology for refining datapaths in Microscaling (MX) Vision Transformers (ViTs) integrates algorithmic quantization strategies with hardware-aware architectural optimizations to address the trade-offs between precision, latency, and resource utilization. A central challenge in deploying ViTs on edge devices is the high computational and memory overhead associated with floating-point operations, particularly the dequantization steps required before matrix multiplications. To mitigate this, recent approaches propose integerization processes based on operation reordering, which delay dequantization until after matrix operations, thereby enabling integerized matrix multiplication and linear modules to process quantized inputs directly [8]. This technique is critical for reducing per-processing element (PE) power consumption in linear layers and bridging the gap between high-accuracy models and efficient hardware implementation [8].
To further enhance efficiency, specific quantization frameworks have been developed to handle the unique characteristics of ViT architectures. For instance, I-Segmenter introduces a fully integer-only ViT segmentation framework that systematically replaces floating-point operations with integer counterparts, addressing the fragility of ViT-based models under low precision [9]. This framework employs a novel activation function, $\lambda$-ShiftGELU, to mitigate the limitations of uniform quantization when handling long-tailed activation distributions, thereby stabilizing both training and inference [9]. Similarly, robust methods for full integer quantization of vision transformer networks have been proposed that eliminate the need for intermediate floating-point computations, allowing for deployment on various hardware platforms, including FPGAs and processor/memory architectures [10].
At the hardware level, the design of Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) units is crucial for supporting Microscaling standards, which enable narrow bit-widths for inference while maintaining large dynamic ranges for training. Existing MX MAC designs often face a critical trade-off: integer accumulation requires expensive conversions from narrow floating-point products, whereas FP32 accumulation suffers from quantization losses and costly normalization [4]. To resolve this, a hybrid precision-scalable reduction tree has been proposed for MX MACs, combining the benefits of both approaches to enable efficient mixed-precision accumulation with controlled accuracy relaxation [4]. This design is integrated into state-of-the-art NPU platforms, such as SNAX, to provide efficient continual learning capabilities [4].
Furthermore, resource-efficient edge-AI accelerators like TREA utilize dual-precision (4/8-bit) SIMD MAC units based on most-significant-digit-first (MSDF) shift-and-add computation [5]. By employing run-time bit truncation, these units eliminate conventional multiplier overhead and reduce accumulator bit-width, supporting 4x FxP4 or 1x FxP8 operations per cycle to achieve up to 4x throughput improvement without hardware duplication [5]. This is complemented by structured hardware-aware reductive pruning (SHARP) strategies, which enable near 50% structured sparsity while maintaining full MAC utilization, allowing complex operations like 3x3 convolution kernels to be computed in a single cycle in FxP4 mode [5].
In the context of FPGA implementation, the regularity of multi-bit signals in datapath circuits is exploited to reduce implementation area [11][12]. While traditional bidirectional routing has been used to exploit this regularity, modern FPGAs increasingly employ unidirectional routing tracks, which are more area and delay efficient [11][12]. Recent investigations into architectures employing multi-bit connections and unidirectional routing resources demonstrate that these designs can effectively transport multiple-bit wide signals, further optimizing the datapath for efficient ViT inference [11][12]. Additionally, compiler-based approaches have been tailored to manage datapath mapping, using tree-parsing tools to produce high-quality results in fast run times by formulating the mapping problem as a linear equation with a linearized solution [13]. These combined algorithmic and hardware methodologies ensure that Microscaling ViTs can be deployed efficiently on resource-constrained platforms while maintaining high accuracy.

# Challenges and Numerical Stability

The deployment of Vision Transformers (ViTs) on hardware accelerators faces significant challenges due to the models' reliance on complex arithmetic operations that are highly sensitive to numerical precision. Specifically, operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm cannot be efficiently mapped onto low-precision hardware, often necessitating execution on the CPU. This separation results in suboptimal performance due to the communication overhead between the CPU and the accelerator, as existing methods typically exploit parallelism only in matrix multiplication operations while leaving these sensitive computations off-chip [14]. Furthermore, the substantial parameter size of ViTs incurs increased memory and computational demands, impeding effective inference on resource-constrained devices [15].
To address these issues, low-precision training and quantization techniques have been widely adopted, yet they introduce distinct numerical stability obstacles. A primary challenge in low-precision training is the loss of gradients during parameter updates and backpropagation through nonlinearity layers, such as Softmax, which mandates accurate calculation in high precision [16]. To mitigate this, techniques such as SGD with Kahan summation have been implemented to virtually extend the bit-width of parameters for reliable updates, while guidelines have been proposed to determine the lower bound of required bit-widths for layers followed by Softmax based on dataset class size [16]. Additionally, the diversity of numerical formats for weights, activations, and gradients in low-precision training has created a fragmented research landscape, making it difficult to achieve a unified overview of efficient training methods [17].
In the context of inference, quantization methods struggle to handle complicated functions like exponential and square root without intermediate floating-point computations, which are often required for robust full integer quantization [10]. Prior quantization approaches for ViTs have often retained floating-point scaling factors, leading to non-negligible re-quantization overhead and hindering efficient integer-only inference [18]. The distinctive distributions of post-LayerNorm and post-GELU activations within ViTs further complicate this process, rendering conventional hardware-friendly quantizers ineffective, particularly in low-bit scenarios where accuracy loss is significant [15]. These distributional irregularities, including outliers, require specialized quantizers, such as per-patch outlier-aware quantizers, to maintain model performance [15].
Moreover, the computational graph of ViTs presents challenges for hardware efficiency, as dequantization before matrix operations incurs significant computational overhead [8]. To bridge the gap between low-bit inference and hardware efficiency, recent works have proposed integerization processes based on operation reordering, which delay dequantization until after matrix operations to enable direct processing of quantized inputs [8]. Similarly, new arithmetic formats, such as Microscaling Integer (MXInt), have been introduced to map all ViT operations onto FPGAs, addressing the limitations of traditional fixed-point formats in handling the dynamic range required by ViT architectures [14]. These advancements aim to reduce per-PE power consumption and improve the overall efficiency of ViT deployment on edge devices [8].

# Performance and Efficiency Analysis

The deployment of Vision Transformers (ViTs) on edge devices and specialized hardware is often constrained by high computational and memory demands, particularly due to the inefficiencies introduced by mixed-precision operations and data movement bottlenecks. Recent advancements in refining datapaths for microscaling and low-bit inference have demonstrated significant improvements in power consumption, throughput, and resource utilization by addressing these specific hardware challenges.

## Reduction of Computational Overhead

A primary source of inefficiency in quantized ViT inference is the computational overhead associated with dequantization before matrix operations. Traditional approaches often retain floating-point scaling factors, which necessitate re-quantization and prevent efficient integer-only execution. To mitigate this, integerization processes based on operation reordering have been proposed, which delay dequantization until after matrix operations. This technique enables integerized matrix multiplication by directly processing quantized inputs, thereby reducing per-Processing Element (PE) power consumption for linear layers and matrix multiplication [8]. Similarly, full integer quantization methods that eliminate intermediate floating-point computations allow for robust execution on processor/memory architectures and FPGAs without the latency penalties associated with complex function handling [10].

## Hardware-Accelerated Dataflow and Memory Optimization

The end-to-end throughput of transformer inference is frequently limited by paged data movement and interconnect bandwidth rather than raw multiply-accumulate (MAC) count. Unified system-accelerator co-design approaches, such as MatrixFlow, address this by employing page-aligned block matrix multiplication with small on-chip buffers and pipelined schedules that overlap compute and data transfer operations [19]. In FPGA implementations, dynamic block-caching strategies have been employed to optimize data interaction, reducing off-chip DDR access frequency by up to 71% while maintaining high MAC utilization [20]. Furthermore, the use of microscaling floating-point formats, which employ a shared scale across a block of values, enables compact one-byte floating-point representations. This approach significantly reduces the memory footprint and computational costs associated with numerical representations in large models, as demonstrated in configurations applied to architectures like GPT-2 [21].

## Precision Scalability and Resource Efficiency

To balance accuracy with hardware constraints, various precision-scalable architectures have been developed. The TREA accelerator, for instance, integrates a dual-precision (4/8-bit) SIMD multiply-accumulate unit that supports both 4-bit and 8-bit fixed-point operations. By utilizing most-significant-digit-first shift-and-add computation with run-time bit truncation, this design achieves up to 4x throughput improvement without hardware duplication, while a structured pruning strategy enables near 50% sparsity with full MAC utilization [5]. In the context of microscaling standards, hybrid precision-scalable reduction trees have been proposed to resolve the trade-off between integer accumulation and floating-point precision. These designs enable efficient mixed-precision accumulation with controlled accuracy relaxation, addressing the quantization losses and costly normalization issues inherent in FP32 accumulation [4].

## Impact on Inference Accuracy and Latency

Despite the aggressive reduction in bit-widths, these refined datapaths maintain high inference accuracy. For example, 8-bit fixed-point quantization in CNN accelerators has shown only a 3.8% drop in mean average precision (mAP) while compressing weight storage significantly [20]. In the specific domain of ViTs, post-training quantization schemes that minimize the use of Power-of-Two scaling factors have been shown to effectively reduce model complexity while preserving performance [18]. Additionally, lightweight global modeling networks that integrate non-parametric transformation operations have been designed to capture global context with lower latency, addressing the inefficiencies of multi-branch parallel architectures [22]. These developments collectively bridge the gap between high-precision model performance and the stringent area-power-latency constraints of edge vision platforms [5].

# Discussion

The refinement of datapaths for microscaling Vision Transformers (ViTs) represents a critical intersection of algorithmic quantization and hardware architecture optimization. As ViTs become the dominant architecture in computer vision, the challenge of deploying them in latency- and power-constrained environments has driven significant research into low-precision inference and efficient hardware acceleration [23]. The core objective of refining these datapaths is to mitigate the computational overhead associated with dequantization and nonlinear operations, which traditionally create bottlenecks in low-bitwidth computing units [8][24].
A primary focus of recent advancements is the elimination of intermediate floating-point computations during inference. Traditional quantization methods often struggle with complex functions such as exponential and square root operations, requiring interaction with floating-point values that negate the benefits of low-precision arithmetic [10]. To address this, integerization processes based on operation reordering have been proposed, which delay dequantization until after matrix operations [8]. This approach enables integerized matrix multiplication by directly processing quantized inputs, thereby reducing per-PE power consumption and bridging the gap between theoretical quantization benefits and practical hardware efficiency [8]. Furthermore, robust methods for full integer quantization of ViTs have been developed to ensure that no intermediate floating-point computations are required, allowing for seamless deployment on various hardware platforms, including FPGAs and processor/memory architectures [10].
The choice of numerical format and scaling granularity significantly impacts the fidelity of the refined datapath. While standard fixed-point integer formats are common, they may not adequately capture the nuanced data distributions present in ViT activations [25]. Emerging techniques, such as block-wise fine-grained mixed formats, assign optimal number formats per block to better represent data, ensuring energy efficiency by selecting representable values as scaled integers [25]. This microscaling approach is particularly relevant for ViTs, where the self-attention mechanism involves global dependencies that can be sensitive to quantization-induced perturbations [24]. By tailoring the datapath to handle these specific numerical behaviors, it is possible to maintain high inference accuracy while utilizing low-bitwidth data representations [24].
Hardware implementation strategies, particularly on Field-Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs), play a pivotal role in realizing these refined datapaths. FPGAs offer reconfigurability and fine-grained parallelism, making them attractive for accelerating transformer workloads [23]. However, existing accelerators often offload nonlinear operations to the host CPU or execute them in floating-point format, leading to performance bottlenecks due to intermediate data transfers [24]. Recent efforts aim to develop inference accelerators with full on-device execution of all operations, including nonlinear functions, using low-bitwidth formats [24]. Additionally, advanced datapath synthesis techniques, such as those leveraging tree-parsing tools for mapping, can optimize total delay including routing delays, resulting in smaller problem sizes and faster mapping times [13]. These hardware-level refinements are essential for ensuring that the theoretical gains from microscaling and integerization are translated into tangible system-level benefits.
It is also important to consider the broader context of model efficiency, including parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and robustness. While PEFT methods like adapters can adjust pre-trained models for downstream tasks with minimal parameter updates [26][27], the underlying inference engine must still efficiently process the resulting model weights and activations. Moreover, the robustness of these efficient models against adversarial attacks, such as adversarial patches that hijack self-attention, remains a concern [28][29]. Defenses like STRAP-ViT, which use statistical properties to segregate anomalous tokens, must be integrated into the datapath without significantly increasing computational overhead [29]. Thus, the refinement of datapaths for microscaling ViTs must balance computational efficiency, numerical precision, and robustness to ensure reliable performance in real-world applications.
