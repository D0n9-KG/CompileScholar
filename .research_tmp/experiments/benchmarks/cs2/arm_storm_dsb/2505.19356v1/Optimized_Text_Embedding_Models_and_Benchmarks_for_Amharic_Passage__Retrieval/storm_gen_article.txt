# Background

Amharic is a Semitic language and one of the most widely spoken languages in Ethiopia, yet it remains significantly under-resourced in the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP)[1][2]. The language is characterized by complex morphological structures, including rich inflectional and derivational morphology, which presents unique challenges for computational processing[3][4]. Historically, the development of NLP tools for Amharic has been hindered by the scarcity of annotated linguistic data, corpora, labeled datasets, and lexicons[3]. While recent efforts have produced substantial resources, such as a cleaned version of the morphologically annotated WIC Corpus containing 210,000 words and a larger corpus of 17 million words derived from web crawls, these datasets are often limited in their specific utility for advanced retrieval tasks[1].
The emergence of transformer-based pre-trained language models, such as BERT and RoBERTa, has revolutionized text representation learning for high-resource languages[5][4]. However, the application of these models to low-resource, morphologically rich languages like Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization strategies[6][4]. Standard subword-based tokenization methods, such as Byte Pair Encoding (BPE), often fail to preserve morphological boundaries, leading to fragmented lexical representations and the loss of critical morphological information[7][8]. This limitation is particularly pronounced in agglutinative or morphologically complex languages, where a single word can encode multiple morphemes carrying syntactic and semantic information[9]. Research has shown that in low-resource contexts, preserving word boundaries via word-level tokenization can sometimes yield better embedding performance than complex statistical subword methods, highlighting the need for morphologically informed segmentation strategies[9][8].
To address these challenges, recent research has focused on developing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models and optimized embedding techniques. For instance, the RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model has demonstrated significant improvements in retrieval metrics, such as MRR@10 and Recall@10, over multilingual baselines like Arctic Embed 2.0[6]. Furthermore, frameworks such as Lexically Grounded Subword Embedding Initialization (LGSE) have been proposed to initialize embeddings for novel tokens by decomposing words into constituent morphemes, thereby constructing semantically coherent representations[8]. Additionally, the development of morpheme-aware tokenizers, such as MoVoC, aims to integrate supervised morphological analysis into subword vocabulary construction to preserve morphological integrity[7]. These advancements are critical for enhancing the performance of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and semantic search systems in underrepresented languages, where effective semantic alignment is often hindered by the lack of high-quality, large-scale datasets[10].

# Dataset Construction

The development of effective text embedding models for Amharic, a low-resource language, has been significantly hindered by the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale supervised datasets. To address this challenge, researchers have employed various strategies for dataset construction, ranging from the creation of large-scale web-crawled corpora to the generation of synthetic data and the curation of specialized retrieval benchmarks.

## Large-Scale Corpora and Preprocessing

Foundational work in Amharic NLP has focused on establishing large-scale text resources to support downstream tasks. One significant contribution is the creation of the largest Amharic text corpus, comprising 17 million words automatically crawled from web pages between 2013 and 2016.[1] This corpus was part-of-speech annotated using a tagger trained and evaluated on the WIC Corpus, a substantially cleaned version of existing morphologically annotated data containing 210,000 words.[1] These resources provide the necessary linguistic foundation for training tokenizers and pre-trained language models, which are critical for addressing the suboptimal tokenization issues often encountered in morphologically rich languages like Amharic.[6]

## Synthetic Data and Adaptation Strategies

Given the lack of pristine, human-verified translations, recent approaches have challenged the assumption that massive datasets are required for effective semantic alignment. For instance, in the context of Armenian, a low-resource language with a unique script, researchers introduced a cost-effective adaptation strategy using small-scale noisy synthetic data.[10] This data was generated by translating English Reddit title-body pairs using open-weights models.[10] Experiments demonstrated a "Less is More" phenomenon, where fine-tuning a multilingual encoder (mE5) on just 10,000 noisy synthetic pairs yielded an 11–12% average improvement in performance.[10] While this specific study focused on Armenian, the methodology highlights the viability of using synthetic data to bridge resource gaps in similar low-resource contexts.

## Retrieval-Specific Benchmarks

For neural retrieval and ranking tasks, specific datasets have been constructed to support contrastive training and benchmarking. A notable resource is the Amharic retrieval-ranking dataset, which contains 1,091 manually verified query-positive-negative document triplets.[11] These triplets were derived from diverse Amharic sources and constructed through a combination of expert-curated queries, web-derived queries, and LLM-assisted generation.[11] The positive and negative documents were selected from the web or synthesized by LLMs and subsequently validated by native speakers to ensure quality.[11] This dataset supports the evaluation of various neural retrievers, including Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR), ColBERT-style late interaction, and SPLADE-style sparse neural retrieval.[11]
Additionally, broader regional efforts have expanded multilingual benchmarks to include African languages. The AfriMTEB benchmark, a regional expansion of the Massively Multilingual MTEB (MMTEB), covers 59 languages, including Amharic, across 14 tasks and 38 datasets.[12] Unlike many existing MMTEB datasets that cover fewer than five languages, the new additions in AfriMTEB span 14 to 56 African languages and introduce new tasks such as hate speech detection, intent detection, and emotion classification.[12] These benchmarks are crucial for evaluating the performance of models like AfriE5, an adaptation of the instruction-tuned mE5 model to African languages.[12]

## Methodological Enhancements for Data Utilization

Beyond dataset creation, specific methodologies have been developed to optimize the use of limited data in retrieval models. For Amharic, researchers have introduced dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones to address data scarcity.[6] The RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model, for example, achieved a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0, despite having significantly fewer parameters.[6] Furthermore, to address the limitations of low data volumes in dense text retrieval, a multi-view contrast learning architecture named MwCR has been proposed to improve model robustness by enhancing information interaction between queries and documents.[13] In the domain of conversational retrieval, the DualReform framework offers a reference-free preference optimization approach that generates pseudo reference passages from conversational datasets containing only queries and responses, thereby reducing the reliance on impractical reference data acquisition.[14]

# Model Development

The development of text embedding models for Amharic has evolved from early statistical approaches to sophisticated neural architectures, driven by the language's morphological complexity and status as a low-resource language. Initial efforts focused on establishing foundational text representations using classical word embedding techniques. Researchers investigated the efficacy of word2vec, GloVe, and fastText, alongside the introduction of Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) models, to address the lack of usable pre-trained models for automatic Amharic text processing[4]. Concurrently, specific semantic models were developed to better capture the nuances of the Ethio-Semitic language. These included fine-tuned pre-trained models and newly trained architectures such as Word2Vec embeddings, distributional thesaurus (DT) models, BERT-like contextual embeddings, and DT embeddings derived via network embedding algorithms[15].
In the domain of information retrieval, the focus shifted toward dense retrieval models capable of handling the specific challenges of Amharic, such as data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization. To address these gaps, Amharic-specific dense retrieval models were introduced based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones. A notable advancement is the RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model, which contains 110 million parameters and achieves a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 and a 9.86% gain in Recall@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0[6]. To balance performance and computational efficiency, more compact variants such as the RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42 million parameters) were also developed, remaining competitive while being over 13 times smaller than the larger model[6]. Additionally, a ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval model was trained, achieving the highest MRR@10 score of 0.843 among evaluated models[6].
Beyond dense retrieval, research has explored sparse and hybrid representation methods to leverage the interpretability and efficiency of term-based lookups. The SPLADE model, which provides highly sparse representations, has been adapted with modifications to its pooling mechanism to improve effectiveness and efficiency in retrieval pipelines[16]. Furthermore, recent frameworks have proposed bi-directional learning between dense and sparse representations through Self-Knowledge Distillation, aiming to overcome the limitations of computationally expensive contrastive pre-training or distillation from frozen dense models[17]. In the context of low-resource languages generally, strategies such as fine-tuning multilingual encoders on small-scale, noisy synthetic data have shown promising results, suggesting that effective semantic alignment does not necessarily require massive, pristine datasets[10].
The development of these models is supported by the creation of specialized datasets and benchmarks. For instance, an Amharic data resource was released comprising a retrieval-ranking dataset with 1,091 manually verified query-positive-negative document triplets, designed to support contrastive training and benchmarking of neural retrievers like DPR, ColBERT, and SPLADE[11]. This dataset complements existing large-scale corpora, such as the 17-million-word Amharic text corpus created from web pages and annotated using a tagger trained on the WIC Corpus[1]. These resources are critical for training and evaluating the aforementioned embedding models, ensuring they are robust for downstream applications such as passage retrieval and semantic search.

# Training Methodology

The training of text embedding models for Amharic passage retrieval addresses the significant challenges posed by the language's low-resource status, including the scarcity of high-quality supervised data and the morphological complexity of the Ethio-Semitic language.[11][15] To overcome these limitations, recent methodologies have shifted away from reliance on massive, human-verified datasets toward more efficient strategies involving synthetic data generation, self-supervised learning, and advanced distillation techniques.

## Data Construction and Synthetic Adaptation

A primary challenge in training retrieval models for Amharic is the lack of large-scale, high-quality datasets.[11][10] To mitigate this, researchers have developed cost-effective adaptation strategies that utilize small-scale, noisy synthetic data. For instance, methodologies inspired by work on other low-resource languages, such as Armenian, demonstrate that fine-tuning multilingual encoders on as few as 10,000 noisy synthetic pairs—generated by translating English text using open-weights models—can yield significant improvements in semantic alignment.[10] This "Less is More" phenomenon suggests that pristine, human-verified translations are not strictly necessary for effective training if the synthetic data is sufficiently diverse.[10]
Specific to Amharic, data resources have been constructed to support both neural retrieval-ranking and instruction-following text generation.[11] The retrieval-ranking dataset includes manually verified query-positive-negative document triplets drawn from diverse sources, including expert-curated queries, web-derived queries, and LLM-assisted generation.[11] These triplets are designed to support contrastive training and benchmarking for various neural retrievers, such as Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR), ColBERT-style late interaction, and SPLADE-style sparse neural retrieval.[11] Additionally, the development of general-purpose corpora, such as the General-Purpose Amharic Corpus (GPAC), and specific datasets for tasks like fake news detection (ETH_FAKE) have provided foundational resources for training word embeddings and downstream models.[18]

## Contrastive Learning and Loss Functions

Contrastive learning is a central component in training embedding models, allowing for the flexible definition of powerful losses by contrasting positive pairs against sets of negative samples.[5] However, standard contrastive losses often fail to account for intra-modality similarities, which can lead to inefficient embeddings where the same content is mapped to multiple points in the embedding space.[5] To address this, advanced loss functions like CrossCLR have been proposed, which define sets of highly related samples based on input embeddings and exclude them from negative samples to avoid false negatives, thereby improving the quality of learned joint embeddings.[5]
In the context of self-supervision, novel parameter-free loss functions have been introduced to exploit the pre-trained language modeling capabilities of encoder models as a training signal.[19] This approach eliminates the need for expensive teacher models and batch sampling by performing implicit hard negative mining.[19] Experiments have shown that such self-distillation methods can match the effectiveness of teacher distillation using only 13.5% of the data, while offering training speedups of 3x to 15x.[19] Furthermore, research into overfitting in unsupervised contrastive learning has highlighted the need for careful monitoring of training dynamics to ensure that models do not fit too closely to the training data, which would result in poor generalization.[20]

## Knowledge Distillation and Model Optimization

Knowledge distillation from cross-encoder rankers is an effective approach to improving the performance of dual-encoder retrievers.[21] Traditional methods often rely on supervised learning settings where a query is paired with a positive passage and a batch of hard negatives.[21] However, empirical observations indicate that even hard negatives from advanced methods can be too trivial for the teacher model to distinguish, limiting the transfer of "dark knowledge" to the student model.[21] To alleviate this, frameworks such as ADAM propose the use of Adaptive Dark exAMples, creating candidate passages with moderate relevance to the query to better facilitate the transfer of knowledge.[21]
Additionally, bi-directional learning between dense and sparse representations has been explored through Self-Knowledge Distillation.[17] This framework enables mutual enhancement by using an integrated similarity score, which is a weighted sum of dense and sparse similarities, to guide the learning process.[17] This approach addresses the limitations of methods that rely solely on computationally expensive contrastive pre-training or distillation from frozen dense models.[17]

## Evaluation and Benchmarking

The training methodology is closely tied to the evaluation of the resulting models, particularly given the morphological complexity of Amharic.[15] Benchmarks such as DiaLex, while originally developed for dialectal Arabic, illustrate the importance of intrinsic evaluation for word embeddings across various syntactic and semantic relations.[22] For Amharic, specific semantic models have been introduced, including Word2Vec embeddings, distributional thesaurus (DT) embeddings, and BERT-like contextual embeddings.[15] These models are fine-tuned or trained from scratch to better fit the needs of the low-resource language, as multilingual pre-trained models often do not align well with the specific linguistic features of Amharic.[15] The availability of these specialized models and datasets allows for the thorough evaluation of embedding quality, which is vital for the success of downstream retrieval applications.[22]

# Evaluation and Results

The evaluation of Amharic passage retrieval models focuses on overcoming the challenges posed by data scarcity and the morphological complexity of the language. Recent studies have introduced Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones to address the suboptimal performance of general multilingual models in low-resource settings.[6] Among these, the RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model, which contains 110 million parameters, demonstrated significant performance gains, achieving a 17.6% relative improvement in Mean Reciprocal Rank at 10 (MRR@10) and a 9.86% increase in Recall@10 compared to the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0, which has 568 million parameters.[6] Furthermore, more compact variants such as the RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42 million parameters) remained competitive while being over 13 times smaller, highlighting the efficiency of these specialized architectures.[6] In addition to dense retrieval, a ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval model was trained and achieved the highest MRR@10 score of 0.843 among all evaluated models, indicating the potential of late interaction methods for this language.[6]
To support rigorous benchmarking, researchers have released an Amharic data resource consisting of two datasets designed for neural retrieval-ranking and instruction-following text generation.[11] The retrieval-ranking dataset comprises 1,091 manually verified query-positive-negative document triplets drawn from diverse Amharic sources.[11] These triplets were constructed through a combination of expert-curated queries, web-derived queries, and large language model (LLM)-assisted generation, with positive and negative documents selected from the web or synthesized by LLMs and subsequently validated by native speakers.[11] This dataset is specifically designed to support contrastive training and benchmarking of various neural retrievers, including Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR), ColBERT-style late interaction, and SPLADE-style sparse neural retrieval.[11]
Beyond specific Amharic models, broader advancements in retrieval techniques offer insights into optimizing performance. For instance, knowledge distillation frameworks like ADAM have been proposed to improve dual-encoder retrievers by transferring "dark knowledge" from cross-encoder rankers, addressing the issue that hard negatives are often too trivial for effective teacher-student learning.[21] Similarly, in the context of sparse representations, improvements to the SPLADE model, such as modified pooling mechanisms, have been shown to enhance both effectiveness and efficiency in neural information retrieval.[16] In the domain of cross-modal retrieval, the CrossCLR contrastive loss has been developed to fix inefficiencies in embeddings by accounting for intra-modality similarities and excluding highly related samples from negative sets, thereby improving the quality of learned embeddings for video-text retrieval.[5] Additionally, research into text embeddings from large language models has revealed that embeddings can be aligned with key tokens in the input text by adjusting the first principal component, a finding that demonstrates universal applicability across different model architectures and training strategies.[23] These methodological advancements, while often developed for high-resource languages or specific domains like Chinese multi-domain retrieval,[24] provide foundational techniques that are increasingly being adapted to improve robustness and accuracy in low-resource language retrieval tasks.[13]

# Technical Challenges and Solutions

Developing optimized text embedding models for Amharic passage retrieval presents distinct technical hurdles, primarily stemming from the language's low-resource status and complex morphological structure. A significant challenge lies in the limitations of standard subword-based tokenization methods, which often fail to preserve morphological boundaries in languages written in the Geez script, such as Amharic and Tigrinya.[7] This fragmentation can lead to a loss of critical morphological information, resulting in inefficient lexical representations.[8] To address this, researchers have proposed hybrid segmentation approaches, such as MoVoC (Morpheme-aware Subword Vocabulary Construction), which integrates supervised morphological analysis into the subword vocabulary by combining morpheme-based and Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) tokens.[7] While such methods may not always yield significant gains in automatic translation quality, they consistently improve intrinsic metrics by preserving morphological integrity.[7] Furthermore, frameworks like Lexically Grounded Subword Embedding Initialization (LGSE) have been developed to initialize embeddings for novel tokens by decomposing words into constituent morphemes, thereby constructing semantically coherent embeddings rather than relying on arbitrary subword units.[8]
In the context of optical character recognition (OCR), which is often a prerequisite for processing digital Amharic text, transformer-based models like TrOCR have historically struggled with African syllabic writing systems.[25] The application of Latin-centric BPE conventions to the Ge'ez script can result in systematic word-boundary failures, rendering unmodified models ineffective.[25] Solutions include extending the byte-level BPE tokenizer to cover specific Ge'ez characters and introducing Word-Aware Loss Weighting to resolve these boundary issues, significantly reducing character error rates.[25]
Beyond tokenization, the training of retrieval models faces challenges related to the quality of negative samples and the efficiency of knowledge distillation. In dual-encoder retriever architectures, standard hard negatives are often too trivial for teacher models to distinguish, preventing the effective transfer of "dark knowledge" to student models.[21] To mitigate this, frameworks such as ADAM propose the use of Adaptive Dark exAMples, creating candidate passages with moderate relevance to better facilitate knowledge distillation.[21] Additionally, the high computational cost of training state-of-the-art bi-encoders using teacher models and batch sampling has led to the development of parameter-free loss functions for self-supervision.[19] These approaches exploit pre-trained language modeling capabilities to perform implicit hard negative mining, allowing self-distillation to match the effectiveness of teacher distillation with a fraction of the data and significantly faster training times.[19]
Finally, the alignment of text embeddings with key tokens remains a critical area of optimization. Recent findings indicate that text embeddings from large language models can be aligned with key tokens in the input text by adjusting the first principal component in the embedding space.[23] This alignment enhances the interpretability and utility of embeddings for retrieval tasks. Moreover, integrating dense and sparse representations through bi-directional learning, such as Self-Knowledge Distillation, allows for the mutual enhancement of retrieval models, combining the efficiency of sparse term-based lookup with the semantic richness of dense representations.[17]

# Limitations and Gaps

Despite advancements in neural information retrieval, significant challenges persist in developing robust passage retrieval systems, particularly for low-resource languages like Amharic. A primary limitation is the scarcity of large, high-quality supervised data required for training neural retrieval and generative models. In low-resource settings, this data deficit hinders the ability of models to learn effective representations, as seen in the Amharic context where only limited manually verified query-positive-negative document triplets are available for contrastive training and benchmarking[11]. This scarcity is compounded by the fact that even advanced hard negative sampling methods often produce negatives that are too trivial for teacher models to distinguish effectively, thereby preventing the transfer of abundant "dark knowledge" to student models during knowledge distillation processes[21].
Furthermore, existing dense text retrieval models face inherent constraints related to the number of available candidates, which limits overall accuracy[13]. Similar queries and documents often suffer from a lack of information interaction at low data volumes, resulting in suboptimal performance[13]. While sparse representations, such as those provided by the SPLADE model, offer advantages in interpretability and efficiency through exact term matching and inverted indexes, they often rely on computationally expensive contrastive pre-training or distillation from frozen dense models, which limits the potential for mutual enhancement between dense and sparse representations[17][16].
In the domain of cross-modal and multilingual retrieval, previous contrastive learning approaches have failed to fully exploit intra-modality similarities, leading to inefficient embeddings where identical content is mapped to multiple points in the embedding space[5]. Additionally, the exclusion of highly related samples from negative sets to avoid false negatives is a critical but often underutilized principle that, when ignored, degrades the quality of learned embeddings[5]. For morphologically complex languages, such as those written in the Geez script, subword-based tokenization methods frequently fail to preserve morphological boundaries, a limitation that can negatively impact intrinsic metrics even if automatic translation quality does not show significant gains[7]. These technical and data-driven gaps highlight the need for more efficient, morphologically aware, and data-efficient frameworks to bridge the disparity in retrieval performance for underrepresented languages[26][11].

# Future Directions

Research into optimized text embedding models for Amharic and other low-resource languages is poised for significant advancements through the integration of morphologically informed techniques and the leveraging of large language models (LLMs). A primary area of focus is addressing the limitations of current vocabulary expansion methods, which often rely on arbitrarily segmented subword units that fragment lexical representations and obscure critical morphological information. To mitigate this, future work may adopt frameworks such as Lexically Grounded Subword Embedding Initialization (LGSE), which utilizes morphologically informed segmentation to construct semantically coherent embeddings by averaging pretrained subword or FastText-based morpheme representations [8]. This approach is particularly relevant for Amharic, a morphologically rich language, where preserving structural integrity in embeddings can enhance retrieval accuracy.
Furthermore, the transition from traditional encoder-only models to decoder-only, large-scale LLMs presents new opportunities for improving embedding quality. Recent surveys indicate a paradigm shift toward using LLMs like GPT, LLaMA, and Mistral as embedding models, employing strategies such as direct prompting and data-centric tuning [27]. Future research could explore how these LLM-based embedding strategies can be adapted for Amharic to bridge the gap in multilingual generalization, particularly in underrepresented languages where standard LLMs often struggle [26]. By developing data-and-compute-efficient methods, researchers aim to improve the generalization capabilities of these models without sacrificing task-specific performance, thereby reducing the disparity in NLP abilities across different linguistic contexts [26].
In addition to model architecture improvements, the development of robust benchmarks and datasets remains a critical challenge. Current NLP research for Ethiopian languages, including Amharic, faces significant hurdles due to the scarcity of annotated linguistic data, corpora, and labeled datasets [2][3]. While centralized repositories for resources in languages like Amharic, Afaan Oromo, Tigrinya, and Wolaytta are being established to identify research gaps and disseminate information [2], the lack of high-quality, annotated data continues to hinder the development of specialized tools. For instance, the absence of usable NLP tools for related languages like Ge'ez, due to data scarcity, highlights the urgent need for comprehensive data collection efforts [3]. Future directions must therefore prioritize the creation of large-scale, high-quality Amharic corpora to support the training and evaluation of advanced embedding models.
Finally, the application of contrastive learning principles offers a promising avenue for enhancing cross-modal and intra-modal embedding quality. Techniques such as CrossCLR, which account for intra-modality similarities and exclude highly related samples from negative sets to avoid false negatives, have shown consistent improvements in embedding quality for video-text retrieval [5]. Adapting these contrastive loss functions to Amharic text retrieval could help address issues where similar content is mapped to multiple points in the embedding space, leading to more efficient and accurate retrieval systems. By integrating these advanced loss functions with morphologically aware embeddings and LLM-based architectures, future research can develop more robust and effective text embedding models for Amharic passage retrieval.
