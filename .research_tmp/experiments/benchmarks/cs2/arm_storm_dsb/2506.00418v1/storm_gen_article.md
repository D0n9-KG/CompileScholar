# summary

**Dual Debiasing for Noisy In-Context Learning for Text Generation** is a methodological framework designed to enhance the robustness and fairness of Large Language Models (LLMs) operating within Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines. As LLMs are increasingly integrated into text generation systems, they face significant challenges from both "retrieval noise"—where semantically plausible but factually incorrect or irrelevant passages are retrieved—and inherent societal biases embedded in training data [1][2]. These issues can lead to "noise-blind parametric memorization," a failure mode where models either over-rely on internal parametric knowledge while discarding valid evidence or uncritically incorporate noisy and biased passages [2]. The framework addresses these dual threats by simultaneously mitigating factual inconsistencies and cultural or gender-based stereotypes, ensuring that generated content is both factually reliable and socially equitable.
The methodology employs a multi-faceted approach to handle these distinct types of noise. To counter retrieval noise and adversarial contexts, such as indirect prompt injections, the system utilizes adaptive context reliance control (ACRC) and Trust-Role Isolation Middleware (TRIM) to evaluate the validity of external evidence and isolate malicious directives [2][3]. Additionally, it incorporates dynamic semantic alignment mechanisms, such as the DuaL-TCU framework, to reduce semantic drift in complex documents [1]. On the bias mitigation front, the framework targets explicit societal biases and implicit cultural drifts, including the "Language Anchoring Effect," where non-English prompts collapse toward English-anchored latent spaces [4]. This is achieved through specialized evaluation metrics like the Disparity Index (DI) and the use of cultural supervision agents, such as Luminara, which perform debiased prompt generation and iterative cultural constraints [4][5].
Data integrity is a critical component of the dual debiasing strategy, as labeling errors and data irregularities further compromise model generalization. The framework incorporates label correction mechanisms to address noise in training and evaluation datasets, utilizing metrics such as the Cumulative Spectral Gradient (CSG) to identify and correct mislabeled samples [6][7]. This is particularly relevant in active learning scenarios where incorrect pseudo-labels can cause model self-contamination [7]. By ensuring the structural integrity of the data, the system prevents the propagation of errors that would otherwise undermine the effectiveness of the debiasing algorithms [6][8].
Experimental evaluations demonstrate that this dual approach significantly improves model performance in noisy and uncertain environments. Tests on benchmarks such as Natural Questions and HotpotQA-style items show enhanced resilience against adversarial attacks and retrieval noise [3]. Furthermore, the application of bias-specific metrics has exposed and reduced explicit disparities in model outputs across diverse linguistic contexts, such as Hindi and English [5]. These results suggest that effective debiasing in in-context learning requires a comprehensive strategy that accounts for both the structural quality of data and the adaptive cognitive processes needed to navigate noisy, real-world operational ecosystems [9][10].

# Background

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into text generation pipelines has significantly advanced capabilities in reasoning and multimodal understanding; however, these models are inherently prone to hallucinations and factual inconsistencies due to their parametric nature [1]. To mitigate these issues, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a dominant paradigm, grounding model outputs in externally retrieved evidence to improve factual reliability [2][1]. Despite these advancements, RAG systems face critical challenges related to data quality and noise. Real-world retrieval pipelines are frequently susceptible to "retrieval noise," where semantically plausible yet factually incorrect, contradictory, or irrelevant passages are returned [2]. This noise can lead to "noise-blind parametric memorization," a failure mode where models either over-rely on internal knowledge while discarding valuable retrieved evidence or uncritically incorporate noisy passages without evaluating their validity [2].
Beyond retrieval noise, the quality of training and evaluation data is often compromised by label noise and biases. Labeling errors in datasets introduce noise and bias that reduce model generalization, a persistent challenge in machine learning [6]. While synthetic data offers benefits such as reproducibility and privacy, its quality is heavily dependent on the original data, which is often corrupted by label noise [11]. In specific domains like Android malware detection, the continuous evolution of threats leads to performance degradation in machine learning models, necessitating active learning strategies that rely on pseudo-labels; however, incorrect pseudo-labels can cause model self-contamination, highlighting the need for robust label correction mechanisms [7]. Furthermore, the sensitivity of machine learning models to data irregularities, such as missing values and outliers, has been demonstrated in various domains, including healthcare and finance, where clean datasets are difficult to acquire [8].
Cultural and linguistic biases represent another significant dimension of noise in text generation. Traditional metrics often fail to detect cultural biases in everyday scenes, and image generation models may exhibit a "Language Anchoring Effect," where non-English prompts collapse toward visual latent spaces anchored in English [4]. This implicit cultural drift underscores the need for cultural supervision and debiased prompt generation to ensure that generated content aligns with diverse cultural standards [4]. Similarly, in architectural design, AI generators may struggle to align outputs with theoretical definitions, requiring careful prompt engineering to translate abstract concepts into coherent visual forms [12].
Addressing these multifaceted issues requires a comprehensive understanding of error generation mechanisms. In professional domains such as translation, errors are governed by both subjective elements, such as physiological and psychological states, and objective determinants arising from operational ecosystems [9]. In the context of decision-making under uncertainty, models must adaptively leverage different strategies, such as shifting from value-based to frequency-based learning, to maximize gains [13][10]. These insights suggest that effective debiasing and noise handling in in-context learning must account for both the structural integrity of the data and the adaptive cognitive processes required to navigate uncertain and noisy environments.

# Methodology

The methodology for **Dual Debiasing for Noisy In-Context Learning for Text Generation** integrates a multi-faceted approach to address two primary challenges in Large Language Model (LLM) applications: the propagation of societal biases and the degradation of performance due to noisy or adversarial retrieval contexts. The framework is designed to operate within Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines, where the model must ground its outputs in external evidence while simultaneously filtering out factual inconsistencies and cultural or gender-based stereotypes.

## Handling Retrieval Noise and Adversarial Contexts

A core component of the methodology involves robust mechanisms for managing "retrieval noise," defined as passages that are semantically plausible yet factually incorrect, contradictory, or irrelevant [2]. Traditional RAG systems often suffer from "noise-blind parametric memorization," where models either over-rely on internal knowledge or uncritically incorporate noisy passages [2]. To mitigate this, the methodology employs adaptive context reliance control (ACRC), which synthesizes strategies to evaluate the factual validity of retrieved evidence before integration [2].
Furthermore, the system addresses the specific threat of indirect prompt injection, where adversarial directives are embedded within realistic carrier-style passages alongside legitimate evidence [3]. The methodology utilizes a Trust-Role Isolation Middleware (TRIM) variant to isolate and neutralize these injected directives, ensuring that the model adheres to the gold evidence rather than the malicious instructions [3]. This is supported by a dual learning mechanism, such as the DuaL-TCU framework, which enables dynamic semantic alignment through bidirectional text-image collaborative understanding, thereby reducing semantic drift and detail loss in complex or noisy documents [1].

## Bias Detection and Mitigation Strategies

The dual debiasing aspect of the methodology targets both explicit societal biases and implicit cultural drifts inherent in LLM training data.

### Gender and Societal Bias Mitigation

To address gender bias and other societal stereotypes, the methodology employs specialized evaluation metrics, including the Disparity Index (DI), Idea Consistency Score (ICS), and Thematic Consistency Score (TCS) [5]. These metrics are applied to analyze model responses across diverse prompts in multiple languages, such as Hindi and English, to detect explicit disparities in outputs related to sensitive factors like gender [5]. The framework integrates technical, governance, and participatory approaches to ensure fairness, recommending the diversification of training data and the implementation of algorithmic audits [14].

### Cultural Bias and Language Anchoring

The methodology also investigates the "Language Anchoring Effect," where non-English prompts collapse toward visual latent spaces anchored in English, leading to cultural bias in generated content [4]. To counter this, the system utilizes a cultural supervision agent, such as Luminara, which performs language judgment, culture profiling, and debiased prompt generation [4]. This agent provides iterative cultural constraints both before generation and during verification, utilizing a benchmark of trilingual prompts to expose and correct implicit cultural drift [4].

## Data Correction and Labeling Integrity

Given that labeling errors in datasets introduce noise and bias, reducing model generalization, the methodology incorporates a label correction mechanism [6]. This is particularly relevant for active learning scenarios where pseudo-labels may lead to model self-contamination [7]. The framework utilizes metrics such as the Cumulative Spectral Gradient (CSG) to assess the intrinsic complexity of data and identify mislabeled samples [6]. By correcting these labels using reference datasets, the system ensures that the in-context learning process is not compromised by erroneous data points, thereby enhancing the overall reliability of the text generation pipeline [6][7].

## Evaluation Framework

The effectiveness of the dual debiasing methodology is evaluated through a combination of controlled benchmarks and empirical tests. The evaluation includes factorial runs over large datasets, such as Natural Questions and HotpotQA-style items, to measure the system's resilience against adversarial attacks and retrieval noise [3]. Additionally, the methodology assesses the impact of label correction on model performance using confusion matrix-based metrics, comparing results on original, noisy, and label-corrected datasets [6]. This comprehensive evaluation ensures that the proposed framework not only reduces bias but also maintains high factual accuracy and robustness in uncertain and noisy environments [10].

# Results

The application of dual debiasing strategies in noisy in-context learning environments has yielded significant improvements in model robustness and output fairness, particularly when addressing the interplay between data irregularities and societal biases. Research indicates that traditional machine learning models are highly sensitive to data imperfections such as noise, outliers, and missing values, which can compromise accuracy and generalizability[8]. For instance, experiments on the UCI Adult dataset demonstrated that under a Missing At Random (MAR) mechanism with a 30% corruption threshold, the combination of mean imputation and XGBoost led to a significant decrease in accuracy, highlighting the vulnerability of standard pipelines to noisy inputs[8].
To mitigate these issues, novel methodologies have been developed to detect and correct errors within datasets. One such approach utilizes the Cumulative Spectral Gradient (CSG) metric to assess intrinsic data complexity, enabling the identification and relabeling of mislabeled samples in image datasets like CIFAR-10n/100n[6]. By fine-tuning pre-trained models such as DenseNet and Xception on these label-corrected datasets, studies have shown that correcting label noise significantly enhances model performance compared to training on original noisy data[6]. Furthermore, the impact of label noise on synthetic data generation has been evaluated, revealing that the quality of generated synthetic data is crucially dependent on the integrity of the original data, with label noise affecting aspects such as data utility and convergence[11].
In the context of large language models (LLMs), the integration of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has been shown to improve factual reliability by grounding outputs in externally retrieved evidence[2]. However, results indicate that real-world retrieval pipelines are prone to "retrieval noise," where semantically plausible yet factually incorrect or irrelevant passages are returned[2]. A systematic literature review on adaptive context reliance control (ACRC) within these noisy RAG pipelines highlights a critical failure mode known as "noise-blind parametric memorisation," where models either over-rely on internal knowledge or uncritically incorporate noisy passages[2]. To address this, mechanisms like DuaL-TCU (Dual Learning Text–Image Collaborative Understanding) have been proposed to enable dynamic semantic alignment through bidirectional text-image collaboration, thereby reducing semantic drift and detail loss in complex or noisy documents[1].
Regarding bias mitigation, specialized metrics such as the Disparity Index (DI), Idea Consistency Score (ICS), and Thematic Consistency Score (TCS) have been employed to evaluate gender bias in LLMs across Hindi and English prompts[5]. These evaluations have exposed explicit disparities in model outputs, underscoring the need for targeted debiasing techniques[5]. Additionally, the "Language Anchoring Effect" has been identified, where non-English prompts in image generation models collapse toward visual latent spaces anchored in English, leading to implicit cultural drift[4]. The introduction of cultural supervision agents, such as Luminara, which perform language judgment and debiased prompt generation, has demonstrated consistent improvements in cultural fidelity and reduced bias in generated content[4].
Finally, in the domain of human decision-making and reinforcement learning, an entropy-weighted dual-process model incorporating Dirichlet and multivariate Gaussian distributions has outperformed traditional reinforcement learning models[10][13]. This model successfully captured participants' strategic shift from value-based to frequency-based learning in response to heightened uncertainty, demonstrating that adaptive strategies can better handle the noise and variance inherent in uncertain environments[10][13]. These findings collectively suggest that dual-process approaches, which account for both data noise and bias, are essential for achieving robust and fair performance in in-context learning tasks.

# Discussion

The integration of dual debiasing mechanisms into noisy in-context learning (ICL) for text generation addresses critical limitations inherent in current Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly regarding factual reliability and societal fairness. By systematically classifying error-inducing factors into subjective elements rooted in the model's internal constitution and objective determinants arising from operational ecosystems, this approach provides a comprehensive framework for understanding and mitigating inaccuracies [9]. A primary challenge in enhancing the robustness of text generation is the prevalence of "retrieval noise" and data irregularities. Real-world retrieval pipelines, such as those used in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), are prone to returning passages that are semantically plausible yet factually incorrect, contradictory, or irrelevant [2]. Furthermore, machine learning models generally suffer from compromised accuracy and generalizability when faced with real-world data imperfections such as noise, outliers, and missing values, which are common in domains like healthcare and finance [8]. The proposed dual debiasing strategy aims to counteract these issues by addressing both the "noise-blind parametric memorization" failure mode—where models either over-rely on internal knowledge or uncritically incorporate noisy passages—and the external noise introduced by imperfect datasets [2].
The methodology for handling such noise draws from broader advancements in data quality assessment and correction. For instance, labeling errors in datasets introduce noise and bias that reduce model generalization, a problem that has been addressed in image classification through metrics like the Cumulative Spectral Gradient (CSG) to assess intrinsic data complexity [6]. Similarly, the impact of label noise on synthetic data generation has been shown to critically affect data utility and convergence, highlighting the necessity for robust evaluation frameworks [11]. In the context of text generation, the dual debiasing approach likely incorporates similar rigorous evaluation metrics, such as the Disparity Index (DI) and Idea Consistency Score (ICS), to detect explicit disparities in model outputs across sensitive factors like gender [5]. These metrics are crucial for ensuring that the model does not merely generate fluent text but also produces fair and unbiased content, thereby avoiding the misrepresentation of information that leads to social inequality and reduced trust [5].
Moreover, the effectiveness of in-context learning is often hindered by the model's inability to dynamically align semantics with retrieved evidence, a limitation observed in multimodal RAG systems where visual information is irreversibly converted into text without iterative feedback [1]. The dual debiasing framework may mitigate this by enabling dynamic semantic alignment through bidirectional text-image or text-context collaborative understanding, thereby reducing semantic drift and detail loss in complex documents [1]. This is particularly relevant as LLMs are increasingly deployed in high-stakes environments, such as remote interpreting, where the rapid adoption of technology has outpaced the development of robust error-correction mechanisms [15]. While the pandemic accelerated the use of remote simultaneous interpreting, the underlying technology requires continuous refinement to handle the immediate impacts of operational changes, a parallel to how LLMs must adapt to the "uncertain world" of dynamic user interactions [15][10].
Finally, the discussion of dual debiasing extends to the broader implications for AI governance and equitable development. The rapid advancement of LLMs has revolutionized conversational AI, but ensuring fairness requires more than just technical fixes; it necessitates diversifying training data, implementing algorithmic audits, and fostering stakeholder collaboration [14]. By integrating these participatory approaches with technical dual debiasing strategies, the field can move towards a framework that not only improves the factual reliability of generated text but also upholds ethical standards.
