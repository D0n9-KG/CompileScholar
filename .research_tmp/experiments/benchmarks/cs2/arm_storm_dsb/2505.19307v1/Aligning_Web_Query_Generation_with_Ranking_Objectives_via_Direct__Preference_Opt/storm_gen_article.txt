# Overview

Aligning web query generation with ranking objectives via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) addresses the challenge of ensuring that generated search queries effectively serve the underlying ranking goals of information retrieval systems. While Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become a prevalent technique for enhancing Large Language Model (LLM) systems by grounding them in factual evidence, it introduces significant complexity regarding the optimization of various components, including the LLM, embedding, and ranker models.[1] The integration of these components often leads to intractably large solution spaces and noisy objective evaluations, making the collective optimization of the entire configuration a critical yet under-explored area, particularly in multi-objective settings involving cost, latency, safety, and alignment.[1]
Traditional alignment methods often rely on simplified signals, such as scalar rewards or binary preferences, which may limit the system's ability to capture complex, hierarchical human expertise or specific retrieval nuances.[2] In the context of RAG, existing reliability methods that depend on scalar confidence scores frequently fail to explicitly distinguish between epistemic uncertainty and inherent data ambiguity, leading to potential knowledge conflicts between internalized parameters and retrieved information.[3] To overcome these limitations, advanced frameworks have begun shifting from scalar probabilities to more explicit evidence distributions or hierarchical evaluation criteria.[3][2] For instance, approaches like Evidence-based Reliability Alignment (ERA) model internal and external knowledge as independent belief masses to enhance abstention behavior, while other methods employ hierarchical, fine-grained evaluation criteria to decompose quality into multiple positive and negative attributes.[3]
The application of Direct Preference Optimization in this domain aims to extend beyond binary preferences to align the target model with non-binary, hierarchical criteria that reflect the specific objectives of web search ranking.[2] By reformulating the alignment problem to simultaneously maximize the probability of positive attributes while minimizing negative ones, such methods seek to bridge the gap between generative query formulation and the precise requirements of downstream ranking tasks.[2] This approach addresses the high cost of evaluations and the need for superior Pareto fronts in multi-objective optimization, ensuring that the generated queries are not only linguistically coherent but also strategically aligned with the retrieval system's performance metrics.[1]

# Background

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into information retrieval systems has significantly advanced the capabilities of query generation and expansion. While LLMs have enabled a generative paradigm for query expansion, their high inference costs often make direct deployment in practical retrieval systems difficult [4]. To address these challenges, various frameworks have been developed to optimize query quality and align generation with retrieval objectives. One prominent approach involves using reinforcement learning (RL) to train LLMs for query generation through trial and error without the need for supervised data, utilizing retrieval metrics as rewards to maximize performance [5]. Similarly, agentic search paradigms leverage RL to enable adaptive and self-improving search behavior, allowing LLMs to plan, retrieve, and reflect through multi-step interactions with search environments [6].
Despite these advancements, the quality of intermediate search queries remains a critical bottleneck. Existing works have largely focused on optimizing reasoning paradigms, often overlooking the accuracy of the generated queries themselves, which can lead to unexpected retrieval results and limit overall effectiveness [7]. To mitigate this, frameworks such as SmartSearch have introduced process rewards and query refinement mechanisms to provide fine-grained supervision for intermediate queries [7]. Furthermore, the variability in quality of synthetic training queries generated by LLMs has been identified as a source of suboptimal downstream retrieval performance, prompting the development of methods that filter noisy query-document pairs or integrate ranking signals directly into the generation process [8].
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has emerged as a key technique for aligning models with specific objectives, offering a simpler and more stable alternative to traditional Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) [9]. In the context of retrieval, DPO has been applied to integrate ranking signals into query generation, aiming to directly optimize models towards generating high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness [8]. Additionally, DPO has been utilized in retrieval-feedback-driven distillation frameworks to transfer retrieval-friendly expansion behavior from strong teacher models to compact student models, using retrieval-metric-driven strategies to form preference pairs [4]. Beyond query generation, DPO and its variants, such as K-order Ranking Preference Optimization (KPO), are also being adapted to enhance ranking abilities by focusing on top-K ranking consistency, which is often more relevant to real-world user needs than full-order consistency [10]. These developments highlight a growing trend toward using preference optimization to bridge the gap between generative query formulation and precise ranking objectives in web search systems.

# Methodology

The methodology for aligning web query generation with ranking objectives via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) addresses the high inference costs of using Large Language Models (LLMs) directly for query expansion in practical retrieval systems. To overcome these limitations, the approach employs a retrieval-feedback-driven distillation and preference-alignment framework that transfers retrieval-friendly expansion behavior from a strong teacher model to a compact student model.[4] This process avoids reliance on few-shot exemplars at inference time by leveraging teacher-generated expansions produced under both zero-shot and few-shot prompting conditions as supervision signals for distillation and as candidate pools for preference construction.[4]
A central component of the methodology is the construction of preference pairs using a retrieval-metric-driven strategy. Rather than relying on arbitrary or heuristic pairings, the framework automatically forms chosen and rejected expansion pairs based on differences in ranking metrics, specifically nDCG@10.[4] This ensures that the preference data is directly aligned with the ultimate goal of improving retrieval quality. The student model is then trained using Direct Preference Optimization to explicitly align its outputs with these retrieval-optimized preferences.[4]
This approach builds upon broader developments in DPO, which allow LLMs to function as implicit ranking models by maximizing the margin between preferred and non-preferred responses.[11] However, standard DPO implementations often face challenges with sparse or limited pairwise feedback, particularly in in-context settings where users may only identify a few relevant items rather than providing detailed comparisons for all possible pairs.[11] The proposed methodology mitigates this by utilizing the structured feedback derived from retrieval metrics to create robust training signals. Furthermore, the framework addresses potential failure modes in preference learning, such as likelihood displacement, where the absolute probability of preferred responses may decrease during training, potentially shifting probability mass toward out-of-distribution responses.[12] By grounding the preference optimization in concrete retrieval performance metrics, the method aims to maintain stable alignment while enhancing the model's ability to generate queries that effectively rank relevant documents.

# Experimental Setup

The experimental evaluation of the proposed framework for aligning web query generation with ranking objectives via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is designed to address the challenge of varying quality in synthetic training queries. The study utilizes large-scale web document collections, such as ClueWeb22, to provide the necessary corpus for generating and evaluating queries [8]. A primary objective of the setup is to demonstrate that integrating ranking signals directly into the query generation process yields superior downstream retrieval performance compared to existing methods that rely on external re-rankers to filter noisy query-document pairs [8].
To assess the effectiveness of the alignment, the experiments focus on maximizing downstream retrieval effectiveness by generating high-quality queries. The evaluation metrics prioritize the ranker-assessed relevance between the generated queries and the target documents, aiming to show that the DPO-integrated model produces queries with higher relevance scores than baseline approaches [8]. This setup contrasts with other retrieval enhancement strategies, such as DeepRetrieval, which employs reinforcement learning with retrieval metrics as rewards to train LLMs for query generation without supervised data [5]. While DeepRetrieval reports significant improvements in recall for literature and trial search, the current experimental setup specifically targets the optimization of query-document pair relevance through preference optimization, addressing the limitations of term-based approaches that may fail to capture semantic relevance despite high term matching [13].
The experimental design also considers the broader context of retrieval-augmented systems, where the quality of the retrieval component significantly impacts the overall performance of language models. By leveraging DPO, the setup aims to mitigate the suboptimal performance often caused by noisy synthetic queries, ensuring that the generated queries are not only semantically aligned with the documents but also optimized for the specific ranking objectives of the search engine [8]. This approach is distinct from methods that modify language model architectures for retrieval-augmented language modeling, as it focuses on the query generation side of the pipeline to improve the input to the retrieval system [14].

# Results

Experimental evaluations demonstrate that aligning web query generation with ranking objectives via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) significantly enhances downstream retrieval performance across various benchmarks. In the domain of literature search, the DeepRetrieval framework, which utilizes reinforcement learning to train Large Language Models (LLMs) for query generation without supervised data, achieved a recall of 65.07% for publication search and 63.18% for trial search. These results substantially outperform previous state-of-the-art methods, which recorded recalls of 24.68% and 32.11%, respectively, when using real-world search engines[5].
Further studies indicate that integrating ranking signals directly into the query generation process via DPO leads to higher ranker-assessed relevance between query-document pairs compared to methods that rely on external re-rankers for filtering noisy synthetic queries[8]. This approach allows models to directly optimize for high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness, addressing the variability in quality often seen in synthetic training data[8]. Additionally, research into retrieval-feedback-driven distillation and preference alignment has shown that transferring retrieval-friendly expansion behavior from strong teacher models to compact student models using DPO can effectively reduce inference costs while maintaining high performance. By automatically forming chosen/rejected expansion pairs based on nDCG@10 differences, this framework successfully aligns the student model's outputs with retrieval metrics[4].
In the context of ranking tasks specifically, optimizing for top-K ranking consistency has been shown to be more appropriate for real-world applications than full-order list ranking consistency. The K-order Ranking Preference Optimization (KPO) method, which extends the DPO Plackett-Luce model to accommodate top-K rankings, addresses the fact that users are typically concerned only with the top results and that tail items often lack precise feedback[10]. Furthermore, systematic experiments on multimodal sequential recommendation have revealed that modifying DPO training by replacing deterministic hard negatives with stochastic sampling from a dynamic top-K candidate pool consistently improves ranking performance. This improvement is attributed to the reduction of erroneous suppressive gradients caused by false negatives and the retention of informative hard signals[15].
While DPO has become a widely used offline preference optimization algorithm due to its simplicity, it is prone to overfitting and collapse, often struggling to balance the generation probability of chosen versus rejected responses[16][17]. To address these limitations, variants such as Bounded-DPO (BDPO) have been proposed to bound the influence of rejected responses, achieving a more balanced optimization of both chosen and rejected responses[16]. Similarly, Linear Preference Optimization (LPO) has been introduced as a novel alignment framework to mitigate the overfitting and collapse issues inherent in standard DPO[17]. These advancements in DPO variants contribute to the robustness of preference alignment in web query generation and ranking systems, ensuring that models can effectively learn from preference data without suffering from the instability of the base DPO algorithm[16][17].

# Discussion

The alignment of web query generation with ranking objectives represents a critical evolution in information retrieval, addressing the fundamental semantic gap between user intent and document relevance. Traditional term-based approaches, which represent queries and documents as bags of words, often fail to capture the nuances of query semantics, leading to scenarios where high term matching does not correlate with high relevance[13]. To bridge this gap, recent research has increasingly focused on leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate more semantically rich queries. However, a significant challenge remains in ensuring that these generated queries are not only semantically accurate but also optimized for the specific ranking utilities of downstream search engines.
A primary area of discussion involves the misalignment between general-purpose LLM outputs and task-specific ranking metrics. Existing systems often suffer from a disconnect where the LLM's latent reasoning does not account for the specific feedback signals provided by the ranking model[18]. To mitigate this, frameworks such as RGAlign-Rec have proposed closed-loop alignment mechanisms that integrate LLM-based semantic reasoners with query-enhanced ranking models[18]. By utilizing downstream ranking signals as feedback, these systems can refine the LLM's reasoning process, effectively closing the loop between query generation and retrieval performance. This approach highlights the necessity of moving beyond static query generation to dynamic, feedback-driven optimization.
Furthermore, the quality of intermediate search queries in agentic search paradigms has emerged as a pivotal factor in overall system effectiveness. While many works focus on optimizing reasoning paradigms, the accuracy of the queries themselves often remains overlooked, leading to suboptimal retrieval results[7]. Frameworks like SmartSearch address this by introducing process rewards that provide fine-grained supervision for each intermediate query, allowing for the selective refinement of low-quality search terms[7]. Similarly, DeepRetrieval employs reinforcement learning to train LLMs for query generation through trial and error, using retrieval metrics as rewards to maximize performance without relying on expensive supervised data[5]. These methods demonstrate that direct optimization against ranking or retrieval objectives can significantly outperform traditional supervised approaches, achieving substantial gains in recall for both publication and trial searches[5].
The application of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its variants has also been central to aligning query generation with ranking objectives. While DPO offers a stable alternative to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), it is prone to overfitting and collapse, particularly when dealing with the complex preference structures inherent in ranking tasks[17]. To address these limitations, novel alignment frameworks such as Linear Preference Optimization (LPO) have been proposed, introducing gradient decoupling and controllable rejection suppression to improve training stability[17]. Additionally, the Overfitting-Underfitting Dilemma in offline preference optimization has been identified, where current margin designs may waste computational effort on correctly ranked samples while failing to adequately correct misranked ones[9]. Algorithms like Adaptive Margin-attached Preference Optimization (AMaPO) aim to resolve this by dynamically reallocating learning effort, thereby enhancing the model's ability to distinguish between relevant and irrelevant query-document pairs[9].
In the context of competitive search environments, where document publishers actively modify content to improve their ranking, the alignment of query generation becomes even more complex. Publishers increasingly leverage LLMs to generate competitive content, necessitating that query generation systems account for the strategies of competing agents[19]. Frameworks like Reinforcement Learning from Ranker Feedback (RLRF) train LLMs using preference datasets derived from ranking competitions, enabling agents to optimize content for improved ranking while adapting to out-of-distribution ranking functions[19]. This competitive dynamic underscores the need for robust alignment techniques that can generalize across different ranking functions and adversarial settings.
Finally, the integration of these alignment techniques into broader Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems presents challenges in multi-objective optimization. RAG systems involve numerous hyperparameters and model choices, and collectively optimizing these for cost, latency, safety, and alignment remains under-explored[1]. Bayesian optimization methods have shown promise in navigating these intractably large solution spaces, offering a path toward superior Pareto fronts for RAG configurations[1]. As LLMs continue to evolve, the ability to dynamically adjust reasoning processes, such as through the Think in Blocks framework, may further enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of query generation by allowing models to allocate computational resources based on task complexity[20]. Collectively, these advancements suggest a future where query generation is not merely a semantic task but a deeply integrated component of the ranking pipeline, optimized through continuous feedback and preference-based learning.
