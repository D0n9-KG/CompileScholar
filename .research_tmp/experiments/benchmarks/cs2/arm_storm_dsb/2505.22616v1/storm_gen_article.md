# summary

PS4PRO (Pixel-to-pixel Supervision for Photorealistic Rendering and Optimization) is a lightweight video frame interpolation model designed to function as a data augmentation method for neural rendering tasks.[1] Neural rendering techniques have gained significant attention for their ability to reconstruct 3D scenes from 2D images by optimizing the reconstructed scene to minimize uncertainty in geometry and appearance across multiple views.[1] However, the quality of these reconstructions is often limited by the number of input views, a constraint that is particularly pronounced in complex and dynamic scenes where certain object angles may never be observed.[1]
To address this limitation, PS4PRO leverages video frame interpolation to generate additional intermediate frames, thereby augmenting the available data for rendering optimization.[1] The model is trained on diverse video datasets, allowing it to implicitly model camera movement as well as real-world 3D geometry.[1] By integrating this interpolation capability into the neural rendering pipeline, PS4PRO helps overcome the scarcity of input views, enhancing the overall reconstruction quality.[1]
This approach aligns with broader trends in computer vision where video frame interpolation is utilized to extract inter-frame motion and appearance information.[2] While many methods focus on general video synthesis, PS4PRO specifically targets the application of these techniques to improve photorealistic rendering and optimization processes.[2] By generating synthetic views that are consistent with the underlying 3D structure, the model provides a robust solution for scenarios where capturing sufficient real-world data is impractical or impossible.[1]
