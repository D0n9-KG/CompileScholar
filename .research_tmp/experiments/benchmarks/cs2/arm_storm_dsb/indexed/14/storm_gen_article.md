# summary

**Self-supervised ControlNet with Spatio-Temporal Mamba for Real-world Video Super-Resolution** is a deep learning framework designed to enhance the fidelity and temporal consistency of video super-resolution (VSR) in real-world scenarios. The method addresses significant limitations in existing diffusion-based VSR approaches, which often suffer from complex degradations and noticeable artifacts due to inherent randomness [1]. By integrating self-supervised learning with Mamba-based state space models (SSMs) within pre-trained latent diffusion models, the framework aims to produce high-resolution video outputs that maintain structural stability and content consistency across adjacent frames [1].
The architecture leverages the linear complexity and large receptive fields of the Mamba architecture to overcome the quadratic computational costs associated with Transformer-based methods and the local receptive field constraints of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) [2]. Specifically, the model employs a global spatio-temporal attention mechanism featuring a Video State-Space block with a 3D Selective Scan module, which reinforces temporal coherence at an affordable computational cost [1]. This design allows for efficient modeling of long-range dependencies and non-local interactions across misaligned frames, a critical requirement for processing long video sequences [3].
To further mitigate artifacts and ensure robustness against real-world degradation, the framework introduces a self-supervised ControlNet component [1]. This module utilizes contrastive learning to extract degradation-insensitive features from low-resolution videos, leveraging high-resolution features as guidance to refine the generation process [1]. This approach complements the spatio-temporal modeling capabilities of the Mamba backbone, ensuring that the reconstructed videos exhibit high fidelity and resistance to distribution shifts in lighting, materials, and viewpoints [4][1].
The proposed method is supported by a three-stage training strategy optimized for real-world performance, distinguishing it from traditional approaches that often treat spatial and temporal super-resolution as independent sub-tasks [1]. By synergizing self-supervised representation learning with efficient state space modeling, the framework offers a targeted solution to the challenges of temporal consistency and artifact reduction, contributing to the evolution of low-level computer vision techniques for industrial and surveillance applications [1][5].

# Overview

Self-supervised video understanding and generation have emerged as critical areas of research, driven by the need to process complex spatio-temporal data without relying on extensive manual annotations. Recent advancements in this field have focused on developing architectures that can effectively model both static semantics and dynamic temporal evolutions, addressing the limitations of traditional methods that often prioritize dense spatio-temporal modeling at the expense of structured clinical or semantic details [6]. For instance, in medical imaging, frameworks like Focus-to-Perceive Representation Learning (FPRL) have been proposed to emulate clinical examination by hierarchically learning intra-frame lesion-centric regions for static semantics and their evolution across frames for contextual semantics [6]. Similarly, in the domain of micro-expression analysis, state space model-based architectures such as ME-TST and ME-TST+ have been introduced to replace conventional sliding-window classification with video-level regression, thereby overcoming the limitations of fixed window lengths and treating spotting and recognition as separate tasks [7].
The broader landscape of video action understanding has witnessed impressive advances, enabling systems to provide coarse- and fine-grained descriptions, extract query-specific segments, and predict context across multiple modalities [8]. These systems are increasingly capable of handling diverse tasks, including recognition of fully observed actions, prediction of ongoing partially observed actions, and forecasting of subsequent unobserved actions [8]. In the realm of generative models, end-to-end human animation has seen remarkable progress, with new frameworks moving beyond single-subject assumptions to enforce strong, region-specific binding of multi-modal conditions to multiple identities, facilitating precise control over human-human and human-object interactions [9]. Furthermore, 4D generation techniques, such as DriveAnyMesh, utilize transformer-based variational autoencoders and diffusion models to animate existing 3D assets by denoising sequences of latent sets, thereby capturing both 3D shape and motion information from monocular video inputs [10].
Despite these advancements, challenges remain in ensuring that models generalize effectively across different domains and conditions. Semantic Domain Generalization (SemanticDG) highlights that while visual recognition models can easily generalize to novel lighting, they often struggle with distribution shifts in materials and viewpoints [4]. To address this, context-aware vision transformers have been proposed to leverage scene context as a bridge for generalization across these semantic shifts [4]. These developments collectively underscore the ongoing effort to create robust, self-supervised frameworks that can accurately perceive and generate video content by integrating spatio-temporal dynamics with high-level semantic understanding.

# Background

Video super-resolution (VSR) is a prominent research topic in low-level computer vision, aiming to generate high-resolution frames from low-resolution counterparts for applications such as video display and surveillance.[11][12] While deep learning technologies have driven significant progress in this field, traditional approaches face distinct limitations. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are constrained by local receptive fields, whereas Transformer-based methods struggle with quadratic computational complexity, posing challenges for processing long video sequences.[2][12] To address these issues, recent research has turned to State Space Models (SSMs), particularly the Mamba architecture, which offers linear complexity and large receptive fields suitable for long-sequence modeling.[2]
A critical challenge in VSR is the effective utilization of spatio-temporal information. Early methods often treated spatial and temporal super-resolution as independent sub-tasks, ignoring the reciprocal relations between them.[13] However, temporal correlations aid in accurate spatial detail representation, while abundant spatial information contributes to the refinement of temporal prediction.[13] Consequently, modern frameworks, such as VSRM, have introduced specific Mamba blocks (Spatial-to-Temporal and Temporal-to-Spatial) and deformable alignment modules to extract long-range spatio-temporal features and align adjacent frames more effectively.[2] Other approaches, like CycMu-Net, employ mutual learning networks to exploit these correlations through iterative up-and-down sampling.[13]
In parallel, self-supervised learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning video representations without manual annotation, which is particularly useful given the scarcity of labeled data in specialized domains.[14][15] Contrastive learning, a key technique in this area, has shown promising potential for spatio-temporal representation learning.[16] However, naive formulations that sample different clips to construct positive and negative pairs often lead to background scene bias, as static backgrounds are easier to discriminate than motion patterns.[16] To mitigate this, advanced methods have proposed decoupling input sequences into static scene and dynamic motion modes[16] or maximizing mutual information between global features and local patches to focus on temporal coherence rather than shared background content.[14] These self-supervised strategies are increasingly being integrated with efficient architectures like Mamba to enhance feature learning in tasks ranging from PolSAR image classification[15] to video action understanding.[8]
Furthermore, the integration of control mechanisms and conditional generation has expanded the capabilities of video models. Techniques such as ControlNet and personalized image synthesis methods (e.g., DreamBooth, LoRA) have advanced the ability to inject specific conditions into generation processes, though they often face challenges regarding storage demands and the need for multiple reference images.[17] Recent works have moved towards enforcing strong, region-specific binding of conditions to spatiotemporal footprints, allowing for precise control over multiple concepts and interactions within a video.[9] These developments in conditional control and efficient spatio-temporal modeling provide the foundational context for advanced frameworks that combine self-supervised learning, Mamba-based architectures, and control mechanisms for real-world video super-resolution.

# Methodology

The proposed framework addresses the limitations of existing diffusion-based video super-resolution (VSR) methods, which often introduce complex degradations and noticeable artifacts due to inherent randomness, by incorporating self-supervised learning and Mamba architectures into pre-trained latent diffusion models.[1] To ensure content consistency across adjacent frames, the method enhances the diffusion model with a global spatio-temporal attention mechanism.[1] This mechanism utilizes a Video State-Space block featuring a 3D Selective Scan module, which reinforces temporal coherence at an affordable computational cost.[1]
To further reduce artifacts in the generated high-resolution details, the framework introduces a self-supervised ControlNet.[1] This component leverages high-resolution (HR) features as guidance and employs contrastive learning to extract degradation-insensitive features from low-resolution (LR) videos.[1] The integration of these components is supported by a three-stage training strategy designed to optimize the model's performance in real-world scenarios.[1]

# Experimental Setup

The experimental evaluation of the proposed Self-supervised ControlNet with Spatio-Temporal Mamba framework for real-world video super-resolution is designed to validate its effectiveness in enhancing high-resolution frame reconstruction while maintaining temporal consistency. The methodology integrates advanced state space models, specifically leveraging Mamba architectures, to address the challenges of modeling long-range dependencies and non-local interactions across misaligned frames, which are critical in video super-resolution tasks [3]. To ensure robust performance, the framework incorporates a content-aware scanning mechanism that facilitates dynamic spatiotemporal interactions, moving beyond the rigid 1D sequential processing typical of conventional vision Mamba methods [3].
A key component of the experimental setup involves the integration of a pre-trained image super-resolution network as the foundational framework, allowing the model to leverage existing expertise for spatial information exploitation [11]. This is complemented by a fast temporal information aggregation module that utilizes deformable convolution to align features of neighboring frames, thereby effectively capturing inter-frame dependencies and temporal cues [11]. The architecture is further enhanced by a spatio-temporal stability module designed to learn self-alignment from inter-frames, employing correlative matching to exploit spatial dependencies and maintain structural stability across the reconstructed video sequence [18].
To evaluate the model's ability to handle complex dynamics and ensure high fidelity, the experiments focus on the precise exploitation of local dependencies between joints or structural elements, a challenge often faced by pure Mamba-based approaches [19]. The proposed hybrid architecture addresses this by introducing local enhancement mechanisms that capture relationships between neighboring elements, producing complementary representations to the global outputs of the Mamba backbone [19]. This adaptive fusion of representations is crucial for achieving superior performance in real-world scenarios where high-precision modeling is required [5].
The training and evaluation protocols are informed by recent advances in self-supervised learning and data augmentation techniques. Specifically, the setup considers the use of self-supervised pre-training strategies, such as odd-one-out learning, which helps in learning temporal representations that generalize well to related tasks like action recognition and video understanding [20]. Additionally, the framework benefits from insights into domain generalization, ensuring that the model can handle distribution shifts in lighting, materials, and viewpoints, which are common in real-world video data [4]. The experimental design also accounts for the efficiency trade-offs inherent in Transformer-based backbones, aiming to resolve the dilemma between accuracy and computational efficiency through the efficient long-range modeling capabilities of state space models [19].
Furthermore, the setup incorporates considerations for data diversity and robustness, drawing from methodologies that utilize diverse-scan collaboration and patch-level augmentations to enhance model performance in semi-supervised settings [21]. The evaluation metrics are aligned with the goals of high-fidelity process modeling and defect detection, ensuring that the reconstructed videos meet the stringent requirements of industrial and surveillance applications [5][11]. By combining these architectural innovations with rigorous experimental protocols, the study aims to demonstrate the superiority of the proposed Spatio-Temporal Mamba framework in real-time video super-resolution tasks.

# Results

The application of State Space Models (SSMs), particularly the Mamba architecture, has yielded significant improvements in video processing tasks by addressing the limitations of traditional recurrent and attention-based mechanisms. In the domain of video super-resolution (VSR), recent studies indicate that while Mamba offers linear-time complexity and strong long-range modeling capabilities, it initially struggles to capture fine-grained spatial dependencies when used in isolation [22]. To mitigate this, hybrid approaches that combine Mamba with other architectures have been explored to balance global context modeling with local detail preservation. For instance, in echocardiography video analysis, a Hierarchical Spatio-temporal Segmentation Network utilizing Mamba in high-level stages to capture spatio-temporal relationships, while using convolutional networks for low-level detail, demonstrated improved accuracy in Ejection Fraction (EF) estimation compared to methods that do not synergize local and global perception [23].
In the context of self-supervised representation learning, results highlight the importance of decoupling static and dynamic features to avoid background bias. A dual contrastive formulation that separates input RGB video sequences into static scene and dynamic motion modes has shown promise in steering models toward motion patterns rather than static backgrounds, which are often more easily discriminated [16]. Similarly, in endoscopic video analysis, the Focus-to-Perceive Representation Learning (FPRL) framework, which employs a hierarchical semantic modeling mechanism to distinguish between static lesion-centric semantics and contextual evolution across frames, has addressed the motion bias inherent in methods developed for natural videos, thereby enhancing the learning of static, structured semantics critical for clinical decision-making [6].
Furthermore, the integration of Mamba into other deep learning frameworks has produced positive outcomes in diverse applications. In semi-supervised medical image segmentation, the Diversity-enhanced Collaborative Mamba (DCMamba) framework leverages Mamba’s efficient handling of long-range dependencies to improve pseudo-label generation, resulting in better segmentation performance by exploiting diversity from data, network, and feature perspectives [21]. In time series forecasting for solar energy, benchmarks comparing various architectures including Mamba, Transformers, and LSTMs revealed that while Transformers achieved the highest predictive accuracy for Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI) forecasting, Mamba exhibited distinct temporal reasoning characteristics, explicitly leveraging past information in a manner that contrasts with the "recency bias" observed in Transformers [24]. These results collectively suggest that while Mamba-based architectures offer computational efficiency and robust long-range dependency modeling, their optimal performance in video and spatio-temporal tasks often depends on hybrid designs that complement their strengths with mechanisms for fine-grained spatial or static feature extraction.

# Discussion

The proposed framework addresses critical limitations in existing diffusion-based video super-resolution (VSR) methods, which often introduce complex degradations and noticeable artifacts due to their inherent randomness [1]. By integrating self-supervised learning with Mamba architectures within pre-trained latent diffusion models, the method aims to enhance content consistency and reduce artifacts in real-world scenarios [1]. This approach is particularly significant given that video super-resolution remains a major challenge in low-level vision, where traditional CNNs are limited by local receptive fields and Transformers suffer from quadratic complexity, making them less efficient for processing long sequences [2].
The utilization of Mamba, a state-space model (SSM), offers a promising alternative to Transformers by replacing expensive self-attention mechanisms with linear recurrences, thereby providing linear complexity and large receptive fields suitable for long-sequence modeling [2][25]. In this context, the framework employs a global spatio-temporal attention mechanism using a Video State-Space block with a 3D Selective Scan module to reinforce coherence between adjacent frames at an affordable computational cost [1]. This aligns with broader trends in the field, such as the development of VSRM, which leverages Spatial-to-Temporal and Temporal-to-Spatial Mamba blocks to extract long-range spatio-temporal features [2]. Furthermore, the integration of SSMs is supported by research indicating that sparsifying these models can enhance performance within given computational budgets by addressing token redundancy in upper layers [25].
To further mitigate artifacts, the proposed method introduces a self-supervised ControlNet that leverages high-resolution (HR) features as guidance [1]. This component employs contrastive learning to extract degradation-insensitive features from low-resolution (LR) videos, a strategy that complements other recent advancements in handling degraded images [26]. For instance, the Real Embedding Extractor (REE) has demonstrated significant recognition performance gains on degraded image content through similar contrastive learning techniques [26]. By combining these elements, the framework not only improves the fidelity of the generated high-resolution videos but also ensures robustness against the specific challenges posed by real-world video degradation, distinguishing it from methods that may struggle with generalizability or condition alignment [27].
The broader context of this work is situated within the rapid progress of deep learning in low-level computer vision, where comprehensive analysis of methodologies is imperative for informed model development [12]. While other domains such as medical image synthesis and 3D generation have also benefited from diffusion models and state-space architectures [27][28][10], the specific application of self-supervised ControlNet with Spatio-Temporal Mamba for VSR represents a targeted solution to the unique challenges of temporal consistency and artifact reduction in video processing [1]. This approach contributes to the ongoing evolution of VSR techniques, moving beyond simple quantitative improvements to address the qualitative aspects of visual content integrity [12][29].
