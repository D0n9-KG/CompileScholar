# summary

**FashionDPO: Fine-tuning Fashion Outfit Generation Models using Direct Preference Optimization** is a methodological approach that integrates Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) into generative AI models to align fashion outfit generation with human aesthetic and functional preferences. Traditional generative models often rely on surrogate objectives such as likelihood or reconstruction loss, which frequently fail to capture perceptual quality, semantic accuracy, or specific user constraints [1]. By shifting from these statistical patterns to preference-based supervision, FashionDPO addresses the misalignment between model outputs and the nuanced expectations of fashion consumers, offering a more direct and computationally efficient alternative to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) [2].
The necessity for this alignment is highlighted by the complexity of multimodal interactions in the retail sector, as evidenced by datasets like MMD, which comprises over 150,000 conversation sessions between shoppers and sales agents [3]. These interactions require models to generate visually coherent outfits while simultaneously understanding specific user requests and constraints. Standard fine-tuning methods are often insufficient for this task because mixed-quality training data can lead to undesired responses that do not meet the specific stylistic and contextual expectations of the domain [4]. Consequently, bespoke alignment pipelines are required to ensure that generated content is culturally and contextually appropriate, particularly in specialized or low-resource domains [5].
FashionDPO operates by leveraging preference data to refine the model’s policy directly against a reference model, allowing it to distinguish between preferred and dispreferred outfit combinations without necessarily requiring a separate reward model [2]. This process steers the generation toward higher-quality results that better match human preferences for style, fit, and context. The method is particularly effective in multimodal applications, where the synergy between improved image understanding and generation assessment suggests that unified reward mechanisms can enhance both the evaluation and creation of visual content [6]. By optimizing for these specific human feedback signals, the approach aims to produce outfit generations that are not only visually appealing but also semantically accurate to the user's intent.

# Overview

The integration of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) into fashion outfit generation models represents a significant advancement in aligning generative AI with human aesthetic and functional preferences. While large language models (LLMs) and multimodal generative models have traditionally relied on surrogate objectives such as likelihood or reconstruction loss, these metrics often fail to capture perceptual quality, semantic accuracy, or specific user preferences [1]. To address this misalignment, recent research has focused on training reward models that provide supervision signals for preference optimization, allowing models to learn from human feedback rather than just statistical patterns [6].
In the context of fashion, the challenge is compounded by the need for domain-specific understanding. The MMD benchmark dataset, which comprises over 150,000 conversation sessions between shoppers and sales agents, highlights the complexity of multimodal, domain-aware interactions in the retail sector [3]. These interactions require models to not only generate visually coherent outfits but also to understand nuanced user requests and constraints. Traditional alignment methods, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), have been effective in enhancing controllability and human alignment across various generative tasks [1]. However, DPO offers a more direct and computationally efficient alternative by optimizing the policy directly against a reference model using preference data, thereby avoiding the need for a separate reward model in some implementations [2].
The application of DPO to fashion generation involves refining the model's output to better match human preferences for style, fit, and context. This process is critical because the mixed quality of training data can lead to undesired responses, and standard fine-tuning may not sufficiently align the model with the specific expectations of fashion consumers [4]. By leveraging preference data, DPO enables the model to distinguish between preferred and dispreferred outfit combinations, effectively steering the generation process toward higher-quality, more relevant results. This approach is particularly beneficial in low-resource or specialized domains where bespoke alignment pipelines are necessary to achieve culturally and contextually appropriate outputs [5]. Furthermore, the synergy between improved image understanding and generation assessment suggests that unified reward mechanisms can foster better performance in both evaluating and creating visual content, a principle that underpins the effectiveness of DPO in multimodal fashion applications [6].
