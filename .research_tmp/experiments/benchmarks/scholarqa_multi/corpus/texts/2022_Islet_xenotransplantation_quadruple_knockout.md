# Islet xenotransplantation with quadruple knock-out porcine islet enhanced graft survival in diabetic mice

Kyungmin Kwak $^{1}$ , Jae-Kyung Park $^{1}$ , Joohyun Shim $^{1}$ , Nayoung Ko $^{1}$ , Hyoung-Joo Kim $^{1}$ , Yongjin Lee $^{1}$ , Jun-Hyeong Kim $^{1}$ , Pulip Kang $^{1}$ , Jonathan Lakey $^{2}$ , Hyunil Kim $^{1}$ , Kimyung Choi $^{1}$ .

$^{1}$ OPTIPHARM Inc, Cheongju-si, Korea; $^{2}$ Department of Biomedical Engineering, University of California, Irvine, CA, United States.

Introduction: Pancreatic islet transplantation has recently emerged as one of the most promising therapeutic approaches for improving glycemic control in Type 1 diabetes patients. However, there were some barriers like deficiency of islet supply and immune-mediated islet destruction after transplantation. Xenotransplantation with pig is one of the candidate for clinical transplantation into diabetic patients. But, some antigen are the obstacle to successful xenotransplantation. For these reasons, we isolated quadruple knockout (GGTA1/CMAH/iGb3s/B4GalNT2 knockout, QKO) porcine islets and transplanted into STZ induced diabetic C57BL/6 and NOD-SCID mice. We identified efficacy of QKO porcine islet with transplantation in diabetic mice with/without immunosuppressive agent (anti-CD154, MR1).

Methods: Quadruple porcine pancreatic islets were isolated from 12 to 18 month old pig using standard enzymatic digestion and purification. Recipients (C57BL/6 and NOD-SCID mice) were injected with STZ for diabetes induction (160 mg/kg, IP injection). Porcine islet were transplanted into the renal capsule to diabetic C57BL/6 and NOD-SCID mice (n=10 per group). For prevent immune rejection, some recipient groups were treated immunosuppressive agent (anti-CD154, MR1). To compare with QKO islet efficiency, we transplanted wildtype (WT) porcine islet into diabetic C57BL/6 and NOD-SCID recipients with or without immunosuppressive agent (anti-CD154, MR-1). We monitored body weight, non-fasting blood glucose level and porcine insulin in serum. Intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test (IPGTT) was performed in POD31 and 123.

Results: Transplantation of QKO and WT porcine islets under the kidney capsule into diabetic mice, hyperglycemia was reversed and became normoglycemia (BGL<200mg/dL) at day 1. However, the WT porcine islet transplanted group became hyperglycemia within 7 days. On the other hands, QKO porcine islet transplanted group maintained normoglycemia up to 100 days without any immunosuppressive agent (p<0.05 ANOVA). Measured porcine insulin and c-peptide level in serum, normoglycemic mice detected porcine insulin, c-peptide and mouse insulin was not detected.

Conclusion: In this study, we identified that xenotransplantation of QKO islets in diabetic mice improved glucose profile and survival rate in STZ induced diabetic mice. Transplantation of transgenic pig islets could have a significant clinical impact on the treatment of type 1 diabetes. Islet transplant studies in diabetic primates are currently underway.

# GGTA1 knockout is advantageous for IBMIR reduction after neonatal pig-to-human islet transplantation

Guoli Huai $^{1}$ , Jie Yan $^{2}$ , Hong Liu $^{2}$ , Xiangyang Xing $^{2}$ , Shaoping Deng $^{1}$ , Dengke Pan $^{1}$ .

$^{1}$ Sichuan Key Laboratory of Clinical Immunological Translational Medicine, Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, Chengdu, People's Republic of China; $^{2}$ Sino-Clone Organ Biotechnology, Co., Ltd., Chengdu, People's Republic of China.

Background: Currently, reducing the instant blood-mediated inflammatory reaction (IBMIR) and achieving long-term survival of transplanted pig islets can be enhanced by modifying single or multiple genes. However, the optimal gene combination has not been determined. To address this issue, we utilized a simple in vitro model by exposing neonatal pig islets of different genotypes to human blood to identify the relationship between genotype of neonatal pig islets and IBMIR, thus facilitating screening for the optimal gene combination.

Methods: Seven day-old neonatal pigs of three genotypes, wild-type [WT](n=6), GTKO(n=8), and GTKO/CD55(n=7) were selected as sources of islets. Some islets were retained after pancreas harvesting for subsequent genotype identification (a-Gal, CD55). Neonatal pig islet-like cell clusters (NICC) were obtained by pancreas digestion. The NICC of the three genotypes were counted, and 500 NICC were mixed with 200μl of anticoagulant-free human blood and incubated at 37°C for 60min. After incubation, the supernatant was collected to detect C-peptide, complement Bb, and cytokines (IL-6, IL-8, IL-1β, IP-10, TNF-α, IFN-γ, and MCP-1), as well as coagulation factors (TAT, β-TG, vWF, TF, F1 + 2, and β-TG). The coagulated clot was also collected for staining for IgM, IgG, CD11b, and CD68.

Results: The expression levels of coagulation factors, including TAT, $\beta$ -TG, and vWF, were significantly higher on WT islets compared to GTKO and GTKO/CD55 islets (P<0.001, P<0.05, P<0.05, respectively). The expression levels of TNF- $\alpha$ and IL-17A were significantly lower in the GTKO/CD55 group than in the WT group (P<0.01), and the expression levels of MCP-1 were significantly lower in both the GTKO and GTKO/CD55 groups than in the WT group (P<0.01). Complement Bb levels in all three genotypes were equivalent (NS). The release of pig-specific C-peptide in the GTKO (P<0.05) and GTKO/CD55 (P<0.05) groups was significantly lower than in the WT group. The deposition of IgM and IgG was significantly greater on WT islets than on GTKO and GTKO/CD55 islets. For the factors tested above, no significant difference was observed between the GTKO and GTKO/CD55 groups. Staining for CD11b and CD68 in the GTKO/CD55 group was slightly weaker than in the WT and GTKO groups, while no significant difference was observed between the WT and GTKO groups.

Conclusions: Following exposure of NICC to human blood, GTKO and GTKO/CD55 islets were associated with reduced coagulation, a weaker inflammatory response, and a reduced humoral response compared to WT islets, thus suggesting less islet damage. There was no significant differences between GTKO and GTKO/CD55 islets. The results indicate that the absence of Gal expression on the islets (i.e., the GTKO genotype) would significantly reduce the IBMIR after islet transplantation. Insertion of the hCD55 gene on the basis of the GTKO genotype did not show significant advantage.
