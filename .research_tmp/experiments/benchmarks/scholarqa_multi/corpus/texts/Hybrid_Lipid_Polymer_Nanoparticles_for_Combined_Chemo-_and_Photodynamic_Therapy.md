# 1. Evaluation of drug loading (DL) and encapsulation efficiency (EE)

Both PS and $\beta$ -Lap encapsulated in the formulations were quantified by measuring their absorption at 417 and $257~\mathrm{nm}$ , respectively. All samples, prepared in triplicate, were dissolved in a mixture of water/methanol/THF (ratio 1:4:5). At the studied wavelengths, neither the polymer nor lipids interfered with drugs absorbance (Figures S1 and S2A). Fluorescence spectroscopy showed evidence of fluorescence quenching in $m$ -THPC nanoparticles (Figure S2B). Fluorescence was partially recovered in water/methanol/THF (ratio 1:4:5).

# 3. Lipid recovery and determination of the number of bilayers adsorbed to the NPs surface

The equivalent number of lipid bilayers found on LNPs surface was estimated by assuming that a lipid bilayer fully covering the NPs had a total surface area $(\mathrm{A_v})$ equal to that of the NPs (i.e., $\mathrm{A_v / A_p = 1}$ ) (Savarala et al., 2010). Based on this theory, the total surface area of the vesicles $(\mathrm{A_v},$ equation 4) was calculated from the mass of dried PDLLA NPs used to prepare the LNPs $(\mathrm{m}_{NP})$ , the surface of one NP $(\mathrm{S}_{NP})$ , NP volume $(\mathrm{V}_{NP})$ and PDLLA density $(\rho_{PDLLA})$ . $\mathrm{S}_{NP}$ and $\mathrm{V}_{NP}$ were calculated from the mean diameter of the NPs using equations (1) and (2). $\rho_{PDLLA}$ is $1.25\mathrm{g.cm}^{-3}$ (Thevenot et al., 2008).

$$
\mathrm {S} _ {N P} = \pi \left(\frac {H D _ {N P}}{2}\right) ^ {2} \tag {1}
$$

$$
\mathrm {V} _ {N P} = \frac {4}{3} \pi \left(\frac {H D _ {N P}}{2}\right) ^ {2} \tag {2}
$$

$$
\mathbf {A} _ {v} = \frac {m _ {P L A} \times S _ {N P}}{V _ {N P} \times \rho_ {P L A}} \tag {3}
$$

3

The "molecular weight" $(M_{\nu})$ of one vesicle was then determined, which allowed calculating the number of lipids per vesicle and, thus, the required lipid concentration, using Clarke and Apell's equation (Clarke and Apell, 1989).

$$
\mathrm {M} _ {\nu} = \frac {\mathcal {N} \left[ \frac {4}{3} \pi \left(\mathrm {r} _ {0} ^ {3} - \mathrm {r} _ {1} ^ {3}\right) \right]}{\bar {\nu}} \tag {4}
$$

Where $\mathcal{N}$ is Avogadro's constant, $\mathrm{r_o}$ and $\mathrm{r_i}$ , the external and internal radii of the lipid bilayer - considering the thin water layer $(\mathrm{T_w})$ of about $2\mathrm{nm}$ and lipid bilayer thickness $(\mathrm{T_b})$ of $\sim 4\mathrm{nm}$ and $\bar{\nu}$ , the partial specific volume of the lipid used. $\mathrm{r_o}$ and $\mathrm{r_i}$ , were estimated to be 90 $(\mathrm{r_o = R_{NP} + T_w + T_b})$ and $86\mathrm{nm}$ $(\mathrm{r_i = R_{NP} + T_w})$ respectively. The value of the partial specific volume of POPC ( $\bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{POPC}}$ ) is $0.987 - 1.0~\mathrm{mL / g}$ (Etklotz and Seelig, 2002; Hianik et al., 1998). Since $\bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{DOTAP}}$ was not found, $M_{\nu}$ was calculated with $\bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{POPC}} = 1~\mathrm{mL / g}$ .

The number of lipid molecules per vesicle $(\mathrm{N}_{LV})$ was determined from $\mathbf{M}_v$ and the molecular weight of POPC and DOTAP mixture $(\mathbf{M}_{PL})$ taking into account their molar ratio $a$ and $b$ , respectively (equation 5).

$$
\mathrm {N} _ {L V} = M _ {v} \left[ \left(\frac {a}{M _ {P O P C}}\right) + \left(\frac {b}{M _ {D O T A P}}\right) \right] (5)
$$

$\mathrm{N}_{LV}$ is related to the number of moles of lipid required for covering a nanoparticle with one bilayer $(\mathfrak{n}_{LB})$ by the following equation (equation 6):

$$
\mathrm {n} _ {L B} = \frac {N _ {L V} \times A _ {v}}{S _ {v} \times \mathcal {N}} \tag {6}
$$

Where $\mathrm{Sv}$ is the surface of one vesicle calculated assuming the diameter of a lipid bilayer to be equal to $2\mathrm{r_o}$ (HDv = 180 nm).

The theoretical concentration of lipid $(C_{TL})$ needed to cover the NP surface by a lipid bilayer is expressed as function of $n_{LB}$ , the volume of the suspension (Vs), the molecular weight of both POPC and DOTAP and their molar ratio, $a$ and $b$ (equation 7).

$$
C _ {T L} = \frac {n _ {L B}}{V _ {S} \left(\frac {a}{M _ {P O P C}} + \frac {b}{M _ {D O T A P}}\right)} \tag {7}
$$

4

Finally, the equivalent number of lipid bilayers (n) was estimated from the theoretical lipid concentration $(\mathrm{C}_{TL})$ and lipid recovery quantified by HPLC-ELSD $(\mathbf{C}_Q)$ (equation 8);

$$
n = \frac {C _ {Q}}{C _ {T L}} \tag {8}
$$

This method for determining the number of bilayers may have some biases. Firstly, we didn't know the real value of $\bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{DOTAP}}$ . However, since the partial specific volume of DOPC is 0.999 mL/g (Nagle et al., 2009) and DOTAP headgroup is believed to be less bulky than that of POPC (Gurtvenko et al., 2004), $\bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{DOTAP}}$ is unlikely to significantly impact the result. Secondly, our calculation assumed that all NPs in the suspension had a unique size of $170~\mathrm{nm}$ which was not completely true, although DLS analysis indicated that the particles were monodisperse.
