# Universal Fluorescence Enhancement Substrate Based on Multiple Heterostructure Photonic Crystal with Super-Wide Stopband and Highly Sensitive Cr(VI) Detecting Performance

Lijing Zhang, Jie Wang, Shengyang Tao, $^{*}$ Chong Geng, and Qingfeng Yan

Universal fluorescence (FL) enhancement is achieved based on a multiple heterostructure photonic crystal (MHPC) with a super-wide stopband. This MHPC film is fabricated by layer-by-layer deposition of annealed colloidal crystal monolayers with gradient sizes, and it displays a broadened stopband (from 280 to $650~\mathrm{nm}$ ) that can overlap with the excitation wavelength $(E)$ and the emission wavelength $(F)$ of most commonly used fluorescent media. More than 100-fold FL enhancement of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_3\mathrm{Cl}_2]$ , rhodamine 6G (Rh6G), and rhodamine B (RhB) on the MHPC is observed compared to that on a glass substrate. This super-wide stopband MHPC may find significant applications for augmenting FL intensity in chemical and biochemical sensing, imaging, disease diagnosis, and environmental monitoring. The function of MHPC/rhodium B hydrazide composites is further demonstrated in the semiquantitative detection of $\mathrm{Cr(VI)}$ , and the limit of detection of $\mathrm{Cr(VI)}$ is effectively improved to 0.2 ppb.

PCs can achieve FL enhancement in two ways: enhancing emission by matching the bandgap position with the fluorophore excitation wavelength (E) and enhancing the emission by blue bandgap matching the emission wavelength (F) or the Bragg mirror effect. $^{[17]}$ Zhou et al. $^{[18]}$ observed a 320-fold luminescence enhancement for $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ dispersed on a PMMA PC with stopband matching with the E, and a 71-fold enhancement of rhodamine B (RhB) was observed by Tao et al. $^{[19]}$ Li et al. $^{[20]}$ demonstrated a PC-based light-amplification method for ultra-sensitive DNA detection with stopband matching of the donor emission and acceptor absorbance. Song and co-workers $^{[21]}$ reported a 162-fold enhancement of Nile Red based on a heterostructure PC with dual stopbands overlapping the E and F of Nile Red. Eftekhari et al. $^{[22]}$ demonstrated an extraordinary FL amplification of RhB with a 3D E–F–E double heterostructure, which provided higher FL enhancement than monolithic E-PCs and F-PCs. However, the abovementioned PC architectures are effective only for a particular fluorescent medium. For different dyes, the stopband structure and position of the PC must be specifically prepared and regulated. On the one hand, a PC with a broadened stopband can overlap with both the excitation and emission maxima, thereby achieving simultaneous amplification of the excitation of incident light and enhancement of the extraction of light generated by fluorescent media. On the other hand, if this stopband is wide enough to cover the E and F of most dyes, it can serve as a universal FL enhancement substrate. Therefore, novel PC architectures with broadened stopbands should be explored further to obtain more remarkable and extensive FL enhancement.

Heterostructure PCs with a broadened stopband have been obtained by sequentially stacking two or three colloidal crystals with appropriate overlapping stopbands. Zhang et al. $^{[23]}$ have theoretically shown that the reflection bands of PCs can be substantially enlarged as desired by using multiple heterostructures. Jiang et al. $^{[24]}$ found that overlapping crystals with similar sphere sizes can create a broader stopband, producing a composite film that inhibits light propagation over a wider range of wavelengths. Yan et al. $^{[25]}$ also fabricated heterostructure PCs with a wide stopband by sequential layer transfer of colloidal crystals with small difference in sphere size. In our previous work, $^{[26]}$ we have studied the quantitative conditions for the preparation of heterostructure PCs with broadened stopbands.

Herein, we demonstrate a novel multiple heterostructure photonic crystal (MHPC) structure that consists of consecutive $(2 + 1)$ D colloidal crystals with gradient diameters from bottom to top, as illustrated in Figure 1. Owing to this novel configuration, the incident light will be reflected at each colloidal crystal interface and interfere with each other to form a broadened stopband. This novel structure displayed an extraordinary ability to enhance the FL signal for most commonly used fluorescent media. More than 100-fold FL enhancement of $[Ru(dpp)_{3}Cl_{2}]$ , rhodamine 6G (Rh6G), and RhB was observed compared to that on a glass substrate. Finally, super-wide stopband MHPC/rhodamine B hydrazide (RBH) composites were used as a FL sensing enhancement platform for semiquantitative detection of Cr(VI) in acid aqueous solution and effectively improved the LOD of Cr(VI).

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Structural and Optical Features of MHPCs

The crystalline quality is among the most important parameters in determining the performance of a heterostructure PC in optical application. In this study, the heterostructure PCs were all fabricated by layer-by-layer transfer and deposition of self-assembled 2D colloidal crystal monolayers (CCMs). In situ toluene vapor annealing treatment was reported to enable the coalescence of PS sphere and result in the 2D CCMs with high mechanical strength, which provided heterostructure PCs with good crystalline quality (i.e., the PS spheres showed a good periodical arrangement in a monolayer, and after layer-by-layer deposition, the PS colloidal monolayers were also in good arrangement). The top view and cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of annealed 245 nm CCM were shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). Figure 2 presents the typical cross sectional SEM images of the resultant heterostructure PCs, which consisted of different homogeneous crystal films in progressive size-descending order starting from 245 nm. Clearly, all the resultant heterostructure PCs exhibited a highly ordered arrangement with (001) planes oriented parallel to the surface of the substrate. In addition, the interface surfaces between two homogeneous crystals of different sizes were clearly observed. Figure S2 (Supporting Information) shows the normal-incidence diffuse reflectance spectra of 245 nm colloidal crystals with different number of layers. When the number of layers reached 6, the bandgap characteristics were fully apparent. In thicker samples, there were no obvious changes in the position and width of the stopband except for the appearance of more minibands and fingerprints. Considering the complexity of the manufacturing process, the optimized film of each homogeneous crystal was fixed at 6 layers.

Heterostructure PCs prepared with different sphere sizes can exhibit different spatial periodicities in their dielectric constants and yield different bandgap characteristics. Figure 3a shows the stopband information and overlapping regions of the constituent homogeneous crystals. The inset of Figure 3a shows the relationship between the stopband position and the PS sphere diameter. The linear fitting results provided $\lambda_{\mathrm{c}} = 2.528\mathrm{D}$ , which was consistent with the results reported in the literature.[27] The key stopband parameters and corresponding $R_{\mathrm{AB}}$ values of the homogeneous films are shown in Table S1 (Supporting Information). The normal-incidence diffuse reflectance spectra of these heterostructure samples shown in Figure 3b provided information about bandgap broadening. The reflection dips located at 218 and $255~\mathrm{nm}$ were the intrinsic absorption peaks of PS spheres, indicating that they remained unchanged during the structural changes in the heterostructure PCs. # 2.2. FL Enhancement of $[Ru(dpp)_{3}]Cl_{2}$ and Its Mechanism

Figure 4a shows the normalized absorption and emission spectra of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ in absolute ethanol solution. These spectra exhibited an absorption maximum at 450 nm and an emission maximum at 620 nm (Figure S3, Supporting Information). To verify the broad stopband effect, we investigated the FL spectra of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ coated on H-IV and the 245 nm homogeneous crystal, while $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ coated on glass was also studied as a control sample. The concentration of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ used in this study was $500 \times 10^{-6}$ M. As shown in Figure 4b, the maximum F of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ coated on the PCs was slightly blueshifted compared with that on glass caused by the stopband modulation, and its FL intensity was much stronger than that on glass, even the luminescence on glass was improved ten times. The FL enhancement factor compared with glass was $\approx$ 65-fold and 25-fold for H-IV and the 245 nm film, respectively. Heterostructure sample H-IV enabled an additional 2.5-fold FL enhancement relative to the homogeneous 245 nm film, which was mainly because the super-wide stopband of H-IV can simultaneously match the E and the F. The optical resonator environment formed by the stopband on one hand coupled with the excitation light and scattering was improved for the modulation through the periodic dielectric constant, on the other hand coupled with the emission light and the emission light extraction was enhanced for the Bragg mirror reflection. In contrast, in the 245 nm PC, only the emission light coupled with the stopband, resulting in enhanced light extraction.

In addition, the angle dependence of the specular reflectance spectra in Figure 4c,d showed that the H-IV with a super-wide stopband possessed improved angle independence. Although minor shift existed, the stopband still had a large degree of spectral overlap with the emission light when the incident angle varied from $8^{\circ}$ to $60^{\circ}$ due to its width, and could couple well with incident excitation light from any angle. In contrast, for the 245 nm film, only the emission light in the range of $8^{\circ}-45^{\circ}$ could be coupled well. This feature further demonstrates that the H-IV sample with a super-wide stopband can enhance the FL intensity in a broader range of light at different incident angles.

To further confirm the bandgap effect, homogeneous crystals with different bandgap positions (392, 454, 502, 576, and $624~\mathrm{nm}$ ) and heterostructure PCs with different bandgap widths (Figure 2) were fabricated. Figure 4e shows the FL spectra of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp}_3)]\mathrm{Cl}_2$ ( $100\times 10^{-6}\mathrm{M}$ ) coated on different samples, and Figure 4f illustrates the corresponding FL enhancement factor of the different samples. The FL intensity of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp}_3)]\mathrm{Cl}_2$ on glass was relatively low, while that on PCs with different stopband structures was enhanced at different levels. PCs with a stopband that coupled with neither the excitation light nor the emission light, such as homogeneous crystals of 160 and $200~\mathrm{nm}$ , had similar enhancement factors of $\approx 30$ -fold. This enhancement was mainly caused by the light scattering mechanism of the PS spheres. When PCs with a stopband that coupled with only excitation light (180 nm homogeneous crystal) or emission light (245 nm homogeneous crystal), the enhancement factor was improved $\approx 40$ -fold, and the enhancement factor of the $E$ -PC (180 nm) was slightly higher than $F$ -PC (245 nm), which was consistent with results reported previously.[21,22,29] For the 230 nm sample, the enhancement factor was slightly lower than that for the 245 nm sample due to the partial coupling effect of the stopband. Compared with that of the homogeneous PCs, the FL enhancement factor of the heterostructure PCs distinctly increased. The H-I sample achieved a 68-fold FL enhancement, which was due to the increased bandgap intensity arising from resonance coupling and the improved angle independence. For samples H-II, H-III, and H-IV, the FL enhancement factors, which were 75-fold, 88-fold, and 102-fold respectively, were mainly caused by their gradually broadened stopbands. With the widening of the bandgap, the heterostructure PCs can not only fully couple with the emission light but also gradually couple with the excitation light, especially the H-IV sample. These results demonstrate that MHPCs with a wide stopband coupled with the E and F can provide remarkable FL enhancement. The overlap between the stopbands and the normalized FL excitation and emission spectra of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ for the homogeneous PCs and heterostructure PCs is shown in Figure S4 (Supporting Information).

FL intensity varies with different dye concentrations. The dependence of the FL intensity and enhancement factor on the $[Ru(dpp)_{3}]Cl_{2}$ concentration is shown in Figure 5a,b. Notably, the FL intensity on H-IV increased gradually as the concentration increased to $500 \times 10^{-6}$ M and then decreased when the concentration increased further. The FL intensity variation with different concentrations of $[Ru(dpp)_{3}]Cl_{2}$ coated on glass substrate is shown in Figure S5 in the Supporting Information. The FL intensity on glass increased as the concentration increased up to $50 \times 10^{-6}$ M and then decreased. The decrease in FL intensity was attributed to FL quenching at high concentrations. As the concentration increased, the distance between $[Ru(dpp)_{3}]Cl_{2}$ molecules gradually decreased, leading to an obvious increase in nonradiative processes and a decrease in the FL intensity. However, when the concentration increased further, e.g., above $500 \times 10^{-6}$ M, the number of nonradiative processes between molecules tended to remain stable, and the FL intensity increased further. Therefore, the FL enhancement factor reaches a maximum value at $100 \times 10^{-6}$ M. These results also indicate that the PC substrate could effectively disperse the dye molecules and prevent nonradiative processes among the dye molecules owing to its large surface area. $^{[12,30,31]}$

In traditional PC structures used for FL enhancement, for each fluorescent medium, the central position of the stopband must be specifically prepared and regulated, which can be difficult and inconvenient in certain practical applications. MHPCs with wide stopbands that coupled with the E and F of fluorescent media simultaneously can realize remarkable enhancement of FL. To verify the universality of the FL modulation of the dye molecules by the super-wide stopband MHPCs, the FL enhancement factors for different dyes dispersed on the H-IV sample were studied. As shown in Figure 6a, the E and F distribution of several commonly used fluorescent media were located in the range of the super-wide stopband of MHPC H-IV.

# 2.4. Highly Improved Cr(VI) Sensing Properties

Based on the FL enhancement by heterostructure PCs, we further researched their function as a FL sensing enhancement platform for detecting heavy metal ions in aqueous solution. Therefore, we demonstrated an example of Cr(VI) determination in acidic water using the H-IV FL enhancement substrate. Cr(VI), a highly toxic environmental pollutant, can cause serious damage to human health and can even lead to cancer when people come into contact with or consume water containing Cr(VI). Therefore, accurate and quick detection of Cr(VI) is crucial for monitoring the environmental and drinking water safety.[32] To detect Cr(VI), the Cr(VI) ion-sensitive fluorescent probe RBH at $100 \times 10^{-6}$ M in 20 μL of ethanol was spin coated on the super-wide stopband H-IV film. Then, 10 μL of different concentration solutions (from 1 to 50 ppb) of Cr(VI) along with $10 \times 10^{-3}$ M H $_{2}$ SO $_{4}$ were sequentially dropped on the surface of the H-IV film with pipettes. The surface wettability of the fabricated super-wide stopband H-IV film was tested, and the film exhibited good hydrophobicity with a contact angle of 133 ± 1°

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we successfully constructed a novel MHPC structure with stopbands overlapping and broadening based on a simple layer-by-layer deposition strategy. The broadened stopband of the MHPC enhanced the FL intensity of $[\mathrm{Ru}(\mathrm{dpp})_{3}]\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ by simultaneously matching the E and the F. This enhancement was mainly attributed to the bandgap modulation and Bragg effect as well as good dispersion of dye molecules. The MHPC with a super-wide stopband from 280 to 650 nm (H-IV) can achieve universal FL enhancement for most commonly used fluorescent dyes, thereby overcoming the shortcoming of requiring specific preparation of PC substrates for different dyes in practical applications. Finally, we demonstrated the function of the H-IV film as a FL sensing enhancement platform for semiquantitative detection of $\mathrm{Cr(VI)}$ in acid aqueous solution. This substrate effectively improved the LOD of $\mathrm{Cr(VI)}$ to 0.2 ppb. Although only $\mathrm{Cr(VI)}$ detection was shown as an example, we found that this FL enhancement sensing platform can be easily extended to the detection of other toxic and hazardous substances. This work is of great significance for practical applications in chemical and biochemical sensing, imaging, disease diagnosis, and environmental monitoring for the augmenting FL intensity.

Synthesis of PS Spheres and Fabrication of High Quality 2D CCMs: PS spheres with different sizes were synthesized by an emulsifier free polymerization method. $^{[35]}$ By changing the amount of styrene (St ≥ 99.0%) and reaction temperature, the diameter of PS spheres could be finely adjusted. The milk white solution with suspended PS spheres was purified with copious ethanol (≥99.7%) and deionized water by several cycles of centrifugation and dispersion before use.

The large-area 2D PS CCMs with high crystalline quality were fabricated by the air–water interface self-assembly combined with the in situ solvent vapor annealing method. $^{[36]}$ The mechanical strength could be finely tuned by controlling the annealing time. Then the 2D CCMs with high mechanical strength were fabricated and ready to be transferred to solid substrates.

Structure Design and Preparation of MHPC: All the heterostructure PCs used in this work were fabricated by simple layer-by-layer transfer and deposition of 2D CCMs with appropriate sphere sizes on to the substrates, as described in the previous paper. $^{[27]}$ Before preparation, a drop of 1 wt% sodium dodecyl sulfonate (SDS, 98%) was added to each glass Petri dish to push out some small cracked films and provide large-area CCM film at the air/water interface. First, several annealed CCMs of A size were transferred to a certain substrate to form the homogeneous $(2 + 1)$ D PC ( $A_{m}$ ). Second, the annealed CCMs of B size were layer-by-layer transferred onto the $A_{m}$ predeposited substrate. The $(2 + 1)$ D colloidal PC heterostructure $A_{m}/B_{n}$ described by listing the sphere size from bottom to top was fabricated. Follow the same operation, dual-heterostructure $(A_{m}/B_{n}/C_{p})$ and multiple heterostructure $(A_{m}/B_{n}/C_{p}/D_{q}\ldots)$ PCs can be easily fabricated by continuing to transfer and deposit other sizes of annealed CCMs. During the preparation process, the thickness of each crystalline subunit and the sphere size of each CCM can be arbitrarily selected and tuned. The substrates used in this study should be hydrophobic treated by spin coating a thin layer of PS toluene solution (0.005%, 200 rpm, 30s).

The Sphere Size Selection and Key Parameters: In order to investigate the influence of broadened stopband on the FL signal, the $[Ru(dpp)_{3}Cl_{2}]$ dispersed in ethanol was first selected as one emitter in the study. The $[Ru(dpp)_{3}Cl_{2}]$ gives off the strongest FL emission at 620 nm under the excitation wavelength of 450 nm. As it was reported before, for the $(2 + 1)D$ colloidal PC, the center bandgap position can be approximated by $\lambda_{c} \approx 2.53D$ , where D is the diameter of PS spheres. $^{[27]}$ Therefore, the E and F matching MHPC was fabricated using PS spheres of 180 and 245 nm.

As the previous work demonstrated, the PC heterostructure with broadened stopband should meet the condition of $0.5 < R_{AB} < 1$ , where the $R_{\mathrm{AB}} = 2(\lambda_{\mathrm{A}} - \lambda_{\mathrm{B}})/(W_{\mathrm{A}} + W_{\mathrm{B}})$ . [26] For the 245 nm/180 nm, R = 1.16, which is out of the range of proper $R_{AB}$ , so the help of other dimensions of the PS spheres is needed as the intermediate component to build MHPCs. Finally, the PS spheres with diameters of 160, 180, 200, 230, and 245 nm were synthesized. All the PS spheres have a coefficient of variation below 3.5%, as shown by the size distribution curves in Figure S11 (Supporting Information).

Preparation of Fluorescent PC Films: The fluorescent dyes were dissolved in absolute ethanol to a certain concentration. Then 20 $\mu$ L of the solution was spin coated onto the outer surface of corresponding MHPCs and control samples at a speed of 2000 r min $^{-1}$ .

Fabrication and Detection of Cr(VI) Sensing Platform: In order to detect the Cr(VI), the RBH was selected as the detection probe. It is based on the oxidation of nonfluorescent RBH by Cr(VI) in acidic aqueous solutions at room temperature, which yields the highly fluorescent RhB. $^{[32]}$ The RBH was synthesized by a modified procedure as Yang et al's. $^{[37]}$ The synthesized RBH was formulated to a concentration of $1.00 \times 10^{-4}$ mol L $^{-1}$ in ethanol solution, then 20 $\mu$ L of the solution was spin coated on the fabricated MHPC sample. The Cr(VI) stock solution ( $1.00 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L $^{-1}$ ) were prepared as follows: in a 10 mL standard flask, 1.47 mg potassium dichromate (K $_2$ Cr $_2$ O $_7$ ) was dissolved in 5 mL deionized water, and then diluted to the mark. When detecting, 10 $\mu$ L of different concentrations (from 1 to 50 ppb) of Cr (VI) mixed with $10 \times 10^{-3}$ M H $_2$ SO $_4$ were sequentially dropped on the surface of the MHPC film through the pipettes. After 10 min, observing the color change and measuring the FL intensity.

Characterization: Microstructures of the heterostructure PCs were observed by field emission-type scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, QUANTA 450) operated at 20 kV. The specimens were coated with gold prior to FE-SEM measurements. The optical reflectance spectra of the colloidal crystal films were measured using a UV/visible/near IR spectrophotometer (Perkin Elmer Lambda 950). The FL spectra were measured by a spectrofluorometer (Hitachi F-7000). Photographs of the colloidal crystals and heterostructure PCs were taken with a Canon camera (70D). The contact angle measurements of a water droplet on the polymer colloid surfaces were conducted by using a Powereach apparatus (JC2000D2, Powereach). At least five separate droplets were investigated. The FL intensity of Cr (VI)-droplets were carried out using the laser confocal microscopy Raman spectrometer (DXR Microscope), and the excitation light is 532 nm.
