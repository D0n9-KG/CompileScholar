# ABSTRACT

Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia caused by Mycoplasma mycoides subsp. mycoides small colony type is a respiratory disease of considerable economic importance in sub-Saharan Africa; control of the disease in Africa is hampered by diagnostic tests which are suited for herd-level but not for individual animal diagnostics. In the work presented we identified 22 potential immunogenic antigens of the Kenyan outbreak strain B237 by using phage display technology.

We determined the relative strength of immunogenicity, the discriminatory capacity between bovine positive and negative sera, and the cross-reactivity with rabbit hyperimmune sera directed against 15 different mycoplasmal species. The three best-performing antigens, a conserved hypothetical protein (MSC_0636), a glycosyl transferase (MSC_0108), and an acyl carrier protein phosphodiesterase (MSC_0029) were considered candidate diagnostic proteins. They were expressed as GST-fusion proteins in Escherichia coli, purified, and used in an ELISA as solid phase antigens.

The diagnostic potential of the recombinant antigens was tested using the sera of ten experimentally infected animals and six control animals. This prototype test resulted in $100\%$ diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. In comparison, the complement fixation test and the competitive ELISA performed with a diagnostic sensitivity of $70\%$ and $60\%$ , respectively.

# 1. Introduction

Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP) is a highly infectious respiratory disease in cattle caused by Mycoplasma mycoides subsp. mycoides small colony type (MmmSC) and solely transmitted by animal to animal contact. The disease is of major economic importance in

sub-Saharan Africa (FAO, 2003) with a constant risk of reintroduction into other parts of the world. Diagnosis is primarily based on serology currently done by complement fixation test (CFT), competitive ELISA (cELISA) (Le Goff and Thiaucourt, 1998) and a lipoprotein Q-based ELISA (Bruderer et al., 2002). Both ELISA tests closely correlate with the CFT reported to have a diagnostic sensitivity of $64 - 70\%$ and a diagnostic specificity of $98\%$ (Amanfu et al., 2000; Marobela-Raborokgwe et al., 2003). For a reliable diagnosis of disease on the individual animal level the diagnostic sensitivity needs to be improved without losing specificity. To achieve this goal, novel diagnostic antigens need to be identified.

Recently it has been shown that this goal can be achieved effectively using a non-lytic phage display approach based on phage M13 and designated as pHORF system (Kugler et al., 2008); it improves the panning and screening process compared to the lytic lambda-based phage display system (Beghetto et al., 2009; March et al., 2006; Kugler et al., 2008). Furthermore, the included open reading frame selection and the packaging into "Hyperphage", a helperphage not encoding pIII protein on its genome, improve the subsequent panning process (Hust et al., 2006; Kugler et al., 2008; Soltes et al., 2007; Rondot et al., 2001).

In the work presented, the phage M13-based pHORF system was used to identify novel immunogenic epitopes

of MmmSC which are potentially suitable not only for herdlevel but also for individual animal diagnostics.

# 2.2. Construction of an MmmSC genomic phage displaylibrary and enrichment of open reading frames

Site-specific mutation of the phagemid vector pHORF3 (Kügler et al., 2008) was performed using primers oMHLacZPro_f, oMHORF3_pme_rX1 and oMHORF3_pme_rXX2 (Table 1) such that the unique Pmel site used for insertion of foreign DNA is shifted by 1 and 2 bp resulting in plasmids pHORF3X and pHORF3XX, respectively (Fig. 1). Genomic DNA of the MmmSC African field strain B237 was isolated as described previously (Meens et al., 2006) and digested using five different blunt end-cutting restriction endonucleases (AluI, Psil, Sspl, HpyCH4V, RsaI). DNA fragments with a size of 100-1000 bp were isolated after separation in an agarose gel using the Qiagen PCR DNA and gel band purification kit

(Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer's instructions, and ligated into Pmel-restricted vectors pHORF3, pHORF3X and pHORF3XX (Fig. 1). The genomic MmmSC phage library was constructed as described previously (Kugler et al., 2008).

# 2.3. Selection of phage encoding immunogenic oligopeptides of MmmSC and production of individual phage clones for screening

The panning of phage presenting immunogenic peptides on the surface was performed using the bovine positive control serum pre-absorbed to Hyperphage in order to prevent unspecific reactivity with antibodies directed against phage-surface epitopes. The pre-absorbed serum was coupled to MaxiSorb 96-well microtitre plates (Nunc, Wiesbaden, Germany) via rabbit anti-bovine IgG (Sigma, Deisenhofen, Germany), and four successive panning rounds were performed as previously described (Kügler et al., 2008). Individual clones were picked from the titration plates of the last panning round.

# 2.4. Sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and Western blot

SDS-PAGE was performed following standard procedures using a Protean II Minigel system (BioRad Inc., Munich, Germany). Western blotting of the SDS-PAGE minigels was done using the Mini Trans-Blot® system (BioRad). The bovine positive and negative control serum and a rabbit anti-GST serum were used as primary antibodies; blots were developed using alkaline phosphatase-conjugated goat anti-bovine or goat anti-rabbit IgG antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch Laboratories) and BCIP (5-bromo-4-chloro-3-indolyl phosphate) and NBT (nitroblue tetrazolium) as substrate.

# 2.5. Preparation of recombinant fusion proteins and ELISA set-up

The MmmSC genes encoding immunogenic epitopes as assessed by ELISA of individual phage clones were identified by database analysis. The respective parts of the genes located between two TGA codons were amplified by PCR using primer pairs with an EcoRI- (upstream primer) or NotI-restriction endonuclease site (downstream primer) at their $5^{\prime}$ ends (Table 1). The corresponding PCR fragments were digested with EcoRI and NotI and ligated into pGEX-5x-2, resulting in plasmids pMSC0029-500, pMSC0108-500 and pMSC0636-500.

Recombinant fusion proteins were prepared as inclusion bodies as described previously (Gerlach et al., 1992) and dissolved in guanidine hydrochloride $(4\mathrm{M})$ . To remove small-sized contaminating material, the solution was washed twice in guanidine hydrochloride $(4\mathrm{M})$ using a $10\mathrm{kDa}$ cut-off (MicroCentricon 10 system, Amicon, Beverly, MA, USA). Using BSA as reference the concentration of the purified protein (desalted by precipitation with trichloroacetic acid [TCA]) was then determined by SDS-PAGE and staining with Coomassie blue. The structure and immunogenicity of the fusion proteins was confirmed by

Western blot analysis
using positive and negative control sera as well as an anti-GST serum.

For use in an ELISA, fusion proteins in guanidine hydrochloride were pipetted in a Petri dish, diluted by adding the appropriate volume of carbonate coating buffer with a $10\mathrm{ml}$ pipette, and used as solid phase antigens on a PolySorb 96-well microtitre plate (Nunc) coating $100\mu \mathrm{l}$ volumes per well overnight at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ .

The coating concentration was optimized by checkerboard titration of the antigen, development of the ELISA using 2-fold serial dilutions of the positive and negative control sera, goat-anti-bovine peroxidase, and ABTS, and analysis of the log-log transformed data. Prior to use in the ELISA sera were absorbed with whole-cell lysate of E. coli-pGEX-5x-2 transformants for $1\mathrm{h}$ at room temperature in order to remove antibodies directed against contaminating E. coli components in the semi-purified antigen preparation.

# 3. Results

# 3.1. Identification of immunogenic peptides

After performing four panning rounds with the positive control serum a total of 147 clones (49 from each of the three libraries based on vectors with the cloning site in three different reading frames with respect to the $3^{\prime}$ pIII protein-encoding ORF) were sequenced. One hundred and thirty four clones contained analyzable sequence and showed an ORF consisting of a MmmSC-derived genomic DNA fragment in frame with both the pelB signal sequence and the PIII protein-encoding ORF. Upon NCBI Blast analysis (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/blast/Blast.cgi) 22 different clones were identified with each clone present at least twice in the random selection of sequenced clones (Table 2).

# 3.2. Selection of clones with diagnostic potential

The individual clones were amplified and the relative strength of serum reactivity was determined by phage ELISA. Eight clones showing a reactivity of more than 30 $\mathrm{OD\%}$ with respect to MmmSC whole-cell lysate (Table 2) were considered to be of potential diagnostic value and were subjected to further investigation. The clones were individually amplified, and the discrimination between positive and negative control serum was investigated by ELISA.

For six of the eight clones (MSC_0029, 0108, 0636, 0656, 1009 and 1062) at least a 3-fold difference in titre was observed between the positive and the negative control serum (data not shown). These six clones were tested with respect to their discriminatory potential using the ten individual positive sera and the six individual negative sera contained in the respective control sera. Four of the clones (MSC_029, 108, 636 and 1062) reacted with all individual positive sera; no overlap in $\mathrm{OD\%}$ -values was seen between positive and negative sera (data not shown).

# 4. Discussion

The control of the continuous spread of CBPP in sub-Saharan Africa is severely limited by the lack of sufficiently sensitive and specific diagnostic tests. In an attempt to improve the diagnostic possibilities we set out to identify novel MmmSC-derived antigens using a non-lytic M13-based phage display system (Kugler et al., 2008).

The construction of phage display libraries in three different reading frames using five different restriction endonucleases with a 4mer recognition sequence was considered to result in a reasonable coverage of the MmmSC genome although the three vectors facilitate the generation of a functional pIII fusion protein only for one of the three possible reading frames (Fig. 1). Thus, in silico analysis of the MmmSC PG1 genome revealed that the restriction enzyme digests resulted in a total of 9178 restriction endonuclease fragments of 100-1000 bp in length. In total

When comparing the open reading frames (ORFs) identified with the results of a previous study by March et al. (2006) used a lytic bacteriophage Lambda ZAP Express vector with insert sizes of $6 - 8\mathrm{kb}$ and screened using rabbit IgG antibodies.

Some of the initially identified antigens are intracellular proteins. This is in accordance with many other studies, where intracellular proteins were found to be immunogenic (Kügler et al., 2008; March et al., 2006; Delvecchio et al., 2006; Cho et al., 2008; Kodzius et al., 2003). A possible explanation for this phenomenon could be the release of intracellular proteins by cell lysis or by programmed cell death (Lewis, 2000).

Four of 22 identified antigens were consistently recognized by all positive sera and showed sufficient discrimination of all negative sera. Using rabbit hyperimmune sera directed against 15 other bovine mycoplasmal species these four antigens were tested for serological cross-reactivity which might limit diagnostic specificity. The cross-reactivity of all four antigens with M. capricolum subsp. capricolum appeared acceptable since sequence analysis of the 16S rRNA (Pettersson et al., 1994, 1996) and of five conserved protein-coding sequences (Manso-Silván et al.

, 2007) showed a high sequence similarity and, consequently, a close phylogenetic relationship between MmmSC, M. mycoides subsp. capri and M. capricolum subsp. capricolum, and the other members of the "mycoides-cluster". In addition, it has been reported that M.

capricolum subsp. capricolum can cause CBPP-like disease in cattle (Ajuwape et al., 2004). A. modicum and M. bovis, however, are not members of the "mycoides-cluster
"; since serological cross-reactivity particularly with the frequently occurring bovine pathogen M. bovis is unacceptable for a diagnostic test, ORF MSC_1062 was excluded from further analyses. Immunoblot analysis of the three remaining antigen-GST-fusion proteins showed a strong reaction particularly of antigens MSC_0108 and MSC_0636 (Fig. 2) thereby confirming the initial panning results where these two peptides had elicited the highest reactivity.

The testing of the cohort sera (16 infected and six non-infected) resulted in a diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of $100\%$ each in comparison to a diagnostic sensitivity of $70\%$ for the CFT and $60\%$ for the cELISA (Fig. 3). This result implies that all three antigens are potentially suitable to improve the reliability of current serological testing which is primarily hampered by low diagnostic sensitivity (Marobela-Raborokgwe et al., 2003).

However, the cohort sera used were by no means representative; in addition, MmmSC is known to show a high antigenic variability (Goncalves et al., 1998; Persson et al., 2002) and different breeds of cattle may show a distinct serological response to MmmSC infection. Therefore, in order to support the potential suitability of these antigens for field application, additional sera from experimentally infected cattle as well as field sera from different geographical regions need to be investigated in the future.
