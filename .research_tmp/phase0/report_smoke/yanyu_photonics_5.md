## Landscape

Photonic metamaterial surfaces have emerged as critical substrates for label-free imaging, primarily through two distinct modalities: photonic crystal-enhanced microscopy (PCEM) and dielectric metasurface-based refractometry. PCEM utilizes a photonic crystal biosensor surface as a substrate to enable quantitative, submicron-resolution, and time-resolved imaging of cell-surface interactions without the need for dyes or stains [4]. The system is typically integrated into standard microscope bodies, such as the Carl Zeiss Axio Observer Z1, to facilitate these measurements [3]. In parallel, dielectric metasurfaces offer a platform for label-free sensing through refractometric, surface-enhanced spectroscopy, and chiral schemes [2]. These metasurfaces rely on the physics of optical Mie scattering from subwavelength resonators, which exhibit strong responses to both electric and magnetic fields, a property that distinguishes them from plasmonic counterparts that respond weakly to magnetic fields [2]. The spectral position of defect modes in these structures is highly sensitive to local environmental changes, such as refractive index shifts caused by analyte binding, making them effective transduction signals [1].

## Comparison

PCEM offers unique advantages over other microscopy techniques by providing a direct quantification and two-dimensional localization of cell attachment at the single-cell level [4]. Unlike surface plasmon resonance imaging (SPRi), which requires illumination to pass through cell structures and suffers from limited lateral resolution due to plasmon propagation distances, PCEM avoids these artifacts [4]. Similarly, while atomic force microscopy (AFM) provides information about biological surfaces, it does not directly analyze the interaction between cells and their substrates and is limited in throughput by probe scanning [4]. PCEM is particularly effective for extended time-course studies where label-based assays are not feasible, such as observing cell migration over days [4]. In terms of refractometric sensing, dielectric metasurfaces allow for precise engineering of resonance wavelengths from the visible to the infrared spectrum by adjusting feature sizes [2]. The quality factor (Q factor) of these resonances directly influences the resolution for measuring refractive index changes [2]. Furthermore, high-index all-dielectric metasurfaces avoid the optical losses associated with free charges found in plasmonic systems [5].

## Open Gaps

Despite the advancements in photonic surfaces, certain technical limitations and challenges remain. For PCEM, the method relies on noncoherent illumination of the photonic crystal biosensor coupled with a spectroscopic scanning system to achieve its capabilities [4]. The sensitivity of the system is characterized by photonic wavelength shifts (PWV), with average shifts of approximately 0.12 nm over 12 hours and a standard deviation of 0.09 nm in uniform media [3] [4]. While PCEM can resolve boundaries of approximately 0.5 nm PWV shifts, other forms of microscopy do not provide direct measurements of live cell-surface attachment localization and strength with the same dynamic morphological detail [4]. In the context of metasurfaces, the development of ultrasensitive biosensors faces challenges that require specific solutions to optimize performance [6]. Additionally, while dielectric metasurfaces offer low loss, the integration of these surfaces into portable, spectrometer-less readers is an area of active development to reduce cost and improve mechanical robustness [5].

## Dynamics

The dynamic capabilities of photonic metamaterial surfaces are highlighted by their ability to monitor biological processes in real-time. PCEM provides rich, dynamic information about the evolution of cell-surface attachment profiles over biologically relevant time scales [4]. As cells attach and spread, positive PWV shifts are observed due to the increased concentration of cellular material within the evanescent field region of the photonic crystal [4]. This method retains sufficient spatial resolution to observe attachment footprints of filopodial extensions and intracellular attachment strength gradients [4]. PCEM has been used to enable the first label-free time-lapse imaging of attachment localization in living cells during chemotaxis [3]. It allows for continuous monitoring of spatial gradients in cell-surface attachment and the extension of fine-structured filopodia over extended periods compatible with processes like apoptosis and differentiation [3]. Because the technology is label-free, populations of cells can be measured repeatedly without disrupting their culture environment [3]. In metasurface-based systems, intensity-based readers can detect analyte binding through spatially resolved intensity changes at a fixed wavelength, offering a compact and portable alternative to traditional spectroscopic methods [5].
